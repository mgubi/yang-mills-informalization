# The work items

## What they are

The Lean development is organized around about one hundred stated obligations. Each is a proposition `YM.Bid.Xnn` in the directory [`YM/Bid`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/tree/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Bid), stated for all gauge data, label systems and tables, and each is proved by a theorem `answer : YM.Bid.Xnn` in its own file `YM/Work/Xnn.lean` (sometimes with a folder `YM/Work/Xnn/` of auxiliary modules). The code calls these obligations *proof requests* (`YM.Bid`); this page calls them *work items*. There are 105 of them, in nine families A to I, with 28,654 lines in total (the file together with its auxiliary modules). Three further entries in `YM/Work/` (`MY1/`, `MY3ActiveGeometry`, `MY3SmoothSeedSectors`) are not work items.

The proofs are written as constraints, with docstrings of the form "no such-and-such is assumed", so the docstrings are often negative. The descriptions below are read from the statement of the proposition together with the docstring. The statements are assembled in the files of `YM/Bid` into named structures (for example `Bid.station`, `Bid.Looms`, `Bid.Arcs`) which take the propositions as hypotheses and produce the objects used by the main theorem.

## Why they are a natural skeleton

Each work item states one mathematical fact about the limit theory, in terms of objects (tables, phases, rotation actions, Hilbert spaces) that are defined elsewhere, and has a self-contained proof. Parts IV to VI of the proof (infinite volume; phases and reconstruction; symmetry) are covered almost entirely by the families D, B, C and A, so the work items give a ready division of an exposition of those parts into statements of moderate size, with the dependencies between them stated explicitly. The families E to I belong to Part III, the renormalization group; they have the same form and are listed here for completeness, but they cover only a part of that construction, namely the estimates for one renormalization-group step.

The families build on each other in the following order within the limit theory: D (the limit table, with derivatives) comes first; then B (phases, positivity in each phase); then C (reconstruction, for each phase); and A (Ward identity, rotations, Lorentz group), where the boost items A16 and A17 enter C09 and the rotation layer A10–A15 feeds B06 through the rotation and orientation data.

Readings of the statements are marked *certain*, *probable* or *tentative* in the sense of the [glossary](../glossary.md): certain where the statement is short and self-explanatory, probable where the statement and docstring were read but not the full proof, tentative where the vocabulary (the code's nicknames) is not resolved. Lean names in code spans are explained in the [glossary](../glossary.md) where they occur there.

## Family descriptions

Families are listed in the order in which they build on each other in the limit theory (D, B, C, A), followed by the renormalization-group families E to I.

### Family D: infinite-volume extraction (Part IV)

D01–D06, 6 items, 1,035 lines. These show that the ordered limit (first the cutoff, then the volume, along a chosen subsequence) is a limit of the lattice tables in a sense strong enough to inherit smoothness. D01–D03 give the coefficient budget, control of the discrepancy between separated tuples and smeared test functions, and convergence of one-slot forward differences to derivatives of the limit kernel. D04 assembles them into `Org.Arrives`: the limit table is a spanning limit, so label derivatives pass to the limit. D05–D06 give the one-cap derivative bound and the compatibility of the limit differential system with reflection and signed symmetries; these are used by `Bid.healthy` to show that the limit table satisfies the Euclidean hierarchy and the orientation law. The family is small: the uniform-in-volume estimates (compactness, Arzelà–Ascoli) are elsewhere.

### Family B: phase decomposition and Osterwalder–Schrader positivity (Part V)

B01–B18, 18 items, 3,603 lines. This family builds the Osterwalder–Schrader Hilbert space structure of the mixed limit theory and splits it into clustering phases. B01–B03 give the time-slab word operators (`Spread`), their norm laws, and the `Theme` (permutation invariance and linearity through the field quotient). B04–B05 pass from lattice grids to continuum points and prove reflection invariance of the phases; B06 upgrades this to invariance under all of O(4). B07 produces the phase decomposition itself: the Gelfand characters of the commutative algebra of vacuum-compressed operators (`Garland`), giving S^mix = ∫ S^ξ. B08–B14 then show that every phase inherits reflection positivity, derivatives and stress laws, the one-cap smoothness bound, separability and the operator bound. B15–B18 supply estimates on cutoff families (cutoff trains with bounded moments, polynomial limits, convergence of character moments, corrected detectors) used to move cluster operators between the cutoff and the limit; they sit at the boundary of Parts IV and V. All are assembled by `Bid.Looms` into `Phase.Loom`, hence into `Materializes` (via `Phase.Loom.cleanse`); B15–B18 first pass through `Bid.episode` and `Bid.fees`. Internal chain: B04 → B05; B06 → B07, B09; B15–B18 are used jointly.

### Family C: Osterwalder–Schrader to Wightman reconstruction (Part V)

C01–C29, 29 items, 6,193 lines. This family proves analytic continuation of the Schwinger functions to Wightman distributions with Poincaré covariance and locality, for any table with the Osterwalder–Schrader data. C01–C03 construct the Osterwalder–Schrader vectors and the strict-time continuation `Waves`; C04–C06 give its Cauchy–Riemann identities, the absolute-frequency identity and the support in the cone; C07–C09 transfer the Ward boost identities to the real Poincaré action on the boundary values (C08: the Poincaré push on joint Schwartz test functions; C09 combines A16 and C08); C10–C16 construct the open-tube emission (cone thickening, damping, `Aurora`, matching with `Waves`, Lorentz covariance); C17–C21 glue the two tubes at adjacent spacelike pairs by imaginary boosts (the step of Bargmann–Hall–Wightman type), which establishes the input for local commutativity; C22–C24 pass from the Euclidean Gram forms to real-time operators; C25–C29 give the tensor density, nulls, locality (C27), strong continuity and word insertion. The statements are collected by the structure `Bid.Arcs` (A16 and C01–C29), which is proved outright as `Integrated.reconstructionArcs`. Internal dependencies: C09 on C08 and A16; C14 on C10; C26 on C25; C28 on C08.

### Family A: stress tensor, Ward identity and rotations (Part VI)

A01–A17, 17 items, 5,022 lines. This family turns the Euclidean limit table into one carrying a conserved stress tensor and an action of O(4), and then of the Lorentz group, on the labels. It has four layers. (i) A01–A03 and A05–A06 take the Ward premises (`Climb.Pre`: the shell maps p and spin rows Y that appear when the angular weight of a cutoff is differentiated) and prove their algebra: compatibility with caps, the unit, mirrors, shell commutation and transversality; A04–A06 also identify their free-Maxwell scaling limits. (ii) A07–A09 show that the limit rows detect the internal Ward kernel, that the differentiated Ward identity gives the so(4) brackets, and a pure Lie-algebra lemma. (iii) A10–A15 integrate the brackets to a group action (`Port`, with O(4) generated by axis reflections, A13), prove the rotation laws, preserve the Casimir polynomial (A11, A12), and average the stress tensor to a conserved tensorial J' (A15). (iv) A16–A17 do the same for boosts. The statements are assembled by `Bid.station`, which builds the `Org.Twist.Commission` (A01–A08, A11, A13–A17 and A09, A10), and by `Bid.masts`, which builds `MaxwellData` (A09, A10, A12). Dependencies: A06 needs A04; A11 and A15 need A10; A14 needs A13; A17 needs A16.

### Family E: energy and pressure estimates for a renormalization-group step (Part III)

E01–E09, 9 items, 3,370 lines. These give one step of the renormalization group an energy bound per seed. E01 packs macro charges; E02 compares the fine link with the tree predictor; E03–E04 bound the row, column and lane sums of the block kernel; E05 gives coarse-row lower bounds in tubular charts; E06–E08 bound the F/I rows, the departures from affected cones and the chord size on unaffected cones; E09 assembles them, with the coarse-row lower bound `Belly` as a remaining input (`Bid.EBare`), into `Pressure.Teller.Grants`. Order: E03 → E04 → E06; E01, E03, E04 → E07; E01, E02 → E08; E09 last. The assembling statement is `Bid.issues`, proved as `Integrated.pressure_grants`; `EBare` is the one input not supplied by the family.

### Family F: stationary point and Hessian of the renormalization-group step (Part III)

F01–F09, 9 items, 5,259 lines. F01–F05 (with E02) describe the saddle point of the block-spin integral: the tree-slice sensitivity (F01), the analytic uncut main term (F02), the canonical Hessian on the tree slice (F03), the critical branch and its quadratic value (F04), and independence of the choice of gauge-fixing tree (F05). F06–F09 construct the positive quadratic-form data of the stack of towers: repair of the positivity margin on the three-form row (F06), square roots of positive spectral symbols by Laurent series (F07), the stack at uniform widths (F08), and the period-by-period algebra (F09). Assembling statements: `Bid.cospons` (E02, F01–F04) gives `Scene`, used by `Integrated.stationary_scene`; `Bid.one_for_all` (F05) transfers station matching between trees; `Bid.pinning` (F06–F09) pins the horizon data.

### Family G: label polynomials (Part III)

G01–G09, 9 items, 2,865 lines. These show that the renormalized lattice observables (labels) are given by invariant polynomials in the local curvature slots, with controlled cost. G01–G02 give gauge and signed-permutation covariance and analytic recovery of link holonomy from radial connection data (`Peg`); G03–G04 give the linearized plaquette sampler and its equivariant splitting; G05 builds the real invariant slot polynomials keeping the lower degrees; G06 averages them to an equivariant section; G07 bounds the polynomial cost. `Bid.teaching` assembles G01–G07 into `Bid.Learns` (`Integrated.source_sections`). G08–G09 are different: they propagate cost bounds along renormalization-group histories between matched trajectories, and feed the ultraviolet extension rather than `Bid`.

### Family H: fusion of forced activities (Part III)

H01–H04, 4 items, 847 lines. Combinatorial identities for the activity expansion at the start of a renormalization-group step: the gas identity for fused activities (H01), the budget of the merger (H02), the forced-family and move receipts (H03), and continuity in the parameter at a fixed period (H04). They are consumed together by `Bid.Oust.begins`.

### Family I: marker supports and widths (Part III)

I01–I04, 4 items, 460 lines. Bookkeeping for the markers of the cluster expansion: support price by covering number (I01), resale of budgets to price tagged weights (I02, using I01), the geometry of the centred link tiling (I03) and stop-width bookkeeping (I04). `Bid.installs` assembles I01–I04 into `Input.Metered`.
