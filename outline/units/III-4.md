Reading convention. The names are arbitrary (glossary item 7). Words such as "price", "bill", "teller", "tariff", "cost" and "paid" mean *a numerical bound or a threshold in the scale parameter $x$*, not an economic object. "$\forall^\infty x$" is the Lean "for all large $x$", where $x$ plays the role of $\hat\beta_m$ of III.9 (the reference coupling at the level of the step). The code has a recurring pattern: "constants are chosen in a fixed order, then for $x$ large everything holds". This pattern accounts for most of the statements below and I write it once (see §5).

## 1. What this unit proves

The unit is the Gaussian analysis of one block-spin step at the level of the fluctuation integral. After the background field of III.6 is chosen, the integral over fine fields at fixed coarse field is rewritten in exponential (log) coordinates. In these coordinates the quadratic part is an exact Gaussian density with a covariance coming from the step. The unit then proves that this Gaussian covariance is uniformly positive, local, and has uniformly controlled determinant. It also proves that the non-Gaussian multipliers have uniformly bounded expectation under the true law.

**Proposition III.4.1 (exact change of Gaussian sector; the licence).** Fix a step (patch, kernel, scale $x$), a coarse configuration $V$, a choice $k$ of gauge-fixing data, a representation $R$ with inverse coupling $\beta=R.\beta>0$ that is "lawful", and a forest/store $(A,F)$ that splits the fine links into *frozen* links (integrated with Haar measure) and *active* links (integrated in log coordinates). Let $C$ be a positive definite covariance on the active log variables ("Gaussian table"). Put $g=\beta^{-1/2}$. Assume the data are *permitted*: $C$ is positive definite, the shift $m(Y)$ is measurable, the chart ratio is local ($J$ near the identity is the Haar Jacobian of the exponential chart), and $4d<$ chart radius. Then all displayed integrands are integrable and
$$\int D\cdot\mathrm{exposed}(R)\cdot \mathrm{enters}\,dU \;=\; \mathrm{pref}\cdot J_{0}^{\,n_a}\cdot(2\pi g^{2})^{n_a/2}\sqrt{\det C}\;\int\!\Big(\int \mathrm{dyed}(Y,z)\,d\gamma_C(z)\Big)\,d\mathrm{Haar}(Y),$$
where $\gamma_C$ is the centred Gaussian of covariance $C$, $n_a$ is the number of active log coordinates, $\mathrm{dyed}$ is the integrand written in the variable $a=m(Y)+g z$ (the Gaussian density has been divided out, the non-quadratic multiplier remains), and $J_0$ is the scale of the chart.
*Status:* theorem, with the hypotheses above carried as the structure `Permitted` and the licence conclusion as `Licenses` (the one displayed statement of the licence).

Standard mathematics.
- Gaussian integration with a general covariance, change of variables, and Schur-complement identities (Reed–Simon, Glimm–Jaffe ch. 1).
- Exponential decay of inverses of banded or local positive matrices (Demko–Moss–Smith 1984; Combes–Thomas estimate); Neumann series for inverses of perturbations (see `inverse_perturb`).
- $\log\det$ as a convergent series of traces: $\log\det(1+S)=\sum_{n\ge1}(-1)^{n+1}\mathrm{tr}S^n/n$ for $\|S\|<1$.
- Balaban's treatment of the fluctuation integral: propagators with exponential decay and the extraction of the Gaussian from the effective action in the small-field region (Balaban, *Propagators and renormalization transformations for lattice gauge theories I, II*, CMP 95, 96 (1984); *Averaging operations*, CMP 98 (1985); *Renormalization group approach to lattice gauge field theories I*, CMP 109 (1987)); Dimock's exposition in *The renormalization group according to Balaban I–III* (2013–2014). References from memory; to be checked by the writer.

- The exponents $x^{-1/8}$, $x^{-1/24}$ for the determinant errors, $x^{-5}$ for the tails and $x^{-11/10}$ for the allowance appear explicitly in `foreshadows` and are not derived from a more conceptual rate; they are chosen to make the sums close.

## 3. Strategy

The method is Balaban's: after blocking, the fluctuation integral over fine fields at fixed coarse field is evaluated around the background field. In the small-field region the action is $\tfrac12\beta\langle a, Qa\rangle$ plus cubic and quartic terms. The proof extracts the exact Gaussian measure with covariance $C=Q^{-1}$, rescaled by $g=\beta^{-1/2}$, and treats the remainder as a bounded multiplier. What is different in the Lean is the *order of operations and the care taken with the exact integral*. The link variables are first split into frozen links (kept with Haar measure, because their field has been integrated out earlier in the step or lies in the large-field region) and active links. The active links are written as $\exp$ of a Lie algebra coordinate. The Jacobian of the exponential map is moved into the multiplier (`reserve`, `doors`, `J`). Then `at_price` gives the exact identity
$$\int f(a)\,da=\sqrt{(2\pi g^2)^{n_a}\det C}\;\int e^{\frac12\langle z,C^{-1}z\rangle}f(m+gz)\,d\gamma_C(z),$$
so that the integrand `dyed` is a bounded function times an explicit exponential. The conclusion is exact (an equality, not an estimate) at fixed volume and fixed $(A,F,V,k)$, and it is "licensed" without assuming any locality; the quantitative estimates are then supplied separately.

The quantitative part has three strands. (a) The covariance $C$, the inverse of the step's Gram form, is shown to be uniformly positive with exponentially decaying inverse, by Neumann-series arguments on Schur-type weighted norms ("Pours"), and its window-compressed version is within $e^{-vr}$ of $C$; the point is that all constants are independent of the volume. (b) The determinant $\det C$ and the constant factor in front of the integral are controlled through the trace series: $\log\det$ is a sum over sites of local terms (letters) plus small tails, which gives the kept-early log-determinant as a sum of local rents, with errors $x^{-1/24}$ and $x^{-1/8}$. This is the quantitative form of "the free energy density is local" and is used by III.11 to compare with the free Gaussian measure. (c) Expectations of the polynomial and ratio multipliers under the true Gaussian law are bounded by the same Gaussian-table data (`same_law`, the Schur-type "bills"). Jets of all block coefficients (predictor, kernel, covariance) are bounded uniformly near the background, using the real-analyticity of the exponential chart and a single gauge branch (`chosen_branch`).

## 5. Where the difficulty lies

- **Mathematically heavy and standard in spirit.** The analytic core is the uniform control of the inverse of a local, gapped quadratic form on a growing lattice: positivity, exponential decay of the inverse, windows that approximate the inverse, determinants as local sums. This is the same input as in Balaban's propagator estimates, done here once, with constants that do not depend on the volume. The Lean organises it as Schur-type weighted norms ("Pours") with explicit tracking of rates $s\le\sigma$ and constants; each use of the Neumann series degrades the rate, which explains the repeated "slightly weaker rate" in the statements.
- **Hypothesis bundles are most of the text.** The statements `foreshadows`, `committed`, `book`, `geared` have ten or more hypotheses. Most are the package `Teller`/`Certified`/`Chips`/`Helps` of numerical conditions in $x$. A written proof should state this package once ("for $x\ge x_0$ and all admissible steps the following list of bounds holds") and refer to it. Without that, the dependence structure is unreadable.
- **The licensing is deliberately exact.** `authorized_change` is an *identity* with absolute integrability on the *actual* sector integral, valid even when some stores are not realizable. The module docstrings say explicitly that no locality, chain, or small-field assumption enters here. This is the right design (it separates the algebra from the estimates), but it means the statement is long. Integrability for the stores whose flags disagree with $V$ is not covered by `Licenses`; they enter through `displaced_wrong` (not read).
- **Determinants.** The kept-early log-determinant is the quantity III.11 needs to compare with the free Gaussian. The result is a decomposition into rents, whose negativity and size $\alpha\,\mathrm{hot}(z)^2$ per share is what makes the interacting measure have a determinant at most that of the free measure up to $e^{O(x^{-1/24})}$ per unit. This is the unit's most delicate quantitative output. The exponents $-1/24$ and $-1/8$ look like bookkeeping outputs (the largest exponents for which the sums close), not natural rates; (tentative).
- **Unusual points.** (i) The sector is "intact": the Gaussian is centred at a shifted mean $m(Y)$ that depends on the frozen variables $Y$, so the integral over $z$ is not a plain Gaussian expectation but includes the shift in the multiplier (`displaced`). (ii) The identity-completion convention (Lemma 4.5) means that inactive positions carry a unit covariance, not a degenerate one; this avoids zero modes by construction. (iii) The covariance of the "true sector law" is not the inverse of the Wilson quadratic form: it is the inverse of the *cut*, *gauge-fixed* Gram form on the active links of a tree, which is why so much of the unit compares it with the free head covariance (4.14).
- **What is routine.** The linear-algebra and Gaussian integration lemmas (4.1, 4.2, 4.9, 4.10, 4.26) and the bookkeeping of constants.

## 7. Open points for the writer

1. **Proofs mostly not checked in detail.** The proofs of `authorized_change`, `committed` (first half), `windows`, `same_purchase`, `rate_pass`, `book_price` (first lines), `foreshadows`, `chains` (conclusion), and the statements and headers of about a dozen further results have been read. The remainder (jets, large-field remainders, free resolvent, insertion lemmas, most of the 4,700 declarations) is described from statements and automatic summaries. The summaries of the key statements in the unit page are paraphrases by readers and sometimes use the arbitrary names; each statement should be checked against the Lean before writing a proof.
2. **Exact role of the Gaussian covariance $C$.** $C$ is identified with the inverse of the Gram form of the cut, gauge-fixed active links (so $\det C^{-1}$ is the "precision determinant" of the abstract). The definition of `fog` and `Rows.gram` has not been traced to the quadratic form of the effective action of III.6. A writer should confirm that $C^{-1}$ is the Hessian of $\beta S_{\mathrm{eff}}$ at the background in the log coordinates, and whether the "mean" `meanLog` is the first-order correction of the background (tentative).
3. **Stores with flags that disagree with $V$.** `committed` relies on `displaced_wrong` (and the `Admitted` predicate) for stores not realizable at $V$. Its statement and the role of the "Admitted" condition were not read.
4. **Source of the exponents.** The exponents $-1/8$, $-1/24$, $-5$ and $-11/10$ appear as local choices in `foreshadows`/`drought`; whether they are forced by the structure (for instance by $\mathrm{radiusB}(x)\sim(\log x)^2$ and cover size) or are one admissible choice was not determined.
5. **Interface with the neighbouring units.** The split of the free-kernel packages (`FREE_RESOLVENT`, `FREE_KERNELS`) between III.3 and this unit is that of the automatic classification; check against III.3. The same holds for the `STATIONARY`, `MergeProof` and `V9_JOINT_ENDPOINT_LOAD` statements (`geared`, `sailsV`, `book`, `post`): they produce the post-$L$ bundles from the schedule and probably belong at the boundary with III.2 and III.10.
6. **Part of the unit listing is unrelated.** The page lists `YM/WilsonBulk/*` (Trees, Grid, Vacuum, Agenda, ...), `MultiNext/VACUUM_STABILITY`, `ProofPorts`, `Next/MULTISOURCE` and `RG08/Flat`. Only `packing_bill` and `preflight` (covariance models serving all patches and horizons) have been read; the rest have not. They may be support for the vacuum/bulk comparison of III.11.
7. **Reference list.** The Balaban and Dimock references were written from memory and need verification of volume and page numbers.
8. **Dependence on the volume.** The statements claim independence of volume, but several results ("Pours", "Revenue p B") carry a volume-growth hypothesis (a counting function $B$ for sites in a ball). The writer should check that $B$ is the lattice growth bound of $\mathbb Z^4$ and nothing else is hidden there (probable).
