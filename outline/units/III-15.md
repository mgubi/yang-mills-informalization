**Reading guide.** This unit is bookkeeping. It proves no statement about the measure or the effective action. It proves that a list of numerical inequalities ("costs", "prices", "rates") that the other RG units demand can all be satisfied at once, *after* the block size $L$ has been fixed and *before* the coupling $x$ is taken large. Where the Lean code speaks of money (poster, quarry, quay, wages, pledge, financed), it means numerical bounds in the coupling. The glossary says the same ("not economic"). Section 3 gives the dictionary I used. Several roles below are inferred, not read off the code; they are marked (probable) or (tentative).

Conventions (read off the Lean). The variable $x$ is the *coarse* inverse coupling $\hat\beta$. The finer coupling $\beta_F(x,L)$ at the next level is polynomially comparable to $x$. A function $w$ has **rate** $\le a$ if for every $v>a$ one has $|w(x)|\le\beta_F(x,L)^{v}$ for all large $x$ ([`Rate`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/AnalyticNext/CONNECTED_ACTIVITY_JETS_E6/Numerics.lean#L22-L23)). A function is a **reserve** if $|p(x)|\le M(x)^k$ for some $k$ and large $x$, where $M$ is a fixed monitor function ([`Reserve`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/RemainderComparison/Access/Geometry.lean#L25-L26)). I take $M$ to be a power of $\hat\beta$ up to logarithms (tentative). Rate $<0$ therefore means decay like a negative power of the coupling.

## 3. Strategy

*What the unit is for.* The RG step of III.10 and the comparison with the Gaussian measure of III.11 produce error terms which are bounded by explicit expressions in the coupling, the block size $L$, the cover size, the polynomial order of the observables, and a number of exponents. For the construction to close, these expressions must satisfy a cascade of inequalities whose *order of quantification* matters: first the pre-$L$ constants, then $L$, then the post-$L$ constants (rates, thresholds, tolerances), then the entry values $\beta^*_j$ in the coupling. This unit proves that this order can be respected, for the budgets that arise when one compares the remainder of the step (including the observable insertions) with the perturbative or Gaussian expressions. The title speaks of *cost accounting for observables* because the same computation prices local observables (slots) and the polymer activities.

*Dictionary.* I use the following, from the glossary and from the code: price/cost/fee/bill = a numerical bound in the coupling or in a norm; rate = exponent of $\hat\beta$ in such a bound; reserve = polynomial bound; poster/quarry = a record of numerical budgets, financed = the budgets obey the rate bounds; quay = the statement that all numerical conditions needed by one step hold for sufficiently large $L$; pledge = the same, collected for all stage pairs and fixed before $L$ is chosen; wages = derivative bounds for observables; captain, heroes, quarry (in Halo) = a certificate of the tree/polymer structure of the output (tentative).

*Mechanism.* The two proof styles are the ones used throughout Part III: (1) each requirement is an elementary asymptotic inequality ("a Gaussian beats a polynomial", "$C/L\to0$"), and the proof collects the thresholds into one, using the fact that there are finitely many requirements at each stage pair (the tool is `fromMax` / `eventually_atTop`); (2) each estimate on activities is obtained by combining a hard and a soft population with the weighted-hit sum of the cluster expansion. For the observables, the new feature is that a slot is a *single group word* (path, plaquette, path reversed) projected to the Lie algebra, so its derivatives of order $\le k$ in link variables cost only a constant independent of $t$ and of the lattice size (III.15.3).

*Relation to the literature.* The structure follows Balaban's treatment of irrelevant terms (the sum over polymers, the large-field cost, the "$L$ chosen before the coupling is large" bookkeeping), as in the other units of Part III. What is specific is that the Lean assembles, in one quantifier pattern, bounds that the written literature handles by "for $L$ large enough and $\beta$ large enough depending on $L$", and organises them as a schedule of purchases.

### 4.2 The pair-step rates

**Lemma 4.3 (III.15.1).** Put $\eta=(r-\rho_F)/4>0$, $h(y)=C\,e^{-\eta P(y)^2}$ and $v=h+2\,\mathrm{mild}$. Since $t(y)/\mathrm{gap}(\beta)\ge P(y)/2$ for $\beta$ in the band, $e^{k}\,\mathrm{strong}\le h(y)$. For every $d'$ one has $(1+\mathrm{cover}(y))^{d'}v(y)\to0$: for the strong part because $\mathrm{cover}(y)$ is polylogarithmic in $y$ while $e^{-\eta P^2}$ decays faster than any power of $\mathrm{gap}$; for the mild part because $P^{e}\mathrm{gap}^{p}\to0$ for $p>0$ and $(1+\mathrm{cover})^{d+d'}/P\to0$. Taking $d'=4$ gives $(2B+1)^4(e^{k}\mathrm{strong}+2\,\mathrm{mild})\le 16(1+\mathrm{cover})^4v\le1$ for $y$ large, and $e^{v}-1\le1$ gives the quotation condition.

**Lemma 4.5 (III.15.3).** The word has at most $2\ell$ link uses, all within lattice distance $\le2\ell$ of $o$ (so inside the shifted cover on the healthy region). ## 5. Where the difficulty lies

- **Mostly routine, but very long.** Every individual estimate is an elementary asymptotic comparison or a combination of two sum bounds. The size comes from the number of record fields that must be threaded (about 1,000 declarations, with structures such as `Cash`, `Captain`, `Pad`, `Roles`, `Airstrip` of 10–30 fields each). A written proof should state *one* lemma "all conditions of a step hold for $L$ large and then $x$ large, with thresholds depending only on earlier constants", and not list the conditions again.
- **The only places with analysis** are: (i) III.15.1 (a Gaussian beats polylog covers, uniformly over the band); (ii) III.15.3 (a path-ordered product has derivatives bounded by a constant independent of $t$); (iii) the horizon bound 4.12 (the contraction $S'(x)^m$ with $S'$ of negative rate), whose mechanism is the cluster-expansion tail but whose proof has not been followed.
- **Odd feature:** the role of the hypothesis $\tfrac38+\mathrm{rate}<0$. It is a strict reserve below $-\tfrac38$ for the rate of *every* target $R_{ji}$, and the run provides $-\tfrac7{16}$, which is stronger. A reader should check that $\tfrac38$ is the number the analysis needs and not just what the code happens to use.
- **Slightly stronger than one expects:** III.15.5 is stated for *all* orders, rays, libraries and records at once. The quantifier $\forall L\ge n\ \exists D\ \forall^{\infty}x$ is the point: $D$ does not depend on $x$.
- **Name versus content.** The unit is named "remainder comparison". The code, however, does not state a comparison of the interacting remainder with a perturbative expansion. It only prepares the numerical side of it. See §7.
