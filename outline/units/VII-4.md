**Conventions.**

- $G$ is the compact gauge group, with simple Lie algebra, and $\mathcal W$ is the Wilson family of the construction. Its lattice levels $k=0,1,2,\dots$ have spacings $a_k\downarrow0$. Larger $k$ means finer. The sequence is *gentle*: $\log(a_k/a_{k+1})/\log(1/a_k)\to0$.
- $I_{N,p}(v,x)$ is the renormalized lattice observable of a lattice label (token) $v$ at the site $x$. $\|F\|_{\mathcal T_k}$ is the chessboard root of a lattice function $F$ in the centred tiling of scale $a_k$. Both follow IV.1.
- $S$ is the limit table along the refined extraction order. It has field spaces $F_d$ and the inclusions $\iota_{m,n}\colon F_m\to F_n$ ($m\le n$) of the field algebra. A label list is $a=(a_i)_{i\in I}$ with $a_i\in F_{d_i}$, and its total degree is $w(a)=\sum_i d_i$.
- $\mathcal A$ is the ground algebra of V.1: the commutative C\*-algebra generated by the compressed slab operators $M(u)$ of separated words $u$. Its vacuum is $\Omega$, its Gelfand spectrum is the phase space $X$ with measure $\nu$ of full support, and its characters are $\chi_\xi$.
  - $S^\xi(u)=\chi_\xi(M(u))$ and $S^{\mathrm{mix}}(u)=\langle\Omega,M(u)\Omega\rangle$.
  - Hence, for $A\in\mathcal A$ and $c\ge0$: $\|A\|\le c$ if and only if $|s(A)|\le c$ for every state $s\in X\sqcup\{\mathrm{mix}\}$ ([`glance_contract`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/OPE/Eye/Views.lean#L67-L68), [`Cards.pressure`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/OPE/Cards/Pressure.lean#L89-L92)).
- Clusters and clearance follow Definition 1.13 of the statement.
  - $(x,o)$ is an *$(r,M,\eta)$-cluster* if $|x_i-o|\le Mr$ and $|x_i-x_{i'}|\ge\eta r$ for $i\ne i'$.
  - $y\in(\mathbb R^4)^J$ is *$c$-clear of $o$* if $|o-y_j|\ge c$ and $|y_j-y_{j'}|\ge c$ for $j\ne j'$.
  - $S^s(a,x;b,y)$ and $M(a,x;b,y)$ denote the concatenated word.
- "For all sufficiently small $r$" means: there is $r_0>0$, depending only on the data quantified before the phrase, such that the assertion holds for all $r\in(0,r_0)$ and all configurations and states quantified after it.

## 1. What this unit proves

**Proposition VII.4.1 (separated OPE; properties (O1)–(O2) of the main theorem).**

*Setting.*

- $(\mathcal W,S,F)$ is a family, limit table and final record satisfying the RG hypotheses of Remark VII.4.7, denoted (RG).
- $\mathcal P$ is any coherent prescription (VII.4.6).
- $\mathcal R$ is the Reader built from $\mathcal P$ and the rotation and reality structures of $F$ (Lemma VII.4.c). It provides strata $E_j\subset F_j$, bases fixed by conjugation $(O_{j,\kappa})_{\kappa\in\mathrm{Ch}_j}$ of $E_j$, and coefficients $c_{j,\kappa}(a;x,o)$.

*Conclusions.*

- **(O1)** Fix a finite $I$, labels $a$, $M\ge0$, $\eta>0$, $\varepsilon>0$, $j$ and $\kappa\in\mathrm{Ch}_j$. For all sufficiently small $r$ and every $(r,M,\eta)$-cluster $(x,o)$: $|c_{j,\kappa}(a;x,o)|\le r^{\,j-w(a)-\varepsilon}$.
- **(O2)** Fix finite $I,J$, labels $a,b$, $M\ge0$, $\eta,c,\varepsilon>0$ and $D\in\mathbb N$. For all sufficiently small $r$, every $(r,M,\eta)$-cluster $(x,o)$ and every $y$ that is $c$-clear of $o$:
  - the points of $(x,y)$ are pairwise distinct, and so are those of $(o,y)$;
  - the remainder satisfies
$$\Big\|M(a,x;b,y)-\sum_{j\le D}\sum_{\kappa\in\mathrm{Ch}_j}c_{j,\kappa}(a;x,o)\,M\big((j,O_{j,\kappa}),o;\,b,y\big)\Big\|_{\mathcal A}\le r^{\,D+1-w(a)-\varepsilon};$$
  - hence $\big|S^s(a,x;b,y)-\sum_{j\le D}\sum_\kappa c_{j,\kappa}(a;x,o)\,S^s((j,O_{j,\kappa}),o;b,y)\big|\le r^{D+1-w(a)-\varepsilon}$ for every $s\in X\sqcup\{\mathrm{mix}\}$.

The coefficients do not depend on $b$, $y$, $s$ or $D$. The thresholds do not depend on $x$, $o$, $y$ or $s$.

*Status:* theorem for every Reader on the given final record under (RG). The main construction supplies the coherent prescription and (RG) in VIII.1.

**Proposition VII.4.2 (integrated remainder; item (D8)).** Fix the data of (O2), a measurable space $K$ and $B\ge0$. For all sufficiently small $r$ the following holds.

*Hypotheses.* Let $\mu$ be a positive measure on $K$, $w\in L^1(\mu;\mathbb C)$ with $\int|w|\,d\mu\le B$, and $k\mapsto(x(k),o(k),y(k))$ an a.e.-measurable configuration map. Assume that $\mu$-a.e. on $\{w\ne0\}$, $(x,o)$ is an $(r,M,\eta)$-cluster and $y$ is $c$-clear of $o$.

*Conclusion.* Take for $s(k)$ either the mixed state, or $\xi(k)$ for an arbitrary a.e.-measurable phase map $\xi\colon K\to X$. Then:

- $w\,S^{s}(a,x;b,y)$, $w$ times the truncated sum, and $w$ times their difference are integrable;
- the integrals satisfy
$$\Big|\int_K w(k)\Big[S^{s(k)}(a,x;b,y)-\sum_{j\le D}\sum_\kappa c_{j,\kappa}(a;x,o)\,S^{s(k)}((j,O_{j,\kappa}),o;b,y)\Big]d\mu(k)\Big|\le B\,r^{D+1-w(a)-\varepsilon}.$$

In particular, for every probability measure $\nu'$ on $X$, the $\nu'$-mixture of the phases satisfies the bound of (O2) ([`probability_mixtures`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/OPE/Orbits/Kernel.lean#L52-L58)). **Proposition VII.4.3 (nested OPE, i.e. composition of coefficients over trees; item (D10)).**

*Trees and plots.*

- $t$ is a finite rooted tree whose root is an inner node, and $v$ labels its leaves.
- $w_p$ is the total degree of the leaves below the inner node $p$.
- A *plot* $z$ assigns points to the leaves and an anchor $z_p$ to each inner node.
- Radii $s_p>0$ are attached to the inner nodes, with $\rho_p=s_p/s_{\mathrm{parent}(p)}$ for non-root $p$.

*Admissibility.* $(s,z)$ is *$(r,M,\eta)$-admissible* if:

- $s_{\mathrm{root}}=r$ and $s_p\le s_{\mathrm{parent}(p)}$;
- at every inner node $p$, all positions of the subtree lie within $Ms_p$ of $z_p$;
- positions in different children of $p$ are at distance $\ge\eta s_p$.

*Costs.* Put
$$\mathrm{toll}_{m}(s)=r^{\,m-w(v)-\varepsilon}\prod_{p\ne\mathrm{root}}\rho_p^{-w_p-\varepsilon},\qquad \mathrm{nest}_D(s)=\sum_{p\ne\mathrm{root}}\rho_p^{\,D_p+1}.$$

*Conclusions.* Fix $m$, $\kappa\in\mathrm{Ch}_m$, truncation degrees $D_p$ at the inner nodes, $M\ge0$ and $\eta,\varepsilon>0$. For all sufficiently small $r$ and every admissible $(s,z)$:

- (a) $|c_{m,\kappa}(v;z_{\mathrm{leaves}},z_{\mathrm{root}})|\le\mathrm{toll}_m(s)$.
- (b) $|c_{m,\kappa}(v;\cdot)-c^{(D)}_{m,\kappa}(v;z)|\le\mathrm{toll}_m(s)\,\mathrm{nest}_D(s)$. Define $c^{(D)}$ recursively: each inner child $q$ contributes a channel $(j_q,\kappa_q)$ with $j_q\le D_q$, its composed coefficient, and the output field $O_{j_q,\kappa_q}$ at $z_q$. Each leaf child contributes its original label and the factor $1$. Multiply these factors by the root coefficient for the resulting child labels, and sum over the inner children's channels. The root output is fixed at $(m,\kappa)$; $D_{\mathrm{root}}$ is unused.
- (c) Fix a finite spectator list $b$ and $\delta>0$ before taking $r$ small. If $y$ is at distance $\ge\delta$ from every point of the plot, with mutual distances $\ge\delta$, then the degree-$m$ remainder of VII.4.1 for the leaves at $z_{\mathrm{root}}$ has $\mathcal A$-norm, and absolute value in every state, at most $\mathrm{toll}_{m+1}(s)$.
- (d) Fix a measurable space $K$. For each small $r$, keep the radii $s$ fixed throughout the integration. Let $\mu$ be a positive measure on $K$ and $q\in L^1(\mu;\mathbb C)$ with $\int|q|\,d\mu\le B$. Let the plot, and in (c) the spectators, be a.e.-measurable maps satisfying the stated geometry a.e. where $q\ne0$. The direct and composed coefficients are integrable after multiplication by $q$; their integral and the difference of their integrals satisfy (a)–(b) with an additional factor $B$. The tested remainder has the integrated conclusion of VII.4.2 with bound $B\,\mathrm{toll}_{m+1}(s)$, for the mixed state or any a.e.-measurable phase map. The thresholds are uniform in $s,\mu,q,B$ and the configuration maps.

**Proposition VII.4.4 (contact extensions; items (D2), (D11)–(D13)).**

*Setting.*

- $\mathcal M$ is a system of contact devices on the final record. Its prescription is the Reader's ($\mathcal M.\mathrm{basis}=\mathcal P$; this is (D11)) and its rotation action is that of $F$.
- (RG) holds.
- For labels $a_1,\dots,a_k$ of degrees $d_i$ and a state $s$:
  - let $\mathrm{cl}(n)>0$ be the clearance of the devices up to degree $n$;
  - put $B_0=\min(1,\mathrm{cl}(w(a)))$ and fix a subtraction length $s_0\in(0,B_0/2]$; the public form uses $s_0=B_0/4$;
  - let $\mathcal D^s_a(f)=\int\langle\mathrm{full}\rangle_s(f;x)\,dx$, the kernel being given by the forest formula of Lemma VII.4.r.

*Conclusions.*

- (a) $\mathcal D^s_a$ is a distribution of order at most $(2+w(a))\,k$. For every compact $K$ there is $C_K$ with $\int|\langle\mathrm{full}\rangle_s(f;x)|\,dx\le C_K\max_{|\alpha|\le(2+w(a))k}\|\partial^\alpha f\|_\infty$ for all $f$ supported in $K$.
- (b) **(D2)** $\mathcal D^s_a(f)=\int f(x)\,S^s(a;x)\,dx$ for every $f\in C_c^\infty$ supported in $\mathrm{Conf}_k$.
- (c) The same construction gives distributions on affine slices $o+P(E)$, where $P$ is a linear isometry whose range contains the normal space of the diagonal of a group $g\subset\{1,\dots,k\}$, and $o$ lies on that diagonal.
- (d) **(D12)** For the admissible stage families described below, the pairing equals the sum over subtraction choices and dyadic shells. Every shell kernel is integrable, the sum of their $L^1$ norms is finite, and the iterated forest sums give the same pairing.
- (e) **(D12)** For the single-block partition $Q=\{g\}$ with $g\ne\emptyset$, use pure dilation in its normal space $K_g$, with $R\in(0,1]$. Put $q_g=4(|g|-1)$ and $f_R(x)=R^{-q_g}f(R^{-1}x_{K_g}+x_{K_g^\perp})$. If $\mathrm{supp}f$ lies in the rescaled stage and $\|f\|_{C^N}\le H$, where $N=(2+\sum_{i=1}^k d_i)(|g|-1)$, then for every $\varepsilon>0$ the integral of the absolute kernel is at most $C H R^{\,u-w(a_g)-\varepsilon}$. Here $u=0$ for the full resolution, $u=n$ for the weight-$n$ coefficient and $u=n+1$ for the remainder after degree $n$. For the last two bounds the stage must also lie within clearance $\mathrm{cl}(n)$ of the $g$-diagonal. For $|g|>1$, tests supported away from that diagonal give the same pairing as the punctured expression, which already includes proper-subcluster subtractions.
- (f) **(D13)** The device strata have bases fixed by conjugation, and the basis in degree 0 contains the unit field.
- (g) **(D13)** For every positive sequence $(f_n)$ there are thresholds $0<t_n\le f_n$ with $4t_{n+1}<t_n$, $t_n\to0$ and $2(n+1)t_n\le\mathrm{cl}(n)$.

In (d)–(e), an admissible stage family consists of a partition $Q$ of a group $g$, affine placements $o+P(E)$, positive subtraction lengths $s$, and block scales $R_i\in(0,1]$. Each $o$ lies on the block diagonals, and the range of $P$ contains their normal spaces. A common ceiling $B\le\min(1,\mathrm{cl}(\sum_i d_i))$ satisfies $2s\le B$. At each placement a common linear frame acts by $R_i$ on every block normal space and preserves each orthogonal complement. The closed stages lie in a fixed ball in frame coordinates and within normal distance $B$ of each block diagonal; for nonsingleton blocks $R_i\le C_1s$ with fixed $C_1$. Original and spectator labels have uniform norm bounds. Spectator positions and labels are continuous on each stage and constant in each block's normal directions. All centroids of nonempty subgroups of different blocks, and all spectators, are uniformly separated by some $\delta>0$. These conditions are the geometric and field hypotheses for the summation and scaling estimates; their constants may depend on the fixed family.

Uniqueness, reflection positivity on overlapping supports, contact Ward identities for arbitrary subtractions and real-time products are not asserted.

## 3. Strategy

A coherent system of probes reads every output degree from the mixed correlation table. For mode $i$, the raw coefficient is $\lambda_i(a;x,o)=\sum_p\lambda_i(p)S(a,x-o;\text{probe }p)$, with each reading set to zero when the combined configuration is not separated ([`Prescription.ope`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/OPE/Splitting/Detector.lean#L80)). Biorthogonality makes these readings exact on single fields ([`sumAll`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/OPE/Splitting/Observe.lean#L27-L31)). Averaging over $O(4)$, symmetrizing the input and output conjugations, and removing lower-degree components gives the Reader's real covariant strata. These choices are fixed before the spectator labels, state and truncation degree.

The RG estimate replaces a lattice cluster by one label $Q$ of prescribed degree $t$. Its remainder gains $(a_m/a_k)^{t+1/16}$, while the label and remainder both pay the factor $a_j^{-w}(a_m/a_j)^{4|I|}$. Along the scale induction, nearby groups fuse and separated groups pass through the next block-spin step. After extraction, exact reconstruction of $Q$ by the probes yields the grid OPE. Reflection positivity bounds decks of widely translated copies by products of chessboard roots, treating a cluster remainder as one factor. The deck limit gives all vacuum moments of $u^*u$ in the ground algebra; full support of the phase measure and the Gelfand isometry then give $\|u\|_{\mathcal A}$. Each spectator costs only its own one-field bound. The resulting norm estimate controls every phase and the mixed state with the same coefficients.

Exponential rates organize the choices of lattice scales. A downward induction in total degree extends the grid bounds to arbitrary configurations: each derivative raises the degree by one, while the distance to the fine grid recovers one power. At sufficiently high degree the crude bound already suffices to start the induction. Bringing the auxiliary scale heights together gives an arbitrary $\varepsilon$-loss; refinement arguments turn these rates into uniform small-radius estimates. Tree expansions repeat this replacement recursively, leaving leaf labels unchanged. Contacts use a forest recursion: each retained weight receives a normal Taylor subtraction of its own order, and the remainder gains enough radial power for integration. Partial collisions are handled by the same recursion on finer partitions. Separately, the slab-operator bound follows by applying the two-scale chessboard estimate to a reflection-positive train and using iterated Schwarz to remove the fixed exterior cost.

### 4.1 Rates and sequences

**Lemma VII.4.a (from sequences and rates to uniform small-$r$ bounds).**

- (i) Let $P(r,z)$ and $Q(r,z)$ be predicates. Suppose that for every choice $r\mapsto z_r$ and every non-trivial refinement of $r\to0^+$ along which $P(r,z_r)$ holds eventually, a further refinement makes $Q(r,z_r)$ hold eventually. Then, for all sufficiently small $r$, $P(r,z)$ implies $Q(r,z)$ for every $z$.
- (ii) *Rates.* A quantity has upper rate $\kappa$ along $T\to\infty$ if $|f|\le e^{\kappa'T}$ eventually, for every $\kappa'>\kappa$. With $T=-\log r$ this means $|f|\le r^{-\kappa-\varepsilon}$ eventually, for every $\varepsilon>0$. Below, “rate” means upper rate unless an exact rate is specified.
- (iii) *Clusters and spectators.* Under $T=-\log r$, an $(r,M,\eta)$-cluster has extent of upper rate $-1$ and reciprocal separation of upper rate $1$. For $c$-clear spectators, both the reciprocal mutual separation and the reciprocal distance to the centre have upper rate $0$.
- (iv) *Heights.* A sequence of levels $m\to\infty$ has height $c$ if $\log(1/a_m)/T\to c$. Every height $c\ge0$ is attained for a gentle spacing sequence.

*Proof sketch.* For (i), choose a bad configuration at each bad radius and refine to a non-trivial filter supported on those radii. The assumed further refinement is a contradiction. Parts (ii)–(iii) follow from $r=e^{-T}$. For (iv), choose the first level whose logarithmic spacing exceeds $cT$ when $c>0$, and $\sqrt T$ when $c=0$; gentleness makes the relative overshoot tend to zero.

$$\bar\sigma_n(a;x,o)=\tfrac12\big(A_n(a;x,o)+\overline{A_n(\bar a;x,o)}\big).$$

- Define recursively $\pi_n=\mathrm{id}-\sum_{i<n}\iota\circ\pi_i\circ\ell_{i,n}$ on $F_n$, where $\ell_{i,n}(\phi)=\bar\sigma_i(\phi;0,0)$.
- The *cut tests* are $\tau_{n,q}=\pi_n\circ\ell_{n,q}$, and the *stratum* is $E_n=\bigcap_{i<n}\ker\tau_{i,n}$.
- The *fresh vector* is $\pi_n\bar\sigma_n(a;x,o)\in E_n$.
- $(O_{n,\kappa})$ is a basis fixed by conjugation of $E_n$, and $c_{n,\kappa}(a;x,o)$ are the coordinates of the fresh vector in it.

*Properties.*

- $\tau_{n,q}=0$ for $q<n$.
- $\sum_{i\le n}\iota\tau_{i,q}=\iota_{q,n}$ for $q\le n$.
- The fresh vectors and cut tests intertwine rotations and conjugation; simultaneous translations and permutations of the inputs preserve the fresh vectors. Coordinates transform in the corresponding channel representations.
- The coefficients are smooth where the probes are clear.

*Proof sketch.*

- On a single field of degree $j\le n$ at the origin, the degree-$n$ averaged reading is $\iota_{j,n}$ ([`checkReal_fixed`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/OPE/Orbits/Real.lean#L81-L84)); this uses coherence and the rotation laws.
- The identities for $\tau$ then follow by strong induction on $n$.
- A complex finite-dimensional space with an antilinear involution has a basis of fixed vectors.

### 4.3 Norms in the ground algebra

**Lemma VII.4.d (norm from decks).** Let $u\in\mathcal A$ come from a finite combination of separated grid words. It has lattice versions $U_{N,p}$ at the cutoffs.

*Hypothesis.* Let $c\ge0$. The lattice labels converge to those defining $u$ along a non-trivial refinement of the extraction. For every $n$ and all sufficiently large translation spacings $t$, eventually along that refinement the lattice moment of the deck $\prod_{l<2n}U^{(\ast)}_{N,p}\circ\tau_{2tl}$ (time translates, even ones conjugated) has modulus $\le c^{2n}$.

*Conclusion.* $\|u\|_{\mathcal A}\le c$.

*Proof sketch.*

- The deck moments converge to the continuum deck correlation.
- As $t\to\infty$, that correlation tends to $\langle\Omega,(u^*u)^n\Omega\rangle=\int|\chi_\xi(u)|^{2n}d\nu$ (B17, V.1).
- Since $\nu$ has full support, the bound for all $n$ gives $|\chi_\xi(u)|\le c$ for every $\xi$.
- By Gelfand, $\|u\|=\sup_\xi|\chi_\xi(u)|$.

### 4.4 The lattice cluster estimate

**Lemma VII.4.f (lattice cluster replacement).** Fix the RG representation, product-cut and transport data of §2 for output degree $t$. Let $I$ be finite and nonempty. Levels $J\le k\le m<m+1\le j<N$ are ordered from coarse to fine. Require $\mathrm{ref}(t)\le k$ and $\mathrm{ref}(d_i)\le j$ for every input degree $d_i$.

*Hypotheses.*

- *Geometry.* Lattice observables $f_i=I_{N,p}(s_i,o_i)$ of degrees $d_i$ sit at level-$j$ sites whose level-$(m+1)$ ancestor is the centre cell. They are pairwise at least $B_j^2$ level-$j$ units apart, where $B_j$ is the polymer scale. Require
$$ (B_{m+1}^2+1)a_{m+1}\le a_J,\qquad \mathfrak c_k^2a_k\le a_J,\qquad 2(a_m\ell_t+\tau_m)<a_J.$$
Here $\mathfrak c_k$ is the supplied cover count, $\ell_t$ is the output stencil radius plus $4$, and $\tau_m=30a_m(\jmath_m+5)$ is the cone-tail bound with supplied aperture parameter $\jmath_m$. All $f_i$ and all $I_{N,p}(z,0)$ are interior to the centre chamber.

- *Weights.* Choose $\alpha_i>0$ with $\alpha_i\,\mathrm{paid}_j(s_i)\le\zeta(d_i,j)$. Here $\mathrm{paid}_j$ is the insertion-size bound and $\zeta$ the allowed RG weight. Choose $0<\alpha\le1$ below every halo allowance from $m$ through $j-1$. With $H=|I|$, require
$$0<A\le\alpha^H\left(\frac{a_j}{a_{m+1}}\right)^{4H}\left(\frac{\alpha}{8}\right)^{H^2}\left(\frac{\delta_j\varphi_j}{8}\right)^H\prod_i\alpha_i.$$

- *Output weights.* Let $\delta_m,x_m,\varphi_m,\zeta(t,k)$ and $c_m$ be the supplied RG constants, with $c_m$ the column count. Write $\mathfrak m_{k,m}(b)$ for the weight obtained by transporting $b>0$ from $k$ to $m$, and $A_{k,t}$ for the output transport map. Require $e>0$ and
$$\mathfrak m_{k,m}(b)\le\frac{\delta_m A}{16x_m},\qquad 8(1+c_m)a_{m+1}^{t}\frac{4x_m}{A}\le e,$$
$$32(1+c_m)b\,\|A_{k,t}\|\frac{e}{(a_k/a_m)^{1/8}}\le\frac{\zeta(t,k)}2.$$

*Conclusion.* There is a lattice label $z$ of degree $t$ with these properties:

- its coefficient size at level $m$ is at most $2e$;
- $R=\prod_if_i-I_{N,p}(z,0)$ is interior to the centre chamber;
- $\|R\|_{\mathcal T_J}\le2\,\mathrm{stakes}(J,k)/b$.

Here $\mathrm{stakes}(J,k)=\exp\big((a_J/a_k)^4\mathrm{treasury}_k+5\big)$ is the supplied per-cell chessboard factor. In Lemma VII.4.g the two coarse levels are chosen so that this factor has upper rate $0$.

*Proof sketch.*

- *Transport.* From $N$ down to $j$, each insertion is transported separately, since the points are separated.
- *Induction from $j$ down to $m+1$.* The carried state is a polymer representation of the RG-pushed generating function, with the points grouped and the representatives spaced. At each step one of two things happens:
  - if the representatives stay spaced, one block-spin step is taken, and the weights lose at most a factor $L_i^4$ per point;
  - otherwise a maximal spaced set is kept and the remaining representatives are fused into it. Enlarging polymer supports to reach a representative costs a bounded factor in halo summability. Each fusion pays a subpower toll.
- *The weights.* Their surviving product contains $(a_j/a_{m+1})^{4|I|}(\alpha/8)^{|I|(|I|-s)}$ when $s$ groups remain. Its reciprocal contributes to the final error bound.
- *Final step.* At level $m+1$, fuse the remaining groups into one variable per cell copy. The RG step to level $m$ splits the result into a coefficient $Q_0$ of degree $\le t$, with its coefficient and remainder bounds, and an irrelevant remainder. Transport to the coarse level gives a factor $(a_m/a_k)^{t+1}$ in the remainder bound.
- The constant $2$ comes from two halves, each $\le\mathrm{stakes}/b$, joined by the triangle inequality for roots.

### 4.6 Arbitrary configurations and exponents

**Lemma VII.4.i (derivative bootstrap).** Fix a finite index set $I$ and levels $j$ of height $d>x_0$. A configuration path $x(T)$ lies in the height-$x_0$ corridor if its extent about the origin has upper rate $-x_0$ and its reciprocal separation has upper rate $x_0$. Let $h_T(v;x)$ be functions for every token list $v$ on $I$. Suppose the following hold for every such list and every configuration path in this corridor, with constants $c_0,d_0$ independent of the list:

- (rough) $h_T(v;x(T))$ has upper rate $x_0w(v)+c_0$;
- (grid) on level-$j$ grid paths with distinct coordinates on every axis, $h_T(v;x(T))$ has upper rate $dw(v)+d_0$;
- (derivative) eventually $h_T(v;\cdot)$ is differentiable at $x(T)$, and its derivative in each point and coordinate equals $h_T$ with that token replaced by its derivative token, of degree one higher.

Then $h$ has rate $d\,w(v)+d_0$ on the whole corridor.

*Proof sketch.*

- Use induction downward on $w(v)$.
- For $w(v)$ large, $(d-x_0)w(v)\ge c_0-d_0$, so the rough bound suffices.
- Otherwise choose a grid path whose distance from $x(T)$ has upper rate $-d$. All intervening points stay in the same corridor. The mean value theorem and the induction bound for the derivative give rate $-d+d(w(v)+1)+d_0=dw(v)+d_0$ for the change.

### 4.9 The slab bound of V.1

**Lemma VII.4.o (point and train bounds).** Take levels $m\le k\le l\le b$.

- (a) *Point bound.* Assume:
  - all points are separated; main–main and main–exterior sup-distances exceed $a_m$, and exterior–exterior sup-distances exceed $a_l$;
  - main points are congruent mod $a_m$ to one of $P$ fixed points;
  - $P\cdot10a_k<a_m$ and $\sharp(\text{ext})\cdot6a_b<a_l$;
  - $A,B\ge0$; main tokens have one-field bounds $A$ at level $k$ for all signed and reflected variants, and exterior tokens have bounds $B$ at level $b$, eventually along the extraction;
  - the cutoff family has the construction properties, $S$ is smooth on separated configurations, and generic point limits hold along this extraction.

  Then $|S(x;v)|\le A^{\sharp\mathrm{main}}B^{\sharp\mathrm{ext}}$.
- (b) *Train bound.* Assume the Euclidean hierarchy and the same cutoff and point-limit hypotheses. Let $w$ be $r$-buffered in a slab of width $d\in a_m\mathbb N$, with $a_m<r$. Let $v$ be a word supported at positive times with buffer larger than $a_l$. Require $2|w|\cdot10a_k<a_m$ and $2|v|\cdot6a_b<a_l$, and the one-field bounds in (a). Then for every $q\in\mathbb N$ the Gram pairing satisfies
$$|S(\Theta v_q\cdot v_q)|\le B^{2|v|}A^{4q|w|}.$$

*Proof sketch.*

- (a) The two-scale chessboard estimate at the cutoff: main factors are in separated coarse cells, and exterior groups in a finer tiling, with no restriction on how many exterior factors share a coarse cell. A translation of less than one exterior cell fits all the margins. Point limits at generic configurations and continuity give the continuum bound.
- (b) The Gram pairing is the correlation of $4q|w|$ main and $2|v|$ exterior insertions at separated points. Apply (a).

**Lemma VII.4.r (the subtraction formula).**

*Notation.*

- $\varphi_i$ is the field $a_i$ at $x_i$.
- For a group $h\subset\{1,\dots,k\}$, $|h|\ge2$: $x^\perp_h$ is the component of $x$ normal to the $h$-diagonal, $\bar x_h$ the centroid, and $L_h=\max\big(0,\ \sum_{i\in h}d_i+1-4(|h|-1)\big)$.

*Definition.* Set $C_{\{i\}}=\varphi_i\otimes1$ and, for $|h|\ge2$,
$$C_h=-\sum_{w<L_h}R^h_w(\Pi_h)\cdot T^h_{L_h-1-w},\qquad \Pi_h=\sum_{\pi}\prod_{\beta\in\pi}C_\beta,\qquad \mathrm{full}_h=\Pi_h+C_h,$$

The kernel of $\mathcal D^s_a$ at $x$ is the $s$-expectation of $\mathrm{full}_{\{1..k\}}$ applied to $f$. Expanding the recursion gives a Zimmermann-type forest sum. The child expressions are constructed recursively first. On the test function, the parent Taylor operator acts first, followed by the operators already present in the child expression; nested operators need not commute.

*Per-group form.* $\big[\Pi_h-\sum_{w<L_h}R^h_w\Pi_h\big]f+\sum_{w<L_h}R^h_w(\Pi_h)\,(1-T^h_{L_h-1-w})f$.

- Put $d_h=\sum_{i\in h}d_i$ and $q_h=4(|h|-1)$. After retaining weights $w<L_h$, the remainder has radial order $O(|x^\perp_h|^{L_h-d_h-\varepsilon})$, with $L_h-d_h\ge1-q_h$.

- Each weight-$w$ coefficient has scaling degree at most $d_h-w$, and receives its own Taylor subtraction.
- If $\sum_{i\in h}d_i+1\le4(|h|-1)$, nothing is subtracted.

## 5. Where the difficulty lies

*Routine but long.*

- The rate bookkeeping (Lemmas VII.4.a, j).
- The $O(4)$-covariant Reader (Lemmas VII.4.c, k).
- The tree combinatorics (Lemma VII.4.n).
- The geometry of normal shells, angular sectors and compact patches.

*Main estimates.*

1. **The OPE comes from the RG.** The basic estimate (Lemmas VII.4.f–g) is a lattice statement: a cluster of renormalized observables equals one observable of degree $t$ plus a remainder that gains $(a_m/a_k)^{t+1/16}$, at a cost $(a_m/a_j)^{4|I|}$. The margin $\tfrac1{16}$ is set by the transport gain $(a_k/a_m)^{1/8}$ of Part III's RG, not by the contraction $(a_m/a_k)^{t+1}$ of the irrelevant part. This is where Part III enters.
2. **Uniformity in states and spectators comes from the C\*-norm.** The norm of $\mathcal A$ is computed by chessboard bounds on decks of translated copies, together with Gelfand and the full support of $\nu$ (Lemmas VII.4.d–e). The spectators then cost only their own one-field bounds, at their own scale, and the bound holds in every phase at once.
3. **The derivative bootstrap** (Lemma VII.4.i). Each derivative costs one power of the fine spacing, so a first-order comparison with the grid gains nothing. The induction therefore starts from high degree, where the crude bound is already strong enough.
4. **One coefficient system for every truncation degree.** It comes from a coherent prescription (VII.4.6) and the strata of the Reader.

*Unusual, weaker or stronger than expected.*

- *Exponents.* The estimates have an arbitrary $\varepsilon$-loss and do not determine logarithmic corrections. The degree $w$ is the sum of the label degrees; the statement does not identify it with a scaling dimension.
- *Coefficients.* They depend on the prescription and the Reader, and nothing relates them to perturbation theory (VII.5, VII.6).
- *Spectator distance.* The remainder bound holds with power-separated spectators, $(r/\rho)^{N+1}$. The main theorem uses only a fixed clearance.
- *Contacts.* The contact extension is an existence statement. Its order $(2+w(a))k$ is crude. Its OPE projection uses the device detectors, with the same prescription as the Reader but separate probe sets.
- *Interface to Part III.* The cluster replacement requires the numerical and geometric hypotheses in Lemma VII.4.f.

## 7. What remains unexplained
1. **RG inputs below the cluster replacement.** Lemmas VII.4.f–g identify the scalar inequalities and the scale induction. The polymer norm estimates inside the one-step representation, fusion and final remainder transport have not been checked line by line. The dependence of their constants, and the complete Part III proofs of these inputs, remain to be reconciled with the stated RG package.
2. **Proofs used through their statements.** The full geometry behind the vacuum deck limit and the two-scale continuum transfer belongs to V.1. The rotated comparison estimates used in Lemma VII.4.k, and the triangular replacement and high-horizon lemmas used in VII.4.n, have not been checked internally. Their uses and exponent arithmetic agree with their stated conclusions.
3. **Contact sector induction.** The global patching argument, subtraction recursion, shell Taylor bound and scaling normalization have been checked. The finer angular decompositions and the full induction through partially coincident strata remain checked only at the level of their theorem calls. Lemma VII.4.r's radial estimate alone does not settle these partial collisions.
4. **Contact projection versus Reader coefficients.** The devices share the Reader's prescription but use separate detectors and a separate graded splitting. An identity between their weight-$w$ rows and $\sum_\kappa c_{w,\kappa}O_{w,\kappa}$ has not been located. No such identity is used in the contact statement above.
5. **Limits of the conclusions.** The results do not assert uniqueness of coefficients or contact extensions, independence of the extraction subsequence, convergence as $D\to\infty$, reflection positivity or Ward identities for the extensions, or agreement with perturbative coefficients.
