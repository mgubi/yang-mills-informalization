## 1. What this unit proves

The unit does not construct the effective actions (that is III.9, with III.5–III.8). It supplies the **estimates on one nonlinear block-spin step** that the construction consumes. They have three kinds: a lower bound on the quadratic energy of large-field configurations ("energy per seed"), bounds on the polymer activities produced by one step (the irrelevant terms), and numerical schedules for the constants. Every analytic statement is of the form "for $L\ge L_0$, then $W$ large, then $x$ large". The thresholds depend on the earlier constants, never on the period or the volume. In the code, a "request to the step estimator" is a `Teller` (a *teller* is one call with its parameters), and "prices", "bills" and "grants" are numerical bounds, as in glossary item 1.

**Proposition III.10.1 (energy per seed).** Let $G$ be the gauge group and let a request $P$ be given: kernel choices, two adjacent stages (clip, width, paths), a step index $j$, and constants $\eta>0$, $C$. Then there is $i_0$ such that for all $L\ge i_0$ and all large $W$ there is $\kappa>0$ with the following property. For all large $x$, every step $S$ of block size $L$ with coarse side $c\ge100(1+B(x))$, every tube $T$ funded by $P$ (two-sided path data with the clips and bounds of $P$), every spanning forest $F$ with the fine configuration $U$ equal to $1$ on its trunks, every finite store $A$ of seeds, every choice $k$ of gauge-fixing tags, and every coarse field $V$ for which $A$ is consistent with $(U,V)$,
$$\kappa\,t(x)^2\,\#A\;\le\;\mathrm{assessed}(T,A,U,V).$$
Here $\#A=\#\text{cost cells}+\#\text{bonds}+\#\text{flags}$ counts the seeds. The right side is the sum of five lower-bounded "work" tallies. They come from the fine-plaquette rows, the interior bond rows, the coarse rows, the coordinate rows, and the linearization defects. Their sum is the quadratic energy of the step integrand, after subtracting the stationary predictor. The Lean docstring says that this is the energy per seed "before tails in output channels", and that "this alone is not enough to start expansion".

## 3. Strategy

The argument is Balaban's: for a fine field $U$ and a coarse field $V$, split the block-spin integral over $U$ into the stationary background field (the predictor, III.6) and a fluctuation. Then bound separately the *energy* of the step and the *activities* of the blocked gas. For the energy, each large-field site, or each coarse bond whose predicted curvature disagrees with $V$, must pay at least a fixed multiple of $t^2$ for the quadratic form. This is III.10.1, which is a non-perturbative lower bound on the sum of five channels (fine rows, interior rows, coarse rows, coordinates, linearization defects). The channels are controlled by (i) packing and charge bounds (E01), (ii) a comparison of fine and coarse fields in a common gauge (E02), (iii) $\ell^1$ row/column bounds for the kernels with locality (E03, E04), (iv) coarse-row lower bounds in tubular charts (E05), (v) fine-row lower bounds (E06), (vi) the cost of the affected cells (E07), and (vii) the unaffected cells, where the fields are close to trivial in a good gauge and the energy lower bound is perturbative (E08, E09, `bare`).

For the activities, the energy bound feeds into the cluster expansion of the blocked gas in a form that treats the nonlinear terms as perturbations of the Gaussian: expand over a finite alphabet of letters, price each letter by $\beta_F^{r}$ with a slightly negative power, and bound the large-field events with Gaussian tails (the catastrophe sets). All estimates are taken eventually in $x$ and with $\beta^{o(1)}$ losses (the `Rate` calculus), because the pre-L constants must not depend on $L$ and the post-L constants must not depend on $W$ (glossary item 1). Finally the numerical thresholds for all levels are merged into one schedule ([`pricing_order`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Bid/Energy.lean#L173-L202), `Wait.engine`) with divisible periods.

What is different from Balaban: the whole argument is organised as a chain of Lean "work items" (E01–E09, H01–H04, I01–I04) with explicit dependency, so each estimate holds at fixed $L$ and for a finite list of quantifier shapes. The threshold $t=\beta^{-1/2}e^{(\log\log\beta)^2}$ replaces a power-law threshold (as in III.9). The step is stated for the full nonlinear integrand, not only the quadratic part; the linearization defects are bounded by $C\,h\,\mathrm{widen}$ with $h$ the smallness of the field in a good gauge (see 4.4).

## 5. Where the difficulty lies

- **The genuinely hard step** is the lower bound on the quadratic energy of the step (III.10.1), nonperturbatively in the large-field region and uniformly in the volume. It requires comparing fine and coarse fields in one gauge (E02), controlling all five channels at once, and keeping every constant independent of the period. This takes about 3,400 lines (E01–E09) plus 1,700 (FLUCTUATIONS). The perturbative part, the unaffected cells (4.11), is short and reads like a standard Cauchy–Schwarz argument.
- **The connected-activity bounds (III.10.2, III.10.3)** make up most of the Lean (about 39,000 lines). Only the structure of the top-level proof has been read. They are the Balaban-type estimates "irrelevant terms stay small after one nonlinear step", but written as a chain of dependent structures (`Cash`, `Utopia`, `Captain`, `Herd`) that have not been decoded in full. The scale is large compared with the content one would expect from a paper proof; much of it is repeated derivative bookkeeping for $j$-fold jets.
- **Routine but long:** the numerical work (rates, entry thresholds, `Wait.engine`, `pricing_order`). The statements say that finitely many inequalities of the type "a power of $\beta$ beats a power of $\log\beta$" can be merged.
- **Looks unusual.** (i) The unit title speaks of "bounds on irrelevant terms", but the energy-per-seed statement is only a lower bound for the *quadratic form*; the docstring warns that it does not suffice to start the expansion. (ii) The constants $\kappa$ in III.10.1 are not explicit and depend on $L$, $W$ and the request. That suffices for a stability bound of the form "each seed costs $\kappa t^2\sim\kappa\beta^{-1}e^{2(\log\log\beta)^2}$ times $\beta$" (probable). (iii) `Bid/Energy.lean` still carries the stale comment "red, need prove" on `EBare`, although `Fluctuations.bare` proves it. (iv) The thresholds are all "eventually in $x$ for fixed $L$, $W$" and do not track the dependence on $L$; the pre-L/post-L order is therefore essential and must be recorded in the written proof.
- **Weaker than one expects:** the rate in the annulus bound ($-3/2$) and in the activity bounds ($r\in(-\tfrac12,0)$) is far from sharp; only strict negativity of the rate is needed downstream.
