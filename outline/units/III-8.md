The title names two different things, and the unit has two largely independent halves.

* **Part A, connected activities.** Estimates for the formal "connected" (logarithmic, cumulant) expansion of a Gaussian average of $e^{V}$. These bound, polynomially in an input width $W$ and uniformly in the geometry, the Taylor coefficients in the coupling $g$ of the effective potential produced by one Gaussian integration.
* **Part B, backward cones.** The locality of the RG pull-back of an observable. A local coarse observable pulled back through the block-spin kernel is local on the fine lattice, in a window of controlled size. Pull-backs of observables on disjoint regions factor exactly.

## 1. What this unit proves

**Proposition III.8.1 (uniform polynomial bounds for connected heat propagation).** Fix a heat-order $j$, a coupling order $e$ and a rank $n$. There is a function $P$, bounded by $C x^t$ for $x\ge1$ with $C,t$ depending only on $(j,e,n)$, such that the following holds for every index set $I$, every metric $d$ on $I$, all $\sigma\in\mathbb R$, $\delta\ge0$ and $W\ge1$.

Let $V,V'$ be "eligible" formal potentials and $\Gamma,\Gamma'$ symmetric propagators (wires) with $\Gamma,\Gamma'$ and $\Gamma-\Gamma'$ bounded by $W$ and $W\delta$. Suppose that for all lower inputs $(a,r)$ in the "tap" range (defined in Step A4) the pair $(V_{a,r},V'_{a,r})$ is bounded by $W$ with difference $\le W\delta$. Then the pair of $j$-th connected heat coefficients $\mathrm{burn}_j(\Gamma,V)_{e,n}$ and $\mathrm{burn}_j(\Gamma',V')_{e,n}$ is bounded by $P(W)$, and their difference by $P(W)\delta$.

The norm is a rooted $\ell^1$ norm of the coefficient tensors with weight $e^{\sigma\cdot\mathrm{diam}}$. Hence $P$ does not depend on the number of cells, the period, the scale or the stage.
*Status:* theorem (both clauses unconditional once the stated input bounds are given).

## 3. Strategy

**Part A.** The expansion is organized by coupling order $e$ (power of $g$), rank $n$ (number of free field legs) and heat time $j$ (power of $t$). The recursion is the Hamilton–Jacobi equation in power-series form: $\mathrm{burn}_{j+1}$ is $\frac1{j+1}$ times one Laplacian contraction on $\mathrm{burn}_j$ plus all pairings of two earlier coefficients joined by one propagator line. The unusual feature is the choice of norm: each coefficient tensor is a function on tuples of cells, and the norm is a rooted $\ell^1$ norm in which one leg is held fixed and the others summed, weighted by $e^{\sigma\cdot\mathrm{diameter}}$ of the tuple. Contracting two legs with a propagator and joining two tensors along one line are then bounded with no loss, with a constant independent of the number of cells. Combinatorial multiplicities are polynomial in the ranks. Induction on $j$, with the polynomial class closed under sums and products, gives a polynomial bound depending only on $(j,e,n)$. The scheme is that of the cluster expansion's tree bounds (Brydges–Kennedy; Balaban's "connected" activities) written for formal series. The novelty in the Lean is the explicit polynomial-width bookkeeping, which keeps fixed-order estimates independent of volume, scale and stage.

**Part B.** The coarse field given the fine field has an explicit density. Conditionally on a finite family of site tags (frame choices) the coarse links are independent, each with an explicit one-link kernel. The tag at a coarse site is read from a bounded fine window. An observable local on $X$ therefore sees only the tags and links of $X$, and the pull-back only the fine windows around $X$. Observables on disjoint sets involve independent tags and links, hence the product formula. Iterating gives a backward dependence cone whose radius is multiplied by $L_i$ and shifted by $O(J_{i+1}L_i)$ at each step.

## 4. Steps

### A. Connected heat propagation

**A1. The recursion.** Define $\mathrm{burn}_0=V$ and
$$\mathrm{burn}_{j+1}(e,n)=\tfrac1{j+1}\Big[\tfrac{(n+2)(n+1)}2\,\Gamma\!:\!\mathrm{burn}_j(e,n{+}2)+\sum_{c\le n,\ i\le j,\ a\le e}\tfrac{(c+1)(n-c+1)}2\ \mathrm{bridge}_\Gamma\big(\mathrm{burn}_i(a,c{+}1),\ \mathrm{burn}_{j-i}(e{-}a,n{-}c{+}1)\big)\Big].$$
The first term is one self-contraction (`lap`). The second joins one leg of each factor with $\Gamma$ (`bridge`). The output $\mathrm{burn}_j(e,n)$ is the coefficient of $t^jg^e$ in $\log\mathbb E_{t\Gamma}e^{V}$ at rank $n$. All positive-order coefficients are symmetric.

**A2. Finiteness of the series in heat time.** Call $V$ eligible if $V_{0,\cdot}=0$ and each nonzero $V_{e,n}$ satisfies $n\le e+2$ and $e+n$ even. Then eligibility propagates to every $\mathrm{burn}_j$, and if $\mathrm{burn}_j(e,n)\ne0$ then $e>0$, $n\le e+2$ and $n+2j\le3e$. Each contraction consumes two legs, so only $j\le 3e$ contribute at fixed order and the formal series in $t$ is a polynomial. "Continuation to heat time one" is therefore the literal sum $\mathrm{ember}=V+\mathrm{cinder}$, with $\mathrm{cinder}=\sum_{j=1}^{3e}t^j\mathrm{burn}_j$.

### B. Backward dependence cones

**B1. Conditional structure of the kernel.** The conditional density of the coarse field $V$ given the fine field $U$ decomposes as
$$\mathrm{density}_\beta(U,V)=\sum_{t}\ \prod_{x}\mathrm{perSite}(U,x,t_x)\ \prod_{e}\mathrm{perLink}(U,t,e,V_e).$$
Here $t$ ranges over site-tag choices, $\mathrm{perSite}$ is the mass of the frame tag read in the fine window of $x$, and each $\mathrm{perLink}$ is a probability density in the single coarse link $V_e$. It is a mixture over the frame predictions of the one-link noise kernel $\mathrm{noiseLink}$, which is a Haar-normalized penalty weight. The site masses sum to $1$. Consequently
$$\mathrm{back}F(U)=\sum_t\Big(\prod_x\mathrm{perSite}\Big)\int\prod_e \mathrm{perLink}(V_e)\,F(V)\,dV.$$
*Lean:* [`density_parts`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Push/GRID_APPROXIMATION/KernelLocal.lean#L39-L47), [`parts_back`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Push/GRID_APPROXIMATION/KernelLocal.lean#L87-L97). As the docstring stresses, the coarse bonds are *not* conditionally independent given $U$ alone, since adjacent bonds share a tag; they are independent given the tags.

**B2. Dependence on actual reads.** The frame and its mass at a point depend only on the configuration in the box of radius $\mathrm{reach}=10\rho$. The proof traces every route (travel along coordinate paths, with length $\le8r$) used by the minimizing-frame test and by the fallback test when the minimum is not unique or the branch test fails. ## 5. Where the difficulty lies

* **Part A is bookkeeping, not a new estimate.** The tree/connected-graph bound is classical. What is demanding is the uniformity: the polynomial depends only on $(j,e,n)$, and the norm is chosen so that contraction and join cost nothing in the width or the number of cells. The delicate choices are the rooted norm with the diameter weight, the restriction of inputs to the "tap" range (so that higher-rank inputs at the same order are not charged), and the explicit treatment of differences, which feed the Lipschitz estimates for the RG map.
* **Part B is the only place where the geometry of the *actual* kernel is used.** The patch is not a product measure and the frame choice involves a minimization with fallbacks. The Lean therefore tracks all reads literally, giving a deliberately loose reach of $10\rho$. The cone bound is independent of $N$ and $p$ (docstring of `Geometry.lean`), which matters for infinite-volume statements.
* **Unusual.** `back_factors` is an *exact* factorization, with no error term and with the observables only bounded and measurable. In particular it does not need conditioning on large-field events. This is stronger than the usual approximate decoupling. It is plausible here because the kernel is a mixture of product kernels over independent tags, but the writer should check that the tag structure really gives independence of site tags for the actual choice of frames (the hypothesis `Branches`).
* **Weaker than one might expect.** Part A bounds only fixed-order coefficients with polynomial dependence on $W$; there is no resummation and no control of the series in $g$. Part B gives cones, not decay: there is no statement about decay of the pull-back away from its cone.
