Conventions. Levels are indexed from coarse to fine, as in III.9.

  • Step and couplings. One step \(S\) goes from a fine torus of side \(f = cL\) to a coarse torus of side \(c\), with odd block factor \(L \geqslant 3\). Write \(x\) for the coarse reference coupling \(\hat{\beta}_m\). The fine coupling is \(\beta_F = x + b_G \log L = \hat{\beta}_{m + 1}\) (clock_next), where \(b_G \geqslant 0\) is the one-loop slope of III.9; \(b_G > 0\) when the Lie algebra of \(G\) is simple.

  • Thresholds, radius and cutoff.

\(\displaystyle t_c (x) = x^{- 1 / 2} e^{(\log \log x)^2} \text{(coarse field)}, \qquad t = t_c (\beta_F) \text{(fine field)}, \qquad T = \beta_F^{- 49 / 100} \text{(large field proper)},\)

with cluster radius \(B = \lceil (\log x)^2 \rceil\) and path-length cutoff \(R = \lfloor B / 10^5 \rfloor\).

  • Sizes. Since \(e^{(\log \log \beta)^2} = \beta^{o (1)}\), we have \(t = \beta_F^{- 1 / 2 + o (1)} \ll T\). On the other hand \(\beta_F t^2 = e^{2 (\log \log \beta_F)^2}\) grows faster than every power of \(\log \beta_F\).

  • Field sizes. \(\chi (g)\) is the chordal distance from \(g \in G\) to \(1\). For a plaquette \(p\), \(|U_p | = \chi (U_{\partial p})\). For a coarse bond \(b\), \(w_b = \chi (V_b Q_b^{- 1})\) measures how far the coarse field \(V\) is from the block-spin predictor \(Q = Q (U, k)\) of the fine field \(U\) with gauge-fixing tags \(k\) (III.1).

  • "For all large \(x\)" means \(x \geqslant x_0\), with \(x_0\) independent of the period and volume. Other dependencies are specified in each statement.

1. What this unit proves

One-step energy, activity, derivative and Gaussian bounds support the effective-action construction of III.9; III.10.6 supplies start-up fusion and marker estimates.

Data of a step.

  • Request. A request \(P\) (Teller) consists of:

    • kernel choices \(K\), with penalty \(a > 0\) and cuts;

    • a fine stage and a coarse stage, each with clip constants \(\alpha, \lambda, \rho > 0\) satisfying \(\lambda < \alpha / 16\) (Clip), a path width and a path budget;

    • a step index \(j\) and constants \(\eta > 0\), \(C\).

  • Tube. A tube \(\mathscr{T}\) (Tube) carries the towers \(\Phi, \Phi', N\) and the fine and coarse path data. \(\Phi\) maps coarse plaquette coordinates to fine plaquette coordinates; \(\Phi'\) maps them to coarse-bond ("interior") coordinates; \(N\) maps them to gauge coordinates.

  • Admissibility. A step with tube \((S, \mathscr{T})\) is admissible for \(P\) (Teller.Funds) if:

    • \(S\) uses \(K\) and index \(j\);

    • the patch is well posed (Branches: unique minimizers whenever the frame or predictor selector uses minimization, and a nonempty set of eligible axial tags for every configuration);

    • the scalar tower coefficients have exponentially weighted absolute sums at rate \(\eta\) bounded by \(C / L^2\) for the fine kernel and \(C\) for the interior and gauge kernels. Finite differences of order \(\ell \leqslant j - 2\) of the one-form and two-form cross kernels have bounds \(C / L^{1 + \ell}\) and \(C / L^{2 + \ell}\), respectively (YM.Precision.Towers.Deep);

    • the clips and path budgets are those of \(P\);

    • the flat, uncut tower matrices satisfy the exact identities Towers.Roll, among them the resolution of the identity

\(\displaystyle \Phi_0^{\ast} \Phi_0 {+ \Phi_0'}^{\ast} \Phi_0' + N_0^{\ast} N_0 = 1.\)

The transported kernels cut at length \(R\) need not satisfy this identity; their defects are estimated in Steps 5–6.

Seeds. A store \(A = (A_F, A_I, A_{\flat})\) (Stores) consists of finite sets of fine plaquettes ("costs"), coarse bonds ("aisles") and coarse sites ("flags"). \(\sharp A\) is the sum of their cardinalities, including coincident seeds of different types. \(D_r (A)\) is the coarse \(r\)-neighbourhood of their positions: all parent sites touched by a fine plaquette, both endpoints of a coarse bond, and each flag site. \(A\) is consistent with \((U, k, V)\) at \((x, W)\) (Plot) if the following hold:

  • whenever a parent site touched by \(p\) meets \(D_{5 B} (A)\), \(p \in A_F\) implies \(|U_p | \geqslant t / 4\), and \(p \not\in A_F\) implies \(|U_p | \leqslant t / 2\);

  • whenever an endpoint of \(b\) meets \(D_{5 B} (A)\), \(b \in A_I\) implies \(w_b \geqslant Wt\), and \(b \not\in A_I\) implies \(w_b \leqslant 2 Wt\);

  • \(A_{\flat}\) agrees with the flags of \(V\) at threshold \(t_c (x)\) on \(D_{5 B} (A)\);

  • each site tag based in \(D_{5 B} (A)\) has positive frame weight.

Nothing is assumed outside \(D_{5 B} (A)\). The spent seeds \(A^{\text{sp}}\) are the costs with \(|U_p | > T\) and the aisles with \(w_b > T\).

Action gap.

  • Clipped density. For a stage with clip \((\alpha, \lambda, \rho)\) and threshold \(s\), the clipped density of a plaquette is \(\phi_s (|U_p |) +\boldsymbol{1}_{\text{kept}} \tfrac{1}{2} | \ell_p (U) |^2\) (crown). Here:

    • \(\phi_s (r) = \alpha r^2 / 2\) for \(r \leqslant s\) and \(\phi_s (r) = \lambda sr\) for \(r > s\) (clipped);

    • \(\ell_p (U)\) is the path series of the stage, cut at length \(R\);

    • "kept" means \(|U_q | \leqslant s \hspace{0.17em} e^{\text{dist} (p, q) / \rho}\) for all \(q\) (kept).

  • Actions. \(\mathcal{A}_F (U)\) is the fine clipped action (fine stage, threshold \(t\)) and \(\mathcal{A}_c (V)\) the coarse one (coarse stage, threshold \(t_c (x)\)).

  • Step functional. \(\mathcal{E}_A (U, V)\) (assessed) is the sum of the row contributions of Step 1 over the rows located in \(D_{2 B} (A)\). Summed over all rows of the torus, the same contributions give exactly

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{A}_F (U) + \frac{a}{2} \sum_b w_b^2 -\mathcal{A}_c (V) .\)

So \(\mathcal{E}_A\) is the action gap of the step (fine action, plus block-spin penalty, minus coarse action), localized near the seeds.

Proposition III.10.1 (energy per seed). Let \(P\) be a request. There is \(L_0\) such that for every block factor \(L \geqslant L_0\) there is \(W_0 (L)\) such that for every \(W \geqslant W_0\) there is \(x_0\) with the following property. Take \(x \geqslant x_0\) and:

  • a step \(S\) of block factor \(L\) with coarse side \(c \geqslant 100 (1 + B)\), and a tube \(\mathscr{T}\) admissible for \(P\);

  • a spanning forest \(F\) of the block structure, and a fine field \(U\) equal to \(1\) on the trunks of \(F\);

  • a store \(A\), tags \(k\) and a coarse field \(V\) such that \(A\) is consistent with \((U, k, V)\) at \((x, W)\).

Then

\(\displaystyle \kappa \hspace{0.17em} t^2 \hspace{0.17em} \sharp A \hspace{0.27em} \leqslant \hspace{0.27em} \mathcal{E}_A (U, V), \qquad \kappa = \tfrac{1}{2} \min \{\lambda_F / 8, a / 32, \alpha_c / 64\},\)

where \(\lambda_F\) is the linear clip constant of the fine stage and \(\alpha_c\) the quadratic clip constant of the coarse stage.

Status: theorem under the stated admissibility, tree-gauge and consistency hypotheses.

  • The Lean statement (Teller.cash, Teller.Grants) asserts only some \(\kappa > 0\) chosen after \(L\) and \(W\). The value above is the one the proof produces (Step 7), and it is independent of \(L\) and \(W\).

  • This deterministic estimate precedes the Gaussian tail and activity bounds of III.10.2.

Proposition III.10.6 (start-up fusion H01–H04; markers I01–I04).

(H) Setting. Fix a budget point and:

  • a pipe \(X\): regular and forced addresses, and supports of logarithmic letters;

  • a tap \(d\): regular activities, forced activities \(K_i\) and logarithmic letters \(\ell_i\).

The fused gas (fused) groups the forced activities and the factors \(e^{\ell_i} - 1\) into connected clusters (pellets). Each pellet contains at least one forced activity, and the forced supports in a pellet are pairwise disjoint.

Hypotheses. Fix a bijective enumeration of the pellets. Let \(\kappa, \gamma \geqslant 0\).

  • \(d\) is digested (Digests): the gas is valid with specified regular and forced bounds; the logarithmic family has a summable uniform majorant, and each letter is measurable, gauge invariant, local with the flag tests included, vanishes on healthy configurations, and is translation and lattice-symmetry covariant.

  • \(d\) is bought with constants \(\kappa, \gamma\) (Buys): \(|K_i | \leqslant e^{- \kappa} f_i\) and \(|e^{\ell_i} - 1| \leqslant f_i\), where \(f \geqslant 0\) satisfies the rooted Kotecký–Preiss condition at support exponent one larger than the budget exponent, with total \(\gamma\), and \((2 B + 1)^4 \gamma \leqslant 1\).

Conclusions.

  • (H01) For every configuration \(V\), the fused gas equals the original gas times \(\exp (\sum_{i = 0}^{\infty} \ell_i (V))\).

  • (H02) The fused activities satisfy the \(K\)-bound with any price \(v \geqslant e^{- \kappa} (e^{\gamma} - 1)\).

  • (H03) The fused system is a forced family and admits the lattice-symmetry moves.

  • (H04) Fix the paths, clip, budget point, addresses and pellet enumeration. Suppose the original representations form a continuous curve on a subset of \(\mathbb{R}\), each forced or exponentiated logarithmic letter varies continuously in the supremum norm over configurations, and a single nonnegative rooted majorant as above works throughout the parameter set. Then the fused representations form a continuous curve in the same representation norm. This is a fixed-volume assertion.

3. Strategy

Seeds cost \(\kappa t^2\) each. At \(\beta \geqslant \beta_F / 2\), their Boltzmann suppression pays every fixed polylogarithmic entropy loss. Completed squares isolate positive row contributions and two kernel defects: affected cells are paid by spent seeds, while local gauge fixing and flat identities reduce unaffected defects to chart errors and exponentially small tails.

Gaussian integration produces packet, old forced, logarithmic and warning letters. Energy and annulus estimates bound packets; simultaneous Gaussian excursions bound warnings. Rooted bounds sum connected molecules. Compatible fluctuation transport controls derivatives, and Cauchy–Schwarz preserves one square root of the simultaneous-warning probability in integrated bounds.

Fine-input coefficients must remain independent of \(L\), and mismatch coefficients independent of \(W\), until those parameters absorb the cross terms. Transported, truncated kernels lack exact completeness: only their flat versions cancel, so local gauge fixing and tail estimates must control the defects after neighbourhood summation. Derivative integration must also retain a gain for every distinct warning address under compatible fluctuation transport.
