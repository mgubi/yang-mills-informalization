1. What this unit proves

Proposition III.12.1 (kernel bounds uniform in the volume). Let \(R\) be a run for a simple gauge group \(G\), with Wilson family \(C\). Fix \(n\), labels \(v_i \in \text{Lab}_{d_i}\), a finite list \(\lambda\) of pairs (slot, axis), and \(r > 0\). There are \(B \geqslant 0, N_0\) such that, for every \(N \geqslant N_0\), every \(p\) and every integer configuration \(x\) with \(a_N \text{dist} (x_i, x_j) \geqslant r\) for \(i \ne j\),

\(\displaystyle \left| \Delta_{\lambda} \mathbb{E}_{N, p} \prod_{i = 1}^n O_{N, p} (v_i ; x_i) \right| \leqslant B.\)

Each difference acts in its indicated slot; distance is periodic \(\ell^{\infty}\) distance. Constants may depend on \(R, n, (v_i), \lambda, r\), but not \(p\). Status: theorem for every run. Lean: genuine.

Proposition III.12.2 (derivative closure). For a run with simple \(G\), every sequence \(w_{N, p} \in \text{Lab}_d\) bounded by some \(K \geqslant 0\) eventually in the mesh, and every finite axis list \(l\), the trial \(\Delta_l O (w)\) is funded. Status: theorem. Lean: Run.financed.

Proposition III.12.3 (closure lemma). Fix \(d\). Assume a consistent Wilson family \(C\) and:

  1. the centred budget;

  2. the scale-selection data;

  3. sound sections \(V_e\) and linear automorphisms \(W_e\) of abstract labels for every degree \(e\), satisfying the section, symmetry and exact local identities described below, with Portfolio;

  4. a presentation \(\pi\) and the full section-comparison data, including quantitative comparison with \(V_e\), identification of \(\ker \pi\) with the dressed equation ideal, and \(\sup_{N, p} \| \pi_{N, p} \| < \infty\) in each degree;

  5. degree-\(d\) detector rows with the placement and bounded-lift conditions and short-distance comparison of VitalRank.

If \(F\) is funded, every \(F_{N, p} (o)\) is continuous in the gauge field, \(l\) is an axis list, and \(x\) is a degree-\(d\) coin with \(O (x) - \Delta_l F\) owed at cost \(u_{N, p} \to 0\) in the mesh, then \(\Delta_l F\) is funded. Status: conditional theorem; a run's ties and short-distance estimates supply these hypotheses. Lean: improved.

Proposition III.12.4 (uniform rank detection). Under hypotheses 1–5 of III.12.3, fix \(l\). For all large \(k\) and all sufficiently small \(b > 0\), there is \(Z \geqslant 0\) such that, eventually in the mesh,

\(\displaystyle \| \pi_{N, p} x\| \leqslant Z \|\mathcal{P}_{k, b, l ; N, p} (\pi_{N, p} x)\| \quad (x \in \text{Lab}_d) .\)

The detector takes correlations with fixed partners and successively averages the first slot in the directions of \(l\) over lengths between \(b\) and \(2 b\); repeated axes give repeated averages. Status: conditional theorem. Lean: trained.

Proposition III.12.5 (negligible equation labels). Assume consistency of \(C\) and hypotheses 2–4 of III.12.3. Every degree-\(d\) coin \(x\) with \(\pi_{N, p} x_{N, p} = 0\) for all \((N, p)\) has \(O (x)\) owed at cost \(a_N^{1 / 32}\). Status: theorem. Lean: banked.

Proposition III.12.6 (coupling power-series bounds). Let \(R\) be a run with a Campaign, Pillars and bounds Sky. Let \(\bar{\beta}\) be the target level-0 coupling and \(w > 0\) the fan width. Fix \(p\), an \(n\)-tuple of labels \(V_0\) and \(r > 0\). There exist \(b, m \in \mathbb{N}\), \(m \geqslant 1\), \(B \geqslant 0\) and \(c \in (0, 1]\) such that the following holds for every \(m \leqslant j < N\) and \(a \in [- 1, 1]\).

Take any family \(F (s)\), \(s \in [- 1, 1]\), of admissible campaign members with common block sequence, sections and labels, whose level-0 inverse coupling at \((N, p)\) is \(\bar{\beta} + was\). For every \(r\)-separated configuration \(o\) in \(\Lambda_{j + 1, p}\) embedded into \(\Lambda_{N, p}\),

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}^{F (cs / 2)}_{N, p} \prod_i O (V_{0, i} ; o_i) = \sum_{i \geqslant 0} P_i s^i, \qquad \sum_i |P_i | \leqslant Ba_{j + 1}^{- b} \quad (s \in [- 1, 1]) .\)

Expectation and representatives belong to the attached auxiliary transfer family; its correlations agree with the successful trajectory at positive cutoffs. Constants depend only on campaign data, \(p, V_0, r\). Status: conditional theorem; VIII.1 supplies the campaign records. Lean: Face.financed.

Proposition III.12.7 (fixed-volume Cauchy property). Fix simple \(G\), one RG construction with stationary-path bounds, free amplification, observable-transfer bounds and common reference sections, and assume its uniform perturbation estimates. Fix \(p\). Let \(C_n\) come from histories at cutoffs \(f (n) \to \infty\). Assume:

  • Each history is a selected branch from level \(k\), agreeing with a fixed kinetic path; stationary kinetic paths converge with strict reserve in their admissible bounds. At every fixed \(i \geqslant k\), inverse couplings are Cauchy and activities have Cauchy representatives in the common weighted space.

  • Each degree used has reference level \(t (d) \geqslant k\) with \(d + 3 \leqslant J (t (d))\); transfer data are based at its reference section and compose branch transfers.

  • For every sufficiently large base level \(r\), multiscale remainder bounds hold for all sufficiently large \(n\). Label allowances \(\zeta (d, r)\) are eventually positive for each degree used; occupancy allowances are at least \(1\) at every level.

For every finite label list and every configuration \(x\) with \(x_i - x_j \not\in L_p \mathbb{Z}^4\) for \(i \ne j\), lying exactly on some common lattice \(x = a_r z\), \(z\) integer, the cutoff-\(f (n)\) correlations at coordinatewise ceiling addresses of \(x\) are Cauchy. Status: conditional theorem. A run with campaign, pillars, fixed-level convergence and uniform perturbation estimates gives \(C_n = C\), \(f (n) = n\). Lean: Rising.cinema.

Proposition III.12.8 (positional ultraviolet limit). Let \(C\) be a Wilson family and \(e\) a strictly increasing mesh sequence. Assume the kernel-bound conclusion of III.12.1 and that ceiling-address correlations are Cauchy along \(e\) whenever \(x_{i \mu} - x_{j \mu} \not\in L_p \mathbb{Z}\) for every \(i \ne j\) and axis \(\mu\). For every \(p, n\) and label list there is a function \(f\) on \((\mathbb{R}^4)^n\), smooth on \(\text{NoHit}_p = \{x : x_i - x_j \not\in L_p \mathbb{Z}^4 (i \ne j)\}\), with these properties:

  • For every smooth compactly supported \(t\) supported off collisions and strictly inside the centred period cell in every coordinate, smeared lattice sums converge along \(e\) to \(\int tf\).

  • Fix any finite list of coordinate directions (slot, axis), with integers \(q_-, q_+ \geqslant 0\), \(q_- + q_+ > 0\) in each direction, using the stencil \([F (x + q_+ e_{\mu}) - F (x - q_- e_{\mu})] / [(q_- + q_+) a_N]\). Write \(g\) for the corresponding iterated derivative of \(f\). Along \(e (s_i)\) for every \(s_i \to \infty\), these differences at lattice configurations whose physical positions converge to \(x \in \text{NoHit}_p\) converge to \(g (x)\). Their multilinear interpolations converge locally uniformly there. For every test \(t\) above, \(tg\) is integrable and smeared differences converge to \(\int tg\).

Status: conditional theorem; the run hypotheses following III.12.7 give the full sequence \(e (s) = s\). Lean: positioned; full-sequence instance Campaign.realUV.

3. Strategy

Normalized differences introduce \(a_N^{- 1}\), beyond the centred budget for bounded labels. Local matching replaces each differenced observable by a primary with sub-power inverse terminal coordinates and vanishing root error. Averaged partner correlations bound its projected physical label through an injective free Maxwell detector; equation labels contribute a vanishing root. This funds differences, and chessboard estimates bound their separated products.

At fixed volume, compare full correlations with scalar-normalized correlations at an intermediate level. Fixed-level convergence makes the latter Cauchy, while an exponentially small tail in the budget radius tends to zero. Positional bounds extend convergence from common-lattice configurations; Taylor estimates identify derivatives. For coupling dependence, polynomial coefficient bounds and smaller parameter intervals turn level differences into summable coefficient errors.

4. Steps

  1. Match differences. For a run with simple \(G\) and an eventually bounded degree-\(d\) label sequence \(w\), normalized differences preserve regularity with sub-power bounds after increasing degree. Local matching gives a degree-\((d + |l|)\) coin \(x\) with \(O (x) - \Delta_l O (w)\) owed at \(a_N^{1 / 16}\). prepared_rows.

  2. Remove equation labels. Under III.12.5's hypotheses, auxiliary equation sections have zero root: their exact distant-spectator identity annihilates the reflected product. Section comparison leaves cost \(a_N^{1 / 32}\). banked.

  3. Detect projected labels. Under III.12.3's hypotheses, averaging differences telescopes and bounds tests by \((2 / b)^{|l|}\) times undifferenced correlations. Short-distance comparison approaches the injective free Maxwell matrix; finite-dimensional stability and the rank bound give III.12.4. trained.

  4. Close the budget. Under III.12.3's hypotheses, set \(X = \pi x\). Apply negligible-equation control to \((x - X) / (1 +\|X\|)\), obtaining \(\|X\| \leqslant Zc + \tfrac{1}{2} (1 +\|X\|)\). Thus \(X\) is bounded; the centred budget funds \(O (X)\), and vanishing errors fund the desired difference. cap, improved.

  5. Bound kernels. Once all fixed-label differences are funded, place separated insertions in distinct cells with interior margins; offset comparison and the chessboard inequality bound products. Slot differences act on their insertions, giving III.12.1. funding.

  6. Control coupling series. Under III.12.6's campaign hypotheses, composed transfer bounds give coefficient sums polynomial in \(a_{j + 1}^{- 1}\). With fixed-level convergence and perturbation inputs, interval reduction converts real \(O (a_j)\) level differences to summable \(C \sqrt{a_j}\) coefficient bounds. For fixed \(p\), labels, compact \(K \subset \text{NoHit}_p\) and \(q \in \mathbb{N}\), this gives a positive parameter width independent of cutoff and a neighbourhood of \(K\) with limits \(C^q\) in position and analytic in the complex parameter; kernels with spatial stencils of length at most \(q\), their interpolations and admissible smeared sums have analytic continuations whose parameter derivatives of every fixed order converging uniformly on compact subdiscs and compact spatial subsets. Fan.standing.

  7. Take the mesh limit. Under III.12.7's hypotheses, fixed-level effective insertions and normalized densities are uniformly Cauchy; Haar integration and positive partition-function bounds give Cauchy correlations. For large fixed \(j\), the full correlation differs by at most \(2 F_0 e^{- B_j / 64}\), uniformly in sufficiently large cutoffs, with \(F_0\) independent of \(j\) and cutoff and \(B_j \to \infty\). Sever.echoes, Rising.arrives.

  8. Recover spatial derivatives. Under III.12.8's hypotheses, equicontinuity extends dense-set convergence. Taylor comparison with differences over a fixed physical interval identifies successive derivative limits; stencil averaging, multilinear interpolation and dominated convergence give the remaining conclusions. positioned.

5. Where the difficulty lies

Matching controls inverse terminal coordinates; rank detection must bound physical labels while section comparison controls the projection kernel. Bounds are volume-uniform, but convergence fixes \(p\) first. Every derivative order is fixed before choosing the cutoff threshold; analytic widths may depend on volume, compact spatial set and derivative order. The additive action scalar need not converge. Collision extension belongs to VII.4.

6. Notation

  • \(a_N = (\ell_0 \cdots \ell_{N - 1})^{- 1}\); \(\Lambda_{N, p}\) has physical period \(L_p\). \(O (v ; o)\) represents \(v \in \text{Lab}_d\); \(\mathbb{E}_{N, p}\) is Wilson expectation.

  • Eventually in the mesh: all \(N \geqslant N_0\), all \(p\); \(N_0\) may depend on a fixed coarse level. Sub-power growth means \(\|w_{N, p} \| \leqslant a_N^{- \varepsilon}\) eventually for every \(\varepsilon > 0\). A Coin is a label sequence \(x\) with \(\| \tau_{d ; N, p}^{- 1} x_{N, p} \|\) of sub-power growth, where \(\tau\) is the terminal coordinate isomorphism.

  • \(\Delta_{\mu} F (o) = [F (o + e_{\mu}) - F (o)] / a_N\); lists indicate iteration. A trial is a site-indexed family of gauge-field functions; it is local if eventually translation covariant, gauge invariant, measurable, bounded and supported on one fixed finite link stencil.

  • \(R_m (H)\) is the absolute reflected-product expectation to power \(1 / (L_p / a_m)^4\) on centred cells of physical side \(a_m\), conjugating odd reflections. A local trial is funded if, for all large \(m\), eventually in the mesh, its value at \(0\) is localized in the central cell and has root at most \(u (m)\) independent of \(N, p\); owed replaces \(u (m)\) by a cost \(v (N, p)\) independent of \(m\).

  • \(\pi_{N, p}\) is an idempotent label map; its dressed-equation kernel is an additional hypothesis. Ceiling addresses are \(\alpha_N (x)_{i \mu} = \lceil x_{i \mu} / a_N \rceil\). \(B_j = \lceil (\log \hat{\beta}_j)^2 \rceil\) is the budget radius.
