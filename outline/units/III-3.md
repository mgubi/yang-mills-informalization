Conventions. $f=cL$ is a fine torus obtained from a coarse torus of side $c$ by an odd block factor $L\ge3$. $j$ is the "smoothing index" (the Lean's $J$, $\ge4$) of the stage. A *Train* is a finite history of block-averaging steps. A *Row* is a translation-invariant (circulant) matrix kernel $R(x)$ over lattice displacements $x$, and its *symbol* is its Fourier transform $p\mapsto\sum_xR(x)e^{ip\cdot x}$. The code's words "price", "receipt" and "fund" are numerical bounds, and "bed", "tower", "flight" and "rack" are described in §6.

## 1. What this unit proves

The unit treats the Gaussian (quadratic) part of one block-spin step. It does not integrate anything. It constructs, for every level $m$ and every torus, the Fourier symbols of the quadratic form and of the transfer kernels, and it proves three kinds of property:

- **Exact algebraic identities.** The symbols satisfy the identities that make "integrate out the fluctuation" an exact Schur-complement computation.
- **Exponential decay.** The kernels decay exponentially in lattice units, with constants independent of $L$, of the number of levels and of the period.
- **Uniform ellipticity and symmetry.** The quadratic forms are bounded above and below, uniformly, and are invariant under the signed lattice symmetries.

The decisive point is the *order of choice*: all constants are fixed before the block sizes $L_m$. This is what makes the later estimates uniform.

**Proposition III.3.1 (priced catalogue before the block length).** Let $K$ be a choice of the four positive kernel parameters (penalty $k$, frame cut, predictor cut, axial factor). There exist, depending only on $K$ and on the stage index, positive numbers $\alpha_j,\Omega_j,\eta_j$, amendment constants $l_j\ge0$, clip bounds $\gamma_j>0$ and integers $b_j$, with the following properties for $j\ge4$.

1. *Bottom.* For every clip whose direct coefficient is $\le\gamma_j$ and every train $R$ of index $j$, there is a "bed" $M$ (five Rows: diagonal, dual diagonal, metric, inverse, square root of the 2-form quadratic form), defined from $R$ by explicit formulas with amendment constant $l_j$. All five Rows satisfy the exponential-decay bound with parameters $(\alpha_j,\Omega_j)$. On every torus the metric and diagonal satisfy $\eta_j\le\text{form}\le\Omega_j$. The bed is invariant under the signed symmetries. If $R$ is the trivial train, the diagonal is the identity.
2. *Hops.* For $t=j$ or $t+1=j$ and every one-step flight $P$ of index $(j,t)$, the transfer kernels of $P$ obey the strip bounds with the extra factors $L^{-(1+|l|)}$ and $L^{-(2+|l|)}$ for $|l|$ forward differences ($|l|\le j-2$). The rolled 3-form Row obeys the strip bound. Whenever $b_j\le L$, it also satisfies $\eta_j\le\cdot\le\Omega_j$ at all real momenta.

*Status:* theorem (no hypothesis beyond the kernel choice).

**Standard mathematics.**

- Fourier analysis on a periodic lattice; translation-invariant operators as multiplication by a symbol; the equivalence "analytic symbol in a strip $\Leftrightarrow$ exponential decay of the kernel" (Paley–Wiener).
- The Balaban-type block-spin averaging operator and quadratic form with penalty (Balaban, *Propagators and renormalization transformations for lattice gauge theories I, II*, CMP 95–96, 1984; *Averaging operations for lattice gauge theories*, CMP 98, 1985). Exponential decay of the propagator of the free fluctuation operator is the analogue here of the decay of Balaban's covariance, with decay constants independent of $L$ after rescaling (Dimock, *The renormalization group according to Balaban I*, Rev. Math. Phys. 25, 2013).
- The elementary geometric-sum identities for averages over $L$ points.
- The Hodge-type decomposition of 2-forms on the torus into closed and coclosed parts (here "$d$", "$d^*$" and the "harmonic convention").

**Imported constants and assumptions.**

- The kernel parameters $K$ (penalty and three cuts) are free positive numbers, supplied by the caller. The penalty is the number $b$ of the main theorem (statement.md).
- The fixed complex domain: the cube of half-width $\pi/4$ ([`zone`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Next/FREE_KERNELS/Chip.lean) in `Chip.lean`) and the disc radius $3\pi/2$. The constants `low`, `high` with $\mathrm{low}\,|z|\le|e^{iz}-1|\le\mathrm{high}\,|z|$ on that disc are obtained by compactness.
- The credit condition (clip direct coefficient $\le\gamma_j$, $b_{j}\le L$) is a hypothesis of III.3.2. It is discharged in III.2.
- The four hypotheses F06–F09 are discharged inside `retained_support` by III.6.

## 3. Strategy

The one-step Gaussian problem is translation-invariant, so everything is a matrix-valued symbol on the torus, with the symbols for different tori (periods) tied together by the rescaling $p\mapsto p+2\pi l/L$ (the "aliases"). The coarse field is a block average of the fine field. In Fourier space the averaging is multiplication by products of $\mathrm{plank}_L(p_i)$. A history of $n$ steps is a *train*, whose cumulative filter is $\mathrm{area}$ at the accumulated block size, raised to the smoothing power $j$ (III.3.5). The quadratic form of the step, its inverse and its square root are written in closed form from the train. These formulas are the "bed". The transfer from coarse 1-forms and 2-forms to fine ones is given by the "towers" built from the aliased filters.

The method is that of Balaban's propagator estimates (exponential decay of the covariance of a gauge-fixed, averaged, penalised quadratic form), carried out entirely in Fourier space. What is unusual is the architecture. There is no estimate of a Green's function by an elliptic argument. Every symbol is proved analytic in a strip, with sup bounds $\Omega_j$ that do not depend on $L$, by writing it as a *finite expression* in the filters. Positivity is proved at real momenta only (III.3.4). The inverse $1/(\sum q^2)$ is never put into a strip. Only products already regularised by the filters are (see the comments in `Chip.lean`, `Bend.lean`).

The uniformity in $L$ is obtained by *ordering the choices* (glossary, family 1). First comes the price table $(\alpha_j,\Omega_j,\eta_j,l_j,\gamma_j,b_j)$, which depends only on the kernel choice and on the stage index (III.3.1). Then come the block sizes $L$, which must satisfy $L\ge b_j$. Then comes a second table $T$ from the repair step. In the symbols, $L$ appears only through explicit negative powers, so a threshold $b_j$ is enough to absorb the $m/L^2$ terms.

## 5. Where the difficulty lies

- **Volume and bookkeeping, not ideas.** The unit is about 9,000 lines, and the argument is a long chain of strip-bound estimates for explicit matrix-valued symbols. The mathematical content is: filters are bounded and bounded below on the principal alias; products of strip-bounded Rows are strip-bounded; positivity at real momenta persists under conjugation by invertible strip-bounded Rows. Writing it in a paper requires a systematic calculus of "budgeted Rows" (a Banach algebra of kernels with weighted $\ell^1$ norm $\sum e^{\alpha|x|}|R(x)|$). The Lean has this calculus implicitly in `Stock`, `Guide`, `Pay`, `Moderate`.
- **The order of constants is the content.** The theorem is stronger than "the kernels decay exponentially". It says the decay constants are those of a table fixed *before* $L$, and that only a lower bound $b_j\le L$ is required. The extra decay factors $L^{-(1+|l|)}$, $L^{-(2+|l|)}$ in `Donate` and `Deep` are what later give the smallness of the non-principal aliases. In the Lean this appears as the alias-indexed decay `decay`$_j(l)$ and `grade`.
- **Positivity only at real momenta.** The code says explicitly that no absence of a complex null cone is inferred from real ellipticity (`Chip.lean`) and that inverses of the symbol $\sum q^2$ are never put in a strip (`Bend.lean`). All strip estimates are for products already regularised by the filters (the "punctured algebra"). A paper has to reproduce this discipline.
- **A priori unusual features.**
  - The "amendment" $l$ (a penalty on closed components in the inverse metric, `Amend.lean`) is a nonstandard modification of the quadratic form. It is not Balaban's. It is introduced so that the rolled form stays above the identity (Lemma 4.6), and the final constants depend on $l_j$ (which grows with $j$).
  - Positivity of the rolled form is only proved for $mB\le L^2$ ($m$ is a loop-correction size); the Lean states that this is the regime in which it is used.
  - The decay is in the lattice unit of the *fine* torus, with an explicit $L^{-p}$ scaling, not in units of the coarse mesh (`Pay at the scaled rate here O(1/L) in fine site units`, `Strolling.lean`). A paper needs to say clearly in which unit each decay is stated.
- **Statement strength.** III.3.2 is a pure existence statement with a large quantifier structure. What later units consume are the structures `Rack`/`Suffices` and not a numerical estimate. A human reader will not see "the covariance decays like $e^{-c|x|/L}$" in any single line. It is the combination of `Deep`, `covered` and the `Roll` identity.

## 7. Open points for the writer

1. **Meaning of Diagonal, Roll and the three blocks.** I interpret `Roll` as the Schur-complement identity of the block-spin quadratic form (coarse part + fluctuation part), with $m_f$ the fluctuation map, $m_n$ the coarse-direction block tied to the penalty $\sqrt k$ and $o$ a remainder block. This is read off the identities in `Stacks.lean` and `Tori.lean`, not from a docstring. The writer should check the roles against the Gaussian-integration unit III.4 (how $m_f,m_n,o$ enter the change of Gaussian sector). (probable)
2. **Flight-level identities.** `Flight.tangent` and `Flight.rebased` (which give Diagonal and the approximate identity for the explicit towers) were read as statements only. They are where the actual computation with aliases happens.
3. **Alias calculus.** The unit contains a large calculus of aliases ($p\mapsto p+2\pi l/L$), the puncture at the harmonic point (the zero mode), and the "two columns" ($A$ rescaled by $L$, $T$ by $L^2$) in `Scripts.lean`, `ZeroAlias.lean`, `Aliases.lean`, `Wire.lean`. These have not been checked in detail. The docstrings stress that the harmonic point is treated by convention and that no continuation claim is made there.
4. **Role of the "KP" package.** `garnet` and `pearl` have been identified with the lattice and continuum covariances of the curvature, but the theorem that compares them has not been located. It may be consumed by the free-Maxwell comparison (VII.1) or by the remainder comparison. The name "KP" is not explained in the code. (tentative)
5. **Quantifier structure of `retained_support`.** The price table $T$ is chosen *after* the sheet but *before* the trajectory, and `before_paid` allows a preliminary sheet. The consumers (`Calendar.lean`, `RG08/Flat/*`, `RG08/Aura/Publisher.lean`, `RG08/Cast/Profile.lean`) use it in several forms. The writer should state the theorem once, in the form of `before_paid`.
6. **Units of the decay.** Whether $\mathrm{size}(x)$ in $\mathrm{Width}$ is the $\ell^1$ or the $\ell^\infty$ norm of the displacement (and whether the displacement is on the fine or the coarse torus) was not checked. (tentative)
7. **Dependence on $G$.** The statement quantifies over a gauge group only because the symmetry statement `Sworn` is about $G$-valued directions. The free-kernel estimates themselves are $G$-independent. A written proof can drop $G$ from III.3.1–III.3.4.
8. **Packages in §4.8.** `CORRECTED_LIFT_COEFFICIENTS`, `CUTOFF_PROJECTORS`, `GRID_RENEWED_RESERVES` and part of `SIGNED_SOURCE_CONTINUATION` (the `strings` proof, the `Powers`, `Mediator`, `Pattern` files) were not read. Their assignment to III.3 looks like an artifact of the clustering and should be checked against the observable units.
9. **Smoothing index.** The constraint $j\le J\le j+1$ in `Train.hop` and the relation of $J$ to the stage profile (and to the "orders" chosen in III.2) is the only link between this unit and the order of choice; confirm with III.2 which constants the table $\{b_j\}$ must be compatible with.
