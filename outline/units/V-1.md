## 1. What this unit proves

**Setting and vocabulary.** $S$ is the limit table (the Euclidean hierarchy of IV.1), a symmetric family of kernels $S(v;x)$. A *word* $w=((x_1,v_1),\dots,(x_n,v_n))$ is a finite list of point–label pairs, and $\langle w\rangle_S$ denotes $S(v;x)$. A word is *separated* if its points are pairwise distinct and *positive* (for the time axis $a=0$) if all $x_i^0>0$. $\theta$ is time reflection. The *OS Hilbert space* of $S$ for the axis $a$ is the completion $H$ of finite combinations of positive words under $\langle w,w'\rangle=\langle\theta w\cdot w'\rangle_S$. The vector of a positive word $b$ is its *plant* $|b\rangle$. A *chamber* is this space with the contraction semigroup $E_t$, $t\ge0$ (Euclidean translation in the $a$-direction, self-adjoint, $\|E_t\|\le1$) and the unitary group $U_y$ of translations in the orthogonal hyperplane. The *ground space* is $D=\{\psi: E_t\psi=\psi\ \forall t\}$, and $\Omega=|\emptyset\rangle\in D$.

The code never assumes clustering, a unique vacuum or a mass gap. The unit produces all of them, or substitutes for them, from the Euclidean hierarchy.

## 3. Strategy

The limit table $S$ is a *mixture*: it is a priori only a subsequential limit of a sequence of finite-volume Wilson measures, and one does not know that it is a pure phase. The unit makes this explicit. On the OS Hilbert space of $S$ in the time direction, the compressed vacuum observables (those obtained by inserting a cluster of fields in the time slab and projecting back on the heat-zero space) generate a commutative C\*-algebra $\mathcal A$, and $\Omega$ is cyclic and separating. Its Gelfand spectrum $X$ with the measure $\mu$ given by $\Omega$ is the space of phases, and the character $\chi_i$ gives a new table $S_i$ by $\langle w\rangle_{S_i}=\chi_i(M(w))$ (the formula in V.1.5(b)); the barycenter formula $S=\int S_i\,d\mu$ is the decomposition. This is the Euclidean analogue of the decomposition of an ergodic/clustering state into extremal ones, done at the level of operators on the reconstructed space instead of through the lattice measure, which is only available for finite volume.

Three kinds of work remain. (1) *Existence of the operators.* Slab operators have to be bounded on the OS space; this is not automatic since the OS norm of a word is not controlled by the lattice norm. This is the role of the bench: a chessboard-type bound on the $n$-fold round trip of the slab operator gives a bound on the norm by a power argument (V.1.3). (2) *Properties of phases.* Every property of $S$ (symmetries, reflection positivity, smoothness, caps, derivative laws, flux laws, rotation invariance) must be shown for $S_i$, for *every* character, not only almost every one. This is done by using characters only as bounded $*$-homomorphisms and proving the required identities between the operators $M(w)$ themselves (B05–B11), before applying the character. (3) *Uniqueness of the vacuum in a phase.* Only almost every phase has a unique ground state, shown by a variance argument: for each word $b$ the average over $\mu$ of $\|\text{ground-state component of }b\|^2$ equals the average of $|\langle b\rangle_{S_i}|^2$, so the two agree for almost every $i$ (V.1.8). To get a *single* null set one needs a countable family of words that is total in the chambers of *all* phases simultaneously (B14). Spectral calculus (SNAG and Paley–Wiener, `Heat`/`SNAG`/`Paley`/`Spectral`) is built from scratch in the same unit to avoid assuming a spectral gap; the positive-energy property is derived from analyticity of the heat semigroup alone.

**Lemma V.1.a (heat-zero space).** For a contraction semigroup $E_t$ on $H$, the space $D$ of vectors fixed by all $E_t$ is closed and $E_t\psi\to\Pi\psi$ as $t\to\infty$ for every $\psi$ (strongly).

No spectral theorem is used.

**Lemma V.1.l (phase reflection; B05).** For every separated word $w$ of plain labels at generic points, $M(w)=M(\theta w)$, where $\theta$ is time reflection acting by the signed permutation on points and labels (Lean `Phase.reflects`).

The statement extends from generic points to all separated points by continuity (smoothness of $M$). The mechanism uses an exchangeable-sequence argument and the fact that the antisymmetric part of an observable is "invisible" to its own pure-phase average.

### 4.5 Retaining the null spaces: detectors (B15–B18)

The labels of $S$ are taken modulo a null space (labels whose correlators vanish). The phases must have the *same* null space, otherwise their field algebras would differ from that of $S$. This is checked with the "corrected" fields of the UV part: for a column of corrected fields, a one-point detector $\Delta_1=M(\text{field at one point})-c\cdot 1$ and a two-point detector $\Delta_2$ (a connected correlation of two fields minus its free value) are shown to have small norm in $\mathcal A$; a bounded element of $\mathcal A$ has small norm in every character, which transfers the vanishing to each phase.

**Lemma V.1.t (tall elements are small in every character).** If $\langle\Omega,((x^*x)^n)\Omega\rangle\le t^{2n}$ for all $n$ (Lean `Tall`), then $|\chi_i(x)|\le t$ for every $i$.
*Proof sketch.* The moments $\int|\chi_i(x)|^{2n}d\mu$ equal $\langle\Omega,(x^*x)^n\Omega\rangle\le t^{2n}$, so $|\chi_i(x)|\le t$ $\mu$-a.e.; continuity of $\chi_i(x)$ in $i$ and full support give it for all $i$.

## 5. Where the difficulty lies

*Mathematically new, or at least not found in the standard texts.* The proof does not use lattice-level clustering or a transfer-matrix spectral gap. It builds the whole phase structure on the *limit* OS space from three things only: the Euclidean hierarchy, a uniform lattice bound on round trips of slab insertions (the bench), and the rotation covariance. In particular, the commutativity of the vacuum algebra and the equality $M(w)=M(\theta w)$ are *proved* (from the symmetry of the table and rotations), not assumed, as they are in a constructive treatment where the infinite-volume state is taken to be a single translation-invariant measure. That is the content of V.1.j and V.1.l; V.1.l is the most unusual argument, with the exchangeable-sequence positivity trick (nonnegative covariance of far-translated copies of an anti-selfadjoint element). It needs the antisymmetrised lattice function to be approximated by bounded measurable functions with controlled pair correlations (`Covering`, `Grill`) — a UV-side input.

*Routine but long.* The B-work items 02, 03, 09, 15–18 (about 2,500 of the 3,600 work item lines) are bookkeeping of smooth dependence on positions, of label quotients, and of finite polynomial limits. The SNAG, Paley and Heat files re-prove, in Lean and with explicit constructions, theorems that are textbook mathematics (SNAG, Paley–Wiener), so their length (about 1,100 lines) reflects the library and not the mathematics.

*What looks unusual or stronger than expected.*
1. The pure phase is selected only by a measure-theoretic existence argument ($\mu$-a.e. uniqueness of the vacuum). Nothing shows that the number of phases is finite or that the selected phase is unique. The final theorem is correspondingly an existence statement for *some* phase (and for *every* phase in V.3).
2. The slab operators are bounded *in the mixture*, with norm controlled by lattice chessboard bounds on a *sub-width* $s\le d$ (the existential in the bench); the width of the lattice-compatible slab is not equal to the requested one. The remaining width is absorbed by the contraction $E_{d-s}$ (V.1.f).
3. The bench is stated for each Seed, not as a consequence of general reflection positivity, and its Lean docstring calls it a "coarsening gate ... not inferred from lattice code yet". It is nevertheless a proved theorem (`Next.Phase.seats`) in the final assembly, but the reader should confirm that the proof does not use an unproved input.
4. Commutativity of $\mathcal A$ uses the full rotation action (in particular, the group $O(4)$ acting on the labels). The rotation action on labels is itself constructed in VI.1 from Ward identities on the *mixed* table; V.1 is then used to transport it to phases. Logically this is acyclic (VI.1 works on $S$ only), but the written proof has to say so.

## 7. Open points for the writer

1. **Not checked.** The proofs of B02, B03, B04, B06 (beyond the geometry of reflections), B08, B09, B10, B12, B15, B16, B17, B18, `Retain.lift_detect`, the supporting `SNAG` and `Paley` modules, and the `PHASE.Seats` module have not been checked in detail. Statements, docstrings and the assembly have been checked. The descriptions of these steps in §4 are taken from docstrings and statements; those that have not been checked are marked (probable) or "statement only".
2. **Logical order between V.1 and VI.1.** `Locals` (rotation action, stress tensor) is an input here, and enters the commutativity of $\mathcal A$ through the ground-state invariance and `exchanges`. VI.1 is listed as both a predecessor and a successor of V.1 on the unit page. Check in the Lean (`Org/Input`, `Spec/Locals`) that VI.1's output used here is stated for the *mixed* table only and does not use any V.1 output; otherwise the written proof has a cycle.
3. **Commutativity.** My account of why $\mathcal A$ is commutative (V.1.j) is a reading of the file structure: the key identity `Ground.exchanges` swaps two middle clusters inside a four-cluster train. A full proof should be read in `Ground/Permute.lean` and `Ground/Lean.lean` before writing.
4. **Bench.** Verify that `Next.Phase.seats` does not rest on a hypothesis that is only a placeholder; its Lean docstring in `Bid/Phase.lean` ("red coarsening-gate ... not inferred from lattice code yet") is out of date or describes the status at the time of writing the bid.
5. **Grid hypothesis and corrected fields.** Where `Grill` and `RankWidths` are proved (UV part) was not determined; they are inputs to B05 and B15–B18.
6. **Heat/Imaginary.** The definition of the unitary group $\mathrm{edge}(t)$ (the boundary values of the holomorphic extension of $E_t$ to the imaginary axis) and the exact assumptions of `Paley.supports` remain to be checked; only the statements in the `Heat.Lift` module have been checked.
7. **Boundary with V.2.** The spectral calculus (V.1.2) and the positivity of the energy might belong more naturally to V.2 (reconstruction). I kept them here because the Lean files are in the V.1 folders and V.1's key statements list them. The page for V.1 also lists `Heat.Lift.heat` and `SNAG.Escape.total` among its key results.
8. **The selected phase.** The statement "for some phase" hides a choice: the later units (V.2, V.3) run the reconstruction for a chosen phase, and V.3 for every phase. Check how `Spec.Final` uses the selected index and whether anything downstream needs the same $i$ for two different axes (chambers were chosen axis by axis in `Selection`; only the time axis appears in the certificate).
9. **Sizes.** The 5,713-line figure counts only declarations used by the main theorem; the B-work items (`B01`–`B18`) total about 3,600 lines in the work-items table. The two together with the `Ground`/`OS`/`Heat`/`SNAG` folders do not add up exactly to 5,713 because the page counts "in part" some work packages.
