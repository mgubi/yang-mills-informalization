## 1. What this unit proves

**Setting and notation.**
- **Tables and words.** $S$ is the limit table of IV.1 (the mixed state $S^{\mathrm{mix}}$ of the main theorem). A *label* is a pair $(d,v)$: its degree $d\in\mathbb N$ and a vector $v$ in the finite-dimensional reference space $V_d$. A *word* $w=((x_1,v_1),\dots,(x_n,v_n))$ is a finite list of points of $\mathbb R^4$ with labels; $|w|=n$, and $S(w)=S(x;v)$ is its value, multilinear in the labels.
- **Time, slabs, buffers.** Time is the last coordinate $t(x)$, with unit vector $e_t$; $y\perp e_t$ denotes a spatial vector.
  - A word is *separated* if its points are distinct, and *positive* if in addition all $t(x_i)>0$.
  - It lies in the *slab* $(0,d)$ if $0<t(x_i)<d$ for all $i$.
  - It is *$r$-buffered* in that slab if its points are at mutual sup-distance $\ge r$ and $r\le t(x_i)\le d-r$.
- **Operations on words.** $w+a$ is the translate, $\tau_dw=w+de_t$, and $w\cdot w'$ is concatenation.
- **Two reflections.**
  - $\Theta w$, the OS reflection: reverse the list, reflect the points in $\{t=0\}$, and apply to the labels the antilinear map (conjugation followed by the time-mirror action).
  - $\vartheta w$, the linear time reflection: reflect the points and apply the time-mirror action to the labels, with neither conjugation nor reversal.
  - For real labels, $\Theta w$ is a permutation of $\vartheta w$.
- **Null spaces.** $N_d(S)\subset V_d$ is the null space: labels whose correlations with all separated spectators vanish. $F_d(S)=V_d/N_d(S)$. A table $S'$ *respects* $S$ if $N_d(S)\subset N_d(S')$ for all $d$.
- **Field algebra (VI.1).** It consists of a derivative plan (label derivatives $\partial_j\colon F_d\to F_{d+1}$ and inclusions $F_d\to F_e$), an action $K\mapsto R(K)$ of $O(4)$ on each $F_d$, and a stress tensor $J_{ab}\in F_4$. A table is *$O(4)$-covariant* if $S(Kx_1,\dots,Kx_n;R(K)v_1,\dots,R(K)v_n)=S(x;v)$ for all $K\in O(4)$ and $x\in\mathrm{Conf}_n$.
- **The OS space.** The OS space $\mathcal H$ of $S$ for an axis $a$ is the completion of the span of $a$-positive words modulo null vectors, with $\langle b,b'\rangle=S(\Theta b\cdot b')$. $|b\rangle$ denotes the vector of $b$, and $\Omega=|\emptyset\rangle$, with $\|\Omega\|=1$.

Nowhere does the unit assume clustering, a unique vacuum or a spectral gap.

**Proposition V.1.1 (chambers and ground space).** Let $S$ satisfy the Euclidean hierarchy and let $a$ be a coordinate axis. Then:
- On the OS space $\mathcal H$ there is a strongly continuous semigroup $(E_t)_{t\ge0}$ of self-adjoint contractions with $E_t|b\rangle=|b+te_a\rangle$.
- There is a strongly continuous unitary group $(U_y)_{y\perp e_a}$ commuting with it, with $U_y|b\rangle=|b+y\rangle$.
- The triple is a *chamber*. Let $\mathcal H_0=\{\psi:E_t\psi=\psi\ \forall t\}$ be its *ground space* and $P_0$ the orthogonal projection onto it. Then $\Omega\in\mathcal H_0$, and $E_t\psi\to P_0\psi$ as $t\to\infty$ for every $\psi$.

## 3. Strategy

**The limit table as a mixture.** The limit table $S$ is a subsequential limit of finite-volume Wilson measures. A priori it is a mixture, and Osterwalder–Schrader reconstruction applied to it may produce many vectors invariant under Euclidean time translation. The unit decomposes the ground space $\mathcal H_0$ of the OS semigroup.
- **The algebra.** Inserting a separated word into a time slab gives a bounded operator; compressing it to $\mathcal H_0$ gives $M(w)$. These operators generate a commutative C\*-algebra $\mathcal A$, for which $\Omega$ is cyclic and separating.
- **Phases.** The Gelfand spectrum $X$ of $\mathcal A$, with the measure $\nu$ representing $p\mapsto\langle\Omega,p\Omega\rangle$, is the space of phases. Each character gives a table, $S^\xi=\chi_\xi\circ M$, and $S=\int S^\xi\,d\nu$.
- **Comparison with the classical case.** This is the operator-level, Euclidean form of the decomposition of a state into pure phases along an abelian algebra of observables "at time infinity" (for a Euclidean measure: the ergodic decomposition under time translations, Fröhlich 1976). Here no infinite-volume measure is available. Everything is done on the OS space of the limit correlations, and every property of the phases is proved for **every** $\xi$: first as an identity between operators $M(w)$, then transported through the $*$-homomorphism $\chi_\xi$.

**Four inputs.** Four inputs make this work, and each is used where the classical argument would use the measure.
- **(i) Boundedness.** Slab insertions are bounded on the OS space. The OS norm is not controlled by any lattice norm, so boundedness comes from a lattice chessboard bound on alternating trains of $4n$ copies (the bench), uniform in $n$, by a power argument. The resulting all-copy bound $C^n(n/r)^{\kappa n}$ has the factorial form of the linear growth condition of Osterwalder–Schrader II, here pointwise at separated points.
- **(ii) $O(4)$-covariance** (VI.1). It shows that ground states are invariant under spatial translations, which makes $M(w)$ well defined. Spatial displacements are rotated into time displacements, which are asymptotically invisible. It also makes $\mathcal A$ commutative: exchanging the time axis with a spatial axis reverses the time order of two clusters inside a four-cluster limit.
- **(iii) Phase reflection** $M(w)=M(\vartheta w)$. Since $M(w)^*=M(\Theta w)$, for real labels this says that each phase assigns real values to real observables. A character of an operator algebra need not do so. The proof shows that the asymptotic covariance of far-separated copies of the real lattice variable $F-\vartheta F$ equals $\int\chi_\xi(D)^2d\nu=-\int|\chi_\xi(D)|^2d\nu$, with $D=M(w)-M(\vartheta w)$; as a variance limit it must also be $\ge0$. Conjugating time reflection then gives invariance of $M$ under all of $O(4)$.
- **(iv) Chessboard detectors.** For the corrected fields they show that the one- and two-point functions of every phase, on a spanning family of fields, are close to free-field values for which the test map is injective. Hence every phase has the null spaces of $S$.

**Selecting a phase.** For a positive word $b$, the quantity $\|P_0^\xi|b\rangle\|^2-|S^\xi(b)|^2\ge0$ has $\nu$-integral zero. This follows by dominated convergence along $E_s$ and the barycenter identity. One countable family of words is total in every phase chamber, so a single null set suffices, and outside it every phase has a one-dimensional ground space. The spectral calculus of V.1.2 (SNAG, positivity of the energy from analyticity of the heat semigroup) is built without assuming a gap and is used downstream (V.2, V.3).

**Lemma V.1.a (chamber).** Under the hierarchy, V.1.1 holds except for the convergence $E_t\to P_0$.
*Proof sketch.*
- **Contraction (OS doubling trick).** For $p$ in the span of positive words, $\|E_tp\|^2=\langle p,E_{2t}p\rangle\le\|p\|\sup_s\|E_sp\|$. The supremum $c$ is finite, because $s\mapsto S(\Theta b\cdot\tau_sb')$ is bounded for $s\ge0$: the points stay at a fixed positive distance, and the zeroth cap applies. Hence $c^2\le\|p\|c$, so $\|E_tp\|\le\|p\|$.
- **Self-adjointness and unitarity.** Self-adjointness of $E_t$ and unitarity of $U_y$ follow from translation invariance.
- **Strong continuity.** It follows from continuity of the Gram functions (smoothness) together with the uniform bounds.

*Proof sketch.*

- **Support theorem.** Let $\mu$ be a finite measure and $F$ a bounded holomorphic function in the upper half-plane, continuous up to the boundary, with $F(b)=\int e^{ix(y)b}d\mu(y)$. Then $\mathrm{supp}\,\mu\subset\{x\ge0\}$. To see this, integrate $F$ against the Gaussian $e^{-ibz-\varepsilon z^2}$ on horizontal lines and shift the contour upward. For $b<0$ this bounds the Gaussian-smoothed mass of $\mu$ near $b$ by a quantity tending to $0$ with $\varepsilon$.
- **Laplace formula.** Both sides of (d) are bounded and holomorphic with the same boundary values, so they agree.

**Lemma V.1.h (ground states are spatially invariant).** Under the hierarchy and $O(4)$-covariance, $U_y\psi=\psi$ for all $\psi\in\mathcal H_0$.

**Lemma V.1.p (phase tables, symmetries, barycenter; B07).**
- **Definition.** $S^\xi(x;v)=\chi_\xi(M(x;v))$ is a table.
- **Properties.**

  - It is translation invariant, respects $S$ (labels in $N_d(S)$ give $M=0$), and is $O(4)$-covariant (V.1.o).

### 4.6 Null spaces of the phases

**Lemma V.1.t (moment bounds give bounds in every phase).** If $p\in\mathcal A$ and $\langle\Omega,(p^*p)^n\Omega\rangle\le\tau^{2n}$ for all $n$, then $|\chi_\xi(p)|\le\tau$ for **every** $\xi$.
*Proof sketch.*

**Lemma V.1.u (detectors; B15–B18).**
- **The detectors.** Fix a degree $d$, a level $k$ of the corrected-field data, a test row with a basis index, and its free one-point values $s,s'$ and free cumulant $\gamma$. There are two detectors in $\mathcal A$:

**Lemma V.1.v (retention of null spaces).** If a table $S'$ is symmetric, respects $S$, and receives the values of some $\chi_\xi$, then $N_d(S')=N_d(S)$.
*Proof sketch.*
- **Closeness to the free values.** By V.1.t and V.1.u, the one- and two-point values of $S'$ on the corrected fields at level $k$ are within $\mathrm{gap}_k(1+|s|+|s'|)$ of the free values. The rescaled test points converge, so the matrices $f_k$ of $S'$-probes on the corrected fields converge to the free test map $F$, which is injective.
- **Injectivity.** Injectivity is an open condition, so $f_k$ is injective for large $k$.
- **Conclusion.** Let $v\in N_d(S')$. Since the $S$-classes of the corrected fields span $F_d(S)$, pick $u$ in their span with the same $S$-class as $v$. Then $u-v\in N_d(S)\subset N_d(S')$, so $u\in N_d(S')$ and $f_k(u)=0$. Injectivity gives $u=0$, hence $v\in N_d(S)$. The reverse inclusion is "respects".

**Lemma V.1.y (almost every phase has a unique ground state).**
- **Statement.** For each positive $b$: for $\nu$-a.e. $\xi$, $P^\xi_0|b\rangle_\xi=S^\xi(b)\,\Omega_\xi$. Consequently V.1.8 holds.

## 5. Where the difficulty lies

**New, not in the standard texts.**
- **The decomposition is built on the OS space of the limit, with no infinite-volume measure.** Three facts that are automatic for a single translation-invariant measure are proved here:
  - spatial invariance of the ground states (V.1.h);
  - commutativity of $\mathcal A$ (V.1.k);
  - reality of phase values (V.1.n).
- **Lattice input after the limit.** It enters at three places only: the bench (V.1.7), phase reflection (V.1.n, through the grid hypothesis), and the detectors (V.1.u).
- **Phase reflection is the least standard argument.** It is a variance-positivity statement for far-separated copies of a real lattice variable. It is equivalent to the claim that the abstract characters assign real values to real observables.
- **Detectors.** They require quantitative UV input: one- and two-point chessboard deviations $\mathrm{gap}_k\to0$ of corrected fields from free values. This is where the identification of $F_d$ with the free-field spaces (assessment, §3) is carried into every phase.

**Routine but long.**
- B02, B03, B09, B12, B14, B16, B18: smooth dependence on positions, label quotients, finite polynomial limits, separability.
- `SNAG`, `Paley`, `Heat` (about 1,100 lines) reprove textbook theorems in an explicit form adapted to the library.

**Unusual or worth checking.**
1. **Selection.** The selected phase comes from a measure-theoretic existence argument and is not canonical. Nothing bounds the number of phases. V.3 later proves unique ground states for every phase.
2. **Sub-width in the bench.** The bench gives the train bound only at a lattice-compatible width $s\le d$. The remaining width is absorbed by the contraction $E_{d-s}$ (V.1.f).
3. **Which symmetries are used where.**
   - Commutativity uses covariance only under the exchange of the time axis with one spatial axis. This is a lattice symmetry, already contained in the real-spin part of the hierarchy.
   - Spatial invariance of $\mathcal H_0$ needs arbitrary hyperplane reflections, i.e. the continuum $O(4)$ action of VI.1. In the Lean, both steps draw on that action.
   - Both use improper elements of $O(4)$ (reflections), not only rotations.
4. **Scope of the certificate.** It is for the time axis only, and for one phase. Downstream, only that phase's table is continued (`Spec.Relativistic`).
5. **Smoothness hypothesis in B02.** B02 needs the derivative identities of the table on which the slab family lives. For phases these come from B09, which uses the slab laws of $S$, so the two applications of B02 are ordered.
