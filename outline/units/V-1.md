## 1. What this unit proves

**Setting and notation.**

- **Tables and words.** $S$ is the limit table of IV.1 (the mixed state $S^{\mathrm{mix}}$ of the main theorem). A *label* is a pair $(d,v)$: its degree $d\in\mathbb N$ and a vector $v$ in the finite-dimensional reference space $V_d$. A *word* $w=((x_1,v_1),\dots,(x_n,v_n))$ is a finite list of points of $\mathbb R^4$ with labels; $|w|=n$, and $S(w)=S(x;v)$ is its value, multilinear in the labels.
- **Time, slabs, buffers.** Time is the last coordinate $t(x)$, with unit vector $e_t$; $y\perp e_t$ denotes a spatial vector. For a different coordinate axis $a$, replace $t(x)$ by $x^a$ in the following definitions.
  - A word is *separated* if its points are distinct, and *positive* if in addition all $t(x_i)>0$.
  - It lies in the *slab* $(0,d)$ if $0<t(x_i)<d$ for all $i$.
  - It is *$r$-buffered* in that slab if its points are at mutual sup-distance $\ge r$ and $r\le t(x_i)\le d-r$.
- **Operations on words.** $w+a$ is the translate, $\tau_dw=w+de_t$, $w\cdot w'$ is concatenation, and $\bar w$ conjugates only the labels.
- **Two reflections.**
  - $\Theta w$, the OS reflection: reverse the list, reflect the points in $\{t=0\}$, and apply to the labels the antilinear map (conjugation followed by the time-mirror action).
  - $\vartheta w$, the linear time reflection: reflect the points and apply the time-mirror action to the labels, with neither conjugation nor reversal.
  - For real labels, $\Theta w$ is a permutation of $\vartheta w$.
- **Null spaces.** $N_d(S)\subset V_d$ consists of labels whose correlations with every separated family of spectators vanish. Write $F_d(S)=V_d/N_d(S)$ and $q_d\colon V_d\to F_d(S)$ for the quotient map. A table $S'$ *respects* $S$ if $N_d(S)\subset N_d(S')$ for all $d$.
- **Field algebra (VI.1).** It consists of label derivatives $\partial_j\colon F_d(S)\to F_{d+1}(S)$, inclusions $F_d(S)\to F_e(S)$ for $d\le e$, an action $R$ of $O(4)$ on these spaces, and a stress tensor $J_{ab}\in F_4(S)$. In a word, $\partial_jv$ and $R(K)v$ mean representatives of $\partial_jq_d(v)$ and $R(K)q_d(v)$. All tables and operators to which this notation is applied factor through the relevant quotients. Covariance means $S(Kx_1,\dots,Kx_n;R(K)v_1,\dots,R(K)v_n)=S(x;v)$ for every $K\in O(4)$ and separated configuration $x\in\mathrm{Conf}_n$.
- **Caps.** For each fixed arity, degree list, label list and derivative order $j$, there are $A\in\mathbb N$ and $C\ge0$ such that $\|D^jS(x;v)\|\le Cr^{-A}$ whenever $0<r\le1$ and the points have mutual sup-distance at least $r$.
- **The OS space.** The OS space $\mathcal H$ of $S$ for an axis $a$ is the completion of the span of $a$-positive words modulo null vectors, with $\langle b,b'\rangle=S(\Theta b\cdot b')$. $|b\rangle$ denotes the vector of $b$, and $\Omega=|\emptyset\rangle$, with $\|\Omega\|=1$.

Nowhere does the unit assume clustering, a unique vacuum or a spectral gap.

**Proposition V.1.1 (chambers and ground space).** Let $S$ satisfy the Euclidean hierarchy and let $a$ be a coordinate axis. Then:

- On the OS space $\mathcal H$ there is a strongly continuous semigroup $(E_t)_{t\ge0}$ of self-adjoint contractions with $E_t|b\rangle=|b+te_a\rangle$.
- There is a strongly continuous unitary group $(U_y)_{y\perp e_a}$ commuting with it, with $U_y|b\rangle=|b+y\rangle$.
- The triple is a *chamber*. Let $\mathcal H_0=\{\psi:E_t\psi=\psi\ \forall t\}$ be its *ground space* and $P_0$ the orthogonal projection onto it. Then $\Omega\in\mathcal H_0$, and $E_t\psi\to P_0\psi$ as $t\to\infty$ for every $\psi$.

## 3. Strategy

**The limit table as a mixture.** The table $S$ is obtained by ordered limits of finite-volume Wilson correlations. Its OS semigroup may have a multidimensional ground space $\mathcal H_0$. The unit decomposes the correlations through an algebra acting on that space.

- **The algebra.** Inserting a separated word into a time slab gives a bounded operator; compressing it to $\mathcal H_0$ gives $M(w)$. These operators generate a commutative C\*-algebra $\mathcal A$, for which $\Omega$ is cyclic and separating.
- **Phases.** The Gelfand spectrum $X$ of $\mathcal A$, with the measure $\nu$ representing $p\mapsto\langle\Omega,p\Omega\rangle$, is the space of phases. Each character gives a table, $S^\xi=\chi_\xi\circ M$, and $S=\int S^\xi\,d\nu$.
- **Comparison with the classical case.** The algebra describes observables at infinite time separation. It replaces the invariant observables used in the ergodic decomposition of a Euclidean measure. Here the construction uses only the OS space of the limit correlations. Operator identities give the hierarchy and field laws for every $\xi$; vacuum uniqueness is first obtained almost everywhere.

**Four inputs.** Four inputs make this work, and each is used where the classical argument would use the measure.

- **(i) Boundedness.** A chessboard estimate controls alternating trains of $4n$ copies with constants independent of $n$. A power argument converts this estimate into the slab-operator bound $C^{|w|}(|w|/r)^{\kappa|w|}$.
- **(ii) $O(4)$-covariance** (VI.1). It shows that ground states are invariant under spatial translations, which makes $M(w)$ well defined. Spatial displacements are rotated into time displacements, which are asymptotically invisible. It also makes $\mathcal A$ commutative: exchanging the time axis with a spatial axis reverses the time order of two clusters inside a four-cluster limit.
- **(iii) Phase reflection** $M(w)=M(\vartheta w)$. Together with $M(w)^*=M(\Theta w)$, this makes every phase value on real labels real. Put $D=M(w)-M(\vartheta w)$ for such a word. Then $D^*=-D$, so $\langle\Omega,D^2\Omega\rangle=-\|D\Omega\|^2$. Bounded real lattice approximants give the reverse inequality by positivity of the variance of many far-separated copies. Thus $D=0$. Conjugating time reflection gives invariance of $M$ under $O(4)$.
- **(iv) Chessboard detectors.** For the corrected fields they show that the one- and two-point functions of every phase, on a spanning family of fields, are close to free-field values for which the test map is injective. Hence every phase has the null spaces of $S$.

**Selecting a phase.** For a positive word $b$, the quantity $\|P_0^\xi|b\rangle\|^2-|S^\xi(b)|^2\ge0$ has $\nu$-integral zero. This follows by dominated convergence along $E_s$ and the barycenter identity. One countable family of words is total in every phase chamber, so a single null set suffices, and outside it every phase has a one-dimensional ground space. The spectral calculus of V.1.2 (SNAG, positivity of the energy from analyticity of the heat semigroup) is built without assuming a gap and is used downstream (V.2, V.3).

### 4.1 OS space, chambers, spectral calculus

**Lemma V.1.a (chamber).** Under the hierarchy, V.1.1 holds except for the convergence $E_t\to P_0$.
*Proof sketch.*

- **Contraction (OS doubling trick).** For $p$ in the span of positive words, $\|E_tp\|^2=\langle p,E_{2t}p\rangle\le\|p\|\sup_s\|E_sp\|$. The supremum $c$ is finite, because $s\mapsto S(\Theta b\cdot\tau_sb')$ is bounded for $s\ge0$: the points stay at a fixed positive distance, and the zeroth cap applies. Hence $c^2\le\|p\|c$, so $\|E_tp\|\le\|p\|$.
- **Self-adjointness and unitarity.** Self-adjointness of $E_t$ and unitarity of $U_y$ follow from translation invariance.
- **Strong continuity.** It follows from continuity of the Gram functions (smoothness) together with the uniform bounds.

**Lemma V.1.b (ground projection).** For a semigroup of self-adjoint contractions, $E_t\psi\to P_0\psi$ for every $\psi$.
*Proof sketch.*

- For $u\le v$, $\langle E_u\psi,E_v\psi\rangle=\|E_{(u+v)/2}\psi\|^2\ge\|E_v\psi\|^2$. Hence $\|E_u\psi-E_v\psi\|^2\le\|E_u\psi\|^2-\|E_v\psi\|^2$, and the decreasing limit of $\|E_t\psi\|^2$ makes $E_t\psi$ Cauchy.
- The limit is fixed by every $E_s$, and $\psi$ minus the limit is orthogonal to $\mathcal H_0$, because $\langle E_t\psi,u\rangle=\langle\psi,u\rangle$ for $u\in\mathcal H_0$.
- No spectral theorem is used.

**Lemma V.1.c (real-time group and SNAG).** V.1.2(a), (b) hold.
*Proof sketch.*

- **Real-time group.** $E_1=E_{1/2}^2$ is positive, with spectrum in $[0,1]$. The functional calculus gives $E_1^z$, holomorphic for $\mathrm{Re}\,z>0$, and $E_1^t=E_t$ for $t>0$ by positive roots and continuity. If $E_t\psi=0$, analyticity makes $E_s\psi=0$ for every $s>0$, and continuity at zero gives $\psi=0$. Self-adjointness then makes the range of $E_1$ dense. Since $\|E_1^{1-is}\psi\|=\|E_1\psi\|$, the map $E_1\psi\mapsto E_1^{1-is}\psi$ extends to an isometry. The power laws give the group law and inverse at $-s$; continuity on the dense range gives strong continuity.
- **SNAG.** Integrate $V$ against Fourier transforms of Schwartz functions to obtain a $*$-representation of continuous functions on the one-point compactification of $\mathbb R^4$. Riesz–Markov gives a projection-valued measure there. The point at infinity carries no mass: evaluate at $x=0$ along an approximate identity.

*Proof sketch.*

Its boundary values are the Fourier transform of $e^{ip\cdot x}d\mu_\psi(p)$ in the variable $p^a$.
- **Support theorem.** Let $\mu$ be a finite positive Borel measure on $\mathbb R^4$, let $\ell\colon\mathbb R^4\to\mathbb R$ be continuous, and let $F$ be bounded and holomorphic in the upper half-plane, continuous up to the boundary, with $F(b)=\int e^{i\ell(y)b}d\mu(y)$. Then $\mathrm{supp}\,\mu\subset\{\ell\ge0\}$. To see this, integrate $F$ against the Gaussian $e^{-ibz-\varepsilon z^2}$ on horizontal lines and shift the contour upward. For $b<0$, choosing height $-b/(2\varepsilon)$ bounds the mass where $|\ell(y)-b|<|b|/2$ by $C e^{-3b^2/(16\varepsilon)}$. Letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ excludes negative $\ell$ from the support. Apply this with $x=0$ in $J$ and $\ell(p)=p^a$.
- **Laplace formula.** Both sides of (d) are bounded and holomorphic with the same boundary values, so they agree.

**Lemma V.1.p (phase tables, symmetries, barycenter; B07).**

- **Definition.** $S^\xi(x;v)=\chi_\xi(M(x;v))$ is a table.
- **Properties.**

### 4.6 Null spaces of the phases

**Lemma V.1.t (moment bounds give bounds in every phase).** Let $\tau\ge0$ and $p\in\mathcal A$. If $\langle\Omega,(p^*p)^n\Omega\rangle\le\tau^{2n}$ for every $n\in\mathbb N$, then $|\chi_\xi(p)|\le\tau$ for every $\xi$.
*Proof sketch.*

- The hypothesis says $\int|\chi_\xi(p)|^{2n}d\nu\le\tau^{2n}$ for all $n$.
- If $|\chi_{\xi_0}(p)|>\tau'>\tau$, then continuity gives an open $O\ni\xi_0$ on which $|\chi_\xi(p)|>\tau'$.
- Full support gives $\nu(O)>0$, so $\nu(O)\tau'^{2n}\le\tau^{2n}$ for all $n$, a contradiction.

**Lemma V.1.u (detectors; B15–B18).**

- **The detectors.** Assume the Seed and phase reflection. Fix a degree $d$, an index $k$ of its corrected-field data, and a test row with a basis index. Let $F$ be a one-point word at a prescribed grid position, with free value $s$ at the rescaled position. For a two-point row, let $G$ be its second one-point word, $s'$ its free value and $\gamma$ the free cumulant. There are detectors in $\mathcal A$:
  - the one-point detector $\Delta_1=M(F)-s\cdot1$;
  - the two-point detector $\Delta_2=M(F\cdot G)-s\,M(G)-s'M(F)+(ss'-\gamma)\cdot1$.
- **Statement.** For every slot there is a one-point detector; for a two-point row there is also the two-point detector. Each satisfies $\langle\Omega,(\Delta^*\Delta)^n\Omega\rangle\le\mathrm{gap}_k^{2n}$ for every $n\in\mathbb N$, where $\mathrm{gap}_k\ge0$ and $\mathrm{gap}_k\to0$.

*Proof sketch.* Each detector is the cluster element $c_E=\sum_i\lambda_iM(\text{word}_i)$ of a finite lattice *episode* $E$, whose chessboard budget is $\mathrm{gap}_k$ (B18, from the corrected-field data). Then:

- **B15 (cutoff level).** The absolute lattice expectation of the product of $2n$ diagonal translates of the episode function, alternately conjugated, is at most $(\mathrm{gap}_k+\varepsilon)^{2n}$ late in the cutoff. This is the chessboard estimate.
- **B16 (limit table).** For fixed $n$ and all sufficiently large translations, expand the product into finitely many monomials. Their points are generic: within each cluster this is part of the data, and between clusters each coordinate tie excludes at most one translation parameter. Point limits apply to each monomial, including the conjugated cutoff labels.
- **B17 (vacuum moments).** Let the translation tend to infinity and apply V.1.j to each ordered monomial, using $M(\bar w)=M(w)^*$ from V.1.i and V.1.n. The limit is $\langle\Omega,(c_E^*c_E)^n\Omega\rangle$. Finally let $\varepsilon\downarrow0$.

**Lemma V.1.v (retention of null spaces).** Assume the corrected-field data and detector bounds of V.1.u. If a table $S'$ is symmetric, respects $S$, and satisfies $S'(w)=\chi_\xi(M(w))$ for every separated word and some fixed $\xi$, then $N_d(S')=N_d(S)$ for every $d$.
*Proof sketch.*

- **Comparison maps.** Let $F_d^{\mathrm{free}}$ be the free-field space in the corrected-field data. Define $U_k\colon F_d^{\mathrm{free}}\to V_d$ by sending its chosen basis to the corrected labels. Compose $U_k$, the quotient map for $S'$, and the finite family of $S'$-probes to obtain $f_k\colon F_d^{\mathrm{free}}\to\mathbb C^m$.
- **Injectivity.** V.1.t and V.1.u give one-point errors at most $\mathrm{gap}_k$ and two-point errors at most $\mathrm{gap}_k(1+|s|+|s'|)$. The free singleton values converge and are bounded, and the rescaled test points converge. Thus $f_k$ converges in norm to the injective free test map. Injectivity is open in this finite-dimensional operator space, so $f_k$ is injective for large $k$.
- **Conclusion.** For all large $k$, $q_dU_k$ is also surjective by the spanning hypothesis. If $v\in N_d(S')$, choose $z\in F_d^{\mathrm{free}}$ with $q_dU_kz=q_dv$. Since $S'$ respects $S$, $U_kz\in N_d(S')$, hence $f_kz=0$. Injectivity gives $z=0$, so $q_dv=0$. The reverse inclusion is the respect hypothesis.

**Lemma V.1.y (almost every phase has a unique ground state).**

- **Statement.** In the setting of V.1.8, for each positive $b$, $P^\xi_0|b\rangle_\xi=S^\xi(b)\,\Omega_\xi$ for $\nu$-almost every $\xi$. This proves the uniqueness assertion of V.1.8.

*Proof sketch.*

## 5. Where the difficulty lies

**Distinctive arguments.**

- **The decomposition uses the OS space of the limit correlations.** Three properties require separate arguments:
  - spatial invariance of the ground states (V.1.h);
  - commutativity of $\mathcal A$ (V.1.k);
  - reality of phase values (V.1.n).
- **Lattice input after the limit.** It enters at three places only: the bench (V.1.7), phase reflection (V.1.n, through the grid hypothesis), and the detectors (V.1.u).
- **Phase reflection is the least standard argument.** It is a variance-positivity statement for far-separated copies of a real lattice variable. It is equivalent to the claim that the abstract characters assign real values to real observables.
- **Detectors.** They require quantitative UV input: one- and two-point chessboard deviations $\mathrm{gap}_k\to0$ of corrected fields from free values. These estimates preserve the field null spaces in every phase.

**Routine but long.**

- B02, B03, B09, B12, B14, B16, B18: smooth dependence on positions, label quotients, finite polynomial limits, separability.
- `SNAG`, `Paley`, `Heat` (about 1,100 lines) reprove textbook theorems in an explicit form adapted to the library.

**Unusual or worth checking.**
1. **Selection.** The selected phase comes from a measure-theoretic existence argument and is not canonical. No finite bound on the number of phases is asserted. V.3 later proves unique ground states for every phase.
2. **Sub-width in the bench.** The bench gives the train bound only at a lattice-compatible width $s\le d$. The remaining width is absorbed by the contraction $E_{d-s}$ (V.1.f).
3. **Which symmetries are used where.**
   - Commutativity uses covariance only under the exchange of the time axis with one spatial axis. This is a lattice symmetry, already contained in the real-spin part of the hierarchy.
   - Spatial invariance of $\mathcal H_0$ needs arbitrary hyperplane reflections, i.e. the continuum $O(4)$ action of VI.1. In the Lean, both steps draw on that action.
   - Both use improper elements of $O(4)$ (reflections), not only rotations.
4. **Scope of the certificate.** Its unique-ground-state assertion concerns the time-axis chamber of the selected phase. It also records the hierarchy, field laws and null-space equality for every phase. The continuation linked to the selected Wightman realization uses that selected table.
5. **Smoothness hypothesis in B02.** B02 needs the derivative identities of the table on which the slab family lives. For phases these come from B09, which uses the slab laws of $S$, so the two applications of B02 are ordered.

## 7. What remains unexplained
1. **Ultraviolet inputs.** The chessboard geometry below [`Next.Phase.travel_price`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Next/PHASE/Trains.lean#L273-L304) belongs to VII.4. Its train estimate and the subsequent scale choice give the bench used here; the supporting packing argument remains outside this unit. The bounded-approximation input to [`Accent.grilled`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/MergeProof/South/FinalG.lean#L76-L83) belongs to III.11. Its output has the cutoff-independent bounds required in V.1.n, but the underlying ultraviolet estimates and the production of the corrected-field data remain inputs.
2. **Order of limits.** In V.1.n, fix the number of copies and approximation index, then fix a sufficiently large translation, take the ordered cutoff limit, and let the translation tend to infinity. Only then let the number of copies tend to infinity and the approximation error tend to zero. The detector argument similarly takes the cutoff limit before the large-translation limit and sends its budget slack to zero last.
3. **Later vacuum uniqueness.** V.1.8 gives uniqueness almost everywhere, which suffices to select $\xi_*$. The stronger result for every phase, [`EveryPhase.every`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/EveryPhase/Vacuum.lean#L146-L153), uses reconstruction in V.3; its proof is outside this unit.
4. **Placement of the spectral calculus.** V.1.2 supplies V.2 and V.3 and can be placed at the start of reconstruction in the written proof. The $O(4)$ action from VI.1 is needed only after the preliminary chamber and heat constructions.
5. **Bibliography.** The reference locations marked “probable” in §2 need bibliographic verification.
