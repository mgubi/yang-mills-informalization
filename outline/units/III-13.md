## 1. What this unit proves

The unit does not prove a new analytic estimate. Its job is **assembly**. The marginal flow (III.9) and the irrelevant-term bounds (III.10–III.12) are available for each label degree separately, with their own choices of constants. The unit shows that all these choices can be made **once**, so that a single Wilson family $C$ carries every property that later parts need. Those properties are the calibration, the ultraviolet limits, the OPE margins, the log package, coverage, and the reconstruction data. This single family is what the public statement calls "the" Wilson family.

Conventions. $G$ is a simple compact gauge group. The Lean object `Family G` is a Wilson family: the RG history, the holonomy data, the label frames, the local sections (lattice representatives of labels, II.1), reference levels, and transfers of labels through the RG. $C.\beta(N,p)$ is the inverse coupling at the Wilson end for cutoff $N$ and period $p$. $C.\mathrm{penalty}=b$ is the penalty of the kernel choice (the free parameter of the order of choice, III.2). The word "table" means the family of limiting Euclidean correlation functions (IV.1).

**Proposition III.13.1 (one family for the UV package; `uv_log`).** Let $G$ be simple and $b>0$. There is a Wilson family $C$ with kernel penalty $b$ such that:
1. $C$ is consistent and calibrated. The selected Wilson trajectory exists; the label sections are sound and fit in the torus; the 'priced' sup bounds hold; every label degree $d$ is calibrated from a reference level $\mathrm{ref}(d)$ on.
2. $C$ is floral. It comes from a single construction (a "cellar" over a rose-parameter) which carries the analytic coupling trajectory on a disc of radius independent of $N$ and the period (see 1.4).
3. The weighted $\ell^1$ (centered) budget bound `Wcmain` holds. For every degree $d$ and all $R,\varepsilon>0$ there is $A\ge0$ such that, for all levels $m\ge\mathrm{ref}(d)$, eventually in $N$, uniformly in the period $p$ and in labels $v$ of norm $\le R$, the centered budget holds with constant $A\,a_m^{-d-\varepsilon}$ (with $a_m$ the spacing at level $m$).
4. The log package holds: a bookkeeping object `LogPackage` with $\mathrm{supportExponent}_m=100$ for all $m$ and the logarithmic OPE margins (consumed in VII.4). So does the "true steps" statement `TrueSteps`.
5. Dense coverage holds.
6. For each extraction order $f$ there are a refinement $g$ and a table $T$ with the base data of the extraction (`Base`) and Maxwell ranks.
7. Complete weak reconstruction holds for every order for which the cell hypotheses hold.

## 3. Strategy

Each of the RG estimates (III.9–III.12) is a statement of the form "there is a threshold in the bare data beyond which...". For the construction of a single Wilson measure one needs the *conjunction* of such statements for all label degrees $d=0,1,2,\dots$, and also the conjunctions of the requirements from the later parts (OPE with logarithmic corrections, coverage for the Gram argument, reconstruction for all phases). The difficulty is the order of quantifiers. Constants that appear before the block sizes $L_j$ (orders, widths, the support exponent, floors) cannot depend on later demands, while the demands for degree $d$ may ask for larger constants. The solution is the one already used in III.9: the stage parameters are chosen as explicit monotone functions of the stage index ("`Orders`": order $4$, power $6+i$, columns $2+i$, support exponent $100$; the choice made in `uv_log`), so that degree $d$ sees them only from a reference level $\mathrm{ref}(d)$ on, and the later constants (protection $\theta$, delays, bars, waits) are chosen by a *monotone* "raise" construction. Any further delay can be raised without invalidating earlier statements.

The proof of 1.1 therefore has three layers.

- **Layer A (one sheet).** From the sheet, the kernel and the stage data, `jointOffers` produces the sheet $s$, prices $T$, a floor $\tau$, and for every $k\ge\tau$ a "provider" $M$ whose rolls $P$ (the $L_j$) exist. For every such roll there is a wage $U$ above which, for any choice of protection, delay, bar and wait raised monotonically, the data is *paid* and the Alloy and its Factor exist. The final conclusion is a long list of facts about the resulting cellar and its yard $C$.
- **Layer B (the cellar and the yard).** The cellar is a bundle of data on which the family $C=\mathrm{yard}(\text{rose},L)$ is defined. Its properties are the content of the many `UVExtension` packages.
- **Layer C (reports).** Short theorems (`ultraviolet`, `uv_log`, `coordination`, `common`, `habitat`) restate the conclusions of the cellar as the requirements of the later parts, and add the OPE and reconstruction services by feeding the seed and grill to producers defined elsewhere.

The mathematics inside layers A and B is the chain: **compose the one-step records for all levels $m$ into a history (`compose`, `repriseQ`, `acclaim`); read the label coefficients along the history; show that observables at injective sites converge as $N\to\infty$ (the Cauchy-sequence argument of `food`); obtain analyticity and uniformity on a disc independent of $N$ and the period (`Spring`, `sheaf`, `inlet`).** Compared with Balaban the novelties are not in the estimates but in the packaging: the existence of the whole history is produced as a single dependent-type record, and its properties are read off one by one.

### 4.1 One sheet, one family

**Lemma 4.10 (convergence at injective sites; `food`, with `seatingM`, `banquet`, `closet`).** This is the proof of 1.6.
1. Fix $\zeta,\varepsilon>0$ (`seatingM`). For each label $u$ with $\mathrm{ref}\le m$ there is, eventually in the level $i$, a weight $w>0$ with $w\,\|\mathrm{alto}\|\cdot4(1+\mathrm{columns})/\text{gain}\le\zeta/2$ and $\mathrm{stock}\cdot(\mathrm{stakes}/w)\le\varepsilon$. This follows from the fact that the growth of the column exponents and the cutoff-dependent quantities is "slow" (sub-exponential in the scale) against the gain $e^{\gamma\,\mathrm{scale}}$.
2. For a finite list of $I$ labels and injectively placed cover points (`banquet`), one has the following. Suppose test functions $g_i$ approximate the label observables on windows of radius $r$ around the sites, with error $q_i$ in the root test, and $t_i$ bounds the observable's root test. Then the product of the lattice observables is within $\prod_i(q_i+t_i)-\prod_i t_i$ of a convergent sequence $F'$. The mechanism is the telescoping identity for products, plus the translation invariance of the observables at the finest level (`P.translation`) and the aggregation of the coarse functions into the fine ones.
3. Choose $q$ with $\Phi(q)=\prod(q+t_i)-\prod t_i<\varepsilon/4$ by continuity at $0$ (using $\Phi(0)=0$). Use the triangle inequality in four terms to show that $(\Psi_N)$ is a Cauchy sequence, which converges by completeness of $\mathbb C$.

## 5. Where the difficulty lies

- **Order of choice, not new estimates.** The unit contains no new analytic estimate of RG type. Its difficulty is a bookkeeping problem of a very particular form. The statement of `jointOffers` has about twenty alternating quantifiers (exists/forall over kernels, wages, protections, delays, bars, waits, menus, auxiliary data) so that every later "raise" can be absorbed. A written proof should state this once, as a general lemma (*monotone choices of constants in a prescribed order*), and cite it, rather than repeating the quantifier chains. About 15,000 of the 22,000 lines are of this kind (the `UVExtension` packages with names such as Wren, Wet, Span, Lacing). The written proof should expect these to be mostly routine once the interface is fixed.
- **Fixed constants.** The explicit stage data $(4,\,6+i,\,2+i,\,100)$ chosen in `uv_log` mean that one global choice is made. That the support exponent is exactly $100$ and the true-steps target rate is $-7/16$ is presumably chosen for convenience; any large enough value should do (the hypothesis in `havens_log` is only $\ge100$). (probable)
- **The family is built from the Wilson measure alone.** The statements assert no property that is not a property of Wilson's lattice measure at the (varying) coupling $C.\beta(N,p)$, apart from the calibration. This is why the result is called *the* Wilson family: IV.1 and VIII.1 read the main theorem off a record `Final` that is built on this $C$.
- **Pointwise versus full convergence.** `food` gives convergence only at injectively placed sites, i.e. for distinct points of the lattice rescaled to the fixed torus, along the *full* sequence of cutoffs $N$. Convergence at coincident points and the infinite-volume limit are not claimed here; they come from the extraction (IV.1) and the OPE (VII.4). Notice also that `food` is stated for a cellar built from a rose-parameter, not for an arbitrary family. The passage to $C$ is made through `Floral`.
- **Statement strength.** `Wcmain` is a statement "in the schedule" (docstring: *a literal estimate in the schedule, not upon eventual extraction*), i.e. it is a bound of the centered budget of the lattice observable with a constant $A\,a_m^{-d-\varepsilon}$ and not a bound after a subsequence is taken. This is stronger than what the physics requires (a bound after extraction would suffice), but it is the natural outcome of the cluster expansion.
- **Names.** The code uses nicknames for everything (`Sheet`, `Rolls`, `Alloy`, `Factor`, `Cellar`, `Wages`, `Bandage`, `Tariffs`, `Purchased`, `Paid`, `Sheltered`), and many of the Lean statements are nearly unreadable. The informal summaries should be reviewed against the Lean before they are used. The glossary translations are in 6.
