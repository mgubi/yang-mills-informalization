## 1. What this unit proves

The unit supplies the "marginal" input of the renormalization map. The effective action at each level is written as a leading part $\mathrm{main}=\text{quadratic}+\text{cubic}+\text{quartic}$ in explicit lattice-path form, plus irrelevant activities. This unit proves two things about the leading part.

* **(A) Start.** At the finest level a leading part exists whose Taylor jet agrees with the Wilson energy through order four, uniformly in the volume.
* **(B) Step.** For one block-spin step, a leading part at the coarse level exists and is related to the fine one by the *classical matching* of its Taylor jets. The one-loop shift of the coupling is explicitly $-\kappa\log L+O(1)$ with $\kappa=11C/(24\pi^2)$. The stationary (background-field) Hessian is uniformly coercive. A finite-bound estimate holds for the second derivative of the one-loop "thermal" function.

The data produced in (B) are exactly the data that III.9 carries as the hypothesis `Kin.Planned` (fields `kinetic`, `ClassicalMatch`, `FiniteBound`). So this unit discharges the main open point of the III.9 note, that the value $11/3$ is imported there.

**Proposition III.7.1 (the objects).**
* A *path datum* $R$ consists of a row family (the quadratic part, one summable sequence of weighted lattice paths per pair of plaquette orientations), a sequence of *loop atoms* with two legs, and sequences of *cubic* and *quartic atoms* with three and four legs. A leg is a lattice path (a "slot") ending in a plaquette, carrying the curvature of that plaquette transported to the base point by the path. An atom is a colour tensor (a polynomial in the transported curvatures) with a coefficient. The atoms are required to be gauge-invariant.

They also say that the loop coefficients satisfy two moment normalisations (total weight zero, first moment zero), and that the row family satisfies a metric identity at zero field.
* The *main term* at a configuration is the patch average of $\text{quadratic}(\text{clipped})+\text{cubic}+\text{quartic}$ evaluated on the path curvatures.

## 3. Strategy

The unit has two independent halves, which meet in III.7.4.

*Half 1 (classical matching, §4.1–4.5).* To write the Wilson energy through order four as a sum of path atoms one expands $1-\tfrac1N\mathrm{Re\,tr}\,U_p$ to fourth order in the Lie-algebra increments. The quadratic term is $\tfrac12|F|^2$ at the lowest order, but in the chosen representation it is the *bed* (a given quadratic form, not necessarily the Wilson one). The cubic and quartic terms must then be reconstructed so that the *total* jet equals that of the Wilson energy. The difference "Wilson jet minus bed's quadratic part" is a gauge-invariant function of the field with vanishing lower jets. To write it as a sum of curvature monomials along paths with exponentially decaying coefficients one needs a Poincaré lemma for the complex of gauge-invariant polynomials in the field. It has to carry a cost (an exponential weight on the support) at every stage. This is the Koszul-type lift of §4.3; Fourier analysis converts "exponential decay of coefficients" into "analyticity in a strip" and back.

*Half 2 (stationary step, §4.6–4.8).* Following Balaban, the effective action at the coarse level is obtained by solving the variational problem for the background field, expanding to second order and integrating the Gaussian fluctuation. The leading term is then $\mathrm{main}_{\rm coarse}=$ classical transform of $\mathrm{main}_{\rm fine}$, plus a one-loop correction. The correction is a $\tfrac12\log\det$ of the Hessian (together with ghost and Jacobian terms), and one needs two facts about it: (i) uniform invertibility (coercivity) of the Hessian in a strip of momenta, with a bound independent of the volume (§4.6); (ii) the coefficient of $F^2$ in the second variation of the one-loop term is explicitly $-\kappa\log L+O(1)$ (§4.8). The latter is obtained by computing the momentum integral of an explicit rational function over the shell $L^{-1}<|k|\le1$ in $\mathbb R^4$ and bounding the rest by absolute value.

What differs from Balaban's papers: (a) the whole matching is in the *path* representation with exponentially weighted summable families, so that the output at each level is again of the form required as input at the next, with the weight clauses and the order of constants (III.2) fixed once and for all. This replaces Balaban's inductive bounds on the $\mathcal{R}$-operation and his explicit expansions of the effective action in polynomial form. (b) Both halves are carried out for an *arbitrary* simple compact gauge group, with the Casimir constant as the only group-dependent quantity in the slope.

### 4.4 Matching the cubic and quartic terms; the Wilson jet

**Lemma 4.8 (classical emission of cubic and quartic atoms).** Let a function $E_i$ on links of side $m_i$ be pointwise defined, have vanishing jets at order $<3$ relative to the quadratic input series, and have recorded series of orders $2$ and $3$ with bound $M$. ### 4.7 The one-loop slope

**Lemma 4.16 (the momentum integral).** Let $\mu\in(0,1]$ and let $\mathrm{orbShell}(\mu)=\{k\in\mathbb R^4:\mu<|k|\le1\}$. Define
$$h(k)=4\,C_G\Big(\frac{k_1^2}{|k|^6}-\frac{4\,k_1^2k_0^2}{|k|^8}-\frac1{|k|^4}\Big).$$
Then
$$\frac1{(2\pi)^4}\int_{\mathrm{orbShell}(\mu)}h(k)\,d^4k=-\,\frac{11\,C_G}{24\pi^2}\,\log\frac1\mu .$$
*Sketch:* by rotation invariance, $\int k_1^2/|k|^6=\tfrac14\int|k|^{-4}$ and $\int k_1^2k_0^2/|k|^8=\tfrac1{24}\int|k|^{-4}$ (the lemmas `joy_two`, `joy_heights`; the latter uses the symmetric average over $e_i\pm e_j$). With $\int_{\mu<|k|\le1}|k|^{-4}d^4k=2\pi^2\log(1/\mu)$ the bracket averages to $(\tfrac14-\tfrac16-1)=-\tfrac{11}{12}$ times $|k|^{-4}$, and $4\cdot\tfrac{11}{12}\cdot2\pi^2/(2\pi)^4=\tfrac{11}{24\pi^2}$. The integrand $h$ is the "pole vector": by `haloRate` it equals half of the difference between the vector fluctuation's second-order coefficient and one half the ghost's (so the $-1$ is the ghost-like term, and the $-4k_1^2k_0^2/|k|^8$ and $k_1^2/|k|^6$ are orbital and spin parts), in the background-field gauge (probable).

## 5. Where the difficulty lies

* **Routine in outline, long in practice.** Half 1 is the algebraic Poincaré lemma with cost: it is standard that gauge-invariant polynomial Taylor coefficients of the Wilson action are sums of curvature monomials along paths. The 19,000 lines come from keeping the exponential weights and the colour and lattice symmetries in the Fourier picture through every operation. The key facts are 4.3 (with the loss $\sigma\to\sigma/4$) and 4.6–4.8.
* **The actual new content is Half 2.** The one-loop $\kappa=11C/(24\pi^2)$ is computed here from an explicit integral (4.16), not imported. In the written proof this is the place where asymptotic freedom first enters, and it should be presented with the background-field gauge, vector, ghost and spin terms explicitly (§7). The only place where $\mathrm{rank}$, gauge and Casimir enter is the factor $C_G$ in $h$.
* **Weak error control in the slope.** The bound is $|\alpha+\kappa\log L|\le D$ with $D$ independent of $L$, which is far weaker than a two-loop statement but enough for $\beta_m\sim\kappa s_m$ (III.9.3(5)). The cost is hidden in the constants $A,D$ (4.17).
* **Everything is for "sequences of sides tending to infinity".** The statements `launch`, `HorizCalc`, `honored` and `post_cert` quantify over $m:\mathbb N\to\mathrm{Side}$ with $m_i\to\infty$ and use the limit as the main tool (an error of size $o(1)\times\#\text{sites}$, jets agreeing "for all $i$"). This is a way of obtaining volume-uniformity without writing explicit finite-volume constants. It means that the output is not a bound at a given finite volume. The conversion to uniform finite-volume bounds is in the consumers (III.9, III.12) (tentative).
* **Unusual features.** The code stores the claims in a conditional form: `Fluent` and the `Craft.Rack`, `Retained`, `Paid` data are hypotheses of `claiming` and `geared`. `pouring` discharges `Fluent`; the others must be discharged by the units named in §2. The exact edge of this interface is the place where a careful reader should look to check that no circularity arises (the amplitude $A$ of the bed depends on the earlier levels, and so do the widths $\sigma_j$).
* **Names.** The code uses arbitrary names: "bed" is the Fourier data of the quadratic form, "film" and "picture" are kernels analytic in a momentum strip, "tactics" are kernel families for the force operator, "grant" is a coercivity bound, "alpha payment" is the $F^2$-coefficient of the one-loop term, "profitable cue" is the film built from the one-loop pole.
