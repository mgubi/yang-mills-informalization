Conventions. A step goes from a fine lattice of side $f=cL$ to a coarse lattice of side $c$, with an odd block factor $L\ge3$. $G$ is the compact gauge group, given as a matrix group of size $N$. For $u\in G$ the code uses the "chord" $\mathrm{ch}(u)=\|u-1\|/\sqrt N$ and the "price" $\mathrm{pr}(u)=1-\mathrm{Re\,tr}\,u/N$, so $\mathrm{ch}^2=2\,\mathrm{pr}$ ([`Gauge/MatrixGroup.lean`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Gauge/MatrixGroup.lean)). The price is the Wilson plaquette density. The code's "tags", "tolls", "wires", "fuel", "port" and "teller" are explained in §6.

## 1. What this unit proves

This unit defines the single coarse-graining step of the renormalization group as an exact probability kernel $\rho_\beta(U,V)$ from fine to coarse configurations. It proves the properties of the step that the rest of Part III needs: normalization, covariance, a uniform choice of the gauge-fixing branches, and the comparison of the predicted coarse field with the fine curvature. It also fixes the data format for activities and path data. Finally it records, as specifications, the energy lower bound and the kinetic data that other units supply.

**Proposition III.1.1 (the block kernel).** Fix a patch $P$ (block factor $L$, smoothing order $J\ge4$) and kernel constants $\mathcal C=(\kappa,\delta,\varepsilon,a)$: penalty $\kappa>0$, frame cutoff $\delta>0$, predictor cutoff $\varepsilon>0$, axial factor $a>0$. Assume the branch hypotheses of 1.3 hold for $(P,\mathcal C,G)$. For $\beta\in\mathbb R$, $U$ a fine configuration and $V$ a coarse configuration, put
$$\rho_\beta(U,V)=\sum_{t}\lambda(U,t)\prod_{b}Z_\beta^{-1}\exp\!\big(-\beta\kappa\,\mathrm{pr}(V_b\,Q_b(U,t)^{-1})\big).$$
Here $t=(t_{\rm site},t_{\rm bond})$ is a finite tag, $\lambda(U,t)\ge0$ is its conditional probability, $Q(U,t)$ is a coarse configuration predicted from $U$ and $t$, $Z_\beta$ is the Haar normalization, and $b$ runs over coarse links. Then $\rho_\beta\ge0$, it is jointly measurable and bounded, and for every $U$
$$\int\rho_\beta(U,V)\,dV=1,$$
where $dV$ is the product of probability Haar measures. Hence the push-forward $F\mapsto\int\rho_\beta(\cdot,\!U)F(U)\,d\mu(U)$ of a density $F$ against the fine prior is defined, and it is the operation used throughout Part III to pass from level $m+1$ to level $m$.
*Status:* theorem, under the branch hypotheses (which are shown to hold by 1.3).

## 3. Strategy

The step follows Balaban's scheme in outline. Given a fine configuration $U$, one produces a *prediction* $Q(U)$ of the coarse field, namely a group-valued average of the fine parallel transports along each block link, and then lets the coarse field fluctuate around it with a Gaussian-type weight $\exp(-\beta\kappa\,\mathrm{pr}(VQ^{-1}))$. Balaban's averaging operator is linear in the Lie algebra and needs a background gauge fixed once and for all. Here, instead, the averaging is made *exactly gauge covariant* by fixing the gauge on each patch by an intrinsic rule. This rule is a minimization over the whole patch of the average price of all transports $h_xW_{xy}h_y^{-1}$, with $h$ at the center equal to $1$. The rule works when the configuration is close to pure gauge on the patch. Otherwise a fallback is used: a random *axial* gauge, whose root and ordering of axes are drawn from a tag $t$. Only axial gauges with small total link size (compared with the curvature) are admitted. The point of 1.4 is that such tags always exist, with a constant $16$.

Everything is randomized so that $\lambda(U,t)$ is a probability distribution on tags and $\rho_\beta(U,\cdot)$ is exactly a probability density on the coarse space (no division by a configuration-dependent density, as the file header insists). Gauge and lattice covariance then follow from the intrinsic nature of the rule. The estimates of 1.6–1.8 are the quantitative content. They say that, whatever branch is realized, the frame is within $O(\text{curvature})$ of any background gauge of one's choice, and that the predicted coarse plaquette is bounded by $C L^{-2}$ times the fine curvature in the surrounding box of size $\sim(J+7)L$. This is what the later chapters use to decide which fine configurations are in the small-field region and to charge the large-field region with energy ("seeds" and the identity of 1.10).

Differences from Balaban: (i) the kernel does not depend on $\beta$; $\beta$ only enters the noise, so the same patch rule serves every coupling and the coupling reset of III.9 only changes the noise weight. (ii) The prediction is not an average but a *minimizer* of an average price, with a random fallback. (iii) The kernel is built as an exact Markov kernel with explicit finite tags, instead of an operator on the Fourier side; no covariance $\Gamma$ or propagator appears in this unit.

### 4.1 The kernel and its covariance

**Lemma 4.4 (axial gauge with small edge sum).** This is 1.4.

### 4.4 Energy accounting and data format

**Lemma 4.15 (tube identity; this is 1.10).**
*Sketch:* each of the six works is written as an inner product of the "vision" $w$ (the thinned coarse curvature) with the transported fine data. ## 5. Where the difficulty lies

- **Routine but long.** The geometry in 4.4–4.14 is elementary: telescoping products, triangle inequalities for the chord, sums over boxes. Most of the 4,200 lines are the bookkeeping of paths on the cover, of the signed symmetries (covariance) and of the data structures for activities, which must be written out so that every later unit can quote them.
- **Genuinely hard.** Two points. (i) Uniqueness of a minimizing gauge frame (4.6) and its stability with constants independent of $L$, $J$ and the number of points; this requires a self-consistency argument for a "nearest group point" fixed point on a product group. (ii) The $L^{-2}$ gain in 1.8, which is what makes the predicted coarse curvature a faithful image of the fine curvature without loss growing with $L$. The decision to use a gauge-covariant randomized kernel rather than a deterministic averaging is what makes exact normalization possible.
- **Unusual.** (a) The kernel constants do not depend on $\beta$ or on $L$ and $J$; they are fixed once for the group. (b) The fallback branch makes the kernel well defined for every fine configuration, including large-field ones, at the price of a random tag. (c) There is no covariance or propagator in this unit. The determinant and the Gaussian integration appear only in the *specification* `Kin.Planned` and are carried out in other units (III.3, III.4, III.6).
- **Weaker or stronger than expected.** 1.11 and 1.12 are *requirements*, not theorems of this unit. If the written proof is to be self-contained, the energy lower bound (work items E01–E09) and the finite-bound clause (the determinant comparison) must be placed in a unit that proves them; see §7.
