**Conventions.** $G\subset U(N)$ is a closed subgroup with probability Haar measure $dg$. Its Lie algebra $\mathfrak g\subset M_N(\mathbb C)$ carries the real inner product $\mathrm{Re\,tr}(X^{\dagger}Y)$, and $\Pi_{\mathfrak g}$ denotes the orthogonal projection onto $\mathfrak g$. The *chord* is $\mathrm{ch}(u)=\|u-1\|/\sqrt N$ (Frobenius norm) and the *price* is $\mathrm{pr}(u)=1-\mathrm{Re\,tr}\,u/N$. They satisfy $\mathrm{ch}^2=2\,\mathrm{pr}$, both are class functions, $\mathrm{ch}(u^{-1})=\mathrm{ch}(u)\le2$, $\mathrm{ch}(uv)\le\mathrm{ch}(u)+\mathrm{ch}(v)$, and $\|u-v\|^2=2N\,\mathrm{pr}(uv^{-1})$. The Wilson energy is $\sum_p\mathrm{pr}(U_p)$.

A step goes from the fine torus $\Lambda_f=(\mathbb Z/f)^4$ to the coarse torus $\Lambda_c$, with $f=cL$ and $L\ge3$ odd. The coarse site $x$ has root $Lx$ and block $Lx+B_{(L-1)/2}$, where $B_r=\{-r,\dots,r\}^4\subset\mathbb Z^4$. Configurations are $U\in G^{\mathrm{links}}$, and $\mu_f$ is the product of the probability Haar measures. The gauge action is $(k\cdot U)_{(y,i)}=k_yU_{(y,i)}k_{y+e_i}^{-1}$. $T_U(y;w)$ is the parallel transport along a walk $w$. $T_U(y\to z;o)$ is the transport along the coordinate route that changes the coordinates in the order given by $o\in S_4$. For a coarse site $x$, $U^{(x)}$ is the periodic lift of $U$ to $\mathbb Z^4$, translated so that $Lx$ sits at the origin. All patch constructions take place on $\mathbb Z^4$, so a patch may be larger than the torus. For $X\subset\mathbb Z^4$, $E_X(U)$ is the sum of the chords of the links with both ends in $X$, and $F_X(U)$ is the sum of the chords of the plaquettes with all four corners in $X$; $F_X$ is gauge invariant. $\mathfrak B_4$ is the group of signed permutations of the axes (384 elements). For $s\in\mathfrak B_4$ and a point $b$, $s_bU$ is the image of $U$ under the lattice symmetry $z\mapsto b+s(z)$; a link reversed by $s$ carries the inverse variable. Further, $\kappa_{\rm slope}=11C_A/(24\pi^2)$, where $C_A$ is the adjoint Casimir, and $\mathrm{temp}(x)=x^{-1/2}e^{(\log\log x)^2}$.

**Definition (the block kernel).** A *patch* is $P=(L,J)$ with $L\ge3$ odd and $J\ge4$. Its radius is $R=(J+3)L$ and its points are those of $B_R$, with centre $0$. The *kernel constants* are $K=(\kappa,\delta,\varepsilon,a)$, all positive: penalty, frame cutoff, predictor cutoff, axial factor. For a configuration $W$ on $\mathbb Z^4$ the following objects are defined.

1. *Frame tags and axial frames.* A frame tag is $t=(r,o)\in B_R\times S_4$. The axial frame of $t$ is $a^t_y=T_W(r\to y;o)$.
2. *Minimizing branch.* $W\in M_\delta$ if $\mathrm{ch}\big(a^{(0,o)}_y\,T_W(y\to z;o')\,(a^{(0,o)}_z)^{-1}\big)<\delta$ for all $o,o'\in S_4$ and $y,z\in B_R$. In words: in each of the 24 axial gauges from the centre, every route transport in the patch is $\delta$-close to $1$.
3. *Score.* For $h:B_R\to G$, $S_W(h)=\mathrm{avg}_{y,z\in B_R}\mathrm{avg}_{o\in S_4}\,\mathrm{pr}\big(h_yT_W(y\to z;o)h_z^{-1}\big)$.
4. *Eligible tags.* $\mathcal E(W)=\{t:\ E_{B_R}(a^t\cdot W)\le a(2R+1)F_{B_R}(W)\}$.
5. *Frame and tag mass.* If $W\in M_\delta$, the frame $h^W$ is a minimizer of $S_W$ subject to $h_0=1$, and the tag mass is uniform, $1/(24|B_R|)$. Otherwise $h^{W,t}_y=(a^t_0)^{-1}a^t_y$ (the axial gauge from $r$, normalized at the centre), with mass $\mathbf 1_{\mathcal E(W)}(t)/|\mathcal E(W)|$.
6. *Weight.* $w$ is the law on $B_R$ of $\xi_1+\dots+\xi_J$, where the $\xi_k$ are i.i.d. uniform on $B_{(L-1)/2}$. It is the $J$-fold convolution of the uniform weight on a centred block; it is $\mathfrak B_4$-invariant and supported in $B_{J(L-1)/2}$.
7. *Connector.* For a coarse link $b=(x,i)$ and frame tags $(t_x)_{x\in\Lambda_c}$, set $Y_y=h^{(x)}_y\,T_{U^{(x)}}(y;\ L\text{ steps along }e_i)\,(h^{(x+e_i)}_y)^{-1}$ for $y\in B_R$. Here $h^{(x)}$ is the frame of $U^{(x)}$ with tag $t_x$. This is the fine transport from $Lx+y$ to $L(x+e_i)+y$, written in the frames of the two patches.
8. *Prediction.* Let $\Phi_Y(u)=\sum_yw_y\,\mathrm{pr}(Y_yu^{-1})$. If $\min\Phi_Y<\varepsilon$, then $Q_b$ is the minimizer, which is the nearest point of $G$ to the matrix mean $\sum_yw_yY_y$ (an extrinsic, chordal mean); the bond tag then has uniform mass $1/|B_R|$. Otherwise $Q_b=Y_{r_b}$, with the bond tag $r_b$ drawn with probability $w_{r_b}$.
9. *Kernel.* The tag law is $\lambda(U,t)=\prod_{x}m^{\rm fr}(U^{(x)},t_x)\prod_b m^{\rm pr}(Y_b,r_b)$, and

$$\rho_\beta(U,V)=\sum_t\lambda(U,t)\prod_{b}Z_\beta^{-1}e^{-\beta\kappa\,\mathrm{pr}(V_bQ_b(U,t)^{-1})},\qquad Z_\beta=\int_Ge^{-\beta\kappa\,\mathrm{pr}(g)}\,dg .$$

The *branch conditions* for $(P,K,G)$ are:
- (B1) for $W\in M_\delta$ the constrained minimizer in item 5 is unique;
- (B2) whenever $\min\Phi_Y<\varepsilon$, the minimizer of $\Phi_Y$ is unique;
- (B3) $\mathcal E(W)\ne\emptyset$ for every $W$.

The Lean definitions are total because they use a fixed choice of minimizer; (B1)–(B2) make that choice irrelevant. ## 1. What this unit proves

The unit defines one block-spin step as an exact Markov kernel $\rho_\beta(U,V)$. It proves that the kernel is normalized and exactly covariant under gauge transformations and the lattice symmetries. It shows that the branch conditions hold with constants depending only on $G$. It proves the geometric estimates that later units use: synchronization of frames and an $L^{-2}$ bound on the predicted coarse curvature. It proves that the kernel is real analytic near the trivial configuration, with a Balaban-type linearization, and it proves the tree-gauge reduction of the step integral. Finally it fixes the format of effective densities and RG histories, and states as requirements the energy-per-seed bound (proved in III.10) and the kinetic data of a history (supplied in III.2).

**Proposition III.1.1 (the block kernel is a Markov kernel).** Assume (B1)–(B3). For every $\beta\in\mathbb R$:
- $\rho_\beta\ge0$;
- $(U,V)\mapsto\rho_\beta(U,V)$ is Borel;
- $\sup\rho_\beta<\infty$;
- $\int\rho_\beta(U,V)\,d\mu_c(V)=1$ for every $U$.

Consequently, for every Banach-space-valued $F\in L^1(\mu_f)$ the push $(\mathcal P_\beta F)(V)=\int\rho_\beta(U,V)F(U)\,d\mu_f(U)$ is defined for every $V$. Moreover $\mathcal P_\beta F\in L^1(\mu_c)$, $\mathcal P_\beta$ is linear, and $\int\mathcal P_\beta F\,d\mu_c=\int F\,d\mu_f$. This is the operation that takes level $m+1$ to level $m$.

**Proposition III.1.8 (predicted versus actual coarse curvature).** For $G$, $K$, $j$ let $C_8=8\max(1,\varepsilon^{-1})(2C+D)$, with $C,D$ from III.1.7. Let $S$ be any step with constants $K$ and $J=j$. Then for all $U$ and $V$, every coarse site $x$, every tag $t$ usable at $x$ and every plane $p$:

$$\mathrm{ch}\big(Q(U,t)_{(x,p)}\big)\le \frac{C_8}{L^2}\,\mathrm{port}(x,U),\qquad \mathrm{ch}\big(V_{(x,p)}\big)\le \mathrm{fuel}(Q,V)_{(x,p)}+\frac{C_8}{L^2}\,\mathrm{port}(x,U).$$

Here $Q_{(x,p)}$ denotes the plaquette holonomy, and $\mathrm{fuel}(Q,V)_{(x,p)}=\sum_{e\in\partial(x,p)}\mathrm{ch}(V_eQ_e^{-1})$ is the sum of the four *wires* of the plaquette. The macro-box has at most $(\ell_jL)^4$ sites, so $L^{-4}\mathrm{port}$ is, up to a factor depending on $J$ only, the average fine plaquette chord there. **Proposition III.1.9 (effective densities and RG histories).** This proposition consists of definitions, with four theorems.

(a) *Budget points, flags, masks.* A budget point is a tuple (power $\ge2$, order $\ge6$, support exponent $>0$, cover size $B\ge1$, threshold $t\in(0,1]$). The flagged sites $\mathrm{flags}_t(U)$ are the sites touched by a plaquette of chord $>t$. $U$ is *healthy* on $X$ if $X\cap\mathrm{flags}_t(U)=\emptyset$, and the masked function is $\mathbf 1_{\{\text{healthy on }X\}}F$. A function is *regular* on $X$ if it depends only on links in $X$, is Borel and gauge invariant, and on configurations healthy on $X$ has left-increment derivatives up to the order. Its *sup bounds* control these derivatives in directions with $|A_e|\le t(1+\mathrm{dist}(e,o))^{\rm power}$. *Theorem:* a function that reads the links in $X$ and the flags in $X$ depends only on the links in $\mathrm{inspected}(X)$, the union of $X$ and the corners of all plaquettes meeting $X$.

(b) *Activities.* There are regular activities $E_i$, with an address (a site $o_i$ in a support $X_i$), and forced activities $K_i$, with a support $Y_i$, an inventory $\emptyset\ne I_i\subset Y_i$ and an extra Borel test. *Validity* means the following:
- $E_i$ is regular on $X_i$ and $E_i(1)=0$;
- if $X_i$ does not wrap around the torus, the Hessian of $E_i$ at the trivial configuration vanishes on affine directions $A_{(y,\mu)}=a_\mu+\sum_\nu(y-o_i)_\nu d_{\mu\nu}$. These are the constant-curvature potentials, so the regular activities carry no marginal $\mathrm{tr}F^2$ term (probable reading);
- $K_i$ is gauge invariant and Borel, reads only links and flags in $Y_i$, and vanishes unless $Y_i\cap\mathrm{flags}_t=I_i$ and the test holds;
- covariance under every element of $\mathfrak B_4$ and every base point (an index permutation that moves addresses and values);
- weighted summability with weights $e^{(\text{support exponent})\cdot(\text{cover number}_B)}$.

The *gas* is $\sum_b\prod_{i\in b}K_i(U)$, summed over finite families $b$ with pairwise disjoint supports whose inventories cover $\mathrm{flags}_t(U)$ exactly. *Theorem:* the gas equals $1$ when $\mathrm{flags}_t(U)=\emptyset$.

(c) *Leading action.* A clip has $c_{\rm dir}>16\,c_{\rm lin}>0$ and a relaxation length. Path data consist of row series $\ell_p(U)=\sum_kr_{p,k}\,\mathrm{slot}_k(U)$ (weighted parallel-transported curvatures) and loop, cubic and quartic atoms. Their bounds require exponentially weighted summability, invariance, the normalization $c_{\rm dir}\delta_{pq}+\sum_k\hat s_k(p)\hat s_k(q)=\delta_{pq}$ of the zero-momentum symbols, and vanishing zeroth and first moments of the loop coefficients. The per-plaquette term is $\mathrm{clip}_t(\mathrm{ch}U_p)+\mathbf 1_{\rm kept}\tfrac12|\ell_p(U)|^2$, where $\mathrm{clip}_t(s)=c_{\rm dir}s^2/2$ for $s\le t$ and $c_{\rm lin}ts$ for $s>t$. The leading action $\mathcal S$ is the $\mathfrak B_4$-average of $\beta\,[\sum_p(\cdot)+\text{cubic}+\text{quartic}]+\text{loop}$. A representation $\mathcal R=(\text{path data, clip, activities}, p,\beta,v)$ evaluates to $\mathrm{eval}_{\mathcal R}=\exp\big(v|\Lambda|-\mathcal S-\sum_i\mathbf 1_{\rm healthy}E_i\big)\cdot\mathrm{gas}$. *Theorem:* if $\mathcal R$ is lawful (path bounds and valid activities), then $\mathrm{eval}_{\mathcal R}$ is Borel and bounded.

(d) *Histories.*
- A *calendar* holds the side/factor schedule $L_m$, the kernel constants, the barrier $\bar\beta$, stage constants, orders $J_m\ge4$ and exponents $\theta$. It sets $\hat\beta_m=\bar\beta+\kappa_{\rm slope}\,\mathrm{scale}(m)$, $t_m=\mathrm{temp}(\hat\beta_m)$ and cover size $\lceil(\log\hat\beta_m)^2\rceil$.
- Step $m$ goes from level $m+1$ to level $m$, with patch $(L_m,J_{m+1})$.
- The *fits* conditions include: $J_m$ nondecreasing with $J_{m+1}\le J_m+1$ and $J_m\to\infty$; $\hat\beta_m>1$; $t_m\le1$; torus sides at least $100(\text{cover size}+1)$ and at least $100(R+1)$; drift and logarithmic-cost bounds.
- *Levels* means a calendar with its fits conditions and block control at every step.
- A history assigns a representation $\mathcal R_{N,p,m}$ to each mesh index $N$, volume $p$ and level $m\le N$. It is *selected* if: the path data do not depend on $p$; each $\mathcal R_{N,p,m}$ is lawful with the level-$m$ constants and $\beta_{N,p,m}\in[\hat\beta_m/2,2\hat\beta_m]$; $\mathcal R_{N,p,N}$ is the bare Wilson density; $\mathrm{eval}_{N,p,m}=\mathcal P_{\beta_{N,p,m+1}}\mathrm{eval}_{N,p,m+1}$ with $|\beta_{m+1}-\beta_m-\kappa_{\rm slope}\log L_m|\le\mathrm{drift}_{m+1}$; $\beta_{N,p,0}=\bar\beta$; and $|\beta_{N,p,m}-\hat\beta_m|=o(\mathrm{scale}(m))$ uniformly in $N,p$.

*Theorem:* for a selected history, $\int\mathrm{eval}_{N,p,m}\,d\mu=Z_{N,p}(\beta_{N,p,N})$, the Wilson partition function, for every $m\le N$.

**Proposition III.1.10 (the tube identity).** A *tube* for a step is a tower of transfer kernels together with path data and clips at the fine level (up) and at the coarse level (down). Fix a path-length cutoff, a fine threshold $t$ and a coarse threshold $s$. From $V$ one builds:
- the *vision* $w$ (the thinned coarse curvature and row data);
- linear maps $\Phi,\Phi',\mathcal N$, assembled from the adjoint action of parallel transports of $V$ along cut-off routes;
- the fine data $X_f(U)$, the coarse data $X_n(V)$, and the noise field $Z_b=\sqrt\kappa\,\mathrm{curv}(V_bQ_b^{-1})$.

With these, set

$$\begin{aligned}
W_F&=\mathrm{crown}_{\rm up}(U)+\tfrac12\|\Phi w\|^2-\langle\Phi w,X_f\rangle, & W_I&=\tfrac\kappa2\,\mathrm{ch}(V_bQ_b^{-1})^2+\tfrac12\|\Phi' w\|^2-\langle\Phi'w,Z\rangle,\\
W_C&=\langle w,X_n\rangle-\tfrac12\|w\|^2-\mathrm{crown}_{\rm down}(V), & W_L&=\langle w,\Phi^{\dagger}X_f+\Phi'^{\dagger}Z-X_n\rangle,\\
W_N&=\tfrac12\|\mathcal Nw\|^2, & W_D&=\tfrac12\langle w,(1-\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi-\Phi'^{\dagger}\Phi'-\mathcal N^{\dagger}\mathcal N)w\rangle .
\end{aligned}$$

Here crown is the clipped quadratic part of the per-plaquette term of III.1.9(c). Then for all $U,Q,V$, summing each work over its index set,

$$\textstyle\sum W_F+\sum W_I+\sum W_C+\sum W_N+\sum W_L+\sum W_D=\sum\mathrm{crown}_{\rm up}(U)+\sum_b\tfrac\kappa2\,\mathrm{ch}(V_bQ_b^{-1})^2-\sum\mathrm{crown}_{\rm down}(V).$$

**Proposition III.1.12 (kinetic specification of a directed history).** The kinetic data of a history are, for each $N$ and $m$: a one-loop shift $\alpha_{N,m}$, path data, and a stationary (background-field) map from coarse Lie-algebra fields to the free links of each forest. The *planned* conditions on them are:
- (i) consistency with the calendar (stage profiles, block factors);
- (ii) at every level $m\le N$, the path data are attached to the tower of the horizon and satisfy the stage bounds of III.1.9(c);
- (iii) at the finest level $N$, the leading term has the same jet through order $4$ at the trivial configuration as the Wilson energy, and the loop coefficients vanish;
- (iv) for $m<N$ and every forest: a price gap, a criticality condition of the stationary map with its location (`Critical`, `Guessed`), and a classical match between consecutive path data (tentative reading of these three clauses);
- (v) $|\alpha_{N,m}+\kappa_{\rm slope}\log L_m|+1\le\mathrm{drift}_{m+1}$;
- (vi) a finite-volume one-loop bound, described next.

For (vi), consider the *thermal* function: loop terms + $\tfrac12\log\det$ (Hessian of the fluctuation integrand at the stationary point) − $\log$ (Jacobian of the exponential chart). It and the classical draft $\alpha_{N,m}\cdot(\text{quadratic term})+\text{loop}$ are analytic at $0$, the Hessian determinant is positive near $0$, and their second derivatives along any two plane waves of amplitude $\le1$ differ by at most $|\Lambda_c|\,\Theta\,\Lambda\,e^{-\gamma c}$.

A *directed* history packages these data with a levels record, a local chart-ratio condition (the exponential chart pushes a local density onto Haar measure), the price conditions and a recipe. A *move* of a directed history is one realized RG step: $\mathrm{eval}_m=\mathcal P_{\beta_{m+1}}\mathrm{eval}_{m+1}$, a bound on the forced activities, and $|\beta_m-\beta_{m+1}-\alpha_{N,m}|\le1$. With (v) this gives the drift bound of III.1.9(d).

## 3. Strategy

The step follows Bałaban's block-spin scheme, but the coarse field is defined by an exact, gauge-covariant Markov kernel on the whole configuration space. Given a fine configuration, one fixes a gauge (a *frame*) on a patch of radius $(J+3)L$ around each block. One then transports the fine field across each coarse link in the frames of the two neighbouring patches. The resulting connectors are averaged with the $J$-fold convolution weight, and the average is projected to the group: the coarse *prediction* is the nearest point of $G$ to $\sum_yw_yY_y$. Finally the coarse field is drawn from the Haar-normalized weight $e^{-\beta\kappa\,\mathrm{pr}(VQ^{-1})}$. On configurations close to pure gauge on the patch (the minimizing branch), the frame is the solution of a least-squares synchronization problem for all route transports of the patch. This solution is intrinsic and unique up to a global left multiple, which is fixed by $h_0=1$. Elsewhere the frame is an axial gauge whose root and axis ordering are drawn uniformly among the *eligible* tags, those whose axial gauge has total link chord at most $a(2R+1)$ times the curvature. When the weighted price is not small, the prediction is a random connector sample drawn from $w$.

All random choices are drawn from symmetric distributions, and each deterministic choice is the unique minimizer of a symmetric functional. Hence $\rho_\beta$ is exactly covariant under all gauge transformations and under $\mathfrak B_4$; this is the averaging over symmetry-breaking gauge choices which later yields Euclidean covariance. The tag probabilities sum to $1$ and the noise is a probability density, so $\rho_\beta$ is exactly normalized, with no configuration-dependent denominator. The branch conditions (uniqueness of the two minimizers, existence of eligible tags) hold with cutoffs depending only on $G$. They follow from a contraction argument for the synchronization problem, from the tubular neighbourhood of $G$, and from a discrete Stokes inequality for axial gauges.

The quantitative content is twofold.
- Whatever branch occurs, the realized frame agrees with any reference gauge up to $O((2R+1)\times\text{curvature})$ (III.1.6). A single axial gauge on the macro-box of radius $(J+7)L$ synchronizes the frames of the $5^4$ surrounding patches, and the predicted coarse plaquette is bounded by $L^{-2}$ times the fine curvature in the macro-box (III.1.7–III.1.8). This is the input for the small-field/large-field decomposition and for the energy accounting of III.1.10–III.1.11.
- Near the trivial configuration the kernel is real analytic, and its linearization is a Balaban-type averaging operator $\mathcal M+d_0\mathcal F$ (line integrals across blocks plus a gauge correction) that maps pure gauge to pure gauge (III.1.14). This is the input for the background-field analysis of III.3–III.7.

The tree gauge (III.1.13) removes the gauge freedom inside blocks when integrating.

Differences from Bałaban:
- (i) Bałaban's averaging is built from parallel transports in a fixed axial-type gauge inside each block. Here the gauge fixing is nonlinear and intrinsic (least-squares synchronization and an extrinsic mean), randomized only where uniqueness may fail, and it acts on all configurations, including large fields.
- (ii) The coarse field is not constrained by a $\delta$-function or a Gaussian in the Lie algebra. It fluctuates with a Wilson-type weight $e^{-\beta\kappa\,\mathrm{pr}}$, and $\beta$ enters only there.
- (iii) The kernel is Borel but not continuous across branch boundaries. Analyticity holds only near the trivial configuration.
- (iv) No propagator or covariance operator appears in this unit.

## 5. Where the difficulty lies

- **Hard.**
  - Uniqueness of the least-squares synchronization with a cutoff independent of the patch size, $L$ and $J$ (4.11). The naive bound on the feedback map only gives a Lipschitz constant $1$. Contraction comes from normalizing by the centroid: the centroid difference has no tangential component, which brings the constant down to $d+\frac14\le\frac12$.
  - The quantitative synchronization on *every* branch (III.1.6), which requires the counting bound 4.15 and the curvature-controlled axial gauge.
  - The resulting $L^{-2}$ bound (III.1.8).
- **Routine but long.** Normalization, measurability and covariance (bijections of tags, uniqueness used to transport minimizers), the axial-gauge induction, the tree gauge, and the data formats.
- **Unusual.**
  - (a) The kernel is exact on the whole configuration space, including large fields. Randomization is used only where the deterministic rule may fail, and it is what makes the gauge fixing symmetric.
  - (b) The kernel is discontinuous across branch boundaries. Smoothness is available only near the trivial configuration (III.1.14), so the small-field analysis must stay on the minimizing branches.
  - (c) The branch constants depend on $G$ only, not on $\beta$, $L$ or $J$; $\beta$ enters only the noise.
  - (d) III.1.6 needs no smallness at all.
- **Stronger or weaker than expected.** III.1.11 and III.1.12 are requirements, not theorems of this unit. The first is proved in III.10. The second is supplied with the directed histories of III.2; inside the main-theorem closure it is not derived from a stationary-phase computation in this unit.
