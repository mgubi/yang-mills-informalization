## 1. What this unit proves

**Setting.** Fix a Wilson family, an extraction and the limit table $S=S^{\rm mix}$, together with a record [`Final`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Spec/Closed.lean#L64-L73) for them. The unit uses the following parts of it.
- The Euclidean axioms of $S^{\rm mix}$ (IV.1).
- The phase decomposition of V.1:
  - a compact metric space $X$ with a Borel probability measure $\nu$ of full support;
  - the commutative C$^*$-algebra $\mathfrak A$ generated by the time-slab operators of $S^{\rm mix}$, compressed to the ground space $\mathcal D$ of the Osterwalder–Schrader (OS) space $\mathcal H^{\rm OS}$ of $S^{\rm mix}$;
  - continuous characters $\chi_\xi$ of $\mathfrak A$, $\xi\in X$, with $\int_X\chi_\xi(M)\,d\nu=\langle\omega,M\omega\rangle$;
  - tables $S^\xi$ satisfying the Euclidean axioms, with $S^\xi(w)=\chi_\xi(M_w)$ for every word $w$ of pairwise distinct points. Here $M_w\in\mathfrak A$ is the compressed slab operator of a time translate of $w$.
- The relativistic theory $\mathfrak W_{\xi_*}$ of V.2 at the selected phase $\xi_*$, and the analytic continuations of $S^{\rm mix}$ and $S^{\xi_*}$.
- The field algebra of VI.1 (derivatives, rotations, the Euclidean stress tensor $T^{\rm E}_{ab}\in F_4$), and the Euclidean Ward identities in every phase.

**Notation.**
- **OS side.** For each $\xi$, $\mathcal H^{\rm OS}_\xi$ is an OS space of $S^\xi$ for the time axis. It carries the transfer semigroup $e^{-sH^{\rm OS}_\xi}$ and the Euclidean vacuum $\omega_\xi$. Its ground space is $\mathcal D_\xi=\{v:\ e^{-sH^{\rm OS}_\xi}v=v\ \text{for all } s\ge0\}$.
- **Wightman theory.** $\mathfrak W_\xi=(\mathcal H_\xi,\mathfrak D_\xi,\Omega_\xi,\phi_\xi,U_\xi,P_\xi)$ consists of:
  - a Hilbert space $\mathcal H_\xi$ and a common domain $\mathfrak D_\xi$ spanned by word vectors;
  - a vacuum $\Omega_\xi$;
  - fields $\phi_\xi(a,f)$ for labels $a\in\mathbb F$ (the union of the $F_d$) and $f\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R^4)$;
  - a unitary Poincaré representation $U_\xi$ and an energy–momentum spectral measure $P_\xi$.
- **Time and energy.** Time is the fourth coordinate. $H_\xi$ generates the time translations, and $P^0_\xi=P_\xi(\{p^4=0\})$.
- **Words.** A *finite sum of words* is $A=\sum_kc_k\,\phi(a_{k1},f_{k1})\cdots\phi(a_{kn_k},f_{kn_k})$. It is *compactly supported* if every $f_{kj}\in C^\infty_c(\mathbb R^4)$. $A^\dagger$ is its formal adjoint (star operation on labels, complex conjugation of tests, reversed order). The spectral measure of $A\Omega_\xi$ is $\mu^\xi_A(B)=\langle A\Omega_\xi,P_\xi(B)A\Omega_\xi\rangle$.
- **Continuations.** $\mathsf F^\xi_n(a;z,\vec x)$ is the analytic continuation of $S^\xi$ to complex times with $\mathrm{Im}z_1<\dots<\mathrm{Im}z_n$. $W^\xi_n(a;g)$, for $g\in\mathcal S((\mathbb R^4)^n)$, are its boundary distributions. $W^{\rm mix}_n$ are those of the continuation of $S^{\rm mix}$ (V.2).

**Proposition V.3.1 (reconstruction in every phase).** For every $\xi\in X$ there exist:
- an OS space $\mathcal H^{\rm OS}_\xi$ of $S^\xi$ with its time-slab operators;
- a continuation $\mathsf F^\xi$ of $S^\xi$ with boundary distributions $W^\xi$;
- a Wightman theory $\mathfrak W_\xi$ on the label space $\mathbb F$, with the same star operation and the same Poincaré action on labels as $\mathfrak W_{\xi_*}$;
- an isometry $J_\xi:\mathcal H_\xi\to\mathcal H^{\rm OS}_\xi$ with $J_\xi\Omega_\xi=\omega_\xi$. It intertwines the space–time translations and sends word vectors to the boundary values of the corresponding Euclidean vectors at imaginary times (Lemma V.3.b).

At $\xi=\xi_*$ these are the data of V.1 and V.2. For **every** $\xi\in X$, with no exceptional set, the following hold.
1. *Wightman axioms.*
   - The vacuum expectation values of products of fields are $W^\xi$.
   - The Hilbert-space adjoint of $\phi_\xi(a,f)$ extends $\phi_\xi(a^*,\bar f)$.
   - Fields with spacelike separated supports commute on $\mathfrak D_\xi$.
   - $U_\xi$ implements Poincaré covariance.
   - The joint spectrum of the translations lies in the closed forward cone $\overline V_+$.
   - Every nonzero label acts nontrivially.
   - Every translation-invariant vector is a multiple of $\Omega_\xi$.
2. *Surjectivity.* $J_\xi$ is unitary.
3. *Euclidean vacuum.* $\mathcal D_\xi=\mathbb C\omega_\xi$.
4. *Zero energy.* $P^0_\xi x=x$ if and only if $x\in\mathbb C\Omega_\xi$.
5. *Low-energy bound.* For every compactly supported finite sum of words $A$ there is $M\ge0$ such that $\mu^\xi_A(\{0<p^4\le u\})\le Mu^2$ for all $\xi\in X$ and $u\in(0,1]$.
6. *Heat decay.* For every such $A$ there is $M\ge0$ such that, for all $\xi\in X$ and $s\ge1$,
$$0\le\big\langle A\Omega_\xi,(e^{-sH_\xi}-P^0_\xi)A\Omega_\xi\big\rangle\le Ms^{-2}.$$
7. *Irreducibility.* Let $B$ be a bounded operator on $\mathcal H_\xi$. Suppose that for all $a$, $f$ and $u,v\in\mathfrak D_\xi$ we have $u\in\mathrm{dom}\,\phi_\xi(a,f)^*$ and $\langle\phi_\xi(a,f)^*u,Bv\rangle=\langle u,B\phi_\xi(a,f)v\rangle$. This holds if and only if $B\in\mathbb C\,1$.
8. *Stress tensor.* The conclusions (T0)–(T6) of Proposition V.3.y hold for $\mathfrak W_\xi$.
9. *Dependence on the phase.*
   - For all $n$, $a\in\mathbb F^n$ and $g\in\mathcal S((\mathbb R^4)^n)$, the map $\xi\mapsto W^\xi_n(a;g)$ is continuous and $\int_XW^\xi_n(a;g)\,d\nu(\xi)=W^{\rm mix}_n(a;g)$.
   - For every word $w$ of pairwise distinct points, $\int_XS^\xi(w)\,d\nu(\xi)=S^{\rm mix}(w)$.

In item 6, $e^{-sH_\xi}$ is defined as $J_\xi^*e^{-sH^{\rm OS}_\xi}J_\xi$. Since $J_\xi$ intertwines the spectral projections (V.3.b), this equals $\int e^{-sp^4}\,dP_\xi(p)$. The projection subtracted in item 6 is the full zero-energy projection. Items 5 and 6 are therefore proved before item 4 and independently of it.

## 3. Strategy

V.2 reconstructs a relativistic theory at one phase $\xi_*$. That phase was chosen among the phases whose OS ground space is one-dimensional, a set of full $\nu$-measure (V.1). The unit runs the reconstruction at every $\xi$ and proves the full Wightman axioms for each, including uniqueness of the vacuum, with no exceptional set. The argument has three layers.

*(a) Reconstruction without vacuum uniqueness, uniformly in $\xi$.* The OS construction of V.1–V.2 is applied to every $S^\xi$. It yields everything except uniqueness of the vacuum. Uniformity in $\xi$ comes from one operator inequality: each time-slab operator of $S^\xi$ has norm at most that of the same slab operator of $S^{\rm mix}$ (V.3.a).
- *Proof of V.3.a.* It uses the multiple-reflection (power) argument. The Gram entries of iterated reflected slabs in $S^\xi$ are character values of compressed operators of $S^{\rm mix}$. They are therefore bounded by $\|\chi_\xi\|$ times operator norms, and this fixed factor disappears under the $n$-th root.
- *Consequences.* The polynomial bounds on $\mathsf F^\xi$ near the boundary of the tube are uniform in $\xi$ (V.3.d). Continuity in $\xi$ is first proved at purely imaginary times, where $\mathsf F^\xi$ is a character value. Vitali's theorem carries it to complex times. A Taylor formula whose remainder has a $\xi$-independent dominator carries it to the boundary values $W^\xi$ (V.3.e–V.3.g). The same bounds give $\int W^\xi\,d\nu=W^{\rm mix}$.

*(b) From almost every phase to every phase.* By V.1, $\mathcal D_\xi=\mathbb C\omega_\xi$ for $\nu$-almost every $\xi$ (a variance identity, V.3.c). For a compactly supported finite sum of words $A$, set
$$r_\xi(s)=\langle A\Omega_\xi,e^{-sH_\xi}A\Omega_\xi\rangle-|\langle\Omega_\xi,A\Omega_\xi\rangle|^2 .$$
- $r_\xi(s)$ is continuous in $\xi$ (V.3.h, V.3.p).
- Where the ground space is one-dimensional, $r_\xi(s)=\langle A\Omega_\xi,(e^{-sH_\xi}-P^0_\xi)A\Omega_\xi\rangle$.
- Suppose this last quantity lies in $[0,\varepsilon(s)]$ for all $\xi$, with $\varepsilon(s)\to0$ independent of $\xi$. Then $\{\xi:\ 0\le r_\xi(s)\le\varepsilon(s)\}$ is closed and of full measure, hence equal to $X$ because $\nu$ has full support.
- Letting $s\to\infty$ gives $\langle A\Omega_\xi,P^0_\xi A\Omega_\xi\rangle=|\langle\Omega_\xi,A\Omega_\xi\rangle|^2$, that is $P^0_\xi A\Omega_\xi=\langle\Omega_\xi,A\Omega_\xi\rangle\Omega_\xi$, for every $\xi$.
- The matrix element $\langle A\Omega_\xi,(e^{-sH_\xi}-P^0_\xi)A\Omega_\xi\rangle$ is never shown to be continuous in $\xi$. Only $r_\xi(s)$ is.

The uniform rate $\varepsilon(s)=Ms^{-2}$ comes from a low-energy bound $\mu^\xi_A(\{0<p^4\le u\})\le Mu^2$ that uses only locality and the spectrum condition (V.3.k–V.3.n):
- the vacuum expectation of $[A^\dagger,A(\vec x,t)]$ vanishes for $|t|\le1$ and $|\vec x|>R$;
- its part odd in $t$, averaged over $t\in(0,1]$, is the spatial Fourier transform of a positive measure;
- that measure carries the weight $\int_0^1\sin(p^4t)\,dt\ge p^4/\pi$ at low energy;
- testing it against a momentum-space bump of radius $u$ gives (mass of the shell at energy $u$)$\cdot u\lesssim u^3$.

*(c) Consequences.*
- $J_\xi$ is onto, independently of vacuum uniqueness (V.3.i–V.3.j). The proof shows that Euclidean vectors at positive imaginary times are images of spectrally damped Wightman vectors.
- With V.3.2, surjectivity gives $\mathcal D_\xi=\mathbb C\omega_\xi$, hence the full Wightman axioms (V.3.s).
- Irreducibility follows from V.3.2 by a Liouville argument in the time variable (V.3.t).
- The Euclidean Ward identities of each phase are continued to the statement that the time-smeared stress charge generates the Poincaré transformations of local fields (V.3.u–V.3.y).

*What differs from the usual route.* In the usual treatment a reflection-positive state is decomposed into extremal states (Glimm–Jaffe, Ch. 19; Bratteli–Robinson, Ch. 4). Each extremal state has a unique vacuum, outside an exceptional null set that is not controlled. Here, V.1 decomposes over a compact space with continuous characters and a measure of full support. This unit removes the null set by continuity, with a uniform rate that comes from locality rather than from clustering estimates. No mass gap is used or proved.

### 4.1 Slab bounds and the family of reconstructions

**Lemma V.3.a (slab operators of a phase are dominated).** Fix $\xi\in X$. Let $\mathcal K$ be any OS space of $S^\xi$ for the time axis, and $\Pi_\xi$ any family of slab operators on it. Then for every thickness $d>0$ and every word $w$ in the slab $(0,d)$,
$$\|\Pi_\xi(d,w)\|\le\|\Pi(d,w)\|,$$
where $\Pi$ are the slab operators of $S^{\rm mix}$ on $\mathcal H^{\rm OS}$. Here $\Pi(d,w)$ maps the OS vector of a positive-time word $v$ to that of $w$ followed by $v$ translated by $d$.

*Sketch.*
1. Let $u=\Pi(d,w)$ and $D\ge\|u\|$. For a positive-time word $v$, let $X(v)$ be the slab operator of $v$ restricted to the ground space $\mathcal D$.
2. The compression of the doubled word $\theta v\,v$ is $X(v)^*X(v)\in\mathfrak A$. Let $r_n$ be $v$ with $n$ reflected pairs of copies of $w$ inserted. Then $X(r_n)=(u^*u)^nX(v)$.
3. Hence the Gram entry of $r_n$ in $S^\xi$ satisfies
$$|S^\xi(\theta r_n\,r_n)|=|\chi_\xi(X(r_n)^*X(r_n))|\le\|\chi_\xi\|\,\|X(v)\|^2D^{4n}.$$
4. The power argument (iterated Cauchy–Schwarz on the span of OS vectors) turns this into an operator on $\mathcal K$ of norm at most $D$ that inserts $w$. The fixed factor $\|\chi_\xi\|\,\|X(v)\|^2$ disappears in the limit.
5. Slab operators are determined by their action on OS vectors, so this operator is $\Pi_\xi(d,w)$. Finally let $D\downarrow\|u\|$.

### 4.3 Surjectivity of the boundary isometry

**Lemma V.3.i (Euclidean vectors at positive heights are in the range).** Fix heights $0<y_1<\dots<y_n$ and a margin $p\in(0,1)$ with $4p<y_1$ and $4p<y_{j+1}-y_j$.
- For a real configuration $q$, let $\Psi^\xi_p(a;q+iy)\in\mathcal H^{\rm OS}_\xi$ be the vector obtained from $\omega_\xi$ by alternating one-point slab operators of thickness $2p$ (labels $a_j$) with the complex-time evolutions and spatial translations given by $q+iy$.
- For $g\in\mathcal S((\mathbb R^4)^n)$ whose Fourier transform has compact support:
$$\int g(q)\,\Psi^\xi_p(a;q+iy)\,dq=J_\xi\,\Phi_\xi(a;g_y).$$
Here $\Phi_\xi(a;\cdot)$ is the continuous extension of word vectors to joint test functions, and $\hat g_y$ is $\hat g$ multiplied by an exponential damping factor in the energies, determined by $y$ (probable as to the precise form).
- Consequently every $\Psi^\xi_p(a;q+iy)$ lies in $\mathrm{ran}\,J_\xi$.

*Sketch.*
1. Call the two sides $h$ and $J_\xi\Phi$. Their norms and their inner product, $\|h\|^2$, $\langle h,J_\xi\Phi\rangle$ and $\|J_\xi\Phi\|^2$, are each expressed through partial boundary values of $W^\xi$ at heights $y$.
2. On tests of compact frequency support these partial boundary values equal $W^\xi$ tested against the damped test functions, so the three numbers coincide and $\|h-J_\xi\Phi\|=0$.
3. Density of compact-frequency tests and continuity in $g$ extend the membership in $\mathrm{ran}\,J_\xi$ to all Schwartz $g$.
4. Testing against all $g$, and using continuity in $q$, gives the pointwise statement. The range is closed since $J_\xi$ is an isometry.

### 4.4 Low-energy bound and heat decay

**Lemma V.3.k (time-averaged commutator).** Let $\rho,\sigma$ be finite positive measures on $\mathbb R^4$ supported in $\overline V_+$, with $\rho(\mathbb R^4)+\sigma(\mathbb R^4)\le M$. Put
$$\hat\rho(\vec x,t)=\int e^{i(\vec p\cdot\vec x+p^4t)}\,d\rho(p),\qquad a=\hat\rho(\cdot)-\hat\sigma(-\,\cdot),\qquad D(\vec x)=\int_0^1\frac{a(\vec x,t)-a(\vec x,-t)}{2i}\,dt .$$
Then $|D|\le M$. If moreover $D(\vec x)=0$ for $|\vec x|>R$, then for $0<u\le1$
$$\rho(\{u/2<p^4\le u\})\le c(R,M)\,u^2,\qquad c(R,M)=16\pi\,|B_1|\,|B_R|\,M/c_3 .$$

*Sketch.*
1. By Fubini, $D$ is the spatial Fourier transform of $\rho^\sharp+\sigma^\sharp$. Here $\rho^\sharp$ is the image under $p\mapsto\vec p$ of $\pi(p^4)\,d\rho(p)$, with $\pi(E)=\int_0^1\sin(Et)\,dt=(1-\cos E)/E$, and $\sigma^\sharp$ is the analogous image of $\sigma$ under $p\mapsto-\vec p$.
2. On $\overline V_+$ one has $\pi(p^4)\ge0$ at all energies, and $\pi(E)\ge E/\pi$ for $0\le E\le1$.
3. Test $D$ against $\check f$, where $f$ is a bump in spatial momentum equal to $1$ on the ball of radius $u$ and supported in the ball of radius $2u$. Then $|\check f|\le|B_{2u}|=8|B_1|u^3$. Since $D$ vanishes outside $B_R$, the pairing is at most $8|B_1||B_R|M\,u^3$.
4. The pairing equals $c_3\big(\int f\,d\rho^\sharp+\int f\,d\sigma^\sharp\big)$, which is at least $c_3\,\tfrac{u}{2\pi}\,\rho(\{u/2<p^4\le u\})$. On that shell $|\vec p|\le p^4\le u$, so $f=1$ there, and $\pi(p^4)\ge u/(2\pi)$.

### 4.6 Irreducibility

**Proposition V.3.t (trivial weak commutant).** Item 7 of V.3.1.

*Sketch.* The weak commutant is first rewritten with $\phi_\xi(a^*,\bar f)$ in place of the Hilbert adjoint, since the former is the restriction of the latter to $\mathfrak D_\xi$. The converse direction is the adjoint relation. Now let $B$ be weakly central and write $\Omega=\Omega_\xi$.
1. For word vectors $u=\phi(w)\Omega$ and $v=\phi(w^\dagger)\Omega$, and $\mathrm{Im}z\ge0$, set
$$p(z)=\langle J_\xi u,e^{izH^{\rm OS}_\xi}J_\xi B\Omega\rangle,\qquad q(z)=\langle J_\xi B^*\Omega,e^{izH^{\rm OS}_\xi}J_\xi v\rangle .$$
Both are bounded, continuous, and holomorphic for $\mathrm{Im}z>0$. On the real axis $p(t)=\langle u,U_\xi(t)B\Omega\rangle$, and at $z=is$ one has $p(is)=\langle u,e^{-sH_\xi}B\Omega\rangle$.
2. Covariance and weak centrality give $p(t)=q(-t)$ for real $t$.
3. Gluing $p$ on the upper half-plane to $q(-\,\cdot)$ on the lower one gives a bounded continuous function, holomorphic off $\mathbb R$. It is entire by Morera's theorem and constant by Liouville's theorem.
4. Hence $\langle u,e^{-sH_\xi}B\Omega\rangle=\langle u,B\Omega\rangle$ for all $s\ge0$. Letting $s\to\infty$ and using V.3.2 gives $B\Omega=a\Omega$ with $a=\langle\Omega,B\Omega\rangle$.

## 5. Where the difficulty lies

*Mathematically new or delicate.*
1. **Removing the exceptional set (V.3.q–V.3.r).** The argument is short once two facts are known: $r_\xi(s)$ is continuous, and $\langle A\Omega_\xi,(e^{-sH_\xi}-P^0_\xi)A\Omega_\xi\rangle\to0$ uniformly in $\xi$. Most of §4.1–4.4 supplies these two facts. Every uniformity in $\xi$ traces back to the single inequality V.3.a.
2. **The low-energy bound (V.3.k–V.3.n).**
   - It uses only locality and the spectrum condition. The constant depends on $A$ only through its support radius and $\sup_\xi\|A\Omega_\xi\|^2$, $\sup_\xi\|A^\dagger\Omega_\xi\|^2$.
   - Its positivity rests on $(1-\cos E)/E\ge0$ at all energies; this avoids differentiating spectral integrals and needs no domain bounds on generators.
   - The exponent $2$ is attained by the free massless scalar field, since $\int_{|\vec p|\le u}d^3p/|\vec p|\sim u^2$. The bound is therefore compatible with massless theories and says nothing about a mass gap.
   - Only its uniformity in $\xi$ is used.
3. **Surjectivity of $J_\xi$ (V.3.i–V.3.j).**
   - $\mathfrak W_\xi$ is built from $W^\xi$, not from the OS space. Identifying the two requires Euclidean vectors at positive imaginary times to be images of spectrally damped Wightman vectors.
   - Working with test functions of compact frequency support avoids any density statement about analytic vectors.
4. **The stress tensor (§4.7).** This is half of the folder. It passes from Euclidean contact identities to the equal-time charge commutator of the Minkowski theory, through partial boundary values of the continuation.

*Routine but long.*
- Continuity in $\xi$ and the barycenter formula (§4.2): dominated convergence with explicit dominators.
- The dyadic and Laplace-transform estimates (V.3.l, V.3.o).
- The assembly (§4.8).

*What looks unusual.*
- Items 2–8 of V.3.1 are conclusions only. They appear in clause (D7), and no other unit uses them; in particular the stress-tensor statement is not used downstream.
- Item 8 quantifies over all admissible time profiles and spatial cutoffs. Their existence is proved ([`Stress.accepts`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/EveryPhase/Charge.lean#L24-L35), [`Stress.reservable`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/EveryPhase/Charge.lean#L42-L59)) but is not part of the conclusion and not used by the main theorem. The same holds for Remark V.3.3.
- The construction is forced to coincide with V.2 at $\xi_*$. The certificate's uniqueness of the vacuum at $\xi_*$ is not needed in this unit.
- The Euclidean half of item 9 restates V.1, and the public clause (R12) is derived from it.
- Nothing here or in the main theorem decides whether $X$ is a single point. Each $\mathfrak W_\xi$ is irreducible, while $S^{\rm mix}$ is the $\nu$-mixture of the $S^\xi$.
- The record bundles eleven statements that a paper would split into three theorems: reconstruction, vacuum and decay, stress tensor.
