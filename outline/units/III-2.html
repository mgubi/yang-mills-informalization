1. What this unit proves

Proposition III.2.1 (order of choice). For simple \(G\), choose:

  1. A sheet: kernel \(K\) with branches on every patch; first stage \(j_0 \geqslant 4\); floors \(f_j \in \mathbb{N}\); stage records with a clip of relaxation \(r_j\), derivative orders \(k_j \geqslant 6\), spatial powers \(q_j \geqslant 2\), support exponents \(a_j > 0\), widths \(w_j > 0\), path counts \(\varpi_j > 0\), drift allowances \(\omega_j \geqslant 0\), column bounds \(\gamma_j \geqslant 1\), \(r_j w_j \geqslant 12\); nondecreasing \(k, q, a\).

  2. Nondecreasing odd \(\ell_j \geqslant \max (3, f_j)\), with, for \(j \geqslant j_0\),

\(\displaystyle \omega_j / \log \ell_j \leqslant \min (\kappa / 4, j^{- 2}), \qquad \log \mathcal{C}_j / \log \ell_j \leqslant j^{- 2}, \qquad \mathcal{C}_j = 1 + \varpi_j + w_j^{- 1} + \gamma_j + r_j + a_j .\)

  1. Positive nonincreasing \(\theta_j \leqslant 1 / j\) for \(j \geqslant j_0\).

  2. Unrestricted delays: real entry couplings \(\beta^{\text{ent}}_j\), real barrier-dependent entry scales \(\sigma^{\text{ent}}_j (\bar{\beta})\), natural shells \(S_j\) and exponents \(g_j\).

  3. A barrier \(\bar{\beta} > 1\), with \(\bar{\beta} \geqslant \beta^{\text{ent}}_{j_0}\), with \(x^{- 1 / 2} e^{(\log \log x)^2} \leqslant 1\) for every \(x \geqslant \bar{\beta}\).

  4. A wait: real readiness scales \(\sigma^{\text{rdy}}_j\), windows \(h_j : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{N}\) with \(h_j (s) / s \to 0\) as \(s \to \infty\) for each fixed \(j\), and natural \(n_{\min}\).

Delays and waits are ordered componentwise. Factors exist for every sheet; \(\theta\) can lie below any prescribed positive sequence; barriers exist above any prescribed real. Proposition III.2.2 (schedule). For simple \(G\) and any III.2.1 data, there are \(J_m\), \(L_m = \ell_{J_{m + 1}}\), \(n_p = 4 (M + p + 1)\) with natural \(M\), retaining the prescribed data, such that:

  • For every \(m\), \(\beta^{\text{ent}}_{J_{m + 1}} \leqslant \hat{\beta}_m\); also \(\sigma^{\text{ent}}_{J_{m + 1}} (\bar{\beta}) \leqslant s_m\) if \(J_{m + 1} > j_0\).

Assume, for every \(N, p\), a continuous admissible Wilson start family on \(\mathfrak{B}_N\) with coupling equal to its parameter, \(v = 0\), density equal to the Wilson weight at that coupling, passing the start test. Assume also that every continuous admissible level-\((m + 1)\) family on any \([l, u]\) with \(l \leqslant u\), \(m < N\), with couplings in \(\mathfrak{B}_{m + 1}\), admits a continuous admissible level-\(m\) family whose density is its block-spin push-forward at the input coupling, passing the transition test, with \(| \beta' - \beta - \kappa \log L_m | \leqslant \omega_{J_{m + 1}}\).

3. Strategy

Choose stage data before factors, then costs, protection, delays, barrier and wait. Maxima combine floors; persistent increases combine delays and waits. Long plateaux provide comparison windows; exponential side growth pays volume demands. Shooting narrows continuous Wilson families into successive coupling bands until the level-zero coupling equals \(\bar{\beta}\). The shooting windows are \([\bar{\beta} + (\kappa / 2) s_m, \bar{\beta} + 2 \kappa s_m] \subset \mathfrak{B}_m\).

4. Steps

  1. Exhaustion. Long plateaux meet entry and window thresholds; one period offset pays the finite first plateau. Program.exhaust, Wait.ready.

  2. Narrowing. Endpoint drift inequalities straddle the next window; first and last hitting points give a subinterval mapping entirely into it. Iterate downward. gates, narrow, steps.

  3. Running law. The coupling error is bounded by \(\sum_{k = 1}^m \omega_{J_k} = o (s_m)\) using \(J_k \to \infty\). trajectory.

  4. Diagonal payment. Raise entry couplings above double exponentials of finite partial sums of array entries. Since \(J_m \to \infty\), every fixed row is eventually included. eventuallyPaid.

  5. Composition. Combine sheet and native suppliers, joint and trace purchases and persistent payments by maxima of floors and transitivity of delay and wait bounds. Choose the schedule only after these increases, then assemble the run. sailsW, plentifulWithPrediction, engageWithCustodyFactor.

  6. Analytic gains. Move differences from vanishing kernels onto crosses: their two bounds give \(L^{- 2}\), fine-site summation gives \(L^4\), and \(d\) differences give \(L^{- d}\). Reflection cancels odd terms; local projection leaves exponentially small wrapping contributions. pullback_gain, mirror_price, unique_part.

5. Where the difficulty lies

Factor-dependent costs require later persistent thresholds. Shooting needs full-family continuity and band membership throughout each interval. The \(\kappa / 4\) drift bound separates endpoints; \(j^{- 2}\) controls accumulated error. Each row is paid eventually; the zeroth post-\(L\) row needs separate all-level payment.

6. Notation

\(m = 0\) is coarsest, \(N\) finest; \(p\) indexes volume, \(j\) stages. \(s_m = \sum_{i < m} \log L_i\), \(\kappa = 11 C_G / (24 \pi^2)\), \(\hat{\beta}_m = \bar{\beta} + \kappa s_m\), \(\mathfrak{B}_m = [\hat{\beta}_m / 2, 2 \hat{\beta}_m]\), \(M_m = e^{(\log \log \hat{\beta}_m)^2}\), \(t_m = \hat{\beta}_m^{- 1 / 2} M_m\), \(B_m = \lceil (\log \hat{\beta}_m)^2 \rceil\), \(R_m = (J_{m + 1} + 3) L_m\). In read-out, \(n, B, a_{\ast}\) are side, cover size, support exponent; \(K_{F, H}\) depends on the frame and holonomy tests. \(\text{span}_{ij}\) is transition support; a prediction is a predicate on the factor recording all choices. Stage records, protection and admitted entrance conditions are Stage, Protection and Paid.
