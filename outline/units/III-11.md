## 1. What this unit proves

The unit has two outputs. The first is the **rank comparison**. In the continuum limit, the space $F_d$ of local fields of degree $d$ is isomorphic to the corresponding space of the free Maxwell field, compatibly with complex conjugation and with the lattice symmetry group. Here $F_d$ is the space of labels of degree $d$ modulo labels whose correlations vanish. The second output is the **stress tensor of the limit**: a nonzero conserved stress tensor that satisfies the translation and rotation Ward identities and is normalized against the free Maxwell stress tensor along a scaling sequence. Both outputs rest on quantitative estimates proved here: a per-site determinant budget, chessboard bounds for connected parts of corrected observables, and comparisons of covariances with the free light-cone kernel. In the code's vocabulary, "price", "work item", "budget", "pay" and "purchase" denote numerical bounds and the inequalities that allocate them.

Notation. $G$ is a compact simple matrix group; $\dim G$ is the dimension of its Lie algebra; $h$ is normalized Haar measure; $\mathrm{chord}(u)\ge0$ is the chordal distance from $u$ to $1$; and $\mathrm{shell}(r)$ is the set of $u$ with $\mathrm{chord}(u)\le r$ (probable). The torus of side $n\ge2$ is $\Lambda_n=(\mathbb Z/n)^4$, with $c=n^4$ sites, $4c$ bonds and $6c$ plaquettes. The prior $\mu_{\rm prior}$ is product Haar measure on bonds. An RG representation $R$ on $\Lambda_n$ has an inverse coupling $\beta=\beta(R)$, an exponent $\mathrm{exponent}_R$ with constant part $\mathrm{scalar}(R)$, and an unnormalized density $e^{R}=e^{\mathrm{exponent}_R}\cdot(\text{polymer gas})$.

**Proposition III.11.1 (determinant budget on the torus, and its per-site price).**
(a) Let $R$ be a lawful representation on $\Lambda_n$, $n\ge2$, with lawful-class parameters $(\sigma,A,\Lambda,\delta,w)$, and assume $e^{R}\ge0$ pointwise. Let $0<s\le1\le b$ be a Haar budget (Lemma 4.1). Let $g>0$ with $g\le\mathrm{threshold}(R)$ and $\beta g^2=1$. Let $F$ be a $\mu_{\rm prior}$-integrable complex function and $z\ge0$, $X\in\mathbb R$ such that $z|F(U)|\le\exp(\mathrm{exponent}_R(U)+cX)$ for every configuration $U$. Then
$$\frac{z\,\big|\int F\,d\mu_{\rm prior}\big|}{\int e^{R}\,d\mu_{\rm prior}}\;\le\;\exp\Big(c\,\pi(R,X,g)-4\dim G\,\log g\Big),$$
where the per-site price is
$$\pi=2\,\mathrm{exCost}(A,\Lambda,\delta)+6+\beta\,\mathrm{baseBudget}(A,g)+X+3\,\mathrm{ent}-4\log s,\qquad \mathrm{ent}=\log b-\dim G\,\log\sigma_R,$$
with $\sigma_R=\min(1,\text{linear coefficient of the clipped plaquette action})$. Here $\mathrm{exCost}$ is the per-site bound for the non-principal part of the exponent (loops, polymer activities), and $\beta\,\mathrm{baseBudget}$ is the per-site bound for the principal plaquette action on configurations whose plaquettes are all within chordal distance $g$ of $1$.
(b) Let $(\mathcal A_m)$ be the levels of an RG schedule for simple $G$, and let $O=(s,b)$ be a Haar budget. For all sufficiently large levels $m$, every period $p$, every representation $P$ on the level-$m$ torus (with $c_m$ sites), every $X\le1+k$, and every $(g,\Lambda,\delta)$ admissible at level $m$, the following holds:
$$\pi(P,X,g)-\frac{4\dim G\,\log g}{c_m}\;\le\;\mathrm{work item}(O,m,k).$$
Admissible at level $m$ means: same clip as the stage of $m$, $\beta(P)\le4\hat\beta_m$, $\beta g^2=1$, $0<g\le\mathrm{threshold}(P)\le\mathrm{threshold}_m$, $\delta\le1$, $\Lambda\le\mathrm{loopCap}_m$. The work item is
$$\mathrm{work item}=2(8\,\mathrm{paths}_m+2)+6+3(\mathrm{direct}_m+\mathrm{paths}_m^2)+(1+k)+3\,\mathrm{ent}-4\log s+1.$$
It depends only on the stage parameters, on $O$ and on $k$; it does not depend on $\beta$, the volume or the period.

## 3. Strategy

The aim is to show that, at the level of field content, asymptotic freedom holds in the continuum limit. In each degree $d$, the local fields of the interacting limit are those of the free Maxwell field: labels modulo the Maxwell equation ideal. The comparison is a dimension count closed by an equivariant identification.

*Upper bound (equation count).* At each cutoff, the projector presentation kills exactly the corrected images of $\mathcal E_d$. The free probes separate the on-shell sector (no residual), so the free null space is exactly $\mathcal E_d$. Hence $\dim\mathcal L_d\le\dim\ker\Pi+\dim F_d^{\rm free}$, with equality.

*Lower bound (short-distance comparison).* Along a trial sequence of levels $k_i$ with test scales $s_i\to0$, the corrected observables have nearly free Gaussian statistics: every connected part relative to the free one- and two-point profile has chessboard root $\le\gamma_i\to0$. Their limit correlations therefore converge to the free Maxwell correlations, which separate $F_d^{\rm free}$. So the corrected vectors become linearly independent modulo the null space of the limit.

The two bounds give $\mathrm{null}_d(T)=\ker\Pi_\infty$ and $\dim F_d(T)=\dim F_d^{\rm free}$. Conjugating the limit projector by an invertible operator close to $1$ turns this into an isomorphism that intertwines conjugation and the lattice symmetries.

The quantitative engine is the chessboard estimate. The product of reflected copies of a connected part, integrated against the Wilson measure at cutoff $N$, is carried by the exact RG history to a coarse level $m$. There the number of cells equals the number of sites $c_m$. The flowed observable is bounded pointwise by $r^{-|s|q}\exp(\mathrm{exponent}+qX)$, and Proposition III.11.1 bounds the resulting ratio of integrals by $e^{c_m\pi}g^{-4\dim G}$.

Despite its name, III.11.1 is not a Hessian determinant. It is a gauge count:
- **Numerator.** Each plaquette weight is at most $e^{1-\mathrm{chord}/(g/\sigma)}$. A triangular choice of $3c-3$ plaquettes, each owning a bond, integrates exactly to $(b(g/\sigma)^{\dim G})^{3c-3}$.
- **Vacuum.** Tree gauge leaves $3c+1$ free bonds. Restricting each to a Haar shell of radius $g/4$ gives the lower bound $(s g^{\dim G})^{3c+1}$.

The powers of $g$ cancel up to $g^{-4\dim G}=\beta^{2\dim G}$, which does not depend on the volume. After the $c_m$-th root this costs at most $1$, because $2\dim G\log\beta\le8\dim G\,\hat\beta_m\le a_m^{-1}\le c_m$ eventually. So the root test costs a per-site work item depending only on the stage parameters, with no $\log\beta$ per site. The constants are then chosen so that the allowance is $e^{2I_k}$ and the budget is $e^{-I_k}$, where $I_k\ge\mathrm{work item}$; the root of every nonempty connected part is then $\le e^{I_k}/e^{2I_k}=e^{-I_k}\to0$.

All conditions are needed only at **infinitely many** good levels $k$. A diagonal choice of depths then produces the trial sequence (III.11.4). The free-covariance comparisons (III.11.2, III.11.3) and the one-step covariance splitting enter through the price conditions "hours" and "vents" and the window conditions checked in `cast_off`.

The stress tensor (III.11.6, III.11.7) is built by comparing the limit with a free Maxwell model along a scaling sequence. The Ward identities follow from a flux argument on shrinking annuli of radii $r_l\to0$:
- by conservation the flux does not depend on the window;
- after scaling, the flux is an integral over the annulus $r_l/4<|y-x_0|<2r_l$;
- an operator-product-type bound on annuli (`zone`) shows that combinations of fluxes annihilating all degree-$D$ testers tend to $0$;
- finite-dimensional duality and finite faithfulness of the testers then give one local field $B$ representing the flux.

## 5. Where the difficulty lies

- **The volume-uniform root test.** A per-site loss of $\log\beta$ in the chessboard root would destroy the comparison, because the budget must tend to $0$ while $\beta\to\infty$. The tree-gauge count of III.11.1 avoids this: it costs only $\beta^{2\dim G}$ in total, not per site. The price for this is a crude, but uniform, per-site constant. A Gaussian determinant of the Hessian is never computed.
- **The direction of the rank argument.** The upper bound (equation count) is algebraic and holds at every cutoff. The lower bound needs the short-distance comparison, at infinitely many scales only, through trial sequences. The equivariant isomorphism comes from conjugating projectors, not from an explicit basis. "Maxwell ranks" is therefore stronger than equality of dimensions: it is an isomorphism of real representations of the lattice symmetry group.
- **Frequent rather than eventual scales.** Every price condition is required only at infinitely many levels; a diagonal extraction then selects the trials. This is weaker than an "eventually in $k$" statement, and it is all the rank argument needs.
- **Routine in principle, long in practice.** Most of the 42k lines are eventual inequalities in $\hat\beta$ and $\log\hat\beta$, marker and cell bookkeeping, and transport along symmetries. A written proof should state once: the determinant budget, the root-test bound with allowance $e^{2I}$ and budget $e^{-I}$, the free-covariance comparisons, and the order of choice of constants (III.2).
- **Placement.** The stress-flux block (443 lines) belongs with VI.1 in the written text. The algebra of the Maxwell equation ideal (193 lines) belongs with II.1. The Lean chapter places both here.
