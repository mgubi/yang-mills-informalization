## 1. What this unit proves

The unit is the "rank comparison" of the Lean. Its purpose is to compare, inside the RG step, the actual interacting measure with the free Gaussian (Maxwell) one. It gives estimates for the *observables* (curvature jets with their lower-order corrections) that feed the extraction of limit kernels and the free-field exclusion later on. It has no short list of clean theorems. Most of it is a ladder of "price" estimates (bounds with explicit constants) whose final outputs are a handful of hypotheses-to-conclusions of the following kinds. In the glossary's vocabulary, "price", "pay" and "purchase" mean numerical error budgets and the inequalities allocating them; they are not economic.

**Proposition III.11.1 (determinant budget on the torus).** Let $G$ be a compact gauge group, $L>1$ the torus side, $c=|\text{sites}|=L^4$, and $R$ a lawful RG representation at inverse coupling $\beta=g^{-2}$ with $g\le$ threshold, whose density $e^{R}$ is non-negative. Let $F$ be integrable on configurations with $z\|F\|\le \exp(\text{exponent}(R)+cX)$ for all configurations. Then, for a Haar budget $O$ (constants $0<s\le1\le b$),
$$\frac{z\,\big\|\int F\,d\mu_{\rm prior}\big\|}{\int e^{R}\,d\mu_{\rm prior}}\;\le\;\exp\Big(c\cdot\text{protection}(O,R,X,g)\;-\;4\dim G\,\log g\Big),$$
where the *protection* (per-site price) is $2\,\mathrm{exCost}+\sharp \text{plaquette axes}+\beta\cdot\text{baseBudget}+X+3\,\mathrm{ent}-4\log s$ and $\mathrm{ent}=\log b-\dim G\log(\text{strength})$.
*Status:* theorem. No hypothesis is carried beyond those stated; $R$ lawful and $e^{R}\ge0$ are hypotheses of the statement.

## 3. Strategy

The unit compares the interacting measure at level $k$ of the history with the Gaussian measure of the free Maxwell field, in two ways. The first way is a *determinant budget*. A numerator (the integral of an observable against the unnormalized density $e^{R}$) is bounded from above by integrating out one gauge-fixed tree and bounding each plaquette factor by a Haar integral. The vacuum (denominator) is bounded from below by restricting to the product of small balls around the tree-gauge configuration where the density is at least $e^{\text{scalar}-c(\dots)}$. The ratio is then $\exp(c\cdot\text{protection}-4\dim G\log g)$: a cost *per site* independent of the volume, plus a constant. This is what lets chessboard "roots" ($\sharp \text{cells}$-th roots of expectations of products of reflected copies) be taken without losing a factor $\log\beta$ per site. The tree gauge count $3c-3$ free bonds replaces a determinant estimate of the actual quadratic form (the docstring of `Axial` says explicitly that this is a gauge count, not an infinite-volume or vanishing-harmonics determinant estimate).

The second way is *comparison of the correlation structure with the free one*. The kernels of the interacting observables, written as polynomials in normalized curvature jets with remainder, are matched in leading order to the free Maxwell light-cone kernel. Their difference is controlled by $D$, the size of the free covariance (`fence`). Wick/pairing expansions (`headed`), Taylor polynomials in link variables (`minted`, `till`), and marker algebra ("packing" of Taylor monomials with marks) express the interacting expectation as a sum of free-type terms plus polynomially bounded remainders. The prices of all remainders are paid in the order of the choice of constants (pre-$L$ costs before the block size, post-$L$ after) so that the conditions become inequalities that hold "eventually in $k$".

From these estimates three records are produced: the Maxwell rank matrix of the limit table (`rated`), a nonzero stable conserved current (`readyA`), and the representation of stress flux by a limit field (`annular_fieldA`). The first is the input of the free-field exclusion (VII.3), the other two of the Ward identities and trace law (VI.1, VII.2). This is the "corrected field estimates" interface U6 of boundary-UV.

### 4.1 Haar and plaquette estimates *(proof read for 4.1–4.3)*

**Lemma 4.1 (Haar budget).** There are $0<s\le 1\le b$ such that for $0<r\le1$, $\mathrm{Haar}(\text{shell}(r/4))\ge s\,r^{\dim G}$ and for $t>0$, $\int e^{-\mathrm{chord}(u)/t}\,dh(u)\le b\,t^{\dim G}$.
*Sketch:* the group is a compact manifold of dimension $\dim G$; small balls have volume of order $r^{\dim}$, and the exponential tail integral is bounded by the same scaling.
*Lean:* [`HaarBudget`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Next/RANK/Determinant.lean#L26-L33), [`integral_budget`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Next/RANK/Haar.lean#L170-L211). *(statement only for `integral_budget`.)*

**Lemma 4.2 (elimination: tree gauge on the torus).** On the torus of side $L>1$, let $F:G\to[0,1]$ be continuous. Then
$$\int \prod_{p}F(U_p)\,d\mu_{\rm prior}(U)\;\le\;\Big(\int F\,dh\Big)^{3|{\rm sites}|-3}.$$
*Sketch:* fix a spanning tree and gauge it to the identity (this is exact for the Haar prior). The remaining free bonds are $3|\text{sites}|+1$ in number. Order them (wrap bonds first, then transverse bonds from higher to lower height) so that each plaquette in a chosen subset of $3|\text{sites}|-3$ plaquettes contains exactly one new free bond. Integrating the free bonds one by one, each Haar integral bounds the corresponding factor by $\int F$; the remaining plaquette factors are bounded by $1$.
*Lean:* [`elimination`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Next/RANK/Axial.lean#L368-L383), with the triangularity of the sweep in the file `Axial.lean` *(statement and docstring read; the sweep proof not read)*.

**Lemma 4.3 (vacuum lower bound and numerator).** Under the hypotheses of III.11.1 (with $g\le$ threshold, $\beta g^2=1$): the unnormalized vacuum integral is at least $\exp\big(\text{scalar}-c(\mathrm{exCost}+\beta\,\mathrm{baseBudget})\big)\,(s g^{\dim G})^{3c+1}$; and the numerator satisfies $z\|\int F\|\le \exp(\text{scalar}+c(\mathrm{exCost}+\sharp \text{axes}+X))\,(b(g/\mathrm{strength})^{\dim G})^{3c-3}$.

## 5. Where the difficulty lies

- **Routine in principle, long in practice.** Once the right quantities are named, most of the 42k lines are bookkeeping: eventual inequalities for polynomials of $\log\beta$ and powers of $\beta$, indexing of markers and cells, and transport along isometries and signed permutations. A written proof should state the mechanism once (determinant budget, free-kernel comparison, Wick/Taylor expansion, eventual affordability) and cite the order of choice of constants (III.2).
- **The determinant budget is a gauge count, not a determinant.** Despite the name, III.11.1 does not estimate $\det$ of a Hessian. It counts $3|\text{sites}|-3$ plaquettes that can be integrated out after fixing a tree, and uses the Haar volume of small balls. This is a weaker (volume-extensive but cheap) input than a Gaussian determinant; the loss per site in the protection is a constant (no $\log\beta$ per site, which is what the docstring of `Axial.lean` insists on). It is natural for chessboard estimates.
- **Stronger than expected.** The comparison of covariance with the light-cone kernel (III.11.2) is uniform in $r\ge1$, in the points, and in the regulator family, with constant depending only on the labels and a bound $D$ on the free covariance.
- **A mixture of stages.** The "stage" analysis puts the stress-flux and conserved-current statements (443 lines) in the Ward/rotation part. They are placed here by the Lean chapter structure. The label algebra `Push.Mark` (193 lines) belongs with the field labels (II.1) or with the Maxwell rank statement.
- **Many hypotheses are named but not discharged here.** `Estimate`, `Tender`, `Metering`, `ShowHours`, `Vents`, `Scrim`, the empire hypothesis, and the `Aroma` budget are inputs of III.11.4–III.11.7. The docstring of `LedgerGate.lean` states that the one-step estimates "are physics inputs, NOT consequences of Shape/Lawful/etc.". If they are not discharged by the chain III.10/III.12/III.14, the rank statements are conditional.
- **Unusual features.** Several docstrings state that something is *not* proved (e.g. `Odometer`: "not saying that the producers above can in fact work with such a plan"; `Cargo`: "No existential conjecture asserted about earning the estimates"). The results are therefore frequently stated as implications from a payment plan to the next estimate.

## 7. Open points for the writer

1. **What is the "rank"?** A one-line definition is not stated. To settle whether equality of ranks is proved, or only an inequality obtained from the interacting side combined with the free one, check `Maxwell.Ranks`, `Extra.FD.no_residual` and `Mark.thrush`.
