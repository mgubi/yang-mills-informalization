## 1. What this unit proves

The unit fixes the setting of the construction. It covers:
- the periodic lattices and their symmetry group;
- parallel transport and gauge transformations;
- the subdivision of a torus into blocks and the schedule of lattice spacings;
- a combinatorial measure of the size of a lattice set;
- the gauge group in its defining representation, with its Lie algebra, Casimir constant, local logarithm and nearest-point retraction;
- the Wilson measure and its invariance;
- the continuum symmetry groups of $\mathbb R^4$.

It contains no renormalization-group estimate and imports no hypothesis. Every output is a theorem or a definition. The only hypothesis appearing in a statement is the simplicity of $\mathfrak g$, in Proposition I.1.6(d)–(f).

*Conventions.* $G$ is a closed subgroup of $U(N)$, $N\ge1$ (in the code $N=n+1$; the statement of the main theorem writes $m$). $\Lambda_\ell=(\mathbb Z/\ell)^4$ is the torus of side $\ell\ge1$. Axes $0,1,2$ are spatial and axis $3$ is time; $e_i$ are the unit vectors. A link $(x,i)$ joins $x$ to $x+e_i$. A configuration is a map $U:\Lambda_\ell\times\{0,1,2,3\}\to G$, and the same definitions are made on the cover $\mathbb Z^4$. $\|\cdot\|_F$ is the Frobenius norm on $M_N(\mathbb C)$.

**Proposition I.1.1 (torus geometry).** For $y\in\mathbb Z^4$ let $[y]_\ell\in\Lambda_\ell$ be its reduction mod $\ell$. For $x\in\Lambda_\ell$ let $r_\ell(x)\in\mathbb Z^4$ be the representative with coordinates in $(-\ell/2,\ell/2]$ (the centred lift; the tie $x_i=\ell/2$ lifts to $+\ell/2$). Then:
- $[r_\ell(x)]_\ell=x$, and $r_\ell(x)$ is the unique $y$ with $[y]_\ell=x$ and $-\ell<2y_i\le\ell$ for all $i$;
- reduction is injective on this centred box, and $|\Lambda_\ell|=\ell^4$.

Put $|x|_\ell=\max_i|r_\ell(x)_i|$. The separation $|x-y|_\ell$ is the sup-metric of the torus:
- it is symmetric and vanishes on the diagonal;
- it satisfies the triangle inequality;
- it is invariant under translations and under the hyperoctahedral group $B_4$ of Proposition I.1.2;
- reduction does not increase distances: $|[y]_\ell|_\ell\le\max_i|y_i|$.

**Proposition I.1.2 (lattice symmetries, transport, gauge transformations).** Let $B_4$ be the group of signed permutations of the four axes (order $2^4\cdot4!=384$). An element $\rho=(\pi,\epsilon)$ acts on $\mathbb Z^4$ and on $\Lambda_\ell$ by $(\rho x)_{\pi(i)}=\epsilon_ix_i$. A lattice affine map is $A=(\rho,b)$, $Ax=\rho x+b$.

A walk $w$ is a finite word of steps $\pm e_i$. The transport $U(x;w)\in G$ is the ordered product along $w$ from $x$: a forward step along the link $(y,i)$ contributes $U(y,i)$, and a backward step from $y$ contributes $U(y-e_i,i)^{-1}$.

The pullback $A^*U$ is
$$(A^*U)(x,i)=\begin{cases}U(Ax,\pi(i)),&\epsilon_i=+1,\\ U(Ax-e_{\pi(i)},\pi(i))^{-1},&\epsilon_i=-1,\end{cases}$$
that is, the transport of $U$ along the image of the link $(x,i)$. Then:
- (a) $1^*=\mathrm{id}$ and $B^*A^*=(A\circ B)^*$, i.e. $(\rho',b')^*(\rho,b)^*=(\rho\rho',\rho b'+b)^*$. For $A=(1,b)$ the pullback is the translation $(\tau_bU)(x,i)=U(x+b,i)$.
- (b) $(A^*U)(x;w)=U(Ax;\rho w)$, where $\rho w$ is the image word.
- (c) For $g:\Lambda\to G$ put $U^g(x,i)=g(x)U(x,i)g(x+e_i)^{-1}$. Then $U^g(x;w)=g(x)\,U(x;w)\,g(x_{\rm end})^{-1}$; for a closed walk this is conjugation by $g(x)$.
- (d) (locality) If $U$ and $V$ agree on every link whose two endpoints are visited by $w$ from $x$, then $U(x;w)=V(x;w)$.
- (e) (plaquettes) A plaquette is $p=(x;i<j)$ with holonomy $U_p=U(x;e_i,e_j,-e_i,-e_j)$. Each $A$ induces an injective map $A_\#$ of plaquettes, bijective on $\Lambda_\ell$. It sends the axis pair $\{i,j\}$ to $\{\pi(i),\pi(j)\}$ in increasing order, and the base point to the lowest corner of the image square. For every $\phi:G\to M$ with $\phi(u^{-1})=\phi(u)$ and $\phi(vuv^{-1})=\phi(u)$, one has $\phi((A^*U)_p)=\phi(U_{A_\#p})$.
- (f) (periodic extension) For $b\in\Lambda_\ell$ let $(E_bU)(y,i)=U([y]_\ell+b,i)$ on $\mathbb Z^4$. Then:
  - $(E_bU)(y;w)=U([y]_\ell+b;w)$;
  - $E_b(\rho,a)^*U=(\rho,0)^*E_{\rho b+a}U$;
  - $E_b(U^g)=(E_bU)^{g([\cdot]_\ell+b)}$;
  - if $F$ depends only on links inside $X\subset\mathbb Z^4$, then $F\circ E_b$ depends only on links inside $[X]_\ell+b$.

A function $F$ of configurations is *gauge invariant* if $F(U^g)=F(U)$ for all $g$. It is *local on $X$* if $F(U)=F(V)$ whenever $U,V$ agree on all links with both endpoints in $X$. These are the notions of (Reg3)–(Reg4) in the main statement.

**Proposition I.1.3 (schedule of spacings; subdivision and blocks).**

(a) *Schedule.* A schedule consists of odd block factors $L_m\ge3$ ($m\ge0$) and strictly increasing periods $P_p$ with $4\mid P_p$. Put
$$D_0=1,\quad D_{m+1}=D_mL_m,\quad a_m=1/D_m,\quad \ell(m,p)=P_pD_m,\quad s_m=\log(1/a_m).$$
Then:
- $D_m$ is odd and strictly increasing;
- $a_0=1$, $a_{m+1}=a_m/L_m$, and $a_m\downarrow0$ strictly;
- $s_0=0$, $s_{m+1}=s_m+\log L_m$, and $s_m\to\infty$;
- $4\mid\ell(m,p)$ and $a_m\ell(m,p)=P_p$;
- the placement $x\mapsto a_m\,r_{\ell(m,p)}(x)$ maps $\Lambda_{\ell(m,p)}$ into $(-P_p/2,P_p/2]^4$.

For $m\le m'$, $\Lambda_{\ell(m,p)}\to\Lambda_{\ell(m',p)}$ is a subdivision of odd ratio $L_m\cdots L_{m'-1}$ that preserves placements.

(b) *Mesh inequality.* If $\log L_m/s_m\to0$ (the schedule is *gentle*), then there is $c>0$ with $a_{m+1}\ge c\,a_m^2$ for all $m$.

(c) *Subdivision.* Let $L\ge1$ and $\ell'=\ell L$. Put $\iota(x)=[L\,r_\ell(x)]_{\ell'}$. Then:
- $r_{\ell'}(\iota x)=L\,r_\ell(x)$;
- $\iota:\Lambda_\ell\to\Lambda_{\ell'}$ is an injective group homomorphism commuting with $B_4$.

The residue map $\mathrm{res}:\Lambda_{\ell'}\to\Lambda_L$ (reduction mod $L$) is additive with $\mathrm{res}\circ\iota=0$. The map $(x,y)\mapsto\iota(x)+[r_L(y)]_{\ell'}$ is a bijection $\Lambda_\ell\times\Lambda_L\to\Lambda_{\ell'}$, with inverse $z\mapsto(\mathrm{par}\,z,\mathrm{res}\,z)$; moreover $\mathrm{par}\circ\iota=\mathrm{id}$.

(d) *Odd ratio.* If $L$ is odd, then:
- $r_L$ commutes with $B_4$ and $\mathrm{par}(\rho z)=\rho\,\mathrm{par}(z)$;
- the block $\mathrm{par}^{-1}(x)$ is $\iota(x)+[\{-\tfrac{L-1}2,\dots,\tfrac{L-1}2\}^4]_{\ell'}$;
- $\mathrm{par}(z+e_i)=\mathrm{par}(z)+e_i$ if $r_L(\mathrm{res}\,z)_i=\tfrac{L-1}2$, and $\mathrm{par}(z+e_i)=\mathrm{par}(z)$ otherwise.

(e) *Metric comparison* (every $L$):
- $|z-\iota\,\mathrm{par}\,z|_{\ell'}\le L/2$;
- $L\,|x-x'|_\ell\le|\iota x-\iota x'|_{\ell'}$;
- $L\,|\mathrm{par}\,z-\mathrm{par}\,z'|_\ell\le|z-z'|_{\ell'}+L$ (foreshortening).

**Proposition I.1.4 (cover numbers of lattice sets).** For $X\subset\Lambda_\ell$ and $M\in\mathbb N$ let $\mathrm{halo}_M(X)=\{y:\exists x\in X,\ |x-y|_\ell\le M\}$. A set $D$ *covers $X$ at scale $M$* if two conditions hold: $X\subset\mathrm{halo}_M(D)$, and $\mathrm{halo}_M(D)$ is connected by nearest-neighbour steps ($|\cdot|_\ell\le1$) inside itself. Let $\mathrm{cov}_M(X)$ be the least $|D|$ over such $D$ (it is finite, since $D=\Lambda_\ell$ covers). All constants below are independent of $\ell$.
- (a) $\mathrm{cov}_M$ is monotone, and $\mathrm{cov}_M(X\cup Y)\le\mathrm{cov}_M(X)+\mathrm{cov}_M(Y)$ if $X\cap Y\neq\emptyset$.
- (b) $|\mathrm{halo}_M(D)|\le(2M+1)^4|D|$ and $|X|\le(2M+1)^4\,\mathrm{cov}_M(X)$.
- (c) $\mathrm{halo}_M(D)$ is nearest-neighbour connected if and only if any two points of $D$ are joined by a chain in $D$ with steps of length $\le2M+1$.
- (d) (diameter) For $x,y\in X$: $|x-y|_\ell\le(4M+2)\,\mathrm{cov}_M(X)+2M$.
- (e) (change of scale) Let $b\ge1$. Then $\mathrm{cov}_b(\mathrm{halo}_{kb}\{x\})\le(2k+1)^4$. If $D$ covers $X$ at scale $mb$, then $\mathrm{cov}_b(X)\le(2m+3)^4|D|$.
- (f) (coarsening) Let $\Lambda_{\ell L}\to\Lambda_\ell$ be a subdivision of ratio $L\ge1$ with parent map $\mathrm{par}$. Let $X\subset\Lambda_{\ell L}$, and let $M,B,s\in\mathbb N$ with $B\ge1$ and $M\le2B$. Then
$$L\cdot\mathrm{cov}_B\big(\mathrm{halo}_{sB}(\mathrm{par}\,X)\big)\le\big(L+10\,\mathrm{cov}_M(X)\big)\,(2s+13)^4,$$
with the left side computed on the coarse torus. Thus a fine set of cover number $k$ has coarse cover number $\le(2s+13)^4(1+10k/L)$.

**Proposition I.1.6 (Lie algebra, colours, Casimir constant, slope constant).**

(a) $\mathfrak g=\{X\in M_N(\mathbb C):e^{tX}\in G\ \forall t\in\mathbb R\}$ is a closed real subspace of skew-Hermitian matrices. It is closed under $[X,Y]=XY-YX$ and under $X\mapsto uXu^{-1}$, $u\in G$. *Tangent criterion:* if $f:\mathbb R\to M_N(\mathbb C)$ takes values in $G$, $f(0)=1$, and $f$ is differentiable at $0$ with $f'(0)=X$, then $X\in\mathfrak g$.

(b) *Colours.* $\langle X,Y\rangle_{\mathfrak g}=-\frac1N\mathrm{tr}(XY)$ is real on $\mathfrak g$ and is an $\mathrm{Ad}(G)$-invariant inner product. Fix an orthonormal basis and write $\mathfrak g\cong\mathbb R^d$, $c\mapsto X_c$, $d=\dim\mathfrak g$. Then:
- $\|X_c\|_F=\sqrt N\,|c|$;
- the orthogonal projection $\Pi:M_N(\mathbb C)\to\mathbb R^d$ (for the real inner product $\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{tr}(XY^*)$) satisfies $|\Pi A|\le\|A\|_F/\sqrt N$;
- $\mathrm{Ad}(u)c=\Pi(uX_cu^{-1})$ is an orthogonal representation of $G$;
- $\mathrm{ad}(c)c'=\Pi[X_c,X_{c'}]$ satisfies $X_{\mathrm{ad}(c)c'}=[X_c,X_{c'}]$;
- $c\mapsto\exp c:=e^{X_c}\in G$ is continuous.

(c) $G$ is *simple* if two conditions hold: there are $X,Y\in\mathfrak g$ with $[X,Y]\neq0$, and every real subspace $I\subset\mathfrak g$ with $[\mathfrak g,I]\subset I$ is $0$ or $\mathfrak g$. This is word for word the hypothesis `GaugeGroup.IsSimple` of the main theorem, since the public Lie-algebra field is characterized by the same exponential condition ([`GaugeGroup.coreSimple`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/WilsonBridge.lean#L12-L17), unit VIII.1).

If $G$ is simple, then:
- (d) every symmetric bilinear form $B$ on $\mathbb R^d$ with $B(\mathrm{ad}(x)y,z)=-B(y,\mathrm{ad}(x)z)$ equals $c\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ for some $c\in\mathbb R$;
- (e) $-\mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{ad}\,x\,\mathrm{ad}\,y)=C_{\mathfrak g}\langle x,y\rangle$ with $C_{\mathfrak g}:=-\frac1d\sum_i\mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{ad}(b_i)^2)>0$;

For $SU(N)\subset U(N)$ one computes $C_{\mathfrak g}=2N^2$ and $\kappa=11N^2/(12\pi^2)$; this computation is not in the Lean.

**Proposition I.1.8 (local logarithm, nearest point, curvature of a group element).** Let $\mathfrak g^\perp$ be the orthogonal complement of $\mathfrak g$ in $M_N(\mathbb C)$ for $\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{tr}(XY^*)$.

(a) There is a map $\log_G:M_N(\mathbb C)\to\mathbb R^d$ with the following properties:
- it is real-analytic near $1$ and $\log_G(1)=0$;
- $\log_G(e^{X_c})=c$ for $c$ near $0$;
- $\exp(\log_G u)=u$ for $u\in G$ near $1$.

It is the first component of the local inverse of $(c,\nu)\mapsto e^{X_c}e^{\nu}$ on $\mathbb R^d\times\mathfrak g^\perp$. Connectedness of $G$ is not assumed, and nothing is claimed away from $1$.

(b) $u\in G$ is a *nearest point* to $X\in M_N(\mathbb C)$ if $\|X-u\|_F\le\|X-v\|_F$ for all $v\in G$. Then:
- nearest points exist;
- $u$ is nearest to $X$ iff $vu$ is nearest to $vX$, iff $uv$ is nearest to $Xv$;
- if $u$ is nearest to $X$, then $u^*X-1\in\mathfrak g^\perp$;
- there is $\delta>0$, depending only on $G$, such that $X$ has exactly one nearest point whenever $\|X-v\|_F<\delta$ for some $v\in G$.

Near $1$ the nearest point is $\mathrm{foc}(X)=\exp(c)$, where $(c,\nu)$ is the local inverse of $\Psi(c,\nu)=e^{X_c}(1+\nu)$ at $X$. The map $X\mapsto\mathrm{foc}(X)$ is real-analytic at $1$, with strict derivative the orthogonal projection $P_{\mathfrak g}$ onto $\mathfrak g$. Consequently, for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is $\delta>0$ such that $\|X-1\|_F,\|Y-1\|_F<\delta$ and $u,v$ nearest to $X,Y$ imply
$$\|u-v-P_{\mathfrak g}(X-Y)\|_F\le\varepsilon\|X-Y\|_F .$$

(c) The curvature of $u\in G$ is $F(u)=\Pi\big(\tfrac12(u-u^{-1})\big)\in\mathbb R^d$, the projected anti-Hermitian part. It is continuous and satisfies:
- $F(1)=0$ and $F(u^{-1})=-F(u)$;
- $F(vuv^{-1})=\mathrm{Ad}(v)F(u)$;
- $|F(u)|\le\chi(u)$;
- $\frac{d}{dt}F(\exp(tc))\big|_{t=0}=c$.

**Proposition I.1.9 (the Wilson measure on the torus).** Let $\mu_\ell=\mu_G^{\otimes(\Lambda_\ell\times\{0,\dots,3\})}$ be the product Haar measure on configurations. Let

$$Z_\ell(\beta)=\int e^{-\beta E_\ell}\,d\mu_\ell,\qquad d\mathbb P_{\ell,\beta}=Z_\ell(\beta)^{-1}e^{-\beta E_\ell}\,d\mu_\ell .$$

- $E_\ell$ is continuous, with $0\le E_\ell\le12\ell^4$;

## 3. Strategy

The lattice is the native torus $(\mathbb Z/\ell)^4$ with the sup-metric of the centred lift. Local objects (walks, stencils, boxes, routes) live on the cover $\mathbb Z^4$ and are transferred by the periodic extension $E_b$, which also recentres at $b$.

The symmetry group $\Lambda_\ell\rtimes B_4$ acts on configurations by pullback of the connection: the new variable on a link is the transport of $U$ along the image link. A reflection reverses some links. So the pullback is a permutation of link variables composed with inversion of some of them, and the image of a plaquette is a plaquette with a different base corner, possibly traversed backwards. Its holonomy changes by conjugation and possibly inversion, which the class function $\sigma$ does not see. Invariance of the Wilson measure (Osterwalder–Seiler) then reduces to two facts: invariance of the product Haar measure under relabelling and inversion of factors, and invariance of the energy under a permutation of plaquettes.

Blocks have odd side $L$. Then the centred window $\{-\frac{L-1}2,\dots,\frac{L-1}2\}$ has no tie point, so the parent map commutes with $B_4$, and block-spin transformations based at a coarse site are covariant under the full lattice symmetry group (III.1). Periods are multiples of $4$, so every torus of the construction is even and splits into half-tori, as reflection positivity requires (II.2, IV.1). The gentle growth $\log L_m=o(\log(1/a_m))$ yields $a_{m+1}\ge c\,a_m^2$.

The size of a polymer is measured by its cover number $\mathrm{cov}_M(X)$: the least number of $M$-balls with connected union covering $X$. It plays the role of the large-set regulator of Brydges–Yau (Commun. Math. Phys. 129 (1990)) and Balaban. It bounds the volume ($|X|\le(2M+1)^4\mathrm{cov}_M$) and the diameter ($O(M\,\mathrm{cov}_M)$). Its key property is the coarsening inequality I.1.4(f): after blocking by $L$, cover number $k$ becomes $O(1+k/L)$. This contraction makes large polymers cheaper at each step of the RG.

The gauge group is used in its defining unitary representation, with no structure theory of compact Lie groups (no tori, roots or classification). The unit proves directly what is needed:
- the Lie algebra by one-parameter subgroups, using the Lie–Trotter limit with a Frobenius-norm telescoping;
- a local analytic logarithm by von Neumann's argument;
- the nearest-point retraction onto $G$, with derivative $P_{\mathfrak g}$ at $1$;
- the curvature of a group element;
- the adjoint Casimir constant, through a spectral-theorem proof of the uniqueness of invariant forms.

The colour normalization $\langle X,Y\rangle_{\mathfrak g}=-\frac1N\mathrm{tr}(XY)$ is chosen so that $\beta=1/g_0^2$ for the formal action $\frac1{2g_0^2}\int\sum_{\mu<\nu}|F_{\mu\nu}|^2_{\mathfrak g}$. Indeed $\sigma(e^{a^2F})=\frac{a^4}2|F|^2_{\mathfrak g}+O(a^6)$. Then $\kappa=11C_{\mathfrak g}/(24\pi^2)$ is the one-loop asymptotic slope of $\beta$ against $\log(1/a)$ (Gross–Wilczek, Politzer).

## 5. Where the difficulty lies

Nothing here is hard in the sense of Part III. The unit collects lemmas proved once, in the form in which later units use them.

*Genuine content:*
- (i) The local logarithm and the nearest-point retraction for a closed, possibly disconnected subgroup of $U(N)$. This is von Neumann's closed-subgroup argument plus a tubular-neighbourhood argument, done from scratch.
- (ii) The coarsening estimate I.1.4(f). It is the only place where the size of a polymer is compared across scales, and it carries the $1/L$ contraction that makes large polymers cheaper at each RG step.
- (iii) The invariant-form lemma and $C_{\mathfrak g}>0$.
- (iv) The Haar lower bound, with its crude exponent $2N^2$.

*Bookkeeping:* invariance of the Wilson law is conceptually trivial, but in coordinates reflections move the base points of links and plaquettes, and most of the Lean text in this part handles that.

*Points to note:*
- (a) **Normalization of $\beta$.** Each unoriented plaquette is counted once, and $\beta$ multiplies $1-\frac1N\mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{tr}\,U_p$. With the colour inner product $-\frac1N\mathrm{tr}(XY)$, $\beta=1/g_0^2$ for the action $\frac1{2g_0^2}\int\sum_{\mu<\nu}|F_{\mu\nu}|^2_{\mathfrak g}$.
  - The slope constant $\kappa=11C_{\mathfrak g}/(24\pi^2)$ is the one-loop coefficient of $\beta$ against $\log(1/a)$. The construction keeps the level-$m$ coupling within a drift of $\bar\beta+\kappa\,s_m$ ([`zeroUp_bound`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Pert/Asymptotics/RawAxis.lean#L15-L30)), and $\kappa=2b_0$ with $b_0=11C_{\mathfrak g}/(48\pi^2)$ ([`b0_normalizes`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Pert/Asymptotics/RawAxis.lean#L14)). This $\kappa$ is the slope $b_*$ of (Reg1).
  - For $SU(N)$: $C_{\mathfrak g}=2N^2$ (the standard $C_A=N$ rescaled by $2N$), $\beta=2N/g^2_{\rm std}$, and $\kappa=11N^2/(12\pi^2)=2N\cdot\frac{11N}{24\pi^2}$, consistent with $\beta(g)=-\frac{11N}{3}\frac{g^3}{16\pi^2}$.
- (b) **Representation dependence.** Everything is stated for $G$ in a fixed faithful representation. Isomorphic groups in different representations have different Wilson actions and different $C_{\mathfrak g}$; the ratio $\beta/\kappa$ does not depend on the normalization.
- (c) **Tie points.** All tori of the construction have even side, so the centred lift $r_\ell$ has a tie at $\ell/2$ and does not commute with reflections there. Separations are still invariant, but placements satisfy $\mathrm{place}(\rho x)=\rho\,\mathrm{place}(x)$ only off the boundary of the fundamental box. The main statement's admissible test functions are supported in the open box, and the block geometry uses odd ratios, so this costs only case distinctions.
- (d) **Non-explicit constants.** The neighbourhoods in I.1.8 come from the inverse function theorem and compactness. They depend only on $G$ and are not explicit, which is enough since $G$ is fixed before all other constants.
- (e) **Lorentz group.** It is $SO^+(1,3)$ acting on the same $\mathbb R^4$, with the Euclidean pairing used for momenta and the dual action $g^\vee=(g^{-1})^T$. There is no spin cover and no time or space reversal. This suffices for gauge-invariant (bosonic) observables and matches the public statement.

## 7. Open points for the writer

1. **Scattered helper lemmas.** The declaration-to-unit table also assigns to I.1 a number of small elementary lemmas outside the six folders (for example in `Cells`, `Extra`, `Further`, `Next`). They are not described here; a writer citing them should check that they are elementary.
2. **Where the hypothesis $M\le2B$ of I.1.4(f) is met.** In the RG units the cover scale at level $m$ is $\lceil(\log\hat\beta_m)^2\rceil$ (III.2). Presumably the coarsening inequality is applied with $M$ and $B$ the cover scales of consecutive levels, where $M\le2B$ holds once $\hat\beta$ is large (tentative). The consumers are in III.5 and III.10.
3. **Use of the Haar lower bound.** I.1.7 is consumed by three declarations: `RG.space` (assigned to V.2), `Vacuum.shell_pZ` and `Corrected.Tests.Offer.stamp`. Its role there is presumably a lower bound on the Haar weight of small-field sets, i.e. on normalizations of the Wilson weight (tentative).
4. **Small tori.** The definitions allow $\ell=1,2$, where plaquettes degenerate (repeated links); no lemma here excludes them. Every torus of the construction has side $\ell(m,p)=P_pD_m\ge4$, and residue tori have odd side $L\ge3$.
5. **Expectations are Bochner integrals.** $\langle F\rangle_{\ell,\beta}$ is $0$ for a non-integrable $F$, so every use must carry an integrability proof. Continuous and bounded measurable integrands are covered by I.1.9.
6. **Reflection positivity** of $\mathbb P_{\ell,\beta}$ is not proved in this unit. I.1.10 gives only invariance; the reflection-positivity and chessboard statements are in II.2 and IV.1 (`Cells`).
