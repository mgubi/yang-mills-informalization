## 1. What this unit proves

The unit defines the centered trace $\Theta$ and proves four things about it. It is a nonzero scalar field with zero vacuum expectation. Its two-point function has the short-distance law $r^8\log^2(1/r)\,S_{\Theta\Theta}\to 3\dim\mathfrak g/\pi^4$. No finite combination of free-field kernels reproduces it. No free Fock-space realization of the vacuum exists. A uniqueness statement for the stress tensor is also proved here. Most of the 20,000 lines are not about $\Theta$ itself. They are the lattice and renormalization-group proof that the coarse trace has the stated log-scaling with the explicit constant $3\dim\mathfrak g/\pi^4$.

**Proposition VII.2.1 (the centered trace).** Let $\Theta:=\sum_\mu T_{\mu\mu}-\langle\sum_\mu T_{\mu\mu}\rangle_{\xi_*}\mathbf 1$ be the trace of the stress tensor minus its Euclidean one-point function in the selected phase. Then $\Theta$ is a degree-4 field label with:
(a) $\langle\Theta\rangle=0$ at every point (Euclidean), and on the reconstructed Wightman side $\langle\Omega,\Phi_\Theta(f)\Omega\rangle=0$ for every Schwartz test $f$;
(b) $\Theta$ is real (invariant under label conjugation), invariant under every rotation $K\in O(4)$, and invariant under the time-reflection adjoint. The pinned vector in the reconstructed Hilbert space is therefore a self-adjoint Lorentz scalar;
(c) the field $\Phi_\Theta(f)$ of the reconstruction equals $\Phi_{\sum T_{\mu\mu}}(f)-\langle\sum T_{\mu\mu}\rangle\int f$.
*Status:* theorem. It is conditional on the Ward and rotation structure of VI.1 and on the reconstruction of V.2–V.3.

## 3. Strategy

*The anomaly law.* Classically $\sum_\mu T_{\mu\mu}=0$ for the Yang–Mills action, and the trace vanishes on smooth connections. In the lattice theory the trace picks up its nonzero value from renormalization: the trace of the stress tensor at scale $r$ is, to leading order, the derivative of the effective action with respect to the logarithm of the scale, i.e. $\beta(g)$ times the curvature-squared operator $F^2$. The argument has three parts. (i) At the lattice level, the trace field at scale $\mu$ is identified with the coarse "pullback" of a fixed observable: the combination of coarse variables that carries the coefficient of $F^2$. (ii) The coefficient has a logarithmic pole in the cutoff scale, with coefficient $12\kappa_G$ (VII.2.2(a)), and the finite part is controlled uniformly (b)–(d). Equivalently, $\Theta_r$, after normalizing with $(\log 1/r)^{-1}$, converges to $(4d_2)\,\mathcal O_{F^2}$, where $\mathcal O_{F^2}$ is the normalized curvature-squared label. (iii) Short-distance convergence to free Maxwell fields (VII.1) says that the two-point function of $\mathcal O_{F^2}$ at distance $x$ tends to the free value $3\dim\mathfrak g/(\pi^4|x|^8)$, so that $(\log 1/r)^{-2}\cdot (4d_2/\ldots)^2\ldots$ yields the amplitude $A_G=12(d_2/b_0)^2\dim\mathfrak g/\pi^4$. Since $d_2=-b_0/2$ the coupling-dependent factor cancels and $A_G=3\dim\mathfrak g/\pi^4$; the numerical one-loop beta-function coefficient cancels from this leading trace amplitude. This reading of (iii) is probable: the final algebra in `tagline` has been checked, but the proofs of the key convergence input have not been checked in detail.

*The proof architecture of the lattice part.* This is not a block-spin proof in itself but a large "pull-back and differentiate" argument. The main steps are: pull the coarse observable back to the fine lattice along centered chambers (`from_center`, [`YM/Trace/Pull`]); show that lattice finite-difference stencils commute with the limit up to fast-decaying errors (`differentiation`, `axis_advance`); show that the fine "relay" map (the variation of the RG map) and the coarse map agree to fourth order at zero (`recoup`, `aroma_transfer`), which is the "directional Ward lift" and is what makes the trace a derivative of the effective action; and then run the RG induction over levels with the "terrace" estimates (`crestship`, `touring`, `expire`) and the Gaussian/cluster decked estimates (`welfare`, `lay`, `quaff`, `garnet`) that control the activities of the corrected fields. The coefficient of the logarithm is read off from the one-loop determinant (`slopeL`, `saga`) and the coefficient sequence $c_i$ (`formulate`) with the value $-\kappa_G/4$.

*Exclusions.* The free-profile exclusion is short: the Laplace transform of a spectral measure of a polynomial-field correlation has the form $\sum a_\nu r^{\alpha_\nu}(\log r)^{k_\nu}+O(1)$, with integer powers, so $r^8\log^2(1/r)\,\mathrm{Re}\,W(r)$ has no nonzero finite limit; this contradicts $A>0$. The Fock exclusion is longer: one writes the spectral measure of $\Theta$ in the $n$-photon sectors of the ambient Fock space, uses locality to prove that the wave functions vanish for $n\ge4$ and have finite width (a spectral-gap-type statement), and shows that finitely many sectors cannot carry the log-enhanced tail of the spectral measure ("does not fit").

## 5. Where the difficulty lies

*Hard.* The only hard mathematics is VII.2.2–VII.2.3: an RG computation showing that the trace, as a lattice observable, becomes at scale $r$ the curvature-squared label multiplied by $d_2/\log(1/r)$ up to $o(1/\log r)$ errors, with the **exact** constant $d_2=-b_0/2$. It must hold for the whole Wilson family uniformly in volume. The dependence on the $12{,}500$ lines under `Store_l` and `agenda` is on the quantitative RG of Part III, not on a new idea.

*Routine but long.* The pullback/differentiation lemmas (VII.2.d–e) are bookkeeping with the lattice, fast sequences and filters. The free-profile exclusion (VII.2.m–n) is short and elementary once the log-law is known. The Fock exclusion is longer, but it is a standard type of argument: a Fock-space sector decomposition with a locality-based finite-width argument.

*Unusual or worth checking.*
1. The theorem is derived *from* the short-distance law with a positive amplitude. The exclusion therefore proves nothing about $\Theta$ unless the law (VII.2.3(c)) is established independently. The statement is conditional on the Scribe/Gallery property for the family.
2. The Lean exclusion `polynomial_excludes` is stated for polynomial fields with finitely many tracks of mass-shell kernels with arbitrary (also zero) masses and arbitrary numerator degrees $j$. The public statement (assessment §7.3) restricts $h$ to continuous homogeneous functions of integer degree. These agree only if `Wick.Mode` and `Track` impose the same condition. The comment in the Lean file says "no claim against a continuous-mass generalized free field", which matches the assessment's remark.
3. The amplitude $A_G$ uses $(d_2/b_0)^2$, which equals $1/4$ identically. The code keeps the two constants separately; their ratio is proved by `force_b`.
