Reading convention. A "polymer" is a label $i$ with a finite support $X_i$ of lattice sites and a real weight $w_i$. The Lean calls labels "addresses" and calls bounds "prices", "budgets" or "receipts". These are numerical inequalities, not economics. A bound $\mathrm{SB}(f;C)$ ("budget") means: $f\ge 0$, $f$ is summable, and $\sum f\le C$ (definition [`SumBudget`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/RG/Activities.lean#L59-L60)). Everything in the unit is an *implication*: if the polymer weights satisfy a stated smallness condition, then the series converge absolutely and the logarithm of the gas has a given form. That the actual activities of the renormalization group satisfy the conditions is proved elsewhere (III.8, III.10, III.4).

## 1. What this unit proves

The unit consists of five packages. Two are general theory: (A) the cluster expansion of a hard-core gas with absolute bounds (`ABSOLUTE_SUMS`), and (B) a second route to the logarithm by deletion identities (`VACUUM_NORMALIZATION`). Three prepare the inputs: (C) the sector decomposition of the blocking integral, giving a gain per seed (`SECTOR_MEASURE`), (D) exponential decay of inverses of gapped matrices (`STATIONARY_SCENES/Green`), and (E) uniform bounds on the precision (Gram) matrix of the step and its window covariances (`CONNECTED_ACTIVITY_BOUNDS`).

**Proposition III.5.1 (Mayer series and exponential of the log series, finite gas).** Let $\iota$ be a finite label set, $R$ a symmetric relation on $\iota$ with $R(i,i)$ ("hard core": $i$ is incompatible with itself), $w:\iota\to\mathbb R$. Put $\Xi(w)=\sum_{s}\prod_{i\in s}w_i$, the sum over subsets $s$ of pairwise $R$-compatible labels. Define the Ursell coefficient of an $(m+1)$-tuple $z$ by $\phi^T(z)=\sum(-1)^{|G|}$, the sum over connected spanning edge sets $G$ of the graph with an edge $\{a,b\}$ whenever $R(z_a,z_b)$ and $a\ne b$. Put $\ell_m=\frac1{(m+1)!}\sum_{z}\phi^T(z)\prod w_{z_a}$. If $|\ell_m|\le Cq^m$ for some $0<q<1$, then $\exp\sum_{m\ge0}\ell_m=\Xi(w)$.
*Status:* theorem.

## 3. Strategy

The unit is a package of *estimates on sums over finite families of polymers*, applied in three places. (i) To exponentiate the gas of large-field/connected activities after a block-spin step, and to control the ratio of partition functions when a region is removed (needed for the vacuum normalization and for locality). (ii) To turn the blocking integral into a sum over sectors, each of which carries an exponentially small factor per seed. (iii) To bound the inverses of the (Gram) precision matrices that appear in the Gaussian integrations of III.4.

The cluster expansion is the classical one in the form of a tree-graph bound. The partition function of a hard-core gas is the sum over tuples of $\sum_{G\subseteq\text{overlap graph}}(-1)^{|G|}$, which equals $1$ if the tuple is compatible and $0$ otherwise (inclusion–exclusion). Splitting off the connected component of a chosen vertex gives the coefficient identity $(m+1)g_{m+1}=\sum_k(k+1)\ell_kg_{m-k}$ between the coefficients of $\Xi$ and of the log series. This is the formal identity $\Xi'=(\log\Xi)'\,\Xi$. The Ursell coefficients are bounded by the number of spanning trees (Penrose partition into intervals of a Boolean lattice). Summing over trees needs the single hypothesis "every site is covered by polymers of total weighted size $\le\gamma$", together with the covering-number factor $e^{a\,\mathrm{cov}_B(X_i)}$ ("currency") that pays for the support of a polymer. A stochastic-matrix argument along the parent structure of a tree controls the sum over labels, and one factorial is cancelled globally by $\sum_p\mathrm{fanout}(p)\le 4^mm!$. For infinite or large label sets, the identity $e^{\log}=\Xi$ is obtained by restricting to finite subsets and passing to the limit by dominated convergence, which is possible because the absolute budgets do not depend on the subset. The difference from Brydges' or Glimm–Jaffe's presentations is that everything is done with *absolute budgets* ($\mathrm{SB}$) that can be composed, restricted, pulled back and scaled by a library of lemmas, so that the estimates can be fed to the next stage without re-deriving them. Real weights only: no complex branches (stated in the module docstring of [`Log.lean`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Push/ABSOLUTE_SUMS/Log.lean)).

The second route (`VACUUM_NORMALIZATION`) avoids graphs. It uses an exact recursion for the gas on a region $B$: removing a site $x$ gives $Z(B)=Z(B\setminus x)-\sum_{P\ni x}u(P)Z(B\setminus P)$. A Möbius inversion of $-\log Z$ defines local logarithms $\lambda(A)$. The recursion plus a site-sum condition with a multiplicative cost then bounds all $\lambda$ and the effect of deleting a set of sites. This is a Kotecký–Preiss-type argument carried out for finite regions, with signed coefficients.

The remaining three packages feed these estimates. The sector decomposition (`SECTOR_MEASURE`) writes the pushforward as a sum over stores of seeds and proves that each seed earns a Gaussian factor from the quadratic energy of the blocking minimizer (small-field region). The matrix bounds are the Combes–Thomas argument, then a windowed version for covariances, then the verification of the hypotheses for the actual Gram matrix.

## 4. Steps

### 4.1 Combinatorics of the hard-core gas

**Lemma 4.1 (gas as a sum over tuples).** For finite $\iota$ and hard $R$, $n!\sum_{|s|=n,\ s\text{ compatible}}\prod_{i\in s}w_i=\sum_{z:\{1..n\}\to\iota}\mathrm{allMass}(z)\prod_a w_{z_a}$, where $\mathrm{allMass}(z)=\sum_{G\subseteq\text{overlap graph of }z}(-1)^{|G|}=\mathbf 1[\text{overlap graph is empty}]$. Hence $\Xi(w)=\sum_n t^n g_n$ at $t=1$ with $g_n=\frac1{n!}\sum_z\mathrm{allMass}(z)\prod w$.
*Sketch:* the alternating sum over all subgraphs vanishes unless there are no edges. A tuple with no edge has distinct entries (hard core on the diagonal), so it is an ordering of a compatible set.
*Lean:* [`Partition.allMass_eq`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Push/ABSOLUTE_SUMS/Partition.lean#L23-L28), [`Gas.row`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Push/ABSOLUTE_SUMS/Gas.lean#L69-L120), [`Gas.sums`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Push/ABSOLUTE_SUMS/Gas.lean#L128-L137).

**Lemma 4.2 (splitting off the component of a vertex).** For a tuple $z$ on a vertex set $\alpha$ and a vertex $o\in\alpha$: $\mathrm{allMass}(z)=\sum_{s\ni o}\phi^T(z|_s)\,\mathrm{allMass}(z|_{s^c})$.
*Sketch:* a subgraph is determined by the vertex set $s$ of the component of $o$, a connected spanning graph on $s$, and an arbitrary subgraph on $s^c$ (no edges between $s$ and $s^c$ are allowed); signs multiply.
*Lean:* [`Partition.partition`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Push/ABSOLUTE_SUMS/Partition.lean#L219-L237).

**Lemma 4.3 (coefficient recurrence).** With $g_n$ as above and $\ell_k=\frac1{(k+1)!}\sum_z\phi^T(z)\prod w$: $(m+1)\,g_{m+1}=\sum_{k=0}^m(k+1)\,\ell_k\,g_{m-k}$.
*Sketch:* sum Lemma 4.2 over all tuples, group by $|s|=k+1$ ($\binom mk$ choices), and use that sums over $s$ and over $s^c$ factor.
*Lean:* [`Coef.unbased`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Push/ABSOLUTE_SUMS/Coef.lean#L71-L76), [`Coef.discrete`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Push/ABSOLUTE_SUMS/Coef.lean#L79-L125), [`Coef.recurrence`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Push/ABSOLUTE_SUMS/Coef.lean#L129-L138).

**Lemma 4.4 (exponential of the log series).** If $|\ell_m|\le Cq^m$ with $q<1$, then $\exp(\sum_m\ell_m)=\Xi(w)$ (III.5.1).
*Sketch:* $P(t)=\sum g_nt^n$ is a polynomial (degree $\le|\iota|$). Lemma 4.3 says $P'=L'P$ with $L(t)=\sum\ell_mt^{m+1}/(m+1)\cdot(m+1)$-type series, which converges for $t<q^{-1}$, a range containing $[0,1]$ because $q<1$. Uniqueness for the linear ODE gives $P=\exp L$ and one evaluates at $t=1$.
*Lean:* [`Gas.exact_polynomial`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Push/ABSOLUTE_SUMS/FiniteLog.lean#L66-L145). *(statement and first lines of the proof read; the ODE argument is reconstructed from the docstring "evaluate the discrete coefficient recurrence as an ODE on a real interval")*

**Lemma 4.5 (tree bound).** $|\phi^T(z)|\le\sharp \{p:\ p\text{ a spanning tree rooted at }z_{\mathrm{none}},\ p(i)\sim i\text{ in the overlap graph}\}$.
*Sketch:* to each connected spanning subgraph attach its breadth-first tree with a fixed label order as tie-breaker (the "seed"). For a fixed tree $p$ the set of subgraphs with that seed is an interval $[\text{stem}(p),\text{allowed}(p)]$ in the Boolean lattice of edge sets. The alternating sum over a non-trivial interval is $0$ (flip one free edge), and over a point it is $\pm1$. This is the Penrose partition scheme. The Lean bounds by the count of all spanning trees, not only Penrose trees.
*Lean:* [`Graph.pay`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Push/ABSOLUTE_SUMS/Graph.lean#L113-L148).

## 5. Where the difficulty lies

- **Routine but long.** The Mayer/Penrose/tree-sum part (4.1–4.3) is the standard cluster expansion. Its Lean length comes from three choices: the weights are real, signed, and indexed by a possibly infinite label set; the "currency" $e^{a\,\mathrm{cov}_B(X_i)}$ of covering numbers is carried through every estimate; and every bound is an absolute budget, valid for all finite subsums. A written proof can follow Brydges' short course (or Glimm–Jaffe) and state the hypotheses once as a single smallness condition ($\mathrm{Ready}$ + Hit). The statement of III.5.3 is close to what a human would write.
- **What is new relative to textbook expansions.** (a) The bound is on $\sum|\phi^T|\prod|w|$ where the compatibility relation $R$ is *finer* than the overlap test (`Tests`: $R\subseteq$ overlap), so the same estimate serves for the exclusions of the gas, for deleted labels and for "forced" labels. (b) The merge-after-log estimates (4.11) keep *exact* repetition factorials. (c) The vacuum-normalization route (4.17–4.21) gives the same conclusion without graphs and without a global smallness of $\ell^1$ norms, only a site-sum with a multiplicative cost $w$ (probable: compare Kotecký–Preiss with $a(P)=a|P|$).
- **Smallness is local, volume dependence only through $n^4$.** The log series bound in III.5.3 is $n^4\gamma/(1-q)$, extensive in the volume. Volume-independent statements are the ratio bounds (III.5.4, 4.20, 4.21).
- **Heavy bookkeeping.** `CONNECTED_ACTIVITY_BOUNDS` has about 100 small files with unusual names (Hour, Draught, Kilns, Bourse, …). The mathematics is a long chain of "frozen background" Gaussian estimates for the step, each stated for all step data at once with $x$ large. The importance of each file has not been assessed.
- **Mixed content.** The unit is a collection of five packages. The nominal title "cluster expansion of the polymer gas" fits only `ABSOLUTE_SUMS` and `VACUUM_NORMALIZATION` (about 4,560 lines); the other three (about 5,190 lines) are inputs for the Gaussian integration and for locality. A writer may prefer to move III.5.8–III.5.11 to III.4 or III.8 (see §7).
- **Strength.** The statements are for fixed finite tori of side $n$ with constants uniform in $n$ (where stated: III.5.4, 4.20, 4.21). The smallness condition $4\,\mathrm{ball}_B\gamma/\delta<1$ is known to be wasteful (the docstring of `Weigh.lean` says "No claim this is necessary"), which is harmless when the polymer weights decay as $e^{-c(\log\log\beta)^2}$ relative to polynomial prices.
