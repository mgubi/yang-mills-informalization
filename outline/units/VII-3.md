## 1. What this unit proves

**Setting.** Let $\mathfrak g$ be the Lie algebra of the gauge group. For $j\ge 0$ let $\mathrm{Sol}_j$ be the real space of on-shell $j$-jets of the free Maxwell curvature with values in $\mathfrak g$ (probable: homogeneous tensors of degree $j$ in the curvature components, satisfying the linearized Maxwell and Bianchi equations, hence harmonic; see [`Sol`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Extra/FREE_DETECTOR_ALGEBRA/Solutions.lean#L115-L115)). Choose a basis of each $\mathrm{Sol}_j$. A *Wick polynomial* is an element of $\mathcal W=\mathbb C[X_{(j,a)}]$, one variable for each basis element of each $\mathrm{Sol}_j$ ([`WickPol`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Maxwell/Equation.lean#L24-L24), [`Slot`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Maxwell/Equation.lean#L22-L22)). The variable $X_{(j,a)}$ stands for a Taylor coefficient at a point of the free curvature field. For distinct points $x_1,\dots,x_n\in\mathbb R^4$ the *free moment* $\langle P_1(x_1)\cdots P_n(x_n)\rangle_0$ is the sum over matchings of all variable occurrences with no pair inside one factor. Each pair contributes a derivative of the free two-point kernel ([`Wick.moment`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Maxwell/Wick.lean#L126-L127)). This is the vacuum expectation of Wick-ordered products at separated points in the free theory. The *free table* is the hierarchy of these moments, read on the labels of the construction ([`freeTable`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Euclid/Comparator.lean#L23-L24)).

For $x\ne y$ let $\mathcal R_{x-y}$ be the orthogonal reflection of $\mathbb R^4$ that exchanges the two points about the hyperplane perpendicular to $x-y$ (the code's `face`), acting on jets ([`pullSol`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Extra/FREE_DETECTOR_ALGEBRA/Symbol.lean#L54)). Let $\bar P$ be $P$ with complex-conjugated coefficients. The *reflected pairing* is
$$\langle P,Q\rangle_{x,y}:=\big\langle P(x)\,\,(\mathcal R_{x-y}\bar Q)(y)\big\rangle_0 ,$$
linear in $P$, antilinear in $Q$ ([`duplex`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Extra/FREE_DETECTOR_ALGEBRA/Duplex.lean#L54-L54), [`aligned`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Extra/FREE_DETECTOR_ALGEBRA/Duplex.lean#L49-L52)). It is the Osterwalder–Schrader form of the free theory in the direction $x-y$.

**Proposition VII.3.1 (strict reflected Gram positivity for Wick polynomials).** Let $x\neq y$ in $\mathbb R^4$ and $P\in\mathcal W$. Then $\langle P,P\rangle_{x,y}>0$ if and only if $P\ne0$. Moreover the form is Hermitian, $\overline{\langle P,Q\rangle_{x,y}}=\langle Q,P\rangle_{x,y}$, and for any linearly independent finite family $V_1,\dots,V_m$ the matrix $(\langle V_j,V_i\rangle_{x,y})$ is positive definite. The statement has no hypothesis on the group. The zero-color case is included and the polynomial degree is unbounded.

**Proposition VII.3.6 (rigidity of the degree-four free data, and the position equations).** Let $G$ be simple.
(a) *Frozenness.* Let $E_4$ be the field space of the free table at degree 4. A *buffer* $B$ is a $\mathbb C$-linear endomorphism of $E_4$ that kills the unit, preserves the filtration by lower degree, and commutes with conjugation and with the signed permutations. If $B$ is an infinitesimal automorphism of all separated free correlations, meaning $\sum_j\langle v_1,\dots,Bv_j,\dots,v_r\rangle_0(x)=0$ for all $r$, all tuples $v$ and all distinct $x$, then $B=0$. The same holds in degrees $d<4$.
(b) *Square position equations.* Because of (a), there are finitely many tests (compactly supported profiles on separated configurations, with fields in $E_4$) such that the derivatives of the tested free readings along buffers form an isomorphism from the space of buffers onto $\mathbb R^m$. These tests are "clean" and the system is "square".
(c) *Leading-order position data.* Let $b$ be a reconstructed theory over a Wilson family and $X$ a scaling sequence for which the leading-order comparison with free Maxwell holds (`FirstExtension`). Then (i) the tested means of the lattice representatives converge to the tested free readings, and (ii) there are buffers $B_k\to0$ such that $1+B_k$ is invertible and the tested means of the corrected representatives equal the tested free values exactly. The $B_k$ also satisfy the lattice real-spin, unit and nesting conditions and agree with the position equations at leading order. This is the structure `FourMeters`.

**Proposition VII.3.7 (non-freeness of the constructed theory; the part of the exclusion not assigned to this unit).** Let $G$ be simple and let $\Theta$ be the centered trace of the stress tensor in the selected phase. Write $G_\Theta(r)$ for its time-axis two-point function ([`traceTwo`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/ChallengeSpec.lean#L149-L149)). Suppose
$$r^8\big(\log\tfrac1r\big)^2\,G_\Theta(r)\longrightarrow A>0\qquad(r\to0^+)$$
(the short-distance law, proved in VII.2, with $A=3\dim\mathfrak g/\pi^4$). Then for every finite list of complex coefficients and tracks of mass-shell modes, $G_\Theta$ is not equal to the corresponding Wick profile on any interval $(0,\delta)$. A *Wick profile* is a finite sum $\sum_\alpha c_\alpha\prod_\beta K_{\mathsf m_{\alpha\beta},q_{\alpha\beta},h_{\alpha\beta}}(r)$, with $K_{\mathsf m,q,h}(r)=\int E_{\mathsf m}(p)^q h(p)\,e^{-rE_{\mathsf m}(p)}/(2E_{\mathsf m}(p))\,d^3p$ ([`wickProfile`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/ChallengeSpec.lean#L165-L166), [`Mode`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/ChallengeSpec.lean#L152-L158), [`Track`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/ChallengeSpec.lean#L162-L164)). The same hypothesis refutes any identification of the boundary two-point function with $\langle\cdot\rangle$ of a polynomial field in Fock space on compactly supported product tests.

## 3. Strategy

The unit has two logically separate parts, plus the exclusion argument that uses their outputs through the headline statement.

*(A) Algebra of the free theory.* The free Maxwell curvature, Wick-ordered at separated points, is encoded as a polynomial algebra $\mathcal W$ with a pairing $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{x,y}$. The point of the first part is that this form is not merely nonnegative (reflection positivity) but strictly positive on all nonzero polynomials. It is proved by a change of variables in which the pairing becomes diagonal. First one deals with linear polynomials, i.e. towers of probes, and shows that the Gram form of probes at the separation $x-y$ is triangular in the basis of translated (re-expanded) probes with diagonal entries $(-1)^j s_j|x-y|^{-2(j+2)}$ times a Frobenius norm. The Kelvin contraction (VII.3.1 step c below) supplies these entries. Then one uses the Wick removal recurrence to show that the pairing of monomials in an orthogonal linear basis is diagonal with entries $\prod\alpha_i!d_i^{\alpha_i}$. This is the combinatorial content of Wick's theorem for the Fock space of a Gaussian field, in the form of an orthonormal basis of Hermite-type monomials.

*(B) Consequences for labels and for the perturbative position data.* Strict positivity means that no nonzero element of the on-shell sector can be invisible to two-point tests, so the quotient of labels by the null space of the free table is exactly the set of polynomials on the shell. It also gives injectivity of the scanner and hence the full-rank columns that the rank and determinant bookkeeping needs. Separately, the stationarity argument in `Pert.Rigid` shows that at degree four a symmetry-compatible infinitesimal change of the identification of lattice fields with free fields is already determined by one- and two-point data. This makes the finite "position equations" square and solvable. The lattice perturbation theory of the Wilson measure (Gaussian Hessian measure, Faddeev–Popov determinant, Taylor readers), collected in `Pert.Lattice`, is what these comparisons refer to; how much of it is consumed is not determined here (§7).

*(C) Exclusion of free profiles.* This argument is not in the algebra above. It is a short asymptotic argument: a Wick profile, or any real Euclidean two-point function of a polynomial of free fields with finitely many masses, has an asymptotic expansion in $r\to0^+$ in a finite $\mathbb R$-span of terms $r^a(\log r)^k$ with $a\in\mathbb Z$ and $k\ge0$. By contrast, a function with $r^8\log^2(1/r)\,G(r)\to A\ne0$ is of order $r^{-8}\log^{-2}(1/r)$, which is not in that class. This is the same principle as the standard argument that a field whose two-point function has a negative power of a logarithm cannot be a composite of free fields (cf. Wilson's "anomalous dimension is not a free dimension"). The Lean version is an elementary lexicographic comparison of exponents along $r=2^{-n}$.

### 4.1 Encoding Wick polynomials by probe towers

**Lemma VII.3.a (exhaustion by towers).** For every $P\in\mathcal W$ there is $n_0$ such that for all $n\ge n_0$, all $N$ and all bases $b$ of $\mathrm{Tow}_n$ over $\mathbb R$ indexed by $\{1,\dots,N\}$, there is a polynomial $q\in\mathbb C[Y_1,\dots,Y_N]$ with $P=\mathrm{encode}_b(q)$. Here $\mathrm{encode}_b$ is the algebra homomorphism sending $Y_i$ to the linear polynomial $\mathrm{code}(b_i)=\sum_{j<n}\mathrm{digit}(b_{i,j})$, and $\mathrm{digit}(P)$ is the linear polynomial of order $j$ attached to the tensor $P\in\mathrm{Sol}_j$.
*Proof sketch.* Induct on the structure of $P$ (constants, sums, multiplication by one variable). A single variable $X_{(j,a)}$ is the code of the basis element at order $j$; for $m$ larger than $j$ it is a linear combination of $\mathrm{code}(b_i)$, since $\mathrm{code}$ is onto the span of linear forms of orders $<m$ once $b$ is a basis.

### 4.2 Positivity of the Gram form of towers

**Lemma VII.3.d (Kelvin contraction).** For $S\in\mathrm{Sol}_j$ and $x\ne0$, applying the order-$j$ differential operator defined by $S$ to the Green function $\mathrm{Green}(x)=\mathrm{normal}\,|x|^{-2}$ gives
$$S(\partial)\,\mathrm{Green}\,(x)=(-2)^j\,\mathrm{normal}\,|x|^{-2(j+1)}\;S^{\mathrm{field}}(x),$$
where $S^{\mathrm{field}}$ is the harmonic field defined by the tensor $S$ ("Kelvin inversion").
*Proof sketch.* Induction on $j$. For $j\to j+1$, the derivative in direction $a$ of $|x|^{-2(j+1)}\,Q_a(x)$ has two terms; the term with $\partial_aQ_a$ vanishes after summing over $a$ because the freeze of $S$ is trace-free (the on-shell condition), and the remaining term uses $\sum_a x_aQ_a=S^{\mathrm{field}}$ (Euler's identity for the homogeneous field).
*Lean:* [`shell_green`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Extra/FREE_DETECTOR_ALGEBRA/Harmonic.lean#L122-L152), [`freeze_diagonal`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Extra/FREE_DETECTOR_ALGEBRA/Harmonic.lean#L109-L119).

**Lemma VII.3.e (triangular structure of the tower Gram).** Let $\mathrm{price}(x,j):=(-1)^j\,\mathrm{strength}_j\,|x|^{-2(j+2)}>0$. For $P,Q\in\mathrm{Sol}_j$, let $\mathrm{shifts}$ denote the Taylor re-expansion of an order-$j$ probe into probes of orders $\le j$ at the other point. Then the "shifted" probe $\mathrm{newLeg}(P)=\sum_{k\le j}\mathrm{origin}_k(\mathrm{shifts}_{j,k}P)$ satisfies
- $B_x(\mathrm{newLeg}_i(P),\mathrm{newLeg}_m(Q))=0$ for $m<i$,
- $B_x(\mathrm{newLeg}_i(P),\mathrm{newLeg}_i(Q))=\mathrm{price}(x,i)\,\langle P,Q\rangle_{\mathrm{Frob}}$.

Consequently $B_x(\mathrm{shiftAll}\,p,\mathrm{shiftAll}\,q)=\sum_i\mathrm{price}(x,i)\langle p_i,q_i\rangle_{\mathrm{Frob}}$.
*Proof sketch.* The response of the free field at $x$ to a shifted probe of order $i$, differentiated $k$ times, vanishes for $k<i$ and equals $\mathrm{price}\cdot$ (Frobenius pairing) for $k=i$. The first is because the response is a polynomial of degree $i$ in the displacement (the order-$k$ Taylor coefficient of an order-$i$ jet at a shifted point vanishes for $k<i$). The second is VII.3.d with a reflection. The $(-1)^j$ comes from the reflection of the second argument and the sign $(-2)^j$.

**Lemma VII.3.r (no power-log law).** If $f$ has an expansion to order $0$ in the board, and $s^8(\log s^{-1})^2f(s)\to A$, then $A=0$.
*Proof sketch.* Replace $f$ by its board element $u$ plus $O(1)$; the $O(1)$ part contributes $s^8\log^2\cdot O(1)\to0$. Evaluate along $s=2^{-n}$. The product $s^8\log^2(1/s)\,u(s)$ is a finite combination $\sum_{t}c_t\,P_t(n)$ with $P_t(n)=\rho_t^n\,n^{k_t+2}$ and $\rho_t=2^{-(8+a_t)}$. Order the terms lexicographically by $(\rho_t,k_t)$. If the leading term has $\rho_t<1$ every term tends to $0$, so $A=0$. If $\rho_t\ge1$ the leading term tends to infinity and dominates all others, so the limit cannot exist as a finite number unless its coefficient is zero. In each case $A=0$.

## 5. Where the difficulty lies

*Where the mathematics is.* There is one place where the argument is not routine: the triangular change of basis of VII.3.e–f. The pairing of probes of different orders at the same separation is not diagonal, and one cannot prove positivity order by order. The Taylor shift makes the Gram form triangular with positive diagonal entries (a Kelvin-type computation), and this single computation underlies the strict positivity for *all* polynomials, with no bound on degree and with a Gram form that is strictly positive-definite rather than semidefinite. The rest of VII.3.1 is algebra: Wick's recurrence and an orthogonal basis.

*Routine, but long.* The matching bijections behind VII.3.3 (the `Pair.*` and `Occurrences` files), the sign bookkeeping behind the frozenness of degree four, and the whole lattice perturbation part are routine by hand but several hundred lines each in Lean.

*Unusual or stronger/weaker than expected.*
1. The strict positivity of the reflected Gram form of the free theory is stronger than reflection positivity (which gives only $\ge0$) and is proved without invoking reflection positivity of the Gaussian measure. Reflection positivity of the free field is the reason the form is Hermitian and nonnegative; strictness comes from the explicit diagonalization (probable: the code never refers to the Gaussian measure in this folder).
2. The exclusion in VII.3.7 is weaker than the physical statement "the theory is not free". It excludes only finite Wick profiles, on one function, in one direction (the time axis), through the failure of a power-log expansion. It does not exclude generalized free fields with continuous mass spectrum, and it does not use the Gram positivity of this unit at all. The Gram positivity and the exclusion argument meet only in the headline statement `WilsonExistence`, which carries the faithfulness of the free table and the position data on one side and `TraceExclusions` on the other.
3. `Frozen` is a statement about buffers constrained by lattice symmetries, so the rigidity at degree four is a combinatorial fact about the invariant quadratic fields and the free two-point readings (a finite-dimensional problem), not an analytic one.
4. The code states in several places that its results are only for the *free* quotient: [`Rank.lean`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Extra/FREE_DETECTOR_ALGEBRA/Rank.lean#L9-L15) says "this is not a quotient of the interacting family" and that O(4) transports are used only for the free table. Nothing in this unit says that the interacting labels have the same null space as the free ones.

## 7. Open points for the writer

1. **What `shellSector`, `residual` and `ProbeFamily` are exactly.** Their use in `no_residual` and `point_probe` is clear, but their definitions are not checked here. The informal description of VII.3.4(a) as "common kernel of all finite point tests on $\mathrm{sh}_d$" is a working reading. (tentative)
2. **Dependence on reflection positivity of the Gaussian measure.** The Gram positivity is expected not to invoke any general reflection positivity theorem, since the only inputs are the kernel formulas of the Maxwell Legendre module and the Kelvin computation. The supporting shift, covariance, translation and Legendre-action declarations have not been checked in full. (probable)
3. **Who uses VII.3.1.** The dependency graph shows the positivity theorem feeding `no_residual`, `scanner_inj`, `reflected_pd`, and through them `vane`, then MergeProof (`Accent.rated`, `Accent.warrant`) and `FullVolume.weighed`. I take this as the rank and determinant bookkeeping of the construction (III.11 or IV.1 by the unit names) but did not follow it. The purpose of the unit title ("the theory is not free") is not directly reflected in these uses. (tentative)
4. **`Pert.Lattice`.** Only the series development, the first part of the precision development and the docstrings have been read. The roles of `Orb`, `Full` and `Bare` in the lattice computation (a covariance given by the actual Hessian, Taylor readers with Wick subtraction) are inferred from the docstrings. It is not determined which of these declarations the main chain uses, nor how `finite_agreement` is used. The `LOOP_COEFFICIENT` declarations of the `Push` module (about 10) in this section have not been read.
5. **Degree-four basis in VII.3.m.** The identification of the basis `star c` with products of curvature components $F_{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}$ (summed over color) is inferred from `flat`, `link`, `tape` and the `Cube` declarations of the free stress certificate, which have not been read. The role of simplicity (only the nonvanishing of $\dim\mathfrak g\cdot$plane factors is visible in the code) should be checked. (tentative)
6. **Role of `FourMeters` in the main theorem.** `WilsonExistence` carries `FirstExtension` and `FourMeters` as the "leading position comparison"; the public statement (`JointExistence`) does not mention them. The place where the position equations are consumed downstream is not identified, except through `Pert.Parity.parity_four` (treated in VIII.1). It may serve the normalization of the stress tensor at the free level for VII.1/VII.2. (tentative)
7. **Dividing line with VII.2.** The declaration table puts `Wick.euclidean_excludes`, `Wick.no_powerlog` and `TraceExclusions` in VII.2. If the writer wants one section on "the theory is not free", §4.6 above can be moved there unchanged. VII.3.1–VII.3.6 are then a section on the free Maxwell Wick algebra and the degree-four position data, whose title does not match its content.
8. **Wick profiles with logarithms.** The expansion class $\mathcal B$ includes $\log$ powers because massive modes give terms like $s\log s$ in Bessel-type kernels. The proof of `moment_expands` in the massive-mode development has not been read; this is checked only against the statement `Track.moments_expand`. (probable)
9. **Which hypotheses of `Headline.TraceExclusions` come from where.** The fields `wick`, `maxwell`, `invariant` (Fock-space, Maxwell-identification and invariant-sector countertests) are produced from `Trace.Target.Result` (`H.exclude`); these proofs have not been read. Only the `wick` field's Euclidean part is described in §4.6. (tentative)
