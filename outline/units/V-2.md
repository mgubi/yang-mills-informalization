## 1. What this unit proves

*Conventions.*
- **Points.** A point of $\mathbb R^4$ is $x=(\vec x,x^0)$; time is the last coordinate, as in the code. A configuration is $x=(x_1,\dots,x_n)\in\mathbb R^{4n}$.
- **Pairing.** $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is the *Euclidean* inner product of $\mathbb R^{4n}$, extended complex-bilinearly. All cones below are taken with respect to it.
- **Cones and groups.** $V_+=\{\lvert\vec x\rvert<x^0\}$, with closure $\bar V_+$. $\mathcal L^\uparrow_+$ is the proper orthochronous Lorentz group. $\Lambda_a(t)$ is the boost of rapidity $t$ along the spatial axis $a$. The Poincaré group $\mathcal P$ acts on tests by $(g_\ast f)(x)=f(g^{-1}x_1,\dots,g^{-1}x_n)$.
- **Heights.** Heights $y\in\mathbb R^n$ may be negative. $E_n=\{y_1<\dots<y_n\}$ is the set of ordered heights, and $\Sigma_n=\{z\in\mathbb C^n:\mathrm{Im}\,z\in E_n\}$ is the strict region. We write $\underline y=((\vec0,y_1),\dots,(\vec0,y_n))\in\mathbb R^{4n}$.
- **Fourier transform.** $\hat f(p)=\int e^{-i\langle x,p\rangle}f(x)\,dx$, $\check f(p)=(2\pi)^{-4n}\int e^{i\langle p,x\rangle}f(x)\,dx$, and $(\mathcal FW)(f)=W(\hat f)$.

*Tables and the OS space.*
- $S$ and $T$ are tables on one label register (glossary). $F_d$ is the quotient label space of $S$ in degree $d$, and $\mathcal F$ is the space of all labels (`S.Union`), into which $F_d$ embeds by $\mathrm{pin}_d$.
- $T(x;v)$ is the Euclidean $n$-point function of $T$ at a non-coincident configuration $x$, read on tags $v_k=(d_k,v_k)$ of $S$ (`S.useAt T`). It is meaningful when $T$ *respects* $S$, i.e. $S.\mathrm{null}_d\subseteq T.\mathrm{null}_d$.
- $\mathcal H$ is the OS Hilbert space of $T$ for the time axis (`Chamber`). It carries the vacuum $\Omega$, a Hamiltonian $H\ge0$ with holomorphic contraction semigroup $e^{-\zeta H}$ ($\mathrm{Re}\,\zeta>0$), the spatial translations $U(\vec a)$, and a unitary space-time translation group.
- A *spread* $A$ gives slab operators $A_d(w)$ for words $w$ in a time slab of width $d$ (V.1).

*Continuations and boundary families.*
- A **continuation** $F$ (`Waves`) is a family of maps $F_n(z;\vec x)$, $z\in\mathbb C^n$, $\vec x\in(\mathbb R^3)^n$, each multilinear on $\mathcal F^n$. We write
  - $F^y_n(x)=F_n(x^0+iy;\vec x)$, with real times $x^0_k$ and heights $y_k$ (`restricted`);
  - $\psi_v(y;f)=\int F^y_n(x)(v)f(x)\,dx$ (`psi`).
- A **boundary family** $W$ (`Surface`) is a family of multilinear maps $W_n:\mathcal F^n\to\mathcal S'(\mathbb R^{4n})$.
  - For a word $b=((u_1,f_1),\dots,(u_n,f_n))$, with $u_k\in\mathcal F$ and $f_k\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R^4)$, we set $W(b)=W_n(u)(f_1\otimes\dots\otimes f_n)$.
  - For an antilinear star $J$ on $\mathcal F$ we set $b^{\ast}=((Ju_n,\bar f_n),\dots,(Ju_1,\bar f_1))$ and $\langle b,c\rangle_W=W(b^{\ast}c)$.

The unit does not construct a Wightman theory for a particular table. It proves thirty implications, each universally quantified over tables, OS data and continuations. Their assembly for the mixed limit table and for the selected phase is made in VIII.1 (§4.8).

**Proposition V.2.5 (Poincaré covariance; A16, C07, C08, C09, C28).**

*Data.*
- A rotation structure $R$ on $S$ (VI.1): an action of $O(4)$ on the $F_d$, with Lie action $\rho$ of $\mathfrak{so}(4)$. It is subject to `R.Oriented T` (the functions $T(x;v)$ are $O(4)$-covariant) and `R.Laws`.
- A spin structure $K$: representations $K(\Lambda)$ of $\mathcal L^\uparrow_+$ on $\mathcal F$ and on each $F_d$, with `K.Descends R`, which consists of:
  - compatibility with $\mathrm{pin}_d$;
  - the representation property and continuity;
  - $\frac{d}{dt}K_d(\Lambda_a(t))\vert_{t=0}=-i\rho(e_0\wedge e_a)$;
  - $K=R$ on spatial rotations;
  - commutation with the reflection adjoint.

(a) (A16) $\mathcal L^\uparrow_+$ is generated, as an abstract group, by the spatial rotations and the coordinate boosts (`beamGens = ⊤`).

(b) (C08, no hypotheses) For all $n$ and $f\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R^{4n})$:
- $g\mapsto g_\ast f$ is continuous from $\mathcal P$ into $\mathcal S(\mathbb R^{4n})$;
- $t^{-1}(\Lambda_a(t)_\ast f-f)\to-D_af$ in $\mathcal S$ as $t\to0$, where $D_a=\sum_k(x^a_k\partial_{x^0_k}+x^0_k\partial_{x^a_k})$.

(c) (C07) Assume $S$ and $T$ satisfy the hierarchy, together with `R.Oriented T` and `R.Laws`. Then every $F$ with `Region`, `Breathes`, `Bounds`, `Lines` and `Along` satisfies the boost identity `Boosty`: for all $y\in E_n$, tags $v$, spatial axes $a$ and $f\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R^{4n})$,
$$\sum_{k=1}^n\psi_{v^{(k)}}(y;f)=\psi_v\big(y;D_{a,y}f\big),\qquad D_{a,y}=\sum_k\Big(x^a_k\,\partial_{x^0_k}+(x^0_k+iy_k)\,\partial_{x^a_k}\Big).$$
Here $v^{(k)}$ is $v$ with $v_k$ replaced by $-i\rho(e_0\wedge e_a)v_k$ (`kick`). The statement also lists $P$.Inclusions, `T.respects S` and `K.Descends`; the proof does not use them.

(d) (C09) Assume A16, C08 and the hypotheses of (c). If $F$ satisfies `Region` and `Boosty` and has boundary family $W$ (`NonTangentialFree`), then $W$ satisfies `Urs`:

## 3. Strategy

The unit carries out Osterwalder–Schrader reconstruction (OS I, Glimm–Jaffe Ch. 19). Its statements are made on joint Schwartz test functions of all arities, never pointwise for the Wightman functions.

*Continuation.* The starting point is the OS Hilbert space $\mathcal H$ of $T$ and a single field insertion between two heat factors, $B_p(v)$. One has $B_{p+u}=e^{-uH}B_pe^{-uH}$, so heuristically $B_p(v)=e^{-pH}\varphi_v(\vec 0,0)e^{-pH}$. The slab bound of V.1 gives $\|B_p(v)\|\le Cp^{-a}$. Interleaving the $B_p(v_k)$ with $e^{-(\tau-2p-it)H}U(\vec a)$ gives matrix elements holomorphic in complex times with strictly ordered imaginary parts. At imaginary times these matrix elements are the Schwinger functions, and they grow polynomially in the inverse gap. The continuation is thus obtained from the semigroup, one time variable at a time; no Laplace transform of the Schwinger functions is needed.

*Boundary values and spectral support.*
- Cauchy–Riemann together with the polynomial bound gives tempered boundary values. Taylor's formula with integral remainder along the fixed direction $(0,1,\dots,n-1)$ bounds the smeared continuation by finitely many $L^1$ norms of time derivatives of the test function, uniformly up to the edge.
- The smeared continuation at height $y$, tested against $\widehat{e^{\langle\underline y,\cdot\rangle}f}$, does not depend on $y$ (a Laplace identity). Letting the heights grow gives the support of $\mathcal FW$ in $\mathcal P_n$.

*Poincaré covariance.* It is obtained without any spectral assumption.
- Translations and spatial rotations follow from the Euclidean ones by a one-variable identity theorem at each fixed height, followed by $y\to0$.
- Boosts follow from the $O(4)$ covariance of $T$:
  1. Differentiate it in the $(0,a)$ plane.
  2. Continue the resulting identity in a complex scaling of the real times (C07).
  3. Pass to the boundary.
  4. Integrate along one-parameter boost groups as an ODE, using the strong differentiability of the Poincaré action on $\mathcal S$ (C08).
  5. Generate $\mathcal L^\uparrow_+$ (A16).
- Covariance upgrades the support to the Wightman spectral set $\mathcal V_n$ (total momentum $0$, partial sums in $\bar V_+$). A Fourier–Laplace transform with a smooth cutoff then extends the continuation to the forward tube.

*Locality.* It is a Jost-point argument, localized to one adjacent pair. At heights that are equal on the pair and ordered elsewhere, an imaginary boost moves a spacelike configuration into both the tube and the swapped tube. Through the complexified covariance this defines two holomorphic germs. At Euclidean points (zero real times) both germs restrict, on open parts of the totally real Wick slice, to the same smooth Schwinger function. So the germs have the same jets and coincide. The agreement is propagated along a rescaling of real times, integrated against tests to equality of partial boundary values, and passed to the limit as the heights tend to $0$.

*Hilbert space.* The Gram matrix of two heated OS vectors is the continuation at the glued, reflected configuration. As the heights tend to $0$ it converges to $\langle b,c\rangle_W$. This one fact gives:
- positivity of $W$;
- an isometric embedding of the Wightman space into $\mathcal H$ that intertwines translations, so the spectrum condition and, for a phase, uniqueness of the vacuum are inherited from the OS side;
- the field operators, as left multiplication on the Kolmogorov space of $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_W$.

*Differences from the textbook route.*
- (i) Everything is tested and quantified over arities, including $n=0$.
- (ii) Lorentz covariance comes from Euclidean $O(4)$ covariance at the infinitesimal level and an ODE, with no spectral input.
- (iii) Locality uses neither the edge-of-the-wedge theorem nor the permuted extended tube. It uses local germs at Euclidean points and adjacent transpositions only.
- (iv) Wightman positivity, the spectrum condition and vacuum uniqueness are inherited from $\mathcal H$ rather than proved separately.
- (v) The energy–momentum measure is constructed (V.2.10 and V.1), not assumed.

**Lemma V.2.b (projection-valued measure).** Let $X$ be a compact Hausdorff space and $A:C(X)\to\mathcal B(\mathcal H)$ a $\ast$-homomorphism. There is a strongly countably additive projection-valued measure on the Borel sets of $X$ representing $A$.

2. $f\mapsto A(f)\psi$ extends to an isometry $L^2(\mu_\psi)\to\mathcal H$.
3. $E(s)\psi$ is the image of $1_s$; additivity holds along increasing unions.

**Lemma V.2.c (Schwartz calculus; tensor density, C25).**
1. A family in $\mathcal S$ whose jets converge locally uniformly, and whose seminorms are bounded, converges in $\mathcal S$.
2. Riemann sums of continuous $\mathcal S$-valued integrands converge in $\mathcal S$.
3. Tensor products are dense in $\mathcal S(\mathbb R^{4n})$.

*Proof sketch.* For 3:
- reduce to compactly supported $v$;
- choose a product cutoff equal to $1$ on the support;
- write $v=\int\hat v(\xi)\,e^{i\langle\xi,\cdot\rangle}\,d\xi$ times the cutoff, by Fourier inversion;

**Lemma V.2.s (imaginary boosts at a spectator profile; C17).** As in V.2.7(a).
*Proof sketch.*

**Lemma V.2.u (agreement at Euclidean points; C20).** As in V.2.7(d).
*Proof sketch.*

## 5. Where the difficulty lies

*Where the mathematics is.*
1. **Lorentz covariance without spectral input (C07, C09).** The infinitesimal Euclidean rotation identity is continued to real times through a complex scaling of the real times, at fixed heights and on compactly supported tests. It is passed to the boundary and integrated along one-parameter boost groups as an ODE. This needs C08 and the finite-dimensionality of the label levels. Rotations and translations come directly from Euclidean covariance by a one-variable identity theorem.
2. **Locality (C17–C21 and `Shift/Exchange`).** The argument stays local:
   - only adjacent transpositions;
   - germs at boundary points of the tube, obtained by one-parameter imaginary boosts;
   - agreement at Euclidean points through jets on the Wick slice, which uses the smoothness of $T$ at non-coincident points with equal pair times;
   - propagation along a time rescaling and partial boundary values.

   The genuinely Euclidean inputs are permutation symmetry and smoothness of $T$. The docstring of `sameGround` notes that equality of values at time zero alone would not suffice.
3. **Boundary values (V.2.h).** One Taylor formula along a single interior direction gives distributions of finite order, uniformly on the closed set of nondecreasing heights. This yields both the non-tangential limit at $0$ and the bounds needed in C05 and C07.
4. **Hilbert space.** Positivity, the spectrum condition and vacuum uniqueness of the Wightman theory all come from the isometric embedding into $\mathcal H$ provided by the Gram limit (C22, C24).

*Routine but long:* C08, C10–C12, C14, C25, C29, Kolmogorov, and the projection-valued measure. Most of the length goes into Schwartz-seminorm estimates and the bookkeeping of ordered heights.

## 7. What remains unexplained
1. **The slab bound.** `AllCopy` is the only quantitative input. It should be checked where it is proved for the mixed limit table and for the selected phase (V.1, B-items), and how its constants depend on the degree and on the norm of the representative.
2. **Unit boundaries.** Some lemmas are filed elsewhere:
   - the boundary-value existence (V.2.h), `tents` and the exchange lemmas (V.2.v) under VI.1;
   - the assembly and the field construction (§4.8) under VIII.1.

   They are part of the reconstruction proof, and a written account should present them with it.
3. **Unused hypotheses (§5).** A written statement can drop them. The extra hypotheses are only premises, so nothing downstream depends on them.
4. **Not read.**
   - The holomorphic semigroup `Heat/Rows`.
   - The `Spark` proofs.
   - The internals of `of_cut`, `hasFDerivAt_shift` and the polydisc lemma behind analyticity in C13.
   - `Compact/Borel`.
   - The spectral measure of translations, `SNAG/Escape` (V.1).
   - `Attachment.with_heat`, used by `Compatible.vacuum`.
5. **Vacuum uniqueness** is available only under `UniqueHeat`, i.e. for a phase. For the mixed table the construction yields `FieldCore` without uniqueness. This is consistent with the assessment (§6.2).
6. **Strength of the limit hypotheses.** C24 genuinely needs the two-parameter (non-tangential) limit, whereas C05 uses only the radial one. Whether C09 could also use only radial limits was not examined.
