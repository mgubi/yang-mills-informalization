## 1. What this unit proves

*Conventions.*

- **Points.** A point of $\mathbb R^4$ is $x=(\vec x,x^0)$; time is the last coordinate, as in the code. A configuration is $x=(x_1,\dots,x_n)\in\mathbb R^{4n}$.
- **Pairing.** $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is the *Euclidean* inner product of $\mathbb R^{4n}$, extended complex-bilinearly. Dual cones are taken with respect to this pairing. Hilbert-space inner products are antilinear in the first argument.
- **Cones and groups.** $V_+=\{\lvert\vec x\rvert<x^0\}$, with closure $\bar V_+$. $\mathcal L^\uparrow_+$ is the proper orthochronous Lorentz group. $\Lambda_a(t)$ is the boost of rapidity $t$ along the spatial axis $a$. The Poincaré group $\mathcal P$ acts on tests by $(g_\ast f)(x)=f(g^{-1}x_1,\dots,g^{-1}x_n)$.
- **Heights.** Heights $y\in\mathbb R^n$ may be negative. $E_n=\{y_1<\dots<y_n\}$ is the set of ordered heights, and $\Sigma_n=\{z\in\mathbb C^n:\mathrm{Im}\,z\in E_n\}$ is the strict region. We write $\underline y=((\vec0,y_1),\dots,(\vec0,y_n))\in\mathbb R^{4n}$.
- **Fourier transform.** $\hat f(p)=\int e^{-i\langle x,p\rangle}f(x)\,dx$, $\check f(p)=(2\pi)^{-4n}\int e^{i\langle p,x\rangle}f(x)\,dx$, and $(\mathcal FW)(f)=W(\hat f)$.

Unless stated otherwise, arities $n,m$ range over $\mathbb N$, including $0$, and label tuples range over $\mathcal F^n$. Supports of functions are closed supports.

*Tables and the OS space.*

- $S$ and $T$ are tables on one label register (glossary). $N_d(Q)$ denotes the raw degree-$d$ labels whose correlations in $Q$ vanish with arbitrary other insertions. The quotient label space of $S$ is $F_d=\mathrm{Label}_d/N_d(S)$, and $\mathcal F$ is their union, into which $F_d$ embeds by $\mathrm{pin}_d$.
- $T(x;v)$ is the Euclidean $n$-point function of $T$ at a non-coincident configuration $x$, read on tags $v_k=(d_k,v_k)$ of $S$. For a tuple of tags, $\mathrm{pin}\,v$ means $(\mathrm{pin}_{d_k}v_k)_k$; the insertion into $\mathcal F$ is implicit in $\psi_v$. It is meaningful when $T$ *respects* $S$, i.e. $N_d(S)\subseteq N_d(T)$.
- $\mathcal H$ is the OS Hilbert space of $T$ for the time axis. It carries the vacuum $\Omega$, a Hamiltonian $H\ge0$ with holomorphic contraction semigroup $e^{-\zeta H}$ ($\mathrm{Re}\,\zeta>0$), the spatial translations $U(\vec a)$, and a unitary space-time translation group.
- A *spread* $A$ gives slab operators $A_d(w)$ for words $w$ in a time slab of width $d$ (V.1).
- A differential plan $P$ consists of linear maps $\iota_{d,e}:F_d\to F_e$ for $d\le e$ and derivative maps $\partial_{d,a}:F_d\to F_{d+1}$. Its inclusions are *compatible with a table* $Q$ if replacing any tag $(d,v)$ by $(e,\iota_{d,e}v)$ leaves $Q(x;v)$ unchanged at every non-coincident configuration, with arbitrary other tags. Its derivatives are compatible with $Q$ if inserting $\partial_{d,a}v$ differentiates the corresponding correlation in that slot and coordinate. Writing $C_d$ for label conjugation and $M_d(s)$ for a signed coordinate symmetry, $P$ is *oriented* if its inclusions commute with $C_d,M_d(s)$, its derivatives commute with conjugation, and
$$M_{d+1}(s)\partial_{d,b}=\sum_a s_{ab}\,\partial_{d,a}M_d(s).$$
The induced star satisfies $J\mathrm{pin}_dv=\mathrm{pin}_d(M_d(r_0)C_dv)$, where $r_0$ is time reflection.

*Continuations and boundary families.*

- A **continuation** $F$ is a family of maps $F_n(z;\vec x)$, $z\in\mathbb C^n$, $\vec x\in(\mathbb R^3)^n$, each multilinear on $\mathcal F^n$. We write
  - $F^y_n(x)=F_n(x^0+iy;\vec x)$, with real times $x^0_k$ and heights $y_k$;
  - $\psi_v(y;f)=\int F^y_n(x)(v)f(x)\,dx$.
- A **boundary family** $W$ is a family of multilinear maps $W_n:\mathcal F^n\to\mathcal S'(\mathbb R^{4n})$.
  - For a word $b=((u_1,f_1),\dots,(u_n,f_n))$, with $u_k\in\mathcal F$ and $f_k\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R^4)$, we set $W(b)=W_n(u)(f_1\otimes\dots\otimes f_n)$.
  - For an antilinear map $J$ on $\mathcal F$ we set $b^{\ast}=((Ju_n,\bar f_n),\dots,(Ju_1,\bar f_1))$ and $\langle b,c\rangle_W=W(b^{\ast}c)$.

The thirty reconstruction statements are conditional results for arbitrary tables, OS data and continuations. VIII.1 applies them to the mixed limit table and to a selected phase (§4.8).

**Proposition V.2.5 (Poincaré covariance; A16, C07, C08, C09, C28).**

*Data.*

- A rotation structure $R$ on $S$ (VI.1): an action of $O(4)$ on the $F_d$, with Lie action $\rho$ of $\mathfrak{so}(4)$. It is subject to Euclidean covariance (the functions $T(x;v)$ are $O(4)$-covariant) and the rotation laws of VI.1.
- A spin structure $K$: representations $K(\Lambda)$ of $\mathcal L^\uparrow_+$ on $\mathcal F$ and on each $F_d$, with spin compatibility with $R$, relative to the real structure of $S$, which consists of:
  - compatibility with $\mathrm{pin}_d$;
  - the representation property and continuity on each finite-dimensional level;
  - $\frac{d}{dt}K_d(\Lambda_a(t))\vert_{t=0}=-i\rho(e_0\wedge e_a)$;
  - $K=R$ on spatial rotations;
  - commutation with the reflection adjoint.

(a) (A16) $\mathcal L^\uparrow_+$ is generated, as an abstract group, by the spatial rotations and the coordinate boosts.

**Proposition V.2.6 (Fourier–Laplace continuation in the forward tube; C10–C16).**

*Definitions.*

- Tube and cones:
  - $V^+_n=\{\eta\in\mathbb R^{4n}:\eta_{k+1}-\eta_k\in V_+,\ 1\le k<n\}$ (the open imaginary-part cone);
  - $\bar V^+_n$ is the analogue with $\bar V_+$ (the closed imaginary-part cone);
  - $\mathcal T_n=\{z\in\mathbb C^{4n}:\mathrm{Im}\,z\in V^+_n\}$ (the tube). This is the forward tube in absolute coordinates; there is no condition on $\mathrm{Im}\,z_1$.
- A *cutoff* $\chi_n$ is a real smooth function whose derivatives are all polynomially bounded, equal to $1$ on the $1$-neighbourhood of $\mathcal V_n$, with closed support contained in its open $3$-neighbourhood.
- *Fourier–Laplace continuation:*
$$\mathcal W_n(v)(z)=(2\pi)^{-4n}\,(\mathcal FW_n(v))\big(e^{i\langle\cdot,z\rangle}\chi_n\big),\qquad z\in\mathcal T_n .$$

- *Partial boundary values:* $W_n(v)[\eta](f)=(\mathcal FW_n(v))\big(e^{-\langle\eta,\cdot\rangle}\chi_n\,\check f\big)$, formally $W_n(v)(\cdot+i\eta)$.

*Statements.*

(a) (C10, cone duality) For $p\in\mathcal V_n$ and $\eta\in\bar V^+_n$, $\langle p,\eta\rangle\ge0$. For $\eta\in V^+_n$ there is $c>0$ with $\langle p,u\rangle\ge c\lvert p\rvert$ for all $p\in\mathcal V_n$ and all $u$ near $\eta$.

(b) (C11) Cutoffs exist.

(c) (C12) Given (a), every cutoff satisfies the damping properties (D):

- $e^{-\langle\eta,\cdot\rangle}\chi_n$ has temperate growth for $\eta\in\bar V^+_n$;
- $\eta\mapsto e^{-\langle\eta,\cdot\rangle}\chi_nf$ is continuous from $\bar V^+_n$ into $\mathcal S$.

(d) (C13) Given (a), $\mathcal W_n(v)$ is holomorphic on $\mathcal T_n$.

(e) (C14, the integral representation) Given (c), for $\eta\in V^+_n$ the identity $W_n(v)[\eta](f)=\int\mathcal W_n(v)(x+i\eta)f(x)\,dx$ holds, with integrable integrand.

(f) (C15) Suppose:

- (c) holds and $\mathrm{supp}\mathcal FW\subseteq\mathcal V_n$;
- (d) and (e) hold;
- $F$ satisfies (SC), (PG), (I) and the Laplace identity V.2.4(a).

  Then $\mathcal W_n(v)(x+i\underline y)=F^y_n(x)(v)$ for all $y\in E_n$ and $x\in\mathbb R^{4n}$. Together with (d) and (e) this is (TC).

(g) (C16) Given $\mathrm{supp}\mathcal FW\subseteq\mathcal V_n$ and (Cov), $\mathcal W_n(v)(z)=\mathcal W_n(K(\Lambda)v)(\Lambda z)$ for $z\in\mathcal T_n$ and $\Lambda\in\mathcal L^\uparrow_+$, with $\Lambda$ acting on real and imaginary parts.

**Proposition V.2.7 (locality; C17–C21, C27).**

*Setting.*

- Let $n\ge2$ and let $Q=(i,i+1)$ be an adjacent pair. $s$ is the transposition of $i$ and $i+1$, and $\sigma$ swaps slots $i$ and $i+1$ of real, imaginary or complex configurations.
- $\Gamma_Q=\{x:x_{i+1}-x_i\text{ spacelike}\}$.
- A height profile $y$ is a *spectator profile* for $Q$ if $y_i=y_{i+1}$ and $y_k<y_l$ for all other pairs $k<l$. Then $\underline y$ and $\sigma\underline y$ lie in $\bar V^+_n$, and for every real $x$ the point $x+i\underline y$ lies in the closures of $\mathcal T_n$ and of $\sigma^{-1}\mathcal T_n$.
- $\Lambda_e(\zeta)$ is the complexified boost of rapidity $\zeta\in\mathbb C$ along a unit spatial direction $e$.

*Statements.*

(a) (C17) There are spectator profiles $y_m\to0$ with the following property. For every $m$ and every $x\in\Gamma_Q$ there are a unit direction $e$, $t>0$, and neighbourhoods $U_{\rm o},U_{\rm t}$ of $x+i\underline{y_m}$ such that, for every $b\in[0,t]$:

- $\Lambda_e(ib)w\in\mathcal T_n$ for all $w\in U_{\rm o}\cap\mathcal T_n$;
- $\sigma\Lambda_e(-ib)w\in\mathcal T_n$ for all $w\in U_{\rm t}\cap\sigma^{-1}\mathcal T_n$;
- the same holds at $b=t$ for $w=x+i\underline{y_m}$ itself.

  The profiles are $y_m=(m+1)^{-1}d$, where $d_k=k-1$ except that $d_{i+1}=i-1$. Thus the entries at $i$ and $i+1$ agree; all later entries keep their original values.

(b) (C18) Under spin compatibility, $t\mapsto K_d(\Lambda_e(t))$ extends to an entire function of $t\in\mathbb C$, for each $d$ and $e$.

(c) (C19) Assume (TC), cone support V.2.4(c), spin compatibility, $S$ symmetric, the inclusions of $P$ compatible with $S$, (b), and the real covariance V.2.6(g). Let $v$ be labels, $Q$ a pair, and $y$ a spectator profile admitting the paths of (a). Then every $x\in\Gamma_Q$ carries two germs:

- $g^{\rm o}_x$, holomorphic near $x+i\underline y$ and equal to $\mathcal W_n(v)$ on $\mathcal T_n$ near that point;
- $g^{\rm t}_x$, equal to $\mathcal W_n(v\circ s)\circ\sigma$ on $\sigma^{-1}\mathcal T_n$ near that point.

(d) (C20) Assume $S$ and $T$ symmetric, the inclusions of $P$ compatible with $S$, $T$ smooth, $T$ respects $S$, (SC) and (TC). For any such germs and any $x\in\Gamma_Q$ with all $x^0_k=0$, $g^{\rm o}_x=g^{\rm t}_x$ near $x+i\underline y$.

(e) (C21) Assume (D) and (TC). Each $x\in\Gamma_Q$ has a radius $r>0$ such that, for compactly supported $f$ with support in $B(x,r)$:
$$W_n(v)[\underline y](f)=\int g^{\rm o}_x(\xi+i\underline y)f(\xi)\,d\xi,\qquad W_n(v\circ s)[\sigma\underline y](f\circ\sigma)=\int g^{\rm t}_x(\xi+i\underline y)f(\xi)\,d\xi.$$

- $\mathrm{supp}\mathcal FW_n(u)\subseteq\mathcal V_n$ for all $u$;
- the cutoff satisfies (D);
- for a sequence of spectator profiles $y_m\to0$ there are germs satisfying (d) and (e).

(g) (C27) Joint locality implies *word locality*: $W(a\,(u,f)(u',g)\,c)=W(a\,(u',g)(u,f)\,c)$ for all words $a$, $c$, labels $u$, $u'$, and tests $f$, $g$ such that $\mathrm{supp}f-\mathrm{supp}g$ consists of spacelike vectors.

## 3. Strategy

The slab bound controls a field insertion $B_p(v)$ between two heat factors. The relation $B_{p+u}=e^{-uH}B_pe^{-uH}$ makes products of these insertions independent of the admissible width. Their vacuum matrix elements give a continuation to strictly ordered imaginary times, with polynomial growth as the time differences shrink. Cauchy–Riemann identities and Taylor's formula along $(0,1,\ldots,n-1)$ then give tempered boundary values for arbitrary approaches through ordered heights. A Laplace identity yields total energy zero and nonnegative suffix energies.

Euclidean covariance gives Poincaré covariance of the boundary family: translations and spatial rotations pass through a one-variable identity theorem; infinitesimal rotations in a time–space plane give the boost identity, which is integrated along the real boost groups. Lorentz covariance improves the spectral support to $\mathcal V_n$. Fourier–Laplace transformation extends the continuation to the forward tube. Locality follows from two holomorphic germs near each spacelike adjacent pair: imaginary boosts construct the germs, Euclidean permutation symmetry identifies their jets on the Wick slice, and continuation along a rescaling of real times propagates their agreement. Partial boundary values then give exchange of adjacent spacelike fields.

Finally, inner products of heated OS vectors equal the continuation at a glued, reflected configuration. Their limit gives positivity of the word kernel and an isometric embedding of its Kolmogorov space into the OS Hilbert space. Left multiplication defines the fields on the common word domain. The embedding intertwines translations, transferring positive energy; Lorentz covariance gives the forward-cone spectrum condition. A one-dimensional OS heat-fixed space gives vacuum uniqueness. These are the operator and local-germ forms of Osterwalder–Schrader reconstruction used here.

### 4.6 Locality

**Lemma V.2.s (imaginary boosts at a spectator profile; C17).** As in V.2.7(a).
*Proof sketch.*

1. **Spectator profiles.** The profiles $y_m=d/(m+1)$ are spectator profiles. Monotone heights give $\underline{y_m}\in\bar V^+_n$, and $\sigma$ fixes $\underline{y_m}$. The closure statements follow by approaching through strictly ordered heights.
2. **Choice of direction.** Let $\xi=x_{i+1}-x_i$ be spacelike, so $\vec\xi\ne0$, and take $e=\vec\xi/\lvert\vec\xi\rvert$. Then
$$\mathrm{Im}\big(\Lambda_e(ib)w\big)_k=\mathrm{Im}\,w_k+\sin b\cdot c_e(\mathrm{Re}\,w_k)+(\cos b-1)\,s_e(\mathrm{Im}\,w_k),$$
   with $c_e(\vec X,t)=(te,e\cdot\vec X)$ and $s_e(\vec X,t)=((e\cdot\vec X)e,t)$.
3. **The pair difference.** Its imaginary part is $0$ at $b=0$, and $c_e(\xi)=(\xi^0e,\lvert\vec\xi\rvert)\in V_+$ exactly because $\lvert\xi^0\rvert<\lvert\vec\xi\rvert$ (the Jost condition). So the difference enters $V_+$ for small $b>0$.
4. **The other consecutive differences.** They are already in $V_+$ and stay there by continuity, uniformly near the base point.
5. **The swapped tube.** After the swap the pair difference is $-\xi$, and the opposite rapidity $-ib$ compensates.

For $n=2$ the profile is identically $0$, so the germs sit at real points.

**Lemma V.2.u (agreement at Euclidean points; C20).** As in V.2.7(d).
*Proof sketch.*

1. **The Wick slice.** Let $M(\vec x,\tau)=(\vec x,i\tau)$. Its complex span is $\mathbb C^{4n}$. If $x$ has zero times, then $M(\vec x,y)=x+i\underline y$.
2. **The original germ.** On the open set of strictly ordered $\tau$, $M$ maps into $\mathcal T_n$, and $g^{\rm o}\circ M=\mathcal W\circ M=F$ at imaginary times. That equals the Schwinger function $T((\vec x_k,\tau_k);v)$ (V.2.6(f), (SC)).
3. **The tilted germ.** On the swapped-ordered set, $g^{\rm t}\circ M$ equals the same Schwinger function, by permutation symmetry of $T$.
4. **Smoothness at the base point.** The point $(\vec x,y)$ is non-coincident: the pair has equal times but $\vec x_i\ne\vec x_{i+1}$ by spacelikeness. So $T$ is smooth there.
5. **Jets.** Both open sets accumulate at $(\vec x,y)$. Hence all real jets of $g^{\rm o}\circ M$ and $g^{\rm t}\circ M$ at that point equal those of $T$.
6. **Conclusion.** Real jets on a slice that spans over $\mathbb C$ determine all complex derivatives, so $g^{\rm o}=g^{\rm t}$ near $x+i\underline y$.

No Schwarz reflection and no edge-of-the-wedge theorem are used.

## 5. Where the difficulty lies

The boost argument must control moving labels and tests without imposing a topology on the full label union. All labels are first placed in one finite-dimensional level; Schwartz estimates then justify the derivative and its boundary limit. The locality argument needs equality of complete jets on the Wick slice. Equality of values at zero real times would not determine the two germs. Smoothness of the Euclidean correlations at non-coincident configurations, including equal pair times, supplies those jets.

The Taylor formula in V.2.h gives a bound uniform over all nondecreasing heights. This uniformity permits varying tests in the Laplace identity and independent heating scales in the Gram limit. Positivity of the word kernel then suffices for the field construction; surjectivity of the embedding into the OS space is not required.

Several formal hypotheses are stronger than the corresponding proof needs. The slab-insertion construction uses the slab bound but not the derivative laws of the spread. C05 and C06 use the tested identities and growth bound without Euclidean root values. C14 uses the damping properties without spectral support; C16 uses spectral support and Lorentz covariance without agreement with the strict-time continuation. These distinctions do not remove the hypotheses from the statements above.

The pairing is Euclidean, so momenta transform by $\tilde\Lambda=\mathsf r\Lambda\mathsf r$. The tube uses absolute coordinates and imposes no condition on its first imaginary point. For $n=1$, it is $\mathbb C^4$ and $\mathcal V_1=\{0\}$. The polynomial bound is per label tuple, with no uniformity in the arity. The chosen value $B_0=0$ is outside the admissible widths. The Laplace identity is stated on compactly supported tests.

## 7. What remains unexplained
1. **Upstream slab estimate.** The constants in (SB) are independent of the slab width and word length for fixed degree bound $D$ and representative bound $R$; they may depend on $D,R$. VIII.1 obtains the mixed and selected slab laws from the phase-data package. The upstream proof of those laws, beyond their use in reconstruction, is outside this check.
2. **Imported analytic results.** The holomorphic heat-semigroup construction and the OS translation spectral measure are used by their stated properties. The construction of the compact-model Borel functional calculus, and the estimates underlying complex differentiation of the Fourier–Laplace tests, have not been checked in full. Their exact uses are identified in V.2.b, V.2.f and V.2.p.
3. **Location of the arguments.** The boundary-value construction, the spectral-support improvement and the germ exchange lemmas are filed under VI.1; the field realization and application to phase data are filed under VIII.1. They belong with the reconstruction argument in a written proof.
4. **Vacuum uniqueness.** The construction for the mixed table supplies the field axioms without vacuum uniqueness. The selected phase has uniqueness because its OS heat-fixed space is one-dimensional. No uniqueness assertion for the mixed table follows here.
5. **References.** The bibliographic pointers in §2 identify the relevant standard methods; their theorem numbering has not been verified.
