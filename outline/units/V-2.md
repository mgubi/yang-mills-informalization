## 1. What this unit proves

Throughout, $S$ and $T$ are *tables* (families of Euclidean correlation kernels on a label register $L$ for a gauge group $G$; see III/IV and the glossary), $S$ carries the quotient label space $S.\mathrm{Union}=\bigoplus_d\mathcal F_d$ of field labels, $\mathsf H$ is the Osterwalder–Schrader (OS) Hilbert space of $T$ for the time axis (the GNS space of positive-time words, with the contraction semigroup of time translations), and $\mathrm{Gen}$ stands for the hypothesis package `Pantry` described in §2.

The unit does not construct a particular Wightman theory. It proves a bundle of *service statements*, each universally quantified over tables and OS data, whose conclusions together give the analytic continuation, the boundary values, the Poincaré covariance and the locality of the reconstructed theory. The application to the limit table of Part IV and to each phase is in V.3.

**Proposition V.2.6 (analytic emission in the whole tube; C10–C16).** Let $\mathcal T_n$ be the forward tube of $n$ points (imaginary parts in the open forward cone, ordered). Let $W$ be a family of boundary distributions with spectral support in the dual cone (hypothesis `cones W`).
(a) (C10) the dual cone is separated by relative gaps (`Wide`): $\langle p,y\rangle\ge0$ for $p$ in the cone, $y$ in the closure of the imaginary-part cone, and locally $\langle p,y\rangle\ge c\|p\|$.
(b) (C11) there is a smooth cutoff $\chi_n$, equal to $1$ on a thickened cone, with a compactly supported unit-integral bump (`Blanket`).
(c) (C12) the exponential multiplier $e^{i\langle p,z\rangle}\chi_n(p)$ has exponential reserve in every Schwartz seminorm, uniformly for $z$ in the closed shift set (`Modified`).
(d) (C13) hence the emitted function $\mathrm{emit}(v)(z)=(2\pi)^{-2n}\,\hat W_n(v)(\text{lens}(z))$ is holomorphic in the whole open tube $\mathcal T_n$.
(e) (C14) assuming `Modified`, `cones W` and C10: the strictly damped contour is a genuine function: for imaginary part $y$ in the open cone, $\partial^{\text{shift}}W_n(v)(f)=\int \mathrm{emit}(v)(x+iy)f(x)\,dx$ with integrable integrand (`Income`).
(f) (C15) the emission equals the strict-time continuation $F$ of V.2.3 at ordered complex times (`FullBody`).
(g) (C16) the emission is covariant under real Lorentz transformations (`wave_turn`), when $W$ is (`Urs`).

**Proposition V.2.7 (gluing of the two tubes at adjacent spacelike pairs, and local commutativity; C17–C21, C27).**
(a) (C17) for every "near pair" $P$ (a pair of adjacent points in the labelled list, the open set `gap` of configurations in which they are spacelike separated, and the exchange map $\mathrm{name}$ swapping them), there are imaginary heights $y_m\to0$ with $y_m$ admissible for $P$ and such that for every $x$ in the gap there is a Lorentz boost ray $e$ and a $t>0$ for which the complex boost $x\mapsto x+\sinh(\pm iu)\,(\text{cross term})+(\cosh(\pm iu)-1)(\text{scale term})$, $0\le u\le t$, keeps $x+iy_m$ inside the forward tube, and keeps its image under $\mathrm{name}$ inside the forward tube, for all points in a neighbourhood. This is the Bargmann–Hall–Wightman path.
(b) (C18) the boost action on labels extends to an entire function of the rapidity (`Weighty`), for any label action satisfying `Descends`.
(c) (C19) given real Lorentz covariance of the emission (`wave_turn`), `Weighty` and the path of (a): both tubes carry a germ of holomorphic function at the points $x+iy$: the original contour and the exchanged one (`Panes`).
(d) (C20) the two germs agree at every point of the gap with all times zero (`sameGround`), and (Lean) hence on the whole gap by analytic continuation along rescaling of time.
(e) (C21) the two partial boundary values of $W$ are realized as partial distributions near each gap point by the respective germs (`Panes.Forms`).
(f) (C27) from (a)–(e): if for each $n$, label list $v$ and near pair $P$ the hypothesis (`Actor`) $W_n(v)-W_n(v\circ s)\circ\mathrm{swap}=0$ on the gap holds, then the Wightman distributions are local in the sense `Regional`: exchange of test functions with spacelike separated supports leaves $W$ unchanged. (The Lean packaging has the vanishing of the difference on `gap` as the conclusion of the lemma `NearPair.actor`; `Actor` is the corresponding universal statement.)

## 3. Strategy

The unit carries out Osterwalder–Schrader reconstruction in the style of Glimm–Jaffe Chapter 19 and Streater–Wightman, but in a form in which every conclusion is a statement about test functions, never about pointwise values of the $n$-point functions. One starts from the OS Hilbert space $\mathsf H$ of the Euclidean table $T$. The lattice representatives become bounded operators on $\mathsf H$ after averaging over a time slab of small width $p$ (C01). Their matrix elements with the semigroup between them are holomorphic in the complex times in the strict region $\mathrm{Im}z_1<\dots<\mathrm{Im}z_n$ (C03), and give the Euclidean kernels at imaginary times. The polynomial bound $p^{-A}$ on the operator norms is what later makes the boundary values tempered.

From the continuation one obtains, by Fourier–Laplace theory and cone support, the boundary distributions $W$ (C04–C06), and one shows that they are invariant under translations and Lorentz transformations (C07–C09, C28). The Lorentz invariance is proved the other way round from the textbook: the Ward identity of Part VI gives the infinitesimal boost identity on imaginary times, and its integration to real motions, using the continuity of the Poincaré action on Schwartz space (C08), gives covariance of the boundary values without any assumption on the spectrum. The emission step (C10–C16) is the passage from the strict region to the full forward tube: with a smooth cutoff near the cone and an exponential damping with reserve in all Schwartz seminorms, the Fourier transform of $W$ against the damped lens defines a holomorphic function in the open tube that agrees with the strict-time continuation. Locality (C17–C21, C27) follows the Bargmann–Hall–Wightman argument: for an adjacent pair of points at spacelike separation the two tubes (original and exchanged) are connected through complex Lorentz boosts. Both germs coincide at Euclidean points, because both are the same smooth Euclidean function, and then everywhere by analyticity. Finally the Gram forms of the heated OS vectors converge to the Wightman kernel (C22–C24), so that the field operators on the OS space realize the boundary values, and the null spaces coincide (C25–C26).

What differs from the textbook route: (i) everything is stated for joint tempered distributions in $\mathbb R^{4n}$ and pairs of ordered heights, including the non-tangential limit for the whole height vector, with explicit integrability statements instead of pointwise convergence; (ii) the Lorentz covariance is obtained from Ward identities in imaginary time; (iii) locality needs only an adjacent pair and local germs, the full permutation group is generated by adjacent transpositions; (iv) the project avoids assuming the spectral theorem: the Wightman spectrum condition is a PVM with support in the cone.

## 5. Where the difficulty lies

*Routine but long.* The thirty statements are mostly well known mathematics (Laplace transforms of cone-supported tempered distributions, Bargmann–Hall–Wightman, GNS). The length (about 6,200 lines in the C-files) is due to doing everything on Schwartz space with explicit seminorm estimates, to the bookkeeping of ordered heights and slopes, and to avoiding any use of the spectral theorem or of pointwise values of Wightman functions.

*Where the mathematics is.*
1. Lorentz covariance without a spectrum assumption (C07–C09). Usually one proves covariance of the Wightman functions from the Euclidean $SO(4)$ invariance and a continuation to complex Lorentz transformations, or from the existence of the Hamiltonian and the boost generator on a domain of analytic vectors. Here the route is: the Ward identity at imaginary times gives an infinitesimal statement, and C08 (strong differentiability of the boost action on Schwartz space) allows integration. This is the place where Part VI enters Part V. The Ward identity in the form `Boosty` is taken to be the one proved in VI.1; whether the hypotheses `Laws`/`Oriented`/`Descends` are discharged for the limit table has not been checked.
2. Locality (C17–C21, C27). The classical proof uses the edge-of-the-wedge theorem for the permuted extended tube. Here only adjacent transpositions are needed, with a local statement on `gap`, and the local identification of the two sides is by analytic continuation along the time-scaling path to a Euclidean point (`Panes.reaches`). The hypothesis `sameGround` is a real input (the docstring says it is not implied by equality of values at $t=0$ only): it requires the Euclidean kernel to be smooth off coincidences, which is the smoothness of IV.1.2.
3. The emission (C10–C16). Replaces the textbook "Laplace transform of a tempered distribution supported in a cone is holomorphic in the tube" by an explicit smooth-cutoff version. The cutoff is a technical device to keep the estimates inside the Schwartz space.

*What looks unusual.*
(a) The statements are *conditional service bundles*: `Bid.Arcs` is a structure with thirty fields, each a universally quantified implication. The theorem `reconstructionArcs` says that each implication is proved; it does not produce a Wightman theory for any particular table. The docstring in `Integrated.lean` says so explicitly ("The consumer lemmas retain their actual data and compatibility premises").
(b) The strict-time boundary values $W$ and `NonTangentialFree` are *hypotheses* of C05, C09, C13–C16, C22, C24. Statement `Waves.Region` asks that the continuation equals the Euclidean table at imaginary times; the existence of $W$ with `NonTangentialFree` (a two-parameter non-tangential limit of the smeared continuation, for the whole height vector, along the ordered cone) is not proved in this unit. It must be supplied by the application in V.3, with the polynomial bounds of C01. This is the one place where I would check carefully that the hypotheses are met by the limit table (see §7).
(c) The E/F/G bundles (`pressure_grants`, `stationary_scene`, `source_sections`) and the folders `Overlay`, `Vac`, `Spark` are not reconstruction; they appear in this unit's size because the unit was cut by proof dependency. `pressure_grants` takes the hypothesis `EBare` ("the separately unfunded EBare input") (probable that it is discharged elsewhere; see the seams analysis for the E-chain).
(d) Names: the code uses arbitrary words for the objects (`Waves` the continuation, `Surface` the boundary distributions, `Aurora` the emission, `Panes` the germs, `Hull` and `Lamp` the Gram identification); see §6.
