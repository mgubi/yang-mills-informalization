1. What this unit proves

The unit has two outputs. The first is the rank comparison. In the continuum limit, the space \(F_d\) of local fields of degree \(d\) is isomorphic to the corresponding space of the free Maxwell field, compatibly with complex conjugation and with the lattice symmetry group. Here \(F_d\) is the space of labels of degree \(d\) modulo labels whose correlations vanish. The second output is the stress tensor of the limit: a nonzero conserved stress tensor that satisfies the translation and rotation Ward identities and is normalized against the free Maxwell stress tensor along a scaling sequence. Both outputs rest on quantitative estimates proved here: a per-site determinant budget, chessboard bounds for connected parts of corrected observables, and comparisons of covariances with the free curvature kernel. The trial systems test a finite separating family of free one- and two-point correlations.

Notation. Set \(\mu_k = \exp ((\log \log \hat{\beta}_k)^2)\). The centered estimate and the trial systems use this fixed schedule quantity. \(G\) is a compact matrix group, assumed simple only where stated; \(\dim G\) is the dimension of its Lie algebra; \(h\) is normalized Haar measure; \(\text{chord} (u) \geqslant 0\) is the chordal distance from \(u\) to \(1\); and \(\text{shell} (r) = \{u \in G : \text{chord} (u) \leqslant r\}\). The torus of side \(n \geqslant 2\) is \(\Lambda_n = (\mathbb{Z}/ n)^4\), with \(c = n^4\) sites, \(4 c\) bonds and \(6 c\) plaquettes. The prior \(\mu_{\text{prior}}\) is product Haar measure on bonds. An RG representation \(R\) on \(\Lambda_n\) has an inverse coupling \(\beta = \beta (R)\), an exponent \(\text{exponent}_R\) with constant part \(\text{scalar} (R)\), and an unnormalized density \(e^R = e^{\text{exponent}_R} \cdot (\text{polymer gas})\).

Proposition III.11.1 (determinant budget on the torus, and its per-site price). (a) Let \(R\) be a lawful representation on \(\Lambda_n\), \(n \geqslant 2\), with lawful-class parameters \((\sigma, A, \Lambda, \delta, w)\), and assume \(e^R \geqslant 0\) pointwise. Let \(0 < s \leqslant 1 \leqslant b\) be a Haar budget (Step 1). Let \(g > 0\) with \(g \leqslant \text{threshold} (R)\) and \(\beta g^2 = 1\). Let \(F\) be a \(\mu_{\text{prior}}\)-integrable complex function and \(z \geqslant 0\), \(X \in \mathbb{R}\) such that \(z |F (U) | \leqslant \exp (\text{exponent}_R (U) + cX)\) for every configuration \(U\). Then

\(\displaystyle \frac{z \hspace{0.17em} | \int F \hspace{0.17em} d \mu_{\text{prior}} |}{\int e^R \hspace{0.17em} d \mu_{\text{prior}}} \hspace{0.27em} \leqslant \hspace{0.27em} \exp \left( c \hspace{0.17em} \pi (R, X, g) - 4 \dim G \hspace{0.17em} \log g \right),\)

where the per-site price is

\(\displaystyle \pi = 2 \hspace{0.17em} \text{exCost} (A, \Lambda, \delta) + 6 + \beta \hspace{0.17em} \text{baseBudget} (A, g) + X + 3 \hspace{0.17em} \text{ent} - 4 \log s, \qquad \text{ent} = \log b - \dim G \hspace{0.17em} \log \sigma_R,\)

with \(\sigma_R = \min (1, \ell_R)\), where \(q_R\) and \(\ell_R\) are the quadratic and linear clip coefficients. Here \(\text{exCost} = \delta + \text{loopsPrice}\) bounds the loop and additive activity terms per site, and \(\text{baseBudget} = 3 (q_R + A^2) g^2\) bounds the remaining action on configurations whose bonds all lie in \(\text{shell} (g / 4)\). (b) Let \((\mathcal{A}_m)\) be the levels of an RG schedule for simple \(G\), and let \(O = (s, b)\) be a Haar budget. For all sufficiently large levels \(m\), every period \(p\), every representation \(P\) on the level-\(m\) torus (with \(c_{m, p}\) sites), every \(k \in \mathbb{N}\), every \(X \leqslant 1 + k\), and every \((g, \Lambda, \delta)\) admissible at level \(m\), the following holds:

\(\displaystyle \pi (P, X, g) - \frac{4 \dim G \hspace{0.17em} \log g}{c_{m, p}} \hspace{0.27em} \leqslant \hspace{0.27em} \text{work item} (O, m, k) .\)

Admissible at level \(m\) means: same clip as the stage of \(m\), \(\beta (P) \leqslant 4 \hat{\beta}_m\), \(\beta (P) g^2 = 1\), \(0 < g \leqslant \text{threshold} (P) \leqslant \text{threshold}_m\), \(\delta \leqslant 1\), \(\Lambda \leqslant \text{loopCap}_m\). The work item is

\(\displaystyle \text{work item} = 2 (8 \hspace{0.17em} \text{paths}_m + 2) + 6 + 3 (\text{direct}_m + \text{paths}_m^2) + (1 + k) + 3 \hspace{0.17em} \text{ent} - 4 \log s + 1.\)

In (b), \(\pi\) uses \(A = \text{paths}_m\) and the clip of stage \(m\). The work item depends on that stage, \(O\) and \(k\), uniformly over the admissible couplings and periods. Status: theorems. Lean: determinant, protection, HaarBudget; (b) affordable, Meets, work item.

Proposition III.11.2 (free pair terms). Fix \(G\), \(d, n \in \mathbb{N}\), and two colored plaquette derivative stencils \(L, S\) based at the origin, with scaling degrees \(\Delta_L = 2 + \text{ord} (L) \leqslant d\) and \(\Delta_S = 2 + \text{ord} (S) \leqslant d\). There is \(C \geqslant 0\), depending only on these data, such that the following holds. Let \(x \in (\mathbb{R}^4)^n\) have distinct entries and let \(i \ne j\). Suppose \(D \geqslant 0\) satisfies \(| \text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} \Gamma_{(i, a), (j, b)} | \leqslant D\) for every pair of free-current keys \(a, b\) of scaling degree at most \(d\). Then, for every \(r \geqslant 1\),

\(\displaystyle r \hspace{0.17em} | r^{\Delta_L + \Delta_S} \hspace{0.17em} \langle \text{ev}_L \otimes \text{ev}_S, B_r (i, j) \rangle - \text{light} (L, S) (x_i, x_j) | \leqslant CD.\)

Here

\(\displaystyle B_r (i, j) = \sum_{a, b} \text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} \Gamma_{(i, a), (j, b)} \hspace{0.27em} r^{- \text{gen} (a)} t_a \otimes r^{- \text{gen} (b)} t_b,\)

where \(t_a\) is the polynomial jet sample of key \(a\), \(\text{gen} (a)\) is its scaling degree, and \(\text{ev}_L\) applies the stencil \(L\). The kernel \(\text{light} (L, S)\) is the corresponding derivative of the free Maxwell curvature two-point function. Status: theorem. Lean: Prayer.fence, deck.

Proposition III.11.3 (window estimate for the free covariance, uniform in the regulator). Let \(\alpha, \eta > 0\), \(\Omega \in \mathbb{R}\) and \(n \in \mathbb{N}\). Then there is \(C \geqslant 0\) with the following property. For every regulator \(f\) in the family \(\text{Firm} (\alpha, \Omega, \eta)\) of III.3, every \(G\), and every pair of colored plaquette stencils \(a, b\), the frequency chart \(K = K_f (a, b)\) is measurable, vanishes for \(| \xi | > 2\), is \(C^n\) away from \(0\), and satisfies:

  • \(|K (0) | \leqslant C\);

  • \(| \partial^i K (\xi) | \hspace{0.17em} | \xi |^i \leqslant C\) for \(0 < | \xi | \leqslant 2\) and \(i \leqslant n\);

  • \(| \partial^i (K - H_{a, b}) (\xi) | \hspace{0.17em} | \xi |^i \leqslant C | \xi |\) for \(0 < | \xi | \leqslant 1\) and \(i \leqslant n\).

Here \(\partial^i\) denotes the \(i\)-th Fréchet derivative, measured in operator norm, and \(H_{a, b}\) is the degree-zero Maxwell curvature symbol for the plaquettes and colors of \(a, b\), with heat parameter \((2 \pi)^2\). Proposition III.11.7 (Ward identity: the flux of the stress against a Killing field is a local field). Let \(C\) satisfy the construction record, let \(f\) be an extraction order with the cell hypothesis and generic point limits \(S\), and suppose \(S\) satisfies the Euclidean axioms with real structure \(H\). Let \(W\) satisfy the derivative laws, and assume the shell estimate of III.11.6. Let:

  • \(\mathcal{J}\) be a stress tensor of \(S\) that is conserved with respect to \(W\), and \(v \in F_d (S)\);

  • \(U\) be a smooth Killing field on \(\mathbb{R}^4\), homogeneous of degree \(p\) (\(U (ry) = r^p U (y)\) for \(r > 0\));

  • \(d, D, p \in \mathbb{N}\) and \(D + 3 + p = d + 4\).

Then there is \(B \in F_D (S)\) with the following property. For every \(n\), all fields \(q_1, \ldots, q_n\), every configuration of distinct points \(x_0, \ldots, x_n\), and every window \(\chi\) adapted to \(x\) (smooth, compactly supported, equal to \(1\) near \(x_0\), with support avoiding \(x_1, \ldots, x_n\)), the flux of \(\mathcal{J}\) against \(U (\cdot - x_0)\) and \(\chi\), inserted with \(v\) at \(x_0\) and \(q_k\) at \(x_k\), equals the correlation of \(B\) at \(x_0\) with the \(q_k\) at \(x_k\). The sign convention is

\(\displaystyle \text{flux} = - \sum_{i, j} \int_{\mathbb{R}^4} \partial_i \chi (y) \hspace{0.17em} U_j (y - x_0) \hspace{0.17em} \langle \mathcal{J}_{ij} (y) \hspace{0.17em} v (x_0) \hspace{0.17em} q_1 (x_1) \cdots q_n (x_n) \rangle \hspace{0.17em} dy.\)

Nonzero homogeneous Killing fields have \(p = 0\) (translations, \(D = d + 1\)) or \(p = 1\) (rotations, \(D = d\)). Status: theorem. Lean: annular_fieldA, tenet, shells_law.

3. Strategy

The Maxwell equations give the upper bound on the interacting field-space dimensions. Corrected observables tested at short distances give the reverse bound: their one- and two-point correlations approach a finite separating family of free correlations, so their classes are eventually independent. The two bounds identify the limit null space with a limit projector kernel. Conjugating a cutoff projector to the limit projector then gives the isomorphism compatible with conjugation and signed permutations.

The quantitative comparison uses the exact RG history to carry reflected products to a coarse torus, then bounds a ratio of Haar integrals. Plaquette elimination gives \(3 c - 3\) numerator factors, while tree gauge leaves \(3 c + 1\) vacuum factors; the remaining coupling cost is only \(g^{- 4 \dim G} = \beta^{2 \dim G}\) in total. Its chessboard root is affordable, allowing connected errors to tend to zero along infinitely many good scales. For the stress tensor, comparison with free Maxwell fixes its normalization along a scaling sequence; conservation, shell estimates and finite-dimensional duality represent each Killing-field flux by a local field.

4. Steps

  1. Haar budget. There are \(0 < s \leqslant 1 \leqslant b\) with \(h (\text{shell} (r / 4)) \geqslant sr^{\dim G}\) for \(0 < r \leqslant 1\) and \(\int e^{- \text{chord} (u) / t} \hspace{0.17em} dh (u) \leqslant bt^{\dim G}\) for \(t > 0\). Exponential coordinates give the local volume estimates; compactness and a shell sum give the integral estimate. integral_budget

  2. Plaquette elimination. For \(n \geqslant 2\) and continuous \(\varphi : G \to [0, 1]\), \(\int \prod_p \varphi (U_p) \hspace{0.17em} d \mu_{\text{prior}} \leqslant (\int \varphi \hspace{0.17em} dh)^{3 c - 3}\). A triangular sweep assigns \(3 c - 3\) plaquettes to distinct bonds, whose successive Haar integrations factor; the unselected factors are at most \(1\). elimination, charged_integral (proof read; sweep count and triangularity statement only)

  3. Vacuum and numerator. Under III.11.1(a), tree gauge and restriction of the \(3 c + 1\) free bonds to \(\text{shell} (g / 4)\) give the vacuum lower bound, while the clipped action retains exponential chord decay for the numerator. Applying Steps 1–2 and dividing gives \(\exp (c \pi) g^{- 4 \dim G}\); the four-factor difference between the counts accounts for the remaining power of \(g\). vacuum_bound, exponent_retains, determinant

  4. Per-site price and chessboard root. Under III.11.1(b), the stage bounds give \(\pi \leqslant \text{work item} - 1\) and \(- 4 \dim G \log g / c_{m, p} \leqslant 1\) eventually, uniformly in admissible parameters and periods. In the trial comparison, the exact vacuum identity and local activity decomposition transfer the determinant estimate to the chessboard root; deleting additive activities costs the \(2 \delta\) already included in the price. Allocation and affordability, with allowance \(e^{2 I}\) and \(I \geqslant \text{work item}\), then give root error \(e^{- I}\) for every nonempty connected part. affordable, s_postage, coupon (proof read; flow identity and cell-stamp bookkeeping statement only)

  5. Free pair and window estimates. In III.11.2, stencil evaluation kills keys below the stencil degree, the equal-degree terms give the free curvature kernel, and every remaining term gains \(r^{- 1}\), giving the bound \(CD / r\) on the scaled remainder. For III.11.3, the regulator chart is a symbol of order zero and differs from the heat symbol by a symbol of order one; the change of variables \(\zeta = 2 \pi \xi\) and the finite number of plaquette pairs give a uniform constant. Prayer.fence, window_prices

  6. Covariance splitting. For an atlas compatible with the schedule and retained unmasked quadratic forms, satisfying symmetry, coercivity, inverse and crossing laws, and a step \(i < N\) with a tree forest and the required torus size, the complete curvature covariances satisfy

    \(\displaystyle \mathcal{C}_{i + 1} - W_i \mathcal{C}_i W_i^{\textsf{T}} = d_f \Gamma_i d_f^{\textsf{T}}, \qquad \Gamma_i \succeq 0.\)

    This is the Schur-complement identity on the curl image; uniform resolvent estimates give the propagation constant for fixed kernel choices and stage indices \(j, t \geqslant 4\) in the range \(d - 1 \leqslant j - 2\) (natural-number subtraction). one_fold, train_issue

  7. Local polynomial comparison. For regular local observables with the prescribed derivative bounds, Taylor expansion in link coordinates gives bounded polynomial coefficients and a remainder of the required order. For zero-mean formal Gaussian data with symmetric covariance vanishing within each location, Wick expansion runs over cross-location pairings without requiring positive covariance; under the comparison's common-mask, support, coefficient, mean and covariance hypotheses, these moments give the corrected connected-component bounds. The allocation, integrability, locality and compatibility hypotheses give exact sector decomposition and factorization over disjoint supports. minted, headed, console, rendered, peer_beams (proof read; local comparison and allocation inputs statement only)

  8. Corrected trials. Under III.11.4, one correction map and polynomial approximations serve all test rows; the free-profile correction cancels the leading terms, and the one-step estimates propagate the remaining source bounds from the middle level to the width level. Steps 3–4 give the purchase bounds, and an inductive choice among the good levels gives increasing depths and widths, decreasing test scales, and bounded real coefficient vectors with the reference unit first. v_paid_factory, v_culminates, v_conditioned, v_obtain_purchases (proof read; analytic inputs and helper estimates statement only)

  9. Supply of good levels. For simple \(G\), the construction record and one-step package with the prescribed weights and multiplicities at least \(1\), a fixed degree-\(d\) column family and stage package with mark exponent \(c \geqslant 2 (w_d + 1)\) satisfy the lantern-mark condition at every sufficiently large metered level. Compatible pre-\(L\) prices and one-step bounds, stage thresholds below block lengths, eventual presence of each degree index, nonnegative mark exponents and a Haar budget give panels and an enlarged waiting window on which every Wilson family paid on that window and every binding with those prices have infinitely many simultaneous good levels for each \(d\). bursts, franchise (proof structure read; analytic inputs and helpers statement only)

  10. Equations and independent columns. Under III.11.5(i), free probes separate the on-shell sector and give \(\dim \mathcal{L}_d \leqslant \dim \ker \Pi_{d, N, p} + \dim F_d^{\text{free}}\) at each cutoff. For a Wilson family satisfying the construction record, with generic point limits and continuous kernels integrable against every separated test function, purchases and iterated coefficient limits give moment errors \(P_k \gamma_k \to 0\), so the limiting corrected columns are independent modulo the integrated-test null space for all sufficiently large trials. Relay estimates put the limit projector kernel inside the null space; with the centred port, raw limits, convergent relay projectors, equation counts and trials in every degree, extraction and the dimension comparison give a smooth table \(T\), its seed, and \(\text{null}_d (T) = \ker \Pi_{\infty}\). savings, measured, arriving_seed (proof read; free positivity, chessboard and analytic inputs statement only)

  11. Equivariant identification. Under the projector presentation and its extraction record, if \(A_i \to \Pi_{\infty}\), then \(Y_i = \Pi_{\infty} A_i + (1 - \Pi_{\infty}) (1 - A_i) \to 1\) and \(Y_i A_i = \Pi_{\infty} Y_i\). For large \(i\), \(Y_i\) is invertible, real and equivariant; composing it with the corrected label map carries \(\mathcal{E}_d\) onto \(\text{null}_d (T)\) and induces III.11.5's quotient isomorphism. drumroll, thrush, Accent.rated

5. Where the difficulty lies

The determinant comparison must avoid a per-site loss of \(\log \beta\): that would overwhelm the vanishing chessboard error. Tree-gauge counting leaves only the volume-independent factor \(\beta^{2 \dim G}\); this is a Haar integral estimate rather than a Hessian determinant calculation. The lower rank bound requires short-distance comparison at infinitely many scales, whereas the upper bound is algebraic at every cutoff. The isomorphism comes from conjugating projectors, and its symmetry statement concerns complex representations with compatible conjugations under signed permutations. Constants must be chosen before block lengths and uniformly in the period; eventual estimates require the specified delays before use on comparison windows.
