Conventions (read off from the Lean). One RG step goes from a fine lattice (side $f$, "Site f") to a coarse lattice (side $c$) with ratio $L$ and a block-spin kernel $S$ (a "step datum"). A coarse field is a vector $x\in\mathfrak g^{\text{coarse bonds}}$. The *background field* is the fine field $U$ that minimizes the effective action among fine fields with block average $x$. The Lean writes the fine field as $U=\exp(a)\cdot\text{replica}(x)$, where $a$ lives on the non-tree fine bonds of a spanning forest $H$ (a "gauge-fixing tree"); this chart is called the axis chart. All statements are about germs at $x=0$ (real-analytic functions near the identity). Quantities named "price", "bill", "work item", "cable" and so on are numerical bounds and certificates (glossary).

## 1. What this unit proves

The unit supplies the *classical* part of every RG step. For a given coarse field $x$ it constructs the minimizing fine field $\rho(x)$ (the background field), its Hessian, and the analytic and quantitative control of both, in forms that the matching of the effective action (III.7), the irrelevant-term bounds (III.10) and the marginal flow (III.9) consume. It does not itself compute the effective action or the one-loop shift. Notation for the tested action: for a coarse field $x$ and a fine chart variable $a$,
$$\mathcal T_k(x,a)=\mathrm{main}(\exp(a)\,\mathrm{replica}(x))+\mathrm{noise}_k(x,a),$$
where "main" is the Taylor-model action (quadratic Wilson part, cubic and quartic atoms, via a path datum $W$ and clip $p$), averaged over the lattice symmetries, and "noise" is the quadratic penalty $\kappa_{\rm pen}\sum_e \mathrm{price}(x_e\,\mathrm{pred}_k(U)_e^{-1})$ tying the fine field to the block-spin predictor, for each choice $k$ of tags of the kernel.

**Proposition III.6.1 (classical Hessian on the tree slice; F03).** Let $G$ be a gauge group, $S$ a step datum that is "larger" ($S$ branches, the macro-radius is at most $f/100$, and $c>1$), $H$ a forest, $B$ a metric bed with positivity floor $(a,A)$ and the symmetry condition, and $W$ a path datum with bounds $(p,\sigma,C,\Lambda)$ for which the main term is analytic (the hypothesis called *Fires*, see III.6.2). Then for every tag $k$, $\mathcal T_k$ is real-analytic at $0$, with $\mathcal T_k(0)=0$, $D\mathcal T_k(0)=0$, and its Hessian at $0$ is
$$D^2\mathcal T_k(0)[(x,a),(x',a')]=\langle \mathrm{stretch}(x,a),\,\mathrm{stretch}(x',a')\rangle_B+\kappa_{\rm pen}\sum_{b}\langle \mathrm{crop}(x,a)_b,\mathrm{crop}(x',a')_b\rangle,$$
where $\mathrm{stretch}(x,a)=\text{linearized replica of }x+a$ is the fine field of the linearization, $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_B$ is the bilinear form of the metric bed $B$ composed with the fine coboundary (so the form is $\sum|d\,\cdot|^2$ in the metric), and $\mathrm{crop}=x-\mathrm{predictor}(\mathrm{stretch})$.
*Status:* theorem, given the hypotheses *Fires*, floor, "attached" and "sworn" (symmetry) of the bed. Its Lean statement is the second conjunct of F03, [`Bid.Comptroller`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Bid/Station.lean#L42-L45), together with analyticity, [`Bid.Prismatic`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Bid/Station.lean#L47).
*Lean:* [`Bid.F03`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Bid/Station.lean#L78-L81), proved by [`Work.F03.answer`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Work/F03.lean#L159-L175).

**Proposition III.6.2 (analyticity of the uncut main term; F02).** If the path datum $W$ has bounds $(c,\sigma,C,\Lambda)$ then, along any real-analytic family of configurations with values near the identity, the main term $x\mapsto \mathrm{main}_c(W;A(x))$ is real-analytic. **Proposition III.6.4 (existence and shape of the critical branch; F04).** Under the hypotheses of III.6.1 and III.6.3 (floor for the target bed $C$, towers diagonal for the pair $(B,C)$), there is a real-analytic map $\rho:x\mapsto\rho(x)\in\text{axis chart}$ defined near $0$, with $\rho(0)=0$, such that

## 3. Strategy

The effective action at level $m$ is obtained by integrating out the fine field at fixed block average. Balaban's method: write the fine field as the *background* (the minimizer of the classical action under the constraint) plus a fluctuation, expand around the background, and treat the Gaussian fluctuation integral with the determinant of the Hessian. This unit supplies the background and the Hessian. The Lean never works with the minimization problem itself. It works with the *tested* action $\mathcal T_k(x,a)$ in coordinates $(x,a)$, where $a$ is a Lie-algebra chart of the non-tree bonds, and obtains the background as the analytic implicit function $a=\rho(x)$ of $D_a\mathcal T_k(x,a)=0$ near $(0,0)$.

The argument has three layers. (i) *Local analytic layer* (F02, F03, F04). The tested action is analytic at the origin, it is critical there, and its second jet is explicit: a metric form of the fine coboundary plus a penalty. A positive gap on the vertical directions (F01) makes the vertical Hessian invertible, so the implicit function theorem gives $\rho$, and differentiating the critical equation along $x$ identifies $D\rho(0)$ with the explicit "relative" linear map. The classical value along the branch then has Hessian $\langle x,x\rangle_C$ by the chain rule at a critical point. (ii) *Independence of gauge* (F05). Two trees give two charts of the same slice; the branches correspond under the chart change, the values agree, and the thermal function (value of the loop part, plus half the log-det, minus the log of the Haar density factors) is the same, because the Jacobian squared in the Hessian determinant cancels the Haar ratio exactly. (iii) *Quantitative layer* (STATIONARY_UNIFORM, JET_PORT, PRIMARY_INTEGRAL, ITERATION). The same objects are shown analytic on a corridor whose radius is uniform in the volume, with localized derivative bounds of every order (so that the later cluster expansion sees locality), and are plugged into the iteration over levels.

What differs from Balaban's treatment is the form of the statements. Balaban constructs the minimizer over an explicit domain of "regular" configurations and proves its derivative bounds by direct estimates. Here everything is a *germ* statement at the identity with explicit jets, supplemented by a uniform-radius implicit function theorem and a "pre-$L$ price list". The localization of the inverse Hessian is proved by a Combes–Thomas-type argument in jet form (`inverse_lines`; (probable)).

### 4.1 The Taylor jet of the tested action (F02, F03)

**Lemma 4.1 (analyticity of the Taylor-model main term; F02).** For a path datum with bounds $(\sigma,C,\Lambda)$, each term $A\mapsto\sum_i\text{atom}_i(A)$ (loop, cubic or quartic, summed over sites) extends to a holomorphic function on a complex polydisc of radius depending only on $\sigma$ in the ambient matrix space, because the sum of the budgeted atoms converges uniformly there; hence the main term is real-analytic along real-analytic families.

## 5. Where the difficulty lies

- **Routine in principle, long in practice.** The existence of the background field is an implicit function theorem with a positive Hessian. The mathematical content is in the positivity: that the Hessian of the *constrained* problem is bounded below uniformly in volume and in the tree (F01 plus the beds of F06–F09). The unit proves this with an explicit constant by deriving, from the shell estimate E02, that the vertical fine field is controlled by the curvature in the macro-cube plus the block-average residue. This is Balaban's coercivity of the background-field problem, in a form where only $O(1)$ lattice cubes enter.
- **Gauge fixing by trees.** Balaban fixes a lattice axial gauge and works with the variational problem on a space of regular fields. Here the gauge is a spanning forest, the unknowns are the non-tree links, and independence of the choice (F05) needs the exact cancellation of the squared Jacobian against the Haar ratio. The cancellation uses that $\mathrm{prob\mathrm{Haar}}$ is the push-forward of a density in the exponential chart ([`LocalRatio`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/PCalc/Loop.lean#L28-L38)). A written proof should isolate this identity.
- **Everything is a germ at the identity.** The branch is constructed near $x=0$ with a neighborhood that is a priori not uniform. Uniformity is a separate layer (4.13–4.15) with its own long proofs. The F04 file says so explicitly: "No uniform control of the neighborhood, determinant result or independence from tree." A reader should not take III.6.4 alone as the statement used downstream.
- **Hypotheses in the statements.** Nearly every statement is conditional on a bundle of predicates (floor, attached, sworn, towers diagonal, price gap, *Fires*, `Rates`). The unit proves the implications; the content of the hypotheses is established in III.1, III.3, III.10 and in F06–F09. Care is needed in the written proof to track which bed and which tree each hypothesis refers to.
- **Weaker than expected: only real analyticity at the identity, no global minimizer.** The branch is not shown to be the global minimizer of the constrained action. What III.7, III.9 and III.10 use is that it is a critical point near the identity in the small-field region, with a positive Hessian; large fields are treated by the large-field cut-offs elsewhere ((probable); where the large-field region is excluded has not been checked).
- **The thermal function omits the one-loop beta function.** `FiniteBound` and `thermal` define the object whose second jet is compared with the prescribed form $\alpha\cdot\text{second term}+\text{loop}$; the module header of `PCalc/Loop.lean` says that this "is **not** a proof of any beta reset" and lists the blockers (on-shell versus off-shell, normalisation, ghosts, divergences). The value of $\alpha$ is therefore not computed here; see §7.
