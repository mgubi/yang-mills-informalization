## 1. What this unit proves

The unit has three parts.

1. **The free Maxwell theory as a table.** It constructs the free Maxwell theory with $\dim\mathfrak g$ colours as a table of the same type as the interacting limit table of IV.1 and proves its Euclidean axioms. It equips the table with derivatives, an $O(4)$ action and the Maxwell stress tensor, and proves the local Ward identities of that tensor. It shows that, up to a scalar, the Maxwell stress is the only centred conserved tensor of its symmetry type, and it computes the Ward constant. These data make up the Maxwell data `Org.MaxwellData`.
2. **A conserved stress on the limit table.** Given a weak short-distance comparison between the Wilson limit table $S$ and the free table, it constructs scale-dependent isomorphisms between their field spaces. It proves that the conserved centred stress tensors of $S$ form a line, and it selects a nonzero conserved $J$ on $S$ whose rescaled image converges to the free stress. III.11 assembles `Org.IR` and `Org.UV` from these outputs.
3. **Short-distance limit of the normalized stress.** Every stress tensor of $S$ normalized by the translation Ward identity has $-T^{\rm free}$ as its short-distance limit, with no further renormalization. All mixed correlations containing such insertions converge, with all derivatives.

*Notation.* $G$ is the gauge group and $\mathfrak g$ its Lie algebra. Colours $c=1,\dots,\dim\mathfrak g$ refer to an orthonormal basis for an $\mathrm{Ad}$-invariant inner product.

*Maxwell waves and the polynomial algebra.*
- A *Maxwell wave* on an open set $U\subset\mathbb R^4$ is a smooth $\mathfrak g$-valued 2-form $f=(f_{ab})$ with $df=0$ and $d^*f=0$, that is, $\partial_af_{bc}+\partial_bf_{ca}+\partial_cf_{ab}=0$ and $\sum_a\partial_af_{ab}=0$.
- $\mathrm{Sh}_j$ is the finite-dimensional space of $j$-jets at a point of Maxwell waves.
- $\mathcal W$ is the algebra of complex polynomials in linear coordinates on $\bigoplus_j\mathrm{Sh}_j$. A coordinate of $\mathrm{Sh}_j$ has weight $j+2$.
- The *shell sector* $\mathcal P_d\subset\mathcal W$ is the image of the gauge-invariant labels of degree $d$ under substitution of on-shell jets. Equivalently, $\mathcal P_d$ consists of the $\mathrm{Ad}$-invariant polynomials of weight $\le d$.
- A *probe* of order $j$ is a linear form $p$ on $\mathrm{Sh}_j$. For a wave $f$, $p(f)(x)$ denotes $p$ applied to the $j$-jet of $f$ at $x$.

*Propagator and Wick moments.*
- The propagator is the curvature two-point function $\langle F^c_{im}(x)F^{c'}_{ab}(y)\rangle_0=\delta^{cc'}\mathcal C_{im,ab}(x-y)$. Here $\mathcal C$ is the curl–curl of $G_0(x)=(4\pi^2|x|^2)^{-1}$, which satisfies $-\Delta G_0=\delta$.
- $f^{p,x}(y)=\langle p(F)(x)\,F(y)\rangle_0$ is the field generated by $p$ at $x$. It is a Maxwell wave on $\mathbb R^4\setminus\{x\}$.
- The Wick moment $\langle p_1(x_1)\cdots p_n(x_n)\rangle_0$ of polynomials at distinct points is a sum over monomials and over complete pairings of their legs *between different sites*, of products of propagators. These are Wick-ordered products (Glimm–Jaffe ch. 6).

*Stress tensor, current and flux.*
- The free Maxwell stress is
$$T^{\rm free}_{ab}=\sum_{c,i}F^c_{ai}F^c_{bi}-\tfrac14\,\delta_{ab}\sum_{c,i,k}F^c_{ik}F^c_{ik}\ \in\mathcal P_4 .$$
It is symmetric and traceless.
- Its polarization $\Theta_{ab}(f,g)=\sum_c(\langle f_{ac},g_{bc}\rangle+\langle f_{bc},g_{ac}\rangle)-\tfrac12\delta_{ab}\sum_{i,k}\langle f_{ik},g_{ik}\rangle$ satisfies $\Theta(f,f)=2\,T^{\rm free}(f)$.
- For a vector field $v$, the current is $j^v_a(f,g)=\sum_bv_b\Theta_{ab}(f,g)$.
- For $\chi\in C_c^\infty$, the outward flux of a current is $\Phi_\chi(j)=-\sum_a\int\partial_a\chi\,j_a$.

*Tables.* Field spaces of a table are written $\mathcal F_d$ (IV.1). Those of the free table $\mathcal M=\mathrm{Bel}(L)$ are $\mathcal F^{\rm free}_d$, and those of the Wilson limit table $S$ are $\mathcal F^S_d$.

**Proposition VI.2.1 (the free Maxwell table).** Let $L$ be a register and $\mathcal M=\mathrm{Bel}(L)$ the free table on its frames.
(a) *Field spaces.* For every $d$, substitution of on-shell jets induces a linear isomorphism $\iota_d:\mathcal F^{\rm free}_d\to\mathcal P_d$. So a label is null for $\mathcal M$ exactly when its polynomial vanishes on all Maxwell jets. Correlations are Wick moments: $\langle v_1(x_1)\cdots v_n(x_n)\rangle^{\rm free}=\langle\iota v_1(x_1)\cdots\iota v_n(x_n)\rangle_0$.
(b) *Euclidean axioms.* $\mathcal M$ satisfies the axioms `Table.Hierarchy` of IV.1:
- symmetry and unit laws;
- covariance under signed permutations of the axes, and reality;
- translation invariance;
- reflection positivity in the coordinate hyperplanes, for point configurations and for smeared tests;
- smoothness (indeed real-analyticity) on $\mathrm{Conf}_n$;
- polynomial caps: correlations and their derivatives are $O(r^{-A})$ on configurations with mutual sup-distance $\ge r$, $0<r\le1$.

(c) *Derivatives and rotations.* The derivation of $\mathcal W$ that raises the jet order in direction $a$ defines a derivative plan $\partial^{\rm free}_a:\mathcal F^{\rm free}_d\to\mathcal F^{\rm free}_{d+1}$. It satisfies the inclusion law, the orientation law, and the derivative law $\langle(\partial_aB)(x_0)\cdots\rangle=\partial_{x_0^a}\langle B(x_0)\cdots\rangle$. The action of $O(4)$ on jets defines a rotation action $g\mapsto R_g$ on each $\mathcal F^{\rm free}_d$, with generators $\rho(\omega)$, $\omega\in\mathfrak{so}(4)$. It satisfies the rotation laws of VI.1: continuity, the group law, extension of the signed permutations, reality, fixing the unit, compatibility with inclusions, $\partial$ transforming as a vector, and $\rho$ being the generator. All free correlations are $O(4)$-invariant.
(d) *Stress tensor.* $T^{\rm free}_{ab}:=\iota_4^{-1}(T^{\rm free}_{ab})$ is symmetric and real, and it is conserved: $\sum_a\partial^{\rm free}_aT^{\rm free}_{ab}=0$ in $\mathcal F^{\rm free}_5$.

**Proposition VI.2.2 (local Ward charge).** Let $U\subset\mathbb R^4$ be open and $f$ a Maxwell wave on $U$. Let $v$ be a smooth conformal Killing field: $\partial_av_b=-\partial_bv_a$ for $a\ne b$, and $\partial_1v_1=\dots=\partial_4v_4$. This class contains translations, rotations, the dilation and the special conformal fields. Let $\chi\in C_c^\infty(U)$ with $\chi\equiv1$ near $x$. Write $\mathcal L_vf=d(\iota_vf)$, that is $(\mathcal L_vf)_{il}=\partial_i(v_bf_{bl})-\partial_l(v_bf_{bi})$; this is the Lie derivative, since $df=0$.
(a) For the zero-order probe $F^c_{il}$: $\Phi_\chi\big(j^v(f^{F^c_{il},x},f)\big)=-(\mathcal L_vf)^c_{il}(x)$.
(b) If $\mathcal L_vf$ is also a Maxwell wave on $U$, then $\Phi_\chi(j^v(f^{p,x},f))=-p(\mathcal L_vf)(x)$ for every probe $p$ of every order $j$. The right side equals:
- $-\partial_a[p(f)](x)$ for $v=e_a$;
- $-(\omega\cdot p)(f)(x)$ for $v=\omega(y-x)$, where $\omega\cdot p$ is the induced action on probes;
- $-(j+2)\,p(f)(x)$ for $v=y-x$.

(c) If $f,g$ are Maxwell waves near $\mathrm{supp}\,\chi$, then $\Phi_\chi(j^v(f,g))=0$. If $f=f^{p,x}$ and $g=f^{q,x}$ are both generated at $x$, and $v$ is homogeneous of degree $e\le1$ about $x$, then also $\Phi_\chi(j^v(f,g))=0$.

**Proposition VI.2.3 (Ward identities of the free table).** Let $x\in\mathrm{Conf}_{n+1}$ and $p_0,\dots,p_n\in\mathcal W$. Let $\chi$ be a *window* for $x$: smooth, compactly supported, $\equiv1$ near $x_0$, with $x_1,\dots,x_n\notin\mathrm{supp}\,\chi$. Let $(v,D)$ be one of the following three pairs:
- $(e_a,\partial_a)$;
- $(\omega(y-x_0),\rho(\omega))$;
- $(y-x_0,\varepsilon)$, where $\varepsilon$ multiplies a monomial by its weight.

Then
$$-\sum_{a,b}\int\partial_a\chi(y)\,v_b(y)\,\langle T^{\rm free}_{ab}(y)\,p_0(x_0)\cdots p_n(x_n)\rangle_0\,dy=-\big\langle(Dp_0)(x_0)\,p_1(x_1)\cdots p_n(x_n)\big\rangle_0 .$$
Consequently, for every real $\tau$, $J=\tau T^{\rm free}$ satisfies the translation and rotation Ward identities `StressLaws` of IV.1, with derivative rows $-\tau\partial^{\rm free}$ and rotation rows $-\tau\rho$. In the normalization of the assessment's equation (13), where the flux equals $+\partial$, the free stress tensor is therefore $-T^{\rm free}$.

**Proposition VI.2.5 (Maxwell data).** Let $G$ be simple, $C$ a Wilson family for $G$, and $\tau\ne0$ real.
(a) The lighthouse $\mathcal T_\tau=(\partial^{\rm free},R,\tau T^{\rm free},\kappa=-\tau)$ has all the properties `Lighthouse.Goes` required by VI.1:
- $\partial^{\rm free}$ satisfies the inclusion, derivative and orientation laws;
- $R$ satisfies the rotation laws and leaves correlations invariant;
- $(\tau T^{\rm free},\kappa\partial^{\rm free},\kappa\rho)$ satisfies `StressLaws`;
- $\partial^{\rm free}\ne0$ on $\mathcal F^{\rm free}_4$;
- *selection*: there is $N$ such that for every $d\ge N$ a finite family of "Ward diagrams" detects the derivative. The diagrams are derivatives, at collinear configurations, of the sums $\sum_k\langle w_1\cdots(t_bw_k)\cdots w_n\rangle$. They detect $\partial$ in this sense: among vector maps $t=(t_a:\mathcal F^{\rm free}_d\to\mathcal F^{\rm free}_{d+1})$ that kill the unit, respect inclusions, and are real and signed-covariant, all diagrams vanish exactly on the real multiples of $\partial^{\rm free}$.

(b) There is a Maxwell datum $M\in$ `Org.MaxwellData`$(C)$ with lighthouse $\mathcal T_\tau$. Besides the free hierarchy, it contains three items:
- a *vacuum*: a representation of the Lorentz group on each $\mathcal F^{\rm free}_d$ and on their union, whose boost generators are $-i\rho$ in the planes $(0,i)$ and whose spatial rotations agree with $R$;
- the Casimir polynomial data of VI.1;
- the *finite Ward test* `erases`. For every $d\ge4$, let $T(u,v)$, with $u,v\in\mathfrak{so}(4)$, be a real-bilinear family of endomorphisms of $\mathcal F^{\rm free}_d$. Suppose it is equivariant under orientation-preserving signed permutations, real and alternating, kills the unit, preserves the images of all $\mathcal F^{\rm free}_k$, $k\le d$, and makes every Ward sum $\sum_i\langle w_1(x_1)\cdots(T(u,v)w_i)(x_i)\cdots w_m(x_m)\rangle$ vanish on $\mathrm{Conf}_m$. Then $T=0$.

(c) *Canonical form.* Let $M$ be any Maxwell datum and $\tau\ne0$. Then there is a Maxwell datum $M'$ with the same derivative plan, rotation action, hierarchy and vacuum; its stress is $\tau T^{\rm free}$, it is $R$-tensorial, it satisfies (FD), and its Ward constant is $\kappa'=-\tau$. Conversely, the Ward constant of any Maxwell datum whose stress is $\tau T^{\rm free}$ is $-\tau$.

## 3. Strategy

*Free half.* The free table is given an explicit model. Its fields are $\mathrm{Ad}$-invariant polynomials in the jets of a $\mathfrak g$-valued Maxwell field, and its correlations are Wick moments of the curvature propagator. Most of the Euclidean axioms then follow from the structure of the propagator: it is translation- and $O(4)$-invariant, real, real-analytic off the diagonal, and homogeneous with harmonic leg kernels. Positivity is the one exception, and it comes from a Fock-space identity. The reflected two-point function of two Wick polynomials at mirror points equals the Fock inner product of the polynomials after a linear change of variables. This change of variables is given by a square root of the positive-definite reflected Gram matrix of coordinate probes. This gives strict positivity, reflection positivity, and the non-degeneracy that identifies $\mathcal F^{\rm free}_d$ with $\mathcal P_d$.

The local Ward charge is a classical computation, done with a regulated source and a limit. The current $j^v=\iota_v\Theta$ of a conformal Killing field is divergence-free between waves. For a smeared curl source at $x$, its divergence is a derivative of an approximate identity. Integrating by parts and removing the regulator gives $-(\mathcal L_vf)(x)$. Differentiating in the source point extends this to jets of every order. Wick's theorem then reduces the Ward identity for arbitrary polynomials to three kinds of single contraction:
- with both stress legs at $x_0$: this vanishes by a scaling argument;
- with one leg at $x_0$ and one outside the window: this gives the derivation $D$ acting on the leg at $x_0$;
- with both legs outside the window: this vanishes because the current is source-free there.

Uniqueness of the stress is a finite-dimensional computation. The symmetric, real, signed-covariant, centred tensors in $\mathcal F^{\rm free}_4$ form a 3-dimensional space, and the admissible vectors in $\mathcal F^{\rm free}_5$ a 2-dimensional one. Explicit two-point tests show that the divergence is onto, so its kernel is a line, spanned by $T^{\rm free}$. The Ward constant is read off a single translation identity: $\kappa=-\tau$.

*Comparison half.* The hypothesis `Stations` provides, at arbitrarily small scales $r$, lifts of free labels to lattice labels whose rescaled smeared Wilson correlations are $\varepsilon$-close to the free ones. Extracting limits gives panels $U_k:\mathcal F^{\rm free}\to\mathcal F^S$ along a sequence $r_k\downarrow0$, approaching weakly. Two steps make these panels exact isomorphisms: they are corrected to respect inclusions of degrees, and the upper bound $\dim\mathcal F^S_d\le\dim\mathcal F^{\rm free}_d$ is used. Weak convergence then upgrades to $C^\infty_{\rm loc}$ convergence. The rescaled derivative $r_kU_k^{-1}\partial^SU_k$ converges to $\partial^{\rm free}$, because finitely many tests separate $\mathcal F^{\rm free}$ and integration by parts transfers derivatives to tests. Gradients of rescaled correlations are again rescaled correlations.

The divergence of $S$, transported by $U_k$ and recentred, converges to the free divergence. Surjectivity is an open condition, so $\mathrm{div}^S$ is onto, and rank–nullity gives a one-dimensional kernel. A nonzero element $B$ of this kernel is rescaled by $\xi_k=\|U_k^{-1}(r_k^4B)\|/\|\mathcal T.J\|$. Compactness of the sphere gives a convergent subsequence, whose limit is a centred conserved free tensor of norm $\|\mathcal T.J\|$, hence $\pm\mathcal T.J$. Changing the sign of $B$ if needed gives the current $J$.

*Normalization.* The stress on $S$ produced above has an unknown normalization $\xi_k$. A stress normalized by the Ward identity on $S$ is proportional to it, $cJ_{\rm phys}=I.J$, and comparing Ward identities along tracks gives $c/\xi_k\to\kappa$. Hence the normalized stress converges to $\kappa^{-1}\mathcal T.J=-T^{\rm free}$ without any field renormalization.

*Relation to standard arguments.* This is the route of §7.1 of the assessment: "the stress tensor is the unique centred symmetric conserved tensor in $\mathcal F_4$, normalized by the Ward identity". It differs from a perturbative construction in that the stress tensor of $S$ is not built as a renormalized composite operator (no point splitting, no counterterms). Instead it is obtained by linear algebra in the finite-dimensional space $\mathcal F^S_4$. Its existence and uniqueness are transferred from the free theory by an openness argument, and its normalization is fixed afterwards by the Ward identity.

**Lemma VI.2.d (derivatives, rotations, conserved stress; VI.2.1(c),(d)).**
*Derivatives.* $\partial_a$ is the derivation of $\mathcal W$ sending a coordinate of $\mathrm{Sh}_j$ to the corresponding coordinate of $\mathrm{Sh}_{j+1}$ in direction $a$ (`glide`). *Rotations.* $R_g$ is the natural action on jets and $\rho$ the corresponding derivation (`wheel`). Wick moments are $O(4)$-invariant, and the rotation laws follow from invariance (VI.1, `Work.A14`).
*Conservation of $T^{\rm free}$.*

2. Evaluate on the jets of the wave $f=A_0+A_1$, where $A_0,A_1$ are homogeneous solutions of degrees 0 and 1 with prescribed jets. This gives $\sum_a\partial_aT^{\rm free}_{ab}(f)(0)=\tfrac12\sum_a\partial_a\Theta_{ab}(f,f)(0)$.
3. By the classical identity (`div_cross`), this equals $\sum_c\langle(d^*f)_c,f_{bc}\rangle=0$.

Symmetry and reality are checked in the same way.

## 5. Where the difficulty lies

*Routine but long.* Most of the 8,200 lines of `Next/MAXWELL` are explicit algebra of Wick polynomials, jets and probes. The analytic content is classical: the regulated-source computation of the Ward charge, differentiation under the integral, and the scaling argument for coincident sources. The stress certificate is a handful of explicit two-point computations followed by linear algebra.

*Where the mathematics is.*
1. **The transfer of the stress line to $S$** (VI.2.u, VI.2.v). The interacting stress tensor is not constructed as a renormalized composite field. Its existence and uniqueness come from:
   - surjectivity being an open condition;
   - exact isomorphisms $U_k$ (hence `DimAbove`);
   - the upgrade from weak convergence to convergence of the rescaled derivative.

   The upgrade rests on the derivative law on $S$ and on finite faithfulness of tests.
2. **The normalization** (VI.2.n, VI.2.x). The sign and size of the Ward constant are fixed in the free theory, $\kappa=-\tau$. The ratio $c/\xi_k\to\kappa$ shows that the normalized physical stress needs no rescaling and converges to $-T^{\rm free}$.
3. **The rigidity lemmas for the Maxwell data** (VI.2.o, VI.2.p): the ghost detection, the selection of the derivative, and the finite Ward test. They are not on the textbook route. They are algebraic uniqueness statements about the free table, needed by VI.1 for the bracket relations and the Casimir selection.

*What is notable.*
- The comparison needs only *upper* bounds on $\dim\mathcal F^S_d$ and order-zero weak estimates. No renormalization of the stress enters, and the normalization $\xi_k$ produced by the compactness argument turns out to converge (VI.2.x).
- The stress space $\mathcal Z$ is defined with the hyperoctahedral group (signed permutations) only. It is therefore 3-dimensional, and conservation cuts it to a line.
- `Killing` admits all conformal Killing fields, but only translations, rotations and the dilation are used.
- Several docstrings ("Not proven", "Production outstanding", "its production is a physical hypothesis") predate the closure and are outdated. `Org.MaxwellData`, `Stations` and `Granular` are all produced in the used closure.
- `current_ready`, `foundation`, `Probed` and `shells_operate` (UV_STRESS/Foundation and Annulus) are not used by the main theorem. III.11's `readyA` and `emanate`, with the shell budget `Aroma`, replace them.
