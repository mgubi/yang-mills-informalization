## 1. What this unit proves

The unit does two things. First, it builds the free Maxwell theory (the gauge-invariant Wick polynomials in the curvature $F$ of a free field with values in $\mathfrak g$, $\dim\mathfrak g=\dim G$) as an object of the same type as the interacting limit table of IV.1. It constructs its Euclidean hierarchy, its rotation action, its conserved stress tensor and the local Ward identity for it. It also proves that the stress tensor is determined up to a scalar by symmetry and conservation. Second, it takes the weak short-distance comparison between the Wilson limit and the free model (a hypothesis called `Stations`) and produces from it a nonzero conserved stress tensor on the limit table, with a coherent scaling along one curve. The output is packaged in the structures `Org.MaxwellData`, `Org.IR` and `Org.UV`. These are the interface to VI.1, VII.1, VII.2 and VII.3.

Notation. $\mathrm{Bel}$ is the free Maxwell table: for each degree $d$ a field space $\mathcal F^{\rm free}_d$ identified with the invariant polynomials of weight $\le d$ in the jets of a solution of the source-free Maxwell equations (the "shell sector"). $\mathrm{Gauge}$ has $G$ compact, and "simple" means $G$ simple. $T^{\rm free}_{ab}$ is the symmetric traceless Maxwell stress, $T_{ab}=\sum_{c}(F_{ac}\!\cdot\!F_{bc}+F_{bc}\!\cdot\!F_{ac})-\tfrac12\delta_{ab}\sum_{ij}F_{ij}\!\cdot\!F_{ij}$ in the Lean's normalization, in terms of the Lie-algebra-valued curvature.

**Proposition VI.2.1 (the free Maxwell table is a Euclidean hierarchy with a conserved stress tensor).** For every register of frames $F$ on a gauge group $G$, the free table is a `Table.Hierarchy` in the sense of IV.1.3, with these properties.
- Its correlation functions are Wick moments, $\langle\prod_i P_i(x_i)\rangle=\mathrm{moment}(x;P_1,\dots,P_n)$, where $P_i$ are gauge-invariant polynomials in the jets of $F$.
- The correlations are symmetric, translation invariant and smooth off coincidences, and have real spin.
- They are reflection positive for every coordinate axis (Prop. VI.2.6 below).
- They have capped derivatives, because the singularity at coincident points is a finite sum of harmonic multipoles.

The table carries a differential plan $\partial_a$ (the Wick derivation of the jet algebra), a rotation action $K$ of $SO(4)$ with Lie algebra action $\mathrm{lie}$, and a stress tensor $T^{\rm free}$ with values in $\mathcal F^{\rm free}_4$. $T^{\rm free}$ is symmetric, real, and satisfies $\sum_a\partial_aT_{ab}=0$ in $\mathcal F^{\rm free}_5$.

**Proposition VI.2.2 (local Ward charge of the free stress tensor).** Let $U\subset\mathbb R^4$ be open, $f$ a free Maxwell wave on $U$ (Bianchi identity and $\sum_a\partial_af_{ab}=0$ on $U$, with values in $\mathfrak g$). Let $v$ be a Killing drive, i.e. a smooth vector field with $\partial_av_b=-\partial_bv_a$ for $a\ne b$ and $\partial_av_a=\partial_bv_b$ (translations, rotations and the dilation are admitted). Let $\chi\in C_c^\infty(U)$ with $\chi\equiv1$ near $x$. Write $\mathrm{flux}_\chi(j)=-\sum_a\int\partial_a\chi\,j_a$ for the shell flux of a current $j$, and $j(f,g)_a=\sum_bv_b\,T_{ab}(f,g)$ for the bilinear stress current of two fields. For the curvature field $\mathrm{pulse}(\omega_{c,i,l},x)$ created by the unit curl source at $x$ (colour $c$, plane $(i,l)$), the stress flux with one contraction against $f$ equals
$$\mathrm{flux}_\chi\big(j(f,\mathrm{pulse}(\omega_{c,i,l},x))\big)=-\,\delta_v f_{il}^{\,c}(x),$$
where $\delta_vf_{il}=\partial_i(v^bf_{bl})-\partial_l(v^bf_{bi})$ is the variation of the curvature under the drive. More generally, for jet probes $p$ of order $j$ (a point-supported distribution of weight $j$), if the variation $\delta_vf$ is again a wave, then $\mathrm{flux}_\chi(j(f,\mathrm{pulse}(p,x)))=-\,\mathrm{sample}_p(\delta_vf)(x)$.
*Status:* theorem. This is the free Ward charge. The sign includes the $-\Delta G_0=\delta$ normalization, the outward flux and the curl at both ends.

**Proposition VI.2.3 (Ward identity for Wick polynomials).** For arbitrary Wick polynomials $P_0,\dots,P_n$ at distinct points $x_0,\dots,x_n$ and a cutoff $\chi$ equal to 1 near $x_0$ and supported away from the other points, the shell flux of the composite stress insertion satisfies
$$\mathrm{flux}_\chi\big(\langle T^{\rm free}_{a\cdot}(\cdot)\,P_0(x_0)\cdots P_n(x_n)\rangle\big)=-\,\langle(\mathcal L_vP_0)(x_0)\,P_1(x_1)\cdots P_n(x_n)\rangle,$$
where $\mathcal L_v$ is the derivation of the polynomial algebra induced by the drive ("rewiring" by the generator). Together with VI.2.2 this says: the free stress flux generates the translation, rotation and dilation action on all free correlations. This is the free counterpart of the interacting Ward identity (13) of the assessment, $-\sum_\mu\int\partial_\mu\chi\,S_{T^{\mu\nu},v_0,\dots}=\partial_{x_0^\nu}S_{v_0,\dots}$ (proved in VI.1).

**Proposition VI.2.4 (uniqueness of the free stress line).** Let $G$ be simple and let $H$ be the free table. The proof computes the dimension of a three-parameter space of "painted" stresses (`exhaust`) and the dimension of the vector space of conserved-current candidates (at most 2, `capped_v`).

**Proposition VI.2.5 (Maxwell data: the free "lighthouse").** For simple $G$ and every real $\tau\ne0$ (the code allows both signs) there is a record of the following kind (the `herald`):
- a differential plan $\partial$, a rotation action $K$ and a stress tensor $J=\tau\,T^{\rm free}$;
- the Ward constant $\kappa=-\tau$;
- the properties that the plan commutes with inclusions of degrees, that $K$ integrates the spin rows, that $\kappa\,\partial$ and $\kappa\,\mathrm{lie}$ realize the Ward identity ($\mathrm{StressLaws}$), that $\partial_4\neq0$, and that for $d\ge n_0$ a "selection" at level $d$ detects the spin.

The record is completed to full `Org.MaxwellData` on any Wilson family $C$ for the same $G$: it contains also a free vacuum, the Casimir selection polynomial, and the internal finite-Ward test `erases`. For every such Maxwell datum with stress $\tau T^{\rm free}$ the Ward constant is forced: $\kappa=-\tau$ (`required`). Any Maxwell datum can be replaced by a canonical one (torch $J=\tau T^{\rm free}$, same vacuum, window and kernel) which is tensorial and has free divergence ($\mathrm{FreeDiv}$: divergence onto, kernel of dimension 1, $J\ne0$ in the kernel).

**Proposition VI.2.8 (weak comparison stations from a quantitative estimate).** A *station* is a radius $r>0$ together with, at each cutoff $(N,p)$, linear lifts $A_{N,p}:\mathcal F^{\rm free}_d\to\mathrm{Ref}_d$ of free labels to lattice labels of the same Family ($d\le D$). It is *admissible* at (rank $n_{\max}$, window radius $R$, tolerance $\varepsilon$) if:
- the lifts are eventually uniformly bounded, unit-preserving, real and signed-covariant;
- for every $n\le n_{\max}$, every free tag list $v$ and every test $t\in C_c^\infty$ with support in the window of radius $R$ and $|t|\le M$, the dilated lattice smears converge as
$$\limsup_{N\to\infty,\;p\to\infty}\Big|\textstyle\int t(x/r)\,\mathcal C_{N,p}(A v;\,x)\,dx\cdot r^{-4n}-\int t\,\langle v\rangle^{\rm free}\Big|\le\varepsilon\,M\prod_i\|v_i\|$$
(the mesh-before-period upper limit of IV.1).

`Stations` says that for every $(D,n_{\max},R,\varepsilon,\delta)$ an admissible station with $r\le\delta$ exists. The quantitative form (`Granular`) asks for sequences of lifts $W_k$ with radii $r_k\to0$ satisfying the "balance" estimate, a finite-basis, arity-determined cost bound on the cutoff smears; it implies `Stations` (`realizes_stations`, via `supply_wheel` and `cutoff_balance`). From an admissible station one reads a panel $U:\mathcal F^{\rm free}\to\mathcal F^{S}$ (via a finite extraction along the ordered filter of IV.1) that is straight (commutes with conjugation and signed permutations) and approximates the continuum kernels on the tests up to $\varepsilon M\prod\|v_i\|$ (`harvested`).

**Standard mathematics.**
- Wick's theorem for Gaussian fields and Wick polynomials of a free field (Glimm–Jaffe, ch. 6, 8).
- Maxwell theory in $\mathbb R^4$: Green's function $-\Delta G=\delta$, the Maxwell stress as the quadratic form of the field strength, divergence of the stress current, divergence theorem.
- Noether-type local Ward identity: the flux of a conserved current through a shell around a point acts as the infinitesimal symmetry on correlators.
- Reflection positivity of the free field: the reflected Wick score is a sum of squares (Osterwalder–Schrader; Glimm–Jaffe ch. 6).
- Harmonic multipoles: a jet probe of order $j$ gives a harmonic polynomial of degree 4+... in the leg kernel.
- Finite-dimensional linear algebra: surjectivity is stable under small perturbation, and a rank computation fixes the dimension of the kernel.

**Imported constants and assumptions.**
- $G$ simple. This enters several statements (`line_dim`, `exposed`, `erasing`). For non-simple $G$ the one-dimensionality of the stress line may fail (the stress of a product group has several independent pieces), so simplicity is genuinely used. (probable)
- The sign and scale: the code allows any real $\tau\ne0$; the value $\kappa=-\tau$ is forced. For $\tau=1$ one has `torch 1 = original`.
- The `Stations` estimate (VI.2.8) with its quantitative form `Granular`, and the shell budget `Probed`, are hypotheses *of this unit*; they are supplied by other units.

## 3. Strategy

The unit follows the standard idea that at short distances an asymptotically free Yang–Mills theory looks like $\dim G$ independent free Maxwell fields, and that the stress tensor of the interacting theory is, in the scaling limit, the Maxwell stress. The proof has two halves, and the second feeds on the first.

*Free half.* The free table is given an explicit algebraic model: its fields are gauge-invariant polynomials in jets of the source-free curvature, its correlations are Wick moments, and its Euclidean axioms (basic laws, spin, translation, positivity, smoothness, caps) are checked directly (`hierarchy`). The local Ward charge is a classical computation in the Maxwell stress tensor: the current $j_a=\sum_bv_bT_{ab}$ of a Killing drive is divergence-free off the sources, and a regulated point source produces a divergence concentrated at the source. A limit of the regulator gives $-\delta_vf(x)$ (`local_force`). Higher jets and Wick polynomials follow by Leibniz and multilinearity (`jet_force`, `moment_force`). Next, the stress is shown to be unique by a finite-dimensional computation (`line_dim`), and the constant $\kappa=-\tau$ is read off from a single three-point function that is computed explicitly (`negated`).

*Comparison half.* One must transfer this to the interacting table. The hypothesis `Stations` gives, at arbitrarily small $r$, lifts of free labels to lattice labels whose smeared Wilson correlators are close to the free ones. Reading off the limit gives linear panels $U_k:\mathcal F^{\rm free}\to\mathcal F^S$ that commute with conjugation and signed permutations. Since the free divergence map is onto with a one-dimensional kernel, and surjectivity is an open condition, the divergence map on $S$ is also onto for large $k$, hence in the limit; the dimension of its kernel is then fixed by rank–nullity (`differential_rank`). This kernel is the line of stress tensors on $S$. A nonzero element $B$ of it is rescaled so that its image under $U_k^{-1}$ has norm $\|T.J\|$. A compactness argument on the sphere picks a convergent subsequence $\to R$. Since $R$ lies in the one-dimensional kernel in the free table, $R=\pm T.J$, and the sign is absorbed into $B$ (`produce_current`). This is the coherent scaling along one curve, with a normalization that does not vary.

The method is the one in the assessment (§7.1): "the stress tensor is the unique centered symmetric conserved tensor in $\mathcal F_4$". What is different from a standard perturbative argument is that no renormalization of the stress is involved in the proof; the stress on the lattice is the unnormalized object produced by linear algebra from the comparison, and its normalization is fixed afterwards by the Ward identity (VI.1).

## 5. Where the difficulty lies

*Routine but long.* The free half is a long sequence of explicit algebra on Wick polynomials. Most of the 8,000 lines in `Next/MAXWELL` are bookkeeping of jets, probes and the Wick derivations (`Shelves`, `Jets`, `Frenzy`, `Fusion`, ...), with the detection lemmas (`detects`, `coordGram`, `exposed`) as the places where something has to be checked.

*Where the mathematics is.* There are three places.
1. The local Ward charge (VI.2.e–f): a single computation that fixes the sign of the Ward identity and shows that the stress tensor in the Lean has the right normalization with respect to the Green's function. Everything later depends on the sign convention $\kappa=-\tau$.
2. The one-dimensionality of the conserved symmetric centered tensors in $\mathcal F_4$ (VI.2.h) and its transfer to the limit table (VI.2.p). The rank argument is elementary, but it is the entire reason why the interacting stress tensor is unique: the paper's "the stress tensor is the unique such tensor" is proved by comparison with the free case, using that surjectivity is an open condition.
3. The finite Ward test `erases` (VI.2.k). It is not in the textbook route: it is an auxiliary algebraic rigidity statement that the bracket relations and the Casimir selection in VI.1 need for the free data.

*What looks unusual.*
- The comparison estimate `Stations` is a physical hypothesis *of this unit*, whose only link to the Wilson construction is through its quantitative form `Granular` or the packaged `Furnace`/`Probed`. The text of the Lean says that this is "input boundary to the stress-grade argument" with "no existence proof for these physical estimates" inside the unit. This is the main thing the writer must trace back to the ultraviolet part (§7).
- The rank argument needs only **upper** bounds on the dimension of the quotient fields (`DimAbove`) and ordinary weak convergence on test functions; it does not need any renormalization of the stress. The price is that the normalization of the stress current is *unknown* (a nonzero scalar $\xi_k$ varying with $k$); it is fixed later by the Ward identity.
- The free stress is shown to be unique **only for simple $G$**.
- Several docstrings are informal and speak of "not proven" or "outstanding", while the corresponding declarations are used in the closure. I took the used-closure report in the seams notes as authoritative.
