## 1. What this unit proves

Throughout, $G$ is a simple compact gauge group, and "the family" is the Wilson family produced by a construction run $R$ (III.14). Its lattice representatives $O_{N,p}(v,x)$ of a field label $v$ at a site $x$ come from II.1 and III.13.

**Definitions used in the statements.**

- A *trial* is a family of functions $F_{N,p}(x)$ on the gauge-field configurations of the lattice of level $N$ and period $p$, indexed by an offset $x$ (a site).
- $F$ is *affordable at cost $u$* if the following holds. $F$ is local, i.e. supported in finitely many cover offsets. For every large block scale $m$, eventually in the mesh (that is, $N\ge N_0(m)$ for all $p$), the observable $F_{N,p}(0)$ is measurable in the cell of side $a_m$ centred at the origin. Its chessboard root $\big|\mathbb E_{\beta_{N,p}}[\prod_{\text{cells}}\text{reflected copies}]\big|^{1/\#\text{cells}}$ is at most $u(m,N,p)$.
- $F$ is *owed at cost $v$* if $u(m,N,p)=v(N,p)$ does not depend on $m$.
- $F$ is *funded* if $u(m,N,p)=u(m)$ depends on $m$ only.
- The $l$-fold *normalized difference* of $F$ along a list $l$ of lattice axes is $F\mapsto a_N^{-1}\big(F(\cdot+e_{\ell})-F\big)$ iterated.

## 3. Strategy

The target is an estimate on a *lattice correlation* of composite observables $O_{N,p}(v,x)$. The estimate is uniform in $p$, and eventually uniform in the cutoff $N$. It has three layers.

1. *A budget for the primary observable.* The renormalization group with a source inserted (the second pass) gives, for a label of degree $d$, a centred chessboard bound of order $a_m^{-d-\varepsilon}$ at every block scale $m$ and every volume. This is `Wcmain`. It is a statement about the observable itself, not about its difference quotients.
2. *Closure under finite differences.* The kernel of the limit theory must be smooth, so one needs the budget for $a_N^{-1}$-normalized differences. These are not directly controlled, because the factor $a_N^{-1}$ diverges. The trick is to compare the difference quotient with another *primary* observable of a higher degree: if the exactly computable equation terms vanish in the Wilson law, the difference of the two is an "owed" trial of tiny cost (the cancellation is inside the Wilson law; the docstring of `Moneys.lean` says an exact equation term has zero root test at these scales). A rank detector (a finite family of tested values) then bounds the part of the label not killed by the projection. Taking the projected part to be bounded and the remaining part to be small gives the budget (III.12.3–III.12.4). Induction on the length of the axis list gives III.12.2 and then, by the chessboard inequality, III.12.1.
3. *Convergence in the mesh.* At a fixed period $p$ the cutoff correlations converge as $N\to\infty$ (III.12.7). The mechanism: the effective actions at a fixed level $j$ for two cutoffs $N<N'$ differ little, because the renormalization-group map contracts the dependence on the early levels. The tail of the telescoping sum over levels $j,\dots,N$ is bounded by a quantity decaying in $j$ (`Sever.echoes`; the factor $F_0/g(j)$ with $g(j)\to\infty$ because $\hat\beta_j\to\infty$). At level $j$ the dependence on $N$ is itself Cauchy (`latter`). The inverse coupling at the lattice end is the output of the reset sequence, so the Cauchy property is not a continuity property in a fixed parameter. This is why the Lean introduces the fan: $\beta_{N,p}$ depends analytically on a complex tuning $s$ in the unit disc (`bareTrial`), and polynomial bounds on the coefficients in $s$ (III.12.6) convert to Cauchy estimates for the dependence on the starting value.

The argument follows the shape of Balaban's treatment (the effective action is controlled uniformly in the volume by a localized estimate at each scale), combined with a chessboard bound in place of Balaban's large-field/small-field bookkeeping of observables. What differs from the textbook route: the observable is *inserted at the start of the construction* (the family `Family` carries its own representatives, normalized by the curvature-jet read-out), so no separate expansion of the observable is needed. The price is that all estimates, including the stationary-branch analysis, are re-proved with the label inserted; this is why the folder is large.

## 4. Steps

### 4.1 Calculus of funded observables

**Lemma 4.1 (closure properties of the budget).** For trials $F,H$: $\mathrm{Affords}(F-H,u+v)$, $\mathrm{Affords}(F+H,u+v)$, $\mathrm{Affords}(cF,|c|u)$, $\mathrm{Affords}(-F,u)$, $\mathrm{Affords}(0,0)$, finite sums; monotone in the cost; unchanged if $F$ is changed on a set of cutoffs outside a mesh-tail.
*Sketch:* measurability in the cell is preserved by linear combination. The root test is a seminorm: it satisfies $\mathrm{root}(F-H)\le\mathrm{root}(F)+\mathrm{root}(H)$, because the chessboard inequality (`Cells.Seam`, reflection positivity) gives a semi-triangle inequality for the geometric mean over reflected cells, and homogeneity ($\mathrm{root}(cF)=|c|\,\mathrm{root}(F)$).

## 5. Where the difficulty lies

- **The difference quotients.** The new idea is not in the estimates but in how the budget is propagated under $a_N^{-1}\Delta$. One does not differentiate the bound. The difference is compared with a *higher-degree primary label*, and the detector (a finite-dimensional linear algebra statement) converts "difference is tiny" into "difference is bounded". This replaces a Taylor expansion of the effective action in the position. The mechanism (4.6–4.9) is clean and short, about 200 lines in the Lean.
- **Everything else is volume.** The bulk, over 15,000 lines, re-proves the one-step estimates with a label inserted and with analytic dependence on a parameter (4.11–4.16). A written proof should state the hypotheses of §4.2 (`Wcmain` with source, owed difference, detector data) as an interface and refer to III.10–III.11 for the source-inserted step, instead of re-deriving them.
- **Why the fan.** The coupling at the lattice end, $\beta_{N,p}$, is *not* a free parameter: it is the output of the reset sequence from the prescribed $\bar\beta$ at the physical end. The Cauchy property in $N$ therefore cannot use continuity in a fixed bare coupling. The Lean makes $\beta_{N,p}$ the unique solution of a "contest" depending analytically on $s$ (`bareTrial`). Polynomial bounds on the $s$-series (III.12.6) then give Cauchy estimates. This device is not in Balaban's papers (probable) and is the least standard part. The uniqueness is only within the fixed recursion (docstring of `Finale/Program.lean`), not among all representations.
- **Quantifiers.** All statements are eventual in $N$ with a threshold that depends on the list of labels, the separation, the radius and $\varepsilon$, but *not* on $p$. The convergence statements (III.12.7–III.12.8) are along the *mesh sequence only after fixing $p$*. There is no claim that is uniform in $p$ for the limits themselves, only for the bounds. This matches the intended infinite-volume argument of IV.1, where the volume subsequence is taken afterwards.
- **Weaker than one might expect.** Convergence is of correlation functions at *non-coincident* points and of their finite differences. No statement is made near collisions, or for unboundedly many labels (the docstring of `Profiles.lean`: "No assertion for unbounded field or test tapes without their own thresholds"). The cost exponent is $-d-\varepsilon$, not $-d$.
- **Hypotheses carried as assumptions.** Most of the lemmas in 4.11–4.16 are stated with large hypothesis lists (`hk`, `he`, `hP`, `Θ`, `v`, `Sav`, ...) that are discharged by the run. A reader who checks only the statements cannot tell what is proved in this unit and what is a hypothesis on the campaign.
