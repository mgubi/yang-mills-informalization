Conventions as in III.9. Levels $m=0,1,\dots,N$ run from the coarsest ($m=0$, physical scale) to the finest ($m=N$, Wilson lattice). A step $m+1\to m$ blocks by an odd factor $L_m$. The inverse coupling is $\beta=1/g^2$ and $\kappa=\frac{11C}{24\pi^2}$ is the one-loop slope ([`Gauge.slopeConstant`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Gauge/Colors.lean#L184-L184)).

This unit proves almost no analysis about gauge fields. It proves that a long list of requests, each of the form "this inequality holds provided the coupling is large enough, the block factor is large enough, or the lattice is large enough", can be met simultaneously by choosing the constants in one fixed order. Throughout, a request is called an "ask", meeting it a "purchase", and a threshold a "price" or "delay". These words are labels only and carry no economic meaning. Here a "price" is a threshold in $\beta$, $L$ or the lattice size. The unit also contains the shooting argument that produces the trajectory of effective actions with $\beta_{N,p,0}=\bar\beta$, together with a collection of analytic estimates that are stated elsewhere and only summarised here (see §5 and §7).

## 1. What this unit proves

**Proposition III.2.1 (the schedule of constants).** Let $G$ be a simple compact gauge group. Fix a *sheet*: a choice of kernel, a sequence of *stage profiles* $j\mapsto X_j$ (order, power, support exponent, width, path count, drift, column count, clipping), a first stage $j_0\ge4$, and *floors* $\mathrm{floor}_j\in\mathbb N$. The order, power and support exponent are monotone in $j$. Then, in the following order, one can choose:

1. block factors $L_j$, odd, with $L_j\ge\max(3,\mathrm{floor}_j)$ and nondecreasing, such that for $j\ge j_0$
 $$\frac{\mathrm{drift}_j}{\log L_j}\le\min\Big(\frac\kappa4,\frac1{j^2}\Big),\qquad \frac{\log\big(1+\mathrm{paths}_j+\mathrm{width}_j^{-1}+\mathrm{columns}_j+\mathrm{relax}_j+\mathrm{supp}_j\big)}{\log L_j}\le\frac1{j^2};$$
2. a decreasing sequence $\theta_j\in(0,1/j]$, which may be taken below any prescribed positive sequence;
3. *delays*: an entry value $\beta^{\rm entry}_j$ for the coupling, an entry value for the scale, and two volume parameters (shells and growth exponent);
4. a barrier $\bar\beta>1$, as large as desired, with $\beta^{\rm entry}_{j_0}\le\bar\beta$ and $t(x)=x^{-1/2}\exp\big((\log\log x)^2\big)\le1$ for all $x\ge\bar\beta$;
5. a *wait*: a readiness scale $\sigma^{\rm ready}_j$, a window function $w_j(s)=o(s)$ and a minimal period length.

*Status:* theorem. Items 1, 2 and 4 are existence lemmas, and 3 and 5 are data that the client chooses, after $\bar\beta$ for the wait. The final order is the one in the Lean's design note: constants before $L$, then $L$, then $\theta$, then delays, then $\bar\beta$, then the wait.

**Proposition III.2.2 (the level schedule exists; key result).** With the data of III.2.1 there is a trajectory of levels (a *calendar* with block sizes $L_{J(m+1)}$, reference couplings $\hat\beta_m=\bar\beta+\kappa s_m$, stage index $J(m)$ and periods) satisfying all requested conditions ("paid"):
- $J(0)=J(1)=j_0$, $J$ is nondecreasing, $J(m+1)\le J(m)+1$, $J\to\infty$;
- $\beta^{\rm entry}_{J(m+1)}\le\hat\beta_m$, and the scale entry value is at most $s_m$ whenever $J(m+1)>j_0$;
- every period is at least the minimal length, and the lattice side at level $m$ is at least $\mathrm{shells}_{j}\,(1+\mathrm{cover}_m+\mathrm{cover}_{m+1})^{\mathrm{growth}_j}$ with $j=J(m+1)$, where $\mathrm{cover}_m=\lceil(\log\hat\beta_m)^2\rceil$;
- for every stage $j\ge j_0$ there are arbitrarily large $k$ such that $J\equiv j$ on a window of length $\pm w_j(s_k)$ around level $k$, with $s_k\ge\sigma^{\rm ready}_j$ (so every stage is held long enough for the finite list of requests that refer to it);
- the calendar satisfies the structural conditions `Fits` of III.9: $\hat\beta_m>1$, $t_m\le1$, patch and cover fit in the lattice, $\theta_{J(m)}\le1/J(m)$, drift and log-cost bounds as in III.2.1, $\log L_m/s_m\le1/J(m+1)$.

*Status:* theorem (existence).

**Proposition III.2.3 (a trajectory following the recipe; key result).** Let $a$ be a level system with $\mathrm{drift}_{m+1}/\log L_m\le\kappa/4$ for all $m$. Let a *recipe* $R$ be given: path data and three predicates that restrict which effective actions are admitted at the start, at each level, and for each pair of consecutive levels. Suppose that for every $N$ and every period $p$ the recipe *issues*:
- (initial) there is a continuous family $F$ of start-up data over $\beta\in[\hat\beta_N/2,2\hat\beta_N]$ with $(F\beta)$ having inverse coupling $\beta$;
- (advance) every continuous family of admissible actions over an interval of $\beta$ at level $m+1$, whose couplings stay in the band, is the image of a continuous family at level $m$ under one step (identity of densities as in III.9.3, and $|\beta_{m+1}-\beta_m-\kappa\log L_m|\le\mathrm{drift}_{m+1}$).

Then there is a family of trajectories $T_{N,p}$ of effective actions, $0\le m\le N$, starting from the Wilson weight at level $N$, with every level in the band $[\hat\beta_m/2,2\hat\beta_m]$, every step admissible, $\beta_{N,p,0}=\bar\beta$, and the path data independent of the period. The family carries the specification `Selected` of III.9.3, in particular the running law: for every $\varepsilon>0$, $|\beta_{N,p,m}-\hat\beta_m|\le\varepsilon s_m$ for all large $m$, uniformly in $N\ge m$ and $p$.

## 2. What it uses

**From other units.**
- The structure of one RG step and its estimator (III.1 block-spin step, III.4 Gaussian integration, III.5 cluster expansion, III.6 stationary branch, III.7 matching, III.10 irrelevant terms). This unit does not prove any estimate of the step. It receives them as the list of *asks* the step needs, and shows that each ask is met beyond a threshold. The Lean packages each ask as a record (a "request", e.g. `Ask`, `Need`, `Expenses`, `Targets`) and has no mathematical content outside its monotonicity in the delays.
- The calendar and the reset of the marginal coupling (III.9), whose `Calendar`, `Levels` and `Selected` are the objects constructed here.
- The free kernels and Gaussian-sector data (III.3, III.11), through the sheet's `kernel` and `Branches`, and the interacting rank budgets (III.11).
- The lattice and Wilson measure (I.1), the lattice representatives (II.1), the norms (II.2).
- The analytic toolkit (III.16): Laurent and strip norms, Banach–Steinhaus, Taylor jets.

**Standard mathematics.** The shooting argument is the intermediate-value theorem for continuous one-parameter families, as in Balaban's treatment of the marginal coupling. The remaining content is real analysis of "eventually" statements (power versus $\log$, $\exp(-c(\log\log x)^2)$ versus any power). The choice of constants in a fixed order is the usual device of Balaban's papers ("choose $L$ large depending on the earlier constants, then $\beta$ large depending on $L$") and of Brydges' lecture notes.

- In III.2.4 and III.2.5 a list of structures (`Draft`, `Tableau`, `sphere`, `Decal`, `Contemple`, `Prediction`/`AllPrices`) that are hypotheses of the theorems. These are supplied by the callers (III.12, III.14, III.15). Whether each downstream request fits the pattern "pre-$L$ constants, then $L$, then post-$L$ delays" is therefore checked where it is made, in the type of the request.

## 3. Strategy

The construction of the effective actions needs, at each step $m+1\to m$, a long list of inequalities of the following kinds. A power of $\beta$ beats a power of $\log\beta$. The small-field threshold $t=\beta^{-1/2}e^{(\log\log\beta)^2}$ is at most 1. The lattice is large compared to the covers and to the patch. The block factor $L$ is large compared with the logarithm of every constant that is paid to the step (so that $L$-dependence is harmless). A constant such as the width or the number of paths depends on the stage but not on $L$, and the $\theta$-exponent of the loop cap depends on the stage but must not depend on later choices. These conditions are mutually dependent, and the only way to see that they can all hold is to order the constants so that each is chosen after everything it depends on and before everything that depends on it.

The order, in the notation of III.2.1, is: *stage profile* (orders, widths, support exponents; "pre-$L$") $\to$ *block factors* $L_j$ (large compared to $\log$ of the profile and to the drift) $\to$ *$\theta_j$* (small compared to $1/j$ and to anything depending on $L$) $\to$ *entries* $\beta_j^{\rm entry}$, volume parameters ("post-$L$") $\to$ *barrier* $\bar\beta$ (above $\beta^{\rm entry}_{j_0}$ and where $t\le1$) $\to$ *wait* (window and minimal period). The remaining freedom is the level schedule. Waits.lean chooses it explicitly: the stage index $J(m)$ stays at stage $j$ until the scale $s_m$ is large enough for stage $j+1$'s entry value, then advances by one, and stays at each stage for an arbitrarily long window, so that every fixed finite request about stage $j$ is evaluated while $J\equiv j$.

The trajectory is then produced by shooting. For each $N$ and period $p$ the construction goes downward from the Wilson end, in a continuous family over the band of $\beta$ at level $N$. At each level the one-step relation moves the family to a continuous family at the coarser level. By the drift bound $|\beta_{m+1}-\beta_m-\kappa\log L_m|\le\mathrm{drift}_{m+1}\le\frac\kappa4\log L_m$, the coupling at level $m$ is strictly below the lower edge of the band at $m+1$ when it started on the lower edge of the band at $m+1$, and strictly above the upper edge when it started on the upper edge (lemma `gates`). So the image of the band at level $N$ crosses the band at each coarser level, and the continuity argument selects the starting $\beta$ such that the endpoint is the prescribed $\bar\beta$ at level 0 ([`successful`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Shoot/Reach.lean) in `Reach.lean`). This is the argument "bare coupling as a function of the cutoff" in Balaban's and Bałaban–Dimock–Brydges language, here carried out as an exact interval shooting rather than a fixed point. (probable; see §7, `Reach.lean` was only read for statements.)

The integration files (`YM/Integration/`) then repeat the same pattern on a large scale: each producer of an estimate downstream is wrapped so that it is bought on the *same* sheet, rolls, protection and delays as the others. This is why the statement III.2.4 has a dozen alternating quantifiers and why the later units can use a single common construction.

## 4. Steps

### 4.1 Numerical existence lemmas (`YM/Price/`)

**Lemma 4.1 (large block factors).** For every stage $i$ there is $M$ such that every odd $n\ge M$ satisfies the largeness conditions of $\mathrm{Large}(i,n)$. Hence a sequence $L_i=2M_i+1$ with $M_i=\max_{j\le i}C_j$ is a monotone choice of rolls.
*Sketch:* each condition is of the form $c/\log n\le\text{const}>0$, true for large $n$ since $\log n\to\infty$; the threshold uses $\kappa>0$; the monotone sequence is the running maximum.
*Lean:* [`Rolls.eventually_large`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Price/Intent.lean#L55-L77), [`Rolls.exists_rolls`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Price/Intent.lean#L79-L84).

**Lemma 4.2 (protection).** For any positive sequence $\alpha_j$ there is a decreasing $\theta_j\le\min(\alpha_j,1/(j+1))$, namely the running minimum.
*Lean:* [`Protection.exists_margin`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Price/Intent.lean#L98-L111).

**Lemma 4.3 (barrier).** For every $b$ there is a barrier $\bar\beta\ge b$, above the first beta-entry, with $\bar\beta>1$ and $x^{-1/2}\exp\big((\log\log x)^2\big)\le1$ for all $x\ge\bar\beta$.
*Sketch:* $(\log\log x)^2\le\frac12\log x$ eventually, because $(\log y)^2=o(y)$.
*Lean:* [`Bar.exists_above`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Price/Length.lean#L19-L37).

**Lemma 4.4 (volume bill).** Define the *bill* of stage $j$ at scale $s$ as the lattice side needed at the output level:
$$\mathrm{bill}_j(s)=\mathrm{shells}_j\,(1+c+c')^{\mathrm{growth}_j}+100(c+1)+100\big((j+3)L_j+1\big),$$
$c=\lceil(\log(\bar\beta+\kappa s))^2\rceil$, $c'=\lceil(\log(\bar\beta+\kappa(s+\log L_j)))^2\rceil$. Then $\mathrm{bill}_j(s)\le e^{s}$ for all large $s$. The bill is polynomial in $\log s$ and $e^s$ is the size of the denominator of the lattice spacing at scale $s$; this is why the volume condition is met at all lattice periods.
*Lean:* [`Bar.bill`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Price/Length.lean#L43-L47), [`Bar.allocated`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Price/Length.lean#L64-L84).

**Lemma 4.5 (entering a stage).** For a stage $j>j_0$ there is $n$ such that for every level $m\ge n$ and every scale $s\ge m\log3$ (the scale grows at least by $\log 3$ per level): $\beta^{\rm entry}_j\le\bar\beta+\kappa s$, scale entry $\le s$, $s>0$, $\log L_j\le s/j$ and $\mathrm{bill}_j(s)\le e^s$.
*Lean:* [`Bar.enters`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Price/Length.lean#L90-L106) (all five conditions are eventual conditions on $s$; take the intersection).

**Lemma 4.6 (waiting windows).** For each stage $j$ the finitely many requests that refer to stage $j$ are met on a window around level $k$ for all large $k$, in the sense that the readiness and window conditions of the wait hold.
*Lean:* [`Wait.lead`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Price/Length.lean#L109-L127).

### 4.2 The level schedule

**Lemma 4.7 (exhaustion).** For any $b,c:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N$ there is a nondecreasing $J$ with $J(0)=J(1)=0$, $J(i+1)\le J(i)+1$, $J\to\infty$, with $b(J(i+1))\le i$ whenever $J(i+1)>0$, and for each $i$ a level $k\ge c(i)$ with $J\equiv i$ on $[k-k/2,k+k/2]$.
*Sketch:* define breakpoints $a_0=0$, $a_{i+1}=a_i+b(i+1)+2k_i+1$ with $k_i=c_i+2a_i+1$ and let $J(k)=i$ for $a_i\le k<a_{i+1}$. Then stage $i$ is entered no earlier than $b(i)$, and the middle of its interval of length $\ge 2k_i$ contains the window.
*Lean:* [`Program.exhaust`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Price/Waits.lean#L41-L84).

**Lemma 4.8 (the calendar is paid).** Take $b(j-1)$ from Lemma 4.5 and $c$ from Lemma 4.6, set $J'(m)=j_0+J(m)$, the scale $s_m=\sum_{i<m}\log L_{J'(i+1)}$, and periods $4(M+i+1)$ with $M$ larger than the minimal length and than all initial bills of the first plateau (finitely many levels, since $J\ge1$ eventually). Then the resulting level system satisfies `Fits` and `Paid`.
*Sketch:* the bounds $m\log3\le s_m\le m\log L_{J'(m)}$ give the entry conditions by Lemma 4.5. The volume condition at $J'(i+1)>j_0$ follows from $\mathrm{bill}\le e^{s_i}=\text{denominator}$, and on the first plateau from the choice of $M$. Mesh: $\log L_i/s_i\le 1/J'(i+1)\to0$. Monotonicity of orders, powers and support exponents is inherited from the sheet, and drift and log-cost bounds from `Large`.
*Lean:* [`Wait.ready`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Price/Waits.lean#L137-L239).

### 4.3 The trajectory (`YM/Shoot/`)

**Lemma 4.9 (running from the transfer inequality).** If the transfer inequality holds for all $N,p,j<N$ and $\beta_{N,p,0}=\bar\beta$, then $\beta$ follows the reference law: for every $\varepsilon>0$ and all large $m$, uniformly in $N,p$, $|\beta_{N,p,m}-\hat\beta_m|\le\varepsilon s_m$.
*Sketch:* induction gives $|\beta_{N,p,m}-\hat\beta_m|\le\sum_{i\le m}\mathrm{drift}_i=:D_m$. Since $\mathrm{drift}_{i+1}\le\log L_i/J(i+1)^2$ and $J\to\infty$, the tail $D_m-D_{j}\le\frac\varepsilon2(s_m-s_j)$ for $m\ge j$ with $J(j+1)^{-2}\le\varepsilon/2$, and $s_m\to\infty$ absorbs $D_j$. This fills the gap in the proof of the `Running` clause that III.9 §4.12 reasoned without reading.
*Lean:* [`trajectory`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Shoot/Race.lean#L23-L53).

**Lemma 4.10 (gates).** If the drift is at most $\frac\kappa4\log L$, the one-step relation maps the bottom of the band at level $m+1$ strictly below the bottom of the band at level $m$ and the top strictly above the top. Hence a continuous family across the whole band at one level produces a continuous family across the whole band at the next, and the mapping property survives going down.
*Lean:* [`Drifting`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Shoot/Reach.lean#L70-L71), `gates` (same file; the statement is as above), and the verification [`Drifting.pay`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Shoot/Reach.lean#L73-L79), which derives the drift hypothesis from `Paid` and the `kick` clause of `Large`.

**Lemma 4.11 (shooting).** For $N,p$ with the recipe issued, there is a trajectory from level $N$ down to level 0 competing with the recipe (every level lives and lies in the band, starts from the exact Wilson weight, each step relays) with $\beta$ at level 0 equal to $\bar\beta$.
*Sketch:* intermediate value theorem for the continuous map $\beta_N\mapsto\beta_0$ over the band, via the gates. The family is continuous in the sense of `Curve`: the couplings are continuous, the activities have fixed regular and forced addresses, and the activities themselves vary continuously in the budgeted sup norm. This is the only place where continuity of the step in $\beta$ is used.
*Lean:* `successful` in [`Reach.lean`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Shoot/Reach.lean) (statement: $\exists T$, `Competes R p N 0 T` and $(T\,0).\beta=\bar\beta$).

**Lemma 4.12 (assembly).** Choose a trajectory for every $N,p$ by Lemma 4.11. Then the family satisfies `Selected`: path data are shared between periods (because the recipe fixes the path at $(N,m)$, not at $p$), every level is represented with the prescribed budget and clip and has lawful activities, the start is the bare Wilson weight, the steps are transfers, level 0 is reached with $\bar\beta$, and the running law of Lemma 4.9 holds.
*Lean:* [`Recipe.races`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Shoot/Race.lean#L67-L82).

## 5. Where the difficulty lies

- **The mathematical content is small and the logical structure is large.** The existence of a level schedule (4.1 to 4.2) is a page of real analysis plus the construction of Lemma 4.7. The shooting argument (4.3) is the intermediate value theorem. A written proof needs these two parts and then a precise statement of the order of choice. It should not reproduce the 17,000 lines of purchase wrappers. Their role is to guarantee that all estimates of the RG hold for *one* choice of constants and one level system, which is a bookkeeping device of a formal proof and not something a paper has to prove step by step. A writer could replace III.2.4 and III.2.5 by a single statement: "For every sheet, there exist $L$, $\theta$, delays and $\bar\beta$, in this order, such that every inequality of Sections X–Y holds."
- **The order of choice is the content.** The pattern is that of Balaban. Constants of the profile (orders, widths, support exponents, number of paths) depend on the stage only. Then $L$ is large with respect to their logarithm. After $L$ come the post-$L$ constants ($\theta$, the entries, and the volume parameters), which may depend on $L$. Finally $\bar\beta$ is chosen above all entries. The reason the stage index $J(m)$ increases only slowly ($J(m+1)\le J(m)+1$, $\log L_m/s_m\le1/J$) is that stage $j$'s constants can be made as large as desired as long as the number of levels spent in stage $j$ is long enough.
- **The coupling is not assumed to run correctly; it is proved to stay in a band.** The drift bound is $\mathrm{drift}\le\frac\kappa4\log L$ at all steps and $\le\log L_m/J^2$ asymptotically. The first bound (a quarter of the one-loop shift) is what makes the gates work and the second is what gives $\beta_m\sim\kappa s_m$. This is much stronger than the weakest sufficient condition for asymptotic freedom, as noted in III.9.
- **Unusual features.** (i) The sheet's `kernel` and every plan are fixed first and "never reselected"; this fixes a particular choice of algebraic dictionary rather than proving that it can be chosen anew at each stage. (ii) Several statements are existential over a very large number of already-chosen objects, so that the logical strength of III.2.4 is easy to lose: one should check that the *order of the quantifiers* is that of III.2.1 (the Lean checks it by type). (iii) The docstring in `Price/Intent.lean` records that the module "does NOT check that the RG arguments actually fit that dependency schedule"; this must be established where each request is typed (`Draft`, `Perceived`, `Sheltered`). (iv) A large part of the Lean text consists of names with no mathematical meaning (`bountyV`, `Alloy`, `Roost.Factor`, `Chateau`, `Bandage`); the glossary identifies only the broad role of each.
