**What the unit is about.** The theorem is stated for correlation functions of *field labels*: formal gauge-invariant local polynomials in the curvature and its covariant derivatives. This unit does two jobs.

- It defines the label spaces. Each label is given a *lattice representative*: a gauge-invariant polynomial in the link matrices on a fixed finite stencil. It proves that the representative has the right Taylor expansion in the lattice spacing, the right symmetries, and the right size on small-field configurations.
- It carries these representatives through the RG history. This produces the "renormalized lattice observable" $I_{N,p}(v,\cdot)$ with an exact density formula.

Convention (as in III.9): levels run from the coarsest ($m=0$) to the finest ($m=N$, the Wilson lattice).

## 1. What this unit proves

**Proposition II.1.1 (label spaces and frames).** Fix a compact simple gauge group. The radial-gauge Taylor coefficients of a connection form real finite-dimensional spaces $\mathrm{Taylor}_j$ (symmetric, homogeneous of degree $j+1$ in the position, with the radial constraint $\sum_i z_i A_i(z)=0$). The generator of weight $j+2$ is the $j$-th coefficient. Let $\mathrm{Label}_d$ be the space of $\mathrm{Ad}$-invariant polynomials in these coordinates (complex coefficients) of total weight $\le d$. It is finite-dimensional, bigraded by (weight, number of factors), and contains the unit $1$. Polynomials are determined by their values on real jets. There is a coherent system of *frames*: for each $d$, a basis of $\mathrm{Label}_d$, starting at the unit, consisting of real (self-conjugate) pure-graded elements, with the basis of $\mathrm{Label}_d$ contained in that of $\mathrm{Label}_{d+1}$. Coordinates, the scaling $x_i\mapsto t^{f_i}x_i$ ($f_i$ the number of factors of the basis element $i$), the orthogonal action, and the norm $\sum_i|x_i|$ are defined on the coefficient space.

**Proposition II.1.2 (lattice representative: the notion of a sound section).** A *section* for a frame $F$ of $\mathrm{Label}_d$ is a finite stencil $S\subset\mathbb Z^4$ together with a $\mathbb C$-linear map $c\mapsto O_c$ from the coefficient space to polynomials in the real and conjugate entries of the link matrices on $S$. It is *sound* (for the holonomy datum $H$) if:
- $0\in S$, and $S$ is stable under the signed permutations of the axes (the hypercubic group);
- each $O_c$ is gauge invariant, the unit is mapped to $1$, and the reflection/conjugation property holds: $O_{\bar c}=\overline{O_c}$;
- it is covariant: $O_c(\text{pulled back by }s)=O_{s\cdot c}$ for signed permutations $s$;
- (*projection property*) for every radial jet $J$, the function $h\mapsto O_c(\text{links }H(J,h))$ is $C^d$ at $0$, and its Taylor coefficients at $0$ up to order $d$ coincide with those of $h\mapsto\mathrm{read}(\mathrm{scale}_h J)(\text{label }c)$, where $\mathrm{scale}_hJ$ multiplies the weight-$(j+2)$ component by $h^{j+2}$.

Here $H(J,h)$ is the parallel transport along the edges of the cover $\mathbb Z^4$ of the radial-gauge connection with jet $J$ and spacing $h$. It solves $\dot u=u\,A$, $u(0)=1$, and is unique.

1. $T_d$ is sound;
2. each basis column is a polynomial in finitely many *slots* on the stencil. A slot is the curvature of a small plaquette read after transport along a lattice path, a Lie-algebra element. The polynomial has no term with fewer slot factors than the basis element has factors (property *pop*);

**Proposition II.1.4 (readout: from a lattice function back to a label).** Let $f$ be a gauge-invariant function of a torus configuration, supported on a stencil $X$ and regular of order $\ge d$ in the sense of the budget machinery (II.2). Then there is a *unique* label $p\in\mathrm{Label}_d$ whose Taylor jets along the test holonomies $H(J,h)$ at the site $o$ agree with those of $f$, if $X$ does not wrap around the torus. If $X$ wraps, the label is the constant $f(1)$ times the unit (probable). Moreover, there is one constant $C\ge1$, depending only on $(G,\text{frame})$ and not on the side, the stencil, or $f$, such that
$$\|\text{scaled coordinates of }p\|_{\ell^1}\ \le\ C\,c$$
whenever $f$ has sup-bound $c$ in the budget norm at threshold $t$. Scaled coordinates are $(t^{f_i}p_i)_i$.

**Proposition II.1.5 (equation sections).** Let $\mathcal E_d\subset\mathrm{Label}_d$ be the *equation ideal*: labels whose value vanishes on the jets of every solution of the Yang–Mills equations in radial gauge (probable; defined as the kernel of the map "reduce modulo the equations"). ## 3. Strategy

The continuum labels are Taylor jets of a connection at a point; the lattice has only link matrices. The bridge is the family $h\mapsto H(J,h)$: take a smooth connection with jet $J$ in radial gauge, scale it to spacing $h$, and parallel-transport along lattice edges. A lattice function $F$ *represents* the label $p$ to order $d$ if $F(H(J,h))$ and $p(\mathrm{scale}_hJ)$ have the same Taylor polynomial in $h$ up to order $d$, for every $J$. (This is what the Lean calls `Projects`.) Constructing such $F$ is an algebraic inversion problem. A finite family of *slots* (transported plaquette curvatures along paths) samples the jet; the sampler is analytic with an injective, equivariant linearization, so it has an analytic equivariant local inverse. Substituting the inverse (to Taylor order $d$) into the label polynomial gives a polynomial in the slots. This is G01–G05. Reynolds averaging over signed permutations makes it exactly covariant (G06), and each slot is of size $t$ on small-field configurations (G07). The proof does not use the Yang–Mills equations, and the section is an off-shell object.

The equations enter afterwards (RELAY_MARK). The lattice Schwinger–Dyson identity says that the Wilson expectation of the transported gradient of the action in one link times a spectator supported away from that link vanishes. At leading order in the spacing this "force" is the Maxwell divergence. A Taylor-jet argument ($\mathbf{force\_span}$) shows that the forces span exactly the linear functionals vanishing on pure-gauge jets. So every pure-grade equation polynomial has a lift made of forces, and this lift is micro, that is, null against remote spectators. This is a lattice version of "labels in the equation ideal vanish in correlation functions", and it is why the final label spaces are quotients.

Finally the lattice observable must be pushed through the RG. The method is Balaban's/Dimock's insertion of a source term into the polymer expansion, to first order in the source parameter $\lambda$ ($\lambda^2=0$): the observable becomes a "marked" polymer in the gas. At the Wilson end the observable is born exactly (`launched`), with the non-small-field part absorbed into forced polymers. The identity `Attach.paid` makes this exact without division by the vacuum. At each coarser level the coefficients are transferred by the step operator; the producers for that are in III.10. What is different from the standard treatments is how much is made exact: the density formula, the bare-end identity and the transfer identity are exact equalities in the formalization.

## 5. Where the difficulty lies

- **The two halves are different in kind.** G01–G07 and the readout are classical analysis (analytic ODE dependence, inverse function theorem, Reynolds averaging), long in Lean but routine on paper. The real content for a writer is in the *interfaces*: what exactly `Sound`, `Credit`, `Based` and `Micro` promise. The proofs follow from the definitions with little further input.
- **Off-shell sections.** The sections of II.1.3 reproduce the polynomial labels, not the labels modulo the equation. The statement that equation-ideal labels become null is a separate construction (II.1.5), which changes the section by a weave $R$ (near-diagonal for the filtration). A written proof should explain why the weave is harmless: it is a linear change of basis of $\mathrm{Label}_d$ preserving gradings up to higher terms.
- **Where the Yang–Mills equation enters.** Only in RELAY_MARK, through the lattice Schwinger–Dyson identity. The unit does not prove that the limit satisfies the Yang–Mills equation as an operator identity. It proves the lattice microness of lifts. Whether this matches the formulation of the main theorem (null labels) is a question for V.1/VIII.1.
- **Unusual:** (i) the cost constants of G07 are non-uniform in the degree $d$, order $k$, power $P$; the unit relies on all later constants being chosen *after* these (the "order of constants", III.2). (ii) the number $C$ in `price_of_readout` is independent of side and stencil; this is stronger than one would expect from a compactness argument and it holds because of the finite set of test jets. (iii) the exact density formula of `launched` has no lower bound on the vacuum and no division. This is the formal counterpart of a well-known point: $\langle O\rangle=Z[O]/Z$ is formed only in the limit.
- **Mixed content.** The bulk of SOURCE_HISTORIES, LOCAL_SPLITTING, ATOM_GUARDED_LAYOUT and RELAY_DIFFERENCE is numerical bookkeeping (gates, delays, price lists, with names such as `Sheet`, `Worth`, `latch`, `buys`, `rent`). I regard these as support for III.10 and III.12 rather than as a separate mathematical theorem. (tentative)
