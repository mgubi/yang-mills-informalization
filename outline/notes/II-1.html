Conventions.

  • Gauge group. \(G \subset U (n)\) is a compact matrix group, with \(n \geqslant 1\); nothing in this unit uses simplicity. Use the \(\text{Ad}\)-invariant inner product \(\langle X, Y \rangle = - \text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} \text{tr} (XY) / n\) on its Lie algebra \(\mathfrak{g}\), and the Frobenius norm on matrices. For a plaquette \(P\), \(F_P (U) \in \mathfrak{g}\) is the orthogonal projection of \(\tfrac{1}{2} (U_P - U_P^{- 1})\), and \(\vartheta (U_P) = \lVert U_P - 1 \rVert / \sqrt{n}\) is its chord. \(S_W (U) = \sum_P (1 - \text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} \text{tr} \hspace{0.17em} U_P / n)\) is the Wilson energy, summed once over each site and axis pair \(\mu < \nu\), and \(\langle \cdot \rangle_{\beta}\) is the Wilson expectation on a torus \(\Lambda_{\ell} = (\mathbb{Z}/ \ell)^4\), where \(\ell \geqslant 1\).

  • Cover and placement. Many objects live on the cover \(\mathbb{Z}^4\). A function \(f\) of finitely many cover links is placed at a site \(o \in \Lambda_{\ell}\) by lifting the torus periodically around \(o\); the result is written \(f (o ; U)\).

  • Small fields. \(\text{Hy}_t (X)\) is the set of configurations in which no plaquette touching \(X\) has chord \(> t\).

  • Levels. \(m = 0\) is the coarsest level and \(m = N\) the Wilson lattice. \(L_m\) is the odd block factor of the step \(m + 1 \to m\), the spacing satisfies \(a_m = L_m a_{m + 1}\), and \(s_m = \log (1 / a_m)\) is the scale.

  • Couplings and level constants (III.9). \(\hat{\beta}_m = \bar{\beta} + b_0 s_m\) with \(b_0 = 11 C_A / (24 \pi^2)\). The small-field threshold is \(\theta_m = \hat{\beta}_m^{- 1 / 2} \exp ((\log \log \hat{\beta}_m)^2)\) and the forced allowance is \(w_m = \exp (- \exp ((\log \log \hat{\beta}_m)^2 / 5))\). \(J (m)\) is the nondecreasing stage index, \(\bar{\kappa}_j \geqslant 1\) the stage-\(j\) constant, \(\kappa_m = \bar{\kappa}_{J (m)}\) the level-\(m\) constant, and \(b_m\) its budget point. \(\rho_m = \rho_{N, p, m} = e^{\mathcal{V}_m} \hspace{0.17em} \text{gas}_m\) is the polymer representation of the level-\(m\) density, with vacuum exponent \(\mathcal{V}_m\) (II.2). \(T^{(m)}_{\beta}\) is the blocking push-forward from level \(m + 1\) to level \(m\) (III.1).

  • Budget norms (II.2). A budget point \(b\) has order \(k_b \geqslant 6\), power \(P_b \geqslant 2\), threshold \(t_b \in (0, 1]\), a support exponent and a cover size. \(f\) is \(b\)-regular on \(X\) if it is local on links with both endpoints in \(X\), measurable and gauge invariant, and \(A \mapsto f (e^A U)\) is \(C^{k_b}\) at \(A = 0\) for every \(U \in \text{Hy}_{t_b} (X)\). All ordered left derivatives through order \(k_b\) must exist there. Write \(\lVert f \rVert_{b ; o, X} \leqslant c\) when \(c \geqslant 0\) bounds every such derivative, including the value, in every list of directions satisfying \(\lvert A_e \rvert \leqslant t_b (1 + \text{dist} (e_-, o))^{P_b}\). Here \(e_-\) is the initial vertex of the positive link \(e\), and \(\text{dist}\) is the periodic \(\ell^{\infty}\) distance. All bounds are pointwise, including at the small-field boundary.

What the unit does. It defines the field labels and their lattice representatives, and specifies the renormalized observables \(O_{N, p}\).

  1. Sections. For each label it constructs gauge-invariant polynomials in the link matrices on a fixed stencil that reproduce the label to Taylor order \(d\) in the spacing, with symmetry and small-field bounds (§4.1–4.3).

  2. Readout. It proves the converse: every regular lattice function has a label, and the label's size is bounded by the function's budget norm uniformly in the lattice side, support and norm parameters (§4.4).

  3. Equation sections. From the lattice Schwinger–Dyson identity it builds a second kind of section on which every label of the free-Maxwell equation ideal becomes orthogonal to remote spectators (§4.5).

  4. Observables. It specifies the record carrying a label through the RG history, proves the exact birth formula at the Wilson end, and collects the numerical conditions and comparison estimates used downstream (§4.6–4.8).

Proposition II.1.1 (label spaces and frames). For \(j \geqslant 0\) let \(\text{Tay}_j\) be the real space of \(4\)-tuples \(A = (A_{\mu})\) of symmetric \((j + 1)\)-linear maps \((\mathbb{R}^4)^{j + 1} \to \mathfrak{g}\) satisfying the radial-gauge condition \(\sum_{\mu} z_{\mu} A_{\mu} (z, \ldots, z) = 0\). Its elements are the Taylor coefficients of weight \(j + 2\) of a connection in radial gauge. A jet is a sequence \(J = (J_j)_{j \geqslant 0}\). Fix a real basis of each \(\text{Tay}_j\), and give its coordinates weight \(j + 2\). Let \(V_d\) be the space of complex polynomials in these coordinates of total weight \(\leqslant d\) that are invariant under \(J \mapsto \text{Ad} (g) J\) for all \(g \in G\). Then:

(a) \(V_d\) is finite-dimensional, \(1 \in V_d \subset V_{d + 1}\), and an element of \(V_d\) is determined by its values on real jets.

(b) \(V_d = \bigoplus_{w, f} V_d^{(w, f)}\), where \((w, f)\) = (weight, number of factors). \(V_d^{(0, 0)} =\mathbb{C}1\), and \(V_d^{(w, 0)} = 0\) for \(w > 0\). Complex conjugation \(p \mapsto \bar{p}\) and the orthogonal group \(O (4)\), acting by rotating the jet, preserve the grades.

(c) There is a coherent system of frames. For each \(d\) there is a basis \(b_0 = 1, b_1, \ldots, b_{K_d}\) of \(V_d\) with these properties:

  • each \(b_i\) is real (\(\bar{b}_i = b_i\)) and of pure grade \((w_i, f_i)\), with \((w_0, f_0) = (0, 0)\), \(w_i \leqslant d\) and \(f_i \geqslant 1\) for \(i \neq 0\);

  • the basis of \(V_d\) is contained in that of \(V_{d + 1}\).

In coordinates \(c \in \mathbb{C}^{K_d + 1}\) we write \((S_t c)_i = t^{f_i} c_i\), \(\lVert c \rVert_1 = \sum_i \lvert c_i \rvert\), \(c^{\circ} = c - c_0 e_0\), and \(s \cdot c\) for the \(O (4)\) action, defined by \(p_{s \cdot c} (J) = p_c (s^{- 1} J)\). Proposition II.1.2 (test holonomies and sound sections).

(a) Fix \(d\), a jet \(J\), \(h \in \mathbb{R}\) and a cover edge \((x, \mu)\), and let \(A^{(d)}_{\mu} (z) = \sum_{j + 2 \leqslant d} J_{j, \mu} (z, \ldots, z)\), with an empty sum when \(d < 2\). Let \(u : \mathbb{R} \to G\) solve \(u' (t) = u (t) \hspace{0.17em} hA^{(d)}_{\mu} (h (x + te_{\mu}))\) with \(u (0) = 1\). The solution exists and is unique, and we put \(U^{(d)}_{J, h} (x, \mu) = u (1)\). Moreover \(U^{(d)}_{J, h} = U^{(d)}_{\text{sc}_h J, 1}\), where \(\text{sc}_h J = (h^{j + 2} J_j)_j\), and \(U^{(d)}_{J, 0} \equiv 1\). A holonomy datum is such a choice of solutions; by uniqueness its links do not depend on the choice.

(b) A section on a frame of \(V_d\) is a pair \((S, \Phi)\): a finite stencil \(S \subset \mathbb{Z}^4\) and a \(\mathbb{C}\)-linear map \(c \mapsto \Phi_c\) into polynomials in the entries, and their conjugates, of the link matrices on \(S\).

(c) The section is sound if:

  • \(0 \in S\) and \(S\) is invariant under signed permutations;

  • each \(\Phi_c\) is gauge invariant, \(\Phi_{e_0} = 1\), and \(\Phi_{\bar{c}} = \overline{\Phi_c}\);

  • \(\Phi_c (s^{\ast} U) = \Phi_{s \cdot c} (U)\) for every signed permutation \(s\), where \(s^{\ast}\) is the pull-back under \(y \mapsto sy\);

  • (projection) for every \(c\) and every jet \(J\), the functions \(h \mapsto \Phi_c (U^{(d)}_{J, h})\) and \(h \mapsto p_c (\text{sc}_h J)\), with \(p_c = \sum_i c_i b_i\), are \(C^d\) at \(0\) and have equal derivatives of order \(\leqslant d\) there.

  1. \(\Sigma_d\) is sound.

  2. (slot structure) Each column is \(\Phi^{(d)}_{e_i} (U) = P_i (\sigma_1 (U), \ldots, \sigma_m (U))\). Here the \(\sigma_l\) are slots with stencils in \(S_d\), \(P_i\) is a complex polynomial in their real components, and every monomial of \(P_i\) has degree \(\geqslant f_i\). A slot \(\sigma = (\omega, P)\) is a lattice walk \(\omega\) from \(0\) followed by a plaquette \(P\) at its end, with \(\sigma (U) = \text{Ad} (U_{\omega}) F_P (U) \in \mathfrak{g}\).

  3. (cost) For all \(k, P \in \mathbb{N}\) there is \(A \geqslant 0\), depending on the chosen section and on \(k, P\), with the following property.   2. there is \(c_d \geqslant 1\) with \(\lvert \Phi^{(d)}_{e_i} \rvert \leqslant c_d\) at every configuration and every placement.

Proposition II.1.4 (readout of a lattice function). Fix a holonomy datum, \(d\), and a frame of \(V_d\).

(a) Let \(b\) be a budget point of order \(\geqslant d\), let \(X \subset \Lambda_{\ell}\), \(o \in \Lambda_{\ell}\), and let \(f\) be \(b\)-regular on \(X\). There is a unique \(p \in V_d\) that reads \(f\) at \(o\):

  • if \(X\) does not wrap around \(o\) (\(10 \hspace{0.17em} \text{dist} (x, o) < \ell\) for all \(x \in X\)), then for every jet \(J\) the function \(h \mapsto f (U^{(d), o}_{J, h})\) is \(C^d\) at \(0\) and has the same derivatives of order \(\leqslant d\) there as \(h \mapsto p (\text{sc}_h J)\);

  • if \(X\) wraps, \(p = f (1) \cdot 1\) by convention.

Here \(U^{(d), o}_{J, h} (x, \mu) = U^{(d)}_{J, h} (\widetilde{x - o}, \mu)\), where the tilde denotes the centred periodic lift. These are torus test links, defined by lifting each initial vertex; they need not be periodic samples of a connection across the seam.

Proposition II.1.5 (equation sections). Let \(\text{Sh}_j \subset \text{Tay}_j\) be the radial potentials whose curvature is a degree-\(j\) homogeneous Taylor coefficient of a \(\mathfrak{g}\)-valued solution of the free Maxwell equations. That is: \(F\) is antisymmetric, satisfies the Bianchi identity, and \(\sum_a \partial_a F_{ab} = 0\). The equation ideal is

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{E}_d = \{p \in V_d : p (J) = 0 \text{ for every jet with } J_j \in \text{Sh}_j \text{ for all } j\} .\)

This is the weight-\(\leqslant d\) part of the ideal of the linearized field equations, imposed degree by degree. For every holonomy datum, every \(d\) and every frame of \(V_d\), there are a section \(\Sigma\) and a \(\mathbb{C}\)-linear automorphism \(R\) of \(V_d\) (the weave) such that:

  • \(\Sigma\) is sound and has the slot structure and cost of Prop. II.1.3;

  • \(R\) commutes with the signed-permutation action and with conjugation;

  • for every \(p \in \mathcal{E}_d\), the cover function \(\Phi_{\text{coord} (Rp)}\) is micro.

A function \(f\) is micro if it is continuous and there are \(r, M \in \mathbb{N}\) with the following property. For every side \(\ell \geqslant M\), site \(o\), coupling \(\beta \neq 0\) and bounded measurable \(S\) depending only on links with both endpoints at distance \(> r\) from \(o\),

\(\displaystyle \left\langle f (o ; \cdot \left) \hspace{0.17em} S \right\rangle_{\beta} = 0. \right.\)

The same holds simultaneously for all \(d\) along a fixed system of frames. The constructed \(R\) is unitriangular: if \(p\) has pure grade \((w, f)\), every nonzero component of \(Rp - p\) has grade \((w', f')\) with \((f', w') > (f, w)\) in lexicographic order. \(\displaystyle O_{N, p} (v, o ; U) = a_N^{- d} \hspace{0.17em} \Phi^{(d)}_{S_{1 / \theta_N} \tau^{- 1} v} (o ; U) .\)

Proposition II.1.8 (birth at the Wilson end). Let \(\rho\) be a lawful polymer representation on \(\Lambda_{\ell}\) with parameter \(w > 0\) (II.2) and threshold \(\theta\). Let \(o \in \Lambda_{\ell}\), and let \(\Sigma\) be a sound section whose placed columns satisfy \(\lvert \Phi_{e_i} (o ; \cdot) \rvert \leqslant B\) with \(B \geqslant 1\). Put \(Y = Y_o\) and \(f_{\max} = \max_i f_i\), and assume the fee condition

\(\displaystyle \text{fee} := B \hspace{0.17em} \theta^{- f_{\max}} \hspace{0.17em} \text{mass} (Y, 1) \hspace{0.17em} (e^{\lvert Y \rvert w} - 1) \leqslant \sqrt{w} .\)

Assume a shelf of reserved forced addresses: for every pair of finite supports \(\varnothing \ne I \subseteq X\) and every auxiliary index in \(\mathbb{N}\), there is a distinct forced address with support \(X\), flagged inventory \(I\), identically zero activity, and an auxiliary eligibility test satisfied at every configuration. For every fixed reservation index there is a coefficient \(E (v)\) with the following properties:

  • \(x = v\) and \(g \equiv 0\);

  • the forced part is the large-field supplement \(\Phi_{S_{1 / \theta} v} (o ; \cdot) - v_0 -\boldsymbol{1}_{\text{Hy}_{\theta} (Y)} \Phi_{S_{1 / \theta} v^{\circ}} (o ; \cdot)\), spliced into the reserved addresses;

3. Strategy

Labels are invariant polynomials in radial-gauge Taylor coefficients. Test holonomies \(U_{J, h}\) compare them with functions of lattice links by matching derivatives in \(h\) through order \(d\). Finitely many transported plaquette curvatures give an analytic map of the truncated jet whose derivative is injective. An equivariant left inverse, followed by Taylor expansion, produces slot polynomials representing every label. Averaging over signed permutations gives covariance. Every slot and each of its admissible left derivatives costs one threshold factor, so a column of factor degree \(f\) has norm \(O (\theta^f)\). Conversely, Taylor expansion of a regular lattice function gives a unique read label. Testing on jets of amplitude \(\theta\) bounds its scaled coordinates uniformly in the lattice side and support.

The equation sections use Haar integration by parts in one link. For \(\ell \geqslant 2\), \(\beta \ne 0\) and a bounded measurable multiplier independent of that link, the expectation of the corresponding Wilson force is zero. Transported forces are linear combinations of slots; their leading continuum symbols are the free Maxwell divergence. Every equation polynomial is a sum of products of a linear Maxwell equation functional and other linear jet functionals. Represent the first factor by a force and the remaining factors by slot polynomials avoiding its link. Each product is orthogonal to remote spectators and reads the prescribed generator modulo higher grade. A unitriangular change of labels absorbs those higher terms. The resulting equation section describes vanishing at separated lattice sites; its transfer to null labels of the limit requires the later comparison arguments.

At the Wilson end the observable is inserted to first order in a parameter \(\lambda\) with \(\lambda^2 = 0\). The density coefficient consists of the vacuum density times a masked local section and regular remainders, together with a gas in which one forced polymer is marked. Splicing the section's large-field complement into reserved forced addresses gives the exact bare-end identity. This uses no division by the vacuum density. Along the RG history the running coordinates describe mixing of local observables; their values at the reference level define \(\tau\), hence the renormalization map \(S_{1 / \theta_N} \tau^{- 1}\). This unit fixes the transfer specification and proves the birth identity; III.10–III.11 supply the per-step estimates.

Lemma 4.4 (analytic germ, G02).

(a) Let \(x\) range over a finite-dimensional real vector space, let \(A_x (t)\) depend linearly on \(x\) and continuously on \(t\), and let \(u_x\) solve \(\dot{u} = uA_x\) with \(u_x (0) = 1\). Then \(x \mapsto u_x (1)\) is analytic at \(0\), equals \(1\) there, and its derivative along every ray is \(\int_0^1 A_x\).

(b) Consequently each link, each walk and each slot is an analytic function of the truncated jet near \(0\). The derivative of the slot \((\omega, P)\) at \(0\) is the circulation of the edge-integrated potential around \(P\). The transport along \(\omega\) does not contribute at linear order. Sketch: write the ODE as \(F = 1 + T (x) F\) on \(C ([0, 1])\), where \(T (x)\) is the Volterra operator, linear in \(x\), with \(\lVert T (x) \rVert < 1\) near \(0\). Then \(F = (1 - T (x))^{- 1} 1\), which is analytic since inversion is analytic near \(1\) in a Banach algebra. Differentiate the fixed-point equation at \(0\). The bounds are link by link; no analytic radius uniform over an infinite stencil is claimed. Lean: endpointGerm, linkGerm, walkGerm, slotGerm, Drops.Peg.

Lemma 4.5 (finitely many slots detect the jet, G03). For each \(d\) there are finitely many slots whose linearizations have no common kernel on the truncated jet space \(\bigoplus_{j \leqslant d - 2} \text{Tay}_j\). Sketch:

  • All slots detect the jet. Suppose all linearized slots vanish on \(A\). Write \(N_l (x, t)\) for the difference of the two iterated averages of partial derivatives over a square of side \(t\) based at \(x\). The unit-square circulation is \(\sum_l N_l (x, 1)\). Homogeneity gives \(N_l (tx, t) = t^l N_l (x, 1)\), hence \(\sum_{l \leqslant k} t^{k - l} N_l (tx, t) = 0\) when \(A_k\) is the top component and \(x \in \mathbb{Z}^4\).

  • Mesh to zero. Letting \(t \to 0\) with \(tx \to y\) gives \(\text{curl} (A_k) (y) = 0\) for the top component. In radial gauge the potential is recovered from its curvature, so \(A_k = 0\). Descend in \(k\).

  • Finitely many suffice. Among finite families choose one whose common kernel has minimal dimension. Adding any slot cannot lower the dimension, so this kernel lies in the common kernel of all slots, which is \(0\).

The choice is non-constructive. Lean: Bid.G03, top_term, zero_of_zero_partials, Work.G03.answer.

Lemma 4.18 (lattice Schwinger–Dyson identity). Assume \(\ell \geqslant 2\). Gradient. For a link \(e\) and \(A \in \mathfrak{g}\), \(\tfrac{d}{dt} S_W (e^{tA} U_e) |_0 = \langle A, \nabla_e S_W (U) \rangle\). \(\displaystyle \nabla_{(x, \nu)} S_W = \sum_{\nu < b} \Delta_b F_{\nu b} (x) - \sum_{a < \nu} \Delta_a F_{a \nu} (x) .\)

\(\displaystyle \langle (W' - \beta \langle A, \nabla_e S_W \rangle W) S \rangle_{\beta} = 0.\)

For \(\beta \neq 0\) and \(W = 1\) this gives \(\langle \langle A, \nabla_e S_W \rangle S \rangle_{\beta} = 0\). 5. Where the difficulty lies

The main analytic estimate is the volume-independent readout bound. Polarization in the full lattice direction space could introduce constants depending on the volume. Pulling each tensor back to the finitely many test directions avoids this, while rescaling each link position gives the required distance weights. The analytic radii of individual links need not be uniform.

The equation lift must serve two purposes at once: reproduce a prescribed jet and permit integration by parts without a derivative of its multiplier. Slot sampling away from the varied link supplies this independence. The triangular argument orders grades first by factor number, then by weight; higher-factor corrections need not have higher engineering weight. The final family retains its primary sections, and the equation sections form an auxiliary family whose comparison requires later estimates.

The birth identity requires reserved addresses in the same forced-polymer alphabet. Lawfulness alone does not supply them. The guarded construction weakens the auxiliary-test requirement while retaining exact eligibility whenever an inserted coefficient is nonzero. Its numerical bound is possible because the forced allowance decays faster than every inverse coupling power.

The finite slot family is chosen nonconstructively. Consequently the stencils and constants are finite but not explicit. Their dependence on degree and derivative parameters must be included before the block factors are chosen.
