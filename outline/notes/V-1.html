1. What this unit proves

Setting and notation.

  • Tables and words. \(S\) is the limit table of IV.1 (the mixed state \(S^{\text{mix}}\) of the main theorem). A label is a pair \((d, v)\): its degree \(d \in \mathbb{N}\) and a vector \(v\) in the finite-dimensional reference space \(V_d\). A word \(w = ((x_1, v_1), \ldots, (x_n, v_n))\) is a finite list of points of \(\mathbb{R}^4\) with labels; \(|w| = n\), and \(S (w) = S (x ; v)\) is its value, multilinear in the labels.

  • Time, slabs, buffers. Time is the last coordinate \(t (x)\), with unit vector \(e_t\); \(y \perp e_t\) denotes a spatial vector. For a different coordinate axis \(a\), replace \(t (x)\) by \(x^a\) in the following definitions.

    • A word is separated if its points are distinct, and positive if in addition all \(t (x_i) > 0\).

    • It lies in the slab \((0, d)\) if \(0 < t (x_i) < d\) for all \(i\).

    • It is \(r\)-buffered in that slab if its points are at mutual sup-distance \(\geqslant r\) and \(r \leqslant t (x_i) \leqslant d - r\).

  • Operations on words. \(w + a\) is the translate, \(\tau_d w = w + de_t\), \(w \cdot w'\) is concatenation, and \(\bar{w}\) conjugates only the labels.

  • Two reflections.

    • \(\Theta w\), the OS reflection: reverse the list, reflect the points in \(\{t = 0\}\), and apply to the labels the antilinear map (conjugation followed by the time-mirror action).

    • \(\vartheta w\), the linear time reflection: reflect the points and apply the time-mirror action to the labels, with neither conjugation nor reversal.

    • For real labels, \(\Theta w\) is a permutation of \(\vartheta w\).

  • Null spaces. \(N_d (S) \subset V_d\) consists of labels whose correlations with every separated family of spectators vanish. Write \(F_d (S) = V_d / N_d (S)\) and \(q_d : V_d \to F_d (S)\) for the quotient map. A table \(S'\) respects \(S\) if \(N_d (S) \subset N_d (S')\) for all \(d\).

  • Field algebra (VI.1). It consists of label derivatives \(\partial_j : F_d (S) \to F_{d + 1} (S)\), inclusions \(F_d (S) \to F_e (S)\) for \(d \leqslant e\), an action \(R\) of \(O (4)\) on these spaces, and a stress tensor \(J_{ab} \in F_4 (S)\). In a word, \(\partial_j v\) and \(R (K) v\) mean representatives of \(\partial_j q_d (v)\) and \(R (K) q_d (v)\). All tables and operators to which this notation is applied factor through the relevant quotients. Covariance means \(S (Kx_1, \ldots, Kx_n ; R (K) v_1, \ldots, R (K) v_n) = S (x ; v)\) for every \(K \in O (4)\) and separated configuration \(x \in \text{Conf}_n\).

  • Caps. For each fixed arity, degree list, label list and derivative order \(j\), there are \(A \in \mathbb{N}\) and \(C \geqslant 0\) such that \(\|D^j S (x ; v)\| \leqslant Cr^{- A}\) whenever \(0 < r \leqslant 1\) and the points have mutual sup-distance at least \(r\).

  • The OS space. The OS space \(\mathcal{H}\) of \(S\) for an axis \(a\) is the completion of the span of \(a\)-positive words modulo null vectors, with \(\langle b, b' \rangle = S (\Theta b \cdot b')\). \(|b \rangle\) denotes the vector of \(b\), and \(\Omega = | \emptyset \rangle\), with \(\| \Omega \| = 1\).

Nowhere does the unit assume clustering, a unique vacuum or a spectral gap.

Proposition V.1.1 (chambers and ground space). Let \(S\) satisfy the Euclidean hierarchy and let \(a\) be a coordinate axis. Then:

  • On the OS space \(\mathcal{H}\) there is a strongly continuous semigroup \((E_t)_{t \geqslant 0}\) of self-adjoint contractions with \(E_t |b \rangle = |b + te_a \rangle\).

  • There is a strongly continuous unitary group \((U_y)_{y \perp e_a}\) commuting with it, with \(U_y |b \rangle = |b + y \rangle\).

  • The triple is a chamber. Let \(\mathcal{H}_0 = \{\psi : E_t \psi = \psi \forall t\}\) be its ground space and \(P_0\) the orthogonal projection onto it. Then \(\Omega \in \mathcal{H}_0\), and \(E_t \psi \to P_0 \psi\) as \(t \to \infty\) for every \(\psi\).

Status: theorem. Lean: OS.realization, OS.implement, OS.Semigroup.emerges; definitions OS.Chamber, OS.Semigroup, Semigroup.ground.

Proposition V.1.2 (spectral calculus in a chamber). In a chamber, write formally \(E_t = e^{- tH}\).

  • (a) There is a strongly continuous unitary group \(s \mapsto e^{isH}\), determined by \(e^{isH} E_1 \psi = E_1^{\hspace{0.17em} 1 - is} \psi\). Here \(E_1^z\), for \(\text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} z > 0\), is the continuous functional calculus of the positive contraction \(E_1\). The map \(V (x) = e^{i \hspace{0.17em} x^a H} U_{x_{\perp}}\), with \(x_{\perp} = x - x^a e_a\), is a strongly continuous unitary representation of \(\mathbb{R}^4\).

  • (b) There is a projection-valued measure \(P\) on \(\mathbb{R}^4\) with \(\langle \psi, V (x) \psi \rangle = \int e^{ip \cdot x} \hspace{0.17em} d \mu_{\psi} (p)\), where \(\mu_{\psi} (B) = \langle \psi, P (B) \psi \rangle\) for Borel \(B\).

  • (c) Every \(\mu_{\psi}\) is supported in \(\{p^a \geqslant 0\}\).

  • (d) For \(\text{Im} \hspace{0.17em} z \geqslant 0\):

\(\displaystyle \langle \psi, V (x) \hspace{0.17em} e^{i (\text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} z) H} E_{\text{Im} \hspace{0.17em} z} \hspace{0.17em} \psi \rangle = \int e^{ip \cdot x} \hspace{0.17em} e^{ip^a z} \hspace{0.17em} d \mu_{\psi} (p) .\)

Status: theorem. It holds for every semigroup with the properties of V.1.1. No gap is assumed or proved. Lean: Semigroup.edge, Semigroup.unit, Spectral.Orbit.total, Orbit.P, Semigroup.nonneg, Semigroup.heat.

Proposition V.1.3 (slab operators). Assume the hierarchy and the bench (V.1.7). Then every chamber of \(S\) for the time axis carries a unique family of bounded operators \(T_d (w)\), one for each \(d > 0\) and each word \(w\) in the slab \((0, d)\), with

\(\displaystyle T_d (w) \hspace{0.17em} |b \rangle = |w \cdot \tau_d b \rangle \qquad \text{for every positive word } b.\)

  • All-copy bound. For every \(D \in \mathbb{N}\) and \(\rho > 0\) there are \(C > 0\) and \(\kappa \in \mathbb{N}\) such that

\(\displaystyle \|T_d (w)\| \leqslant C^{|w|} (|w| / r)^{\kappa |w|}\)

for every \(d > 0\), every \(r \in (0, 1]\), and every word \(w\) that is \(r\)-buffered in \((0, d)\) with degrees \(\leqslant D\) and label norms \(\leqslant \rho\). The right side is \(1\) for the empty word.

  • Smoothness. If moreover \(S\) satisfies the derivative identities of the plan \(\partial\), then for fixed \(d\) and labels the map \(x \mapsto T_d (w (x))\) is \(C^{\infty}\) in operator norm on the open set where \(w (x)\) lies in the slab. Its derivatives are \(\partial_{x_p^j} T_d (w (x)) = T_d (w' (x))\), where \(w'\) replaces the label \(v_p\) by \(\partial_j v_p\).

Status: theorem, given the bench. More generally, smoothness follows for any slab family with the all-copy bound on a time-axis chamber of a table \(S'\) that satisfies the hierarchy, respects \(S\), and satisfies the derivative identities of the plan on \(S\). Lean: Work.B01.answer, Work.B02.answer; definitions Spread, Spread.AllCopy, Spread.Laws.

Proposition V.1.4 (the compressed vacuum algebra). Assume the hierarchy and \(O (4)\)-covariance of \(S\). Fix a time-axis chamber and any family \(T_d (w)\) with the defining identity of V.1.3. Then:

  • (a) \(U_y \psi = \psi\) for every \(\psi \in \mathcal{H}_0\) and every spatial \(y\).

  • (b) For a separated word \(w\), the operator \(M (w) := P_0 \hspace{0.17em} T_d (w + s) |_{\mathcal{H}_0}\) does not depend on the choice of \(s \in \mathbb{R}^4\) and \(d > 0\) with \(w + s\) in the slab \((0, d)\). For positive \(b\), \(M (b) \Omega = P_0 |b \rangle\). For every separated \(w\), \(\langle \Omega, M (w) \Omega \rangle = S (w)\).

  • (c) Cluster limit. Let \(q_1, \ldots, q_m\) be separated words and \(a_1 (n), \ldots, a_m (n) \in \mathbb{R}^4\) with \(t (a_j (n) - a_i (n)) \to + \infty\) for \(i < j\); the spatial components are arbitrary. Then for large \(n\) the word \((q_1 + a_1 (n)) \cdots (q_m + a_m (n))\) is separated, and its value under \(S\) tends to \(\langle \Omega, M (q_1) \cdots M (q_m) \Omega \rangle\).

  • (d) The norm-closed \(\ast\)-algebra \(\mathcal{A} \subset B (\mathcal{H}_0)\) generated by the \(M (w)\) is commutative, \(\mathcal{A} \Omega\) is dense in \(\mathcal{H}_0\), and \(p \in \mathcal{A}\) with \(p \Omega = 0\) implies \(p = 0\).

  • (e) Suppose in addition that the family satisfies the smoothness part of V.1.3. Then:

    • \(M\) is invariant under permutations of the word and under all translations; \(M (\emptyset) = 1\); a unit label may be dropped together with its point; and \(M (w)^{\ast} = M (\Theta w)\).

    • For \(x \in \text{Conf}_n\), the map \(v \mapsto M (x ; v)\) is multilinear and factors through \(F_{d_1} (S) \times \cdots \times F_{d_n} (S)\).

    • \(x \mapsto M (x ; v)\) is \(C^{\infty}\) in norm on \(\text{Conf}_n\).

    • \(\mathcal{A}\) is norm-separable.

Status: theorem. Lean: Ground.inert, Ground.overline, Ground.almost, Spread.insertValue, Ground.arcs, Ground.adjunction, Ground.full, Ground.attains, Work.B03.answer, Work.B12.answer; definitions Ground.M, Spread.algebra, Bid.Theme.

Proposition V.1.5 (decomposition into phases). Assume the setting of V.1.4(a)–(e). Then:

  • There are a compact metrizable space \(X\) (the Gelfand spectrum of \(\mathcal{A}\)), a Borel probability measure \(\nu\) on \(X\) with \(\nu (O) > 0\) for every nonempty open \(O\), and characters \(\chi_{\xi}\) (\(\xi \in X\)).

  • \(\xi \mapsto \chi_{\xi}\) is a bijection onto the characters of \(\mathcal{A}\); each \(\xi \mapsto \chi_{\xi} (p)\) is continuous; and \(\int_X \chi_{\xi} (p) \hspace{0.17em} d \nu (\xi) = \langle \Omega, p \Omega \rangle\) for every \(p \in \mathcal{A}\).

  • For each \(\xi\), the formula \(S^{\xi} (x ; v) = \chi_{\xi} (M (x ; v))\) on \(\text{Conf}_n\) (and \(0\) off it) defines a table.

  • \(S^{\xi} (w) = \chi_{\xi} (M (w))\) for every separated \(w\); \(\xi \mapsto S^{\xi} (w)\) is continuous; and the barycenter identity holds:

\(\displaystyle \int_X S^{\xi} (w) \hspace{0.17em} d \nu (\xi) = S (w) \qquad \text{for every separated word } w.\)

Status: theorem. Lean: Ground.resolution, Work.B07.state, Resolution.barycenter; definitions Spread.Resolution, Resolution.Out, Resolution.receives.

Proposition V.1.6 (every phase satisfies the hierarchy and the field laws). In addition to V.1.3–V.1.5 (with the smoothness laws for the plan of the field algebra), assume:

  • the Seed of \(S\) (IV.1);

  • the grid hypothesis (§2);

  • the laws of the field algebra for \(S\) (VI.1).

Then two operator identities hold before any character is applied:

  • (e) phase reflection: \(M (w) = M (\vartheta w)\) for every separated word with arbitrary complex labels;

  • (f) \(M (w) = M (Kw)\) for every separated \(w\) and every \(K \in O (4)\), where \(K\) acts on points and, through \(R (K)\), on labels.

As a consequence, for every \(\xi \in X\):

  • (a) \(S^{\xi}\) satisfies the Euclidean hierarchy:

    • permutation symmetry, \(S^{\xi} (\emptyset) = 1\), and unit labels;

    • invariance under signed permutations of the axes, and reality \(S^{\xi} (x ; \bar{v}) = \overline{S^{\xi} (x ; v)}\);

    • translation invariance;

    • reflection positivity for every coordinate axis, in point and smeared form;

    • smoothness on \(\text{Conf}_n\), and caps on all derivatives.

  • (b) \(S^{\xi}\) respects \(S\) and is \(O (4)\)-covariant for the same action \(R\). It satisfies the inclusion and derivative identities of the plan and the stress-tensor Ward identities of VI.1.

  • (c) Uniform cap: for every \(D \in \mathbb{N}\) and \(\rho > 0\) there are \(K > 0\) and \(\kappa \in \mathbb{N}\) with

\(\displaystyle |S^{\xi} (w) | \leqslant K^{|w|} (|w| / r)^{\kappa |w|}\)

for all \(\xi\), all \(r \in (0, 1]\), and all words with degrees \(\leqslant D\), label norms \(\leqslant \rho\) and points at mutual sup-distance \(\geqslant r\).

  • (d) \(N_d (S^{\xi}) = N_d (S)\) for every \(d\), hence \(F_d (S^{\xi}) = F_d (S)\).

Proposition V.1.7 (bench). For every \(D \in \mathbb{N}\) and \(\rho > 0\) there are \(C_0 > 0\) and \(\kappa \in \mathbb{N}\) with the following property. Let \(d > 0\), \(r \in (0, 1]\), and let \(w \neq \emptyset\) be \(r\)-buffered in \((0, d)\), with degrees \(\leqslant D\) and label norms \(\leqslant \rho\). Then there are a lattice level \(b\) and a width \(s \leqslant d\) with \(s \in a_b \mathbb{N}\) and \(w\) in the slab \((0, s)\), such that every positive word \(v\) has a constant \(B_v\) with

\(\displaystyle | S (\Theta v_n \cdot v_n) | \leqslant B_v \hspace{0.17em} A^{4 n} (n \geqslant 0), \qquad A = C_0^{|w|} (|w| / r)^{\kappa |w|}, \qquad v_0 = v, v_{n + 1} = \tau_s (\Theta w) \cdot \tau_s (w \cdot \tau_s v_n) .\)

Here \(v_n\) is \(v\) preceded by \(2 n\) consecutive slabs of width \(s\), holding alternately \(w\) and its reflection. Thus \(|v_n \rangle = (T_s (w)^{\ast} T_s (w))^n |v \rangle\) once \(T_s (w)\) exists, and \(S (\Theta v_n \cdot v_n)\) is a reflection-symmetric train of \(4 n\) copies of \(w\).

3. Strategy

The limit table as a mixture. The table \(S\) is obtained by ordered limits of finite-volume Wilson correlations. Its OS semigroup may have a multidimensional ground space \(\mathcal{H}_0\). The unit decomposes the correlations through an algebra acting on that space.

  • The algebra. Inserting a separated word into a time slab gives a bounded operator; compressing it to \(\mathcal{H}_0\) gives \(M (w)\). These operators generate a commutative C*-algebra \(\mathcal{A}\), for which \(\Omega\) is cyclic and separating.

  • Phases. The Gelfand spectrum \(X\) of \(\mathcal{A}\), with the measure \(\nu\) representing \(p \mapsto \langle \Omega, p \Omega \rangle\), is the space of phases. Each character gives a table, \(S^{\xi} = \chi_{\xi} \circ M\), and \(S = \int S^{\xi} \hspace{0.17em} d \nu\).

  • Comparison with the classical case. The algebra describes observables at infinite time separation. It replaces the invariant observables used in the ergodic decomposition of a Euclidean measure. Here the construction uses only the OS space of the limit correlations. Operator identities give the hierarchy and field laws for every \(\xi\); vacuum uniqueness is first obtained almost everywhere.

Four inputs. Four inputs make this work, and each is used where the classical argument would use the measure.

  • (i) Boundedness. A chessboard estimate controls alternating trains of \(4 n\) copies with constants independent of \(n\). A power argument converts this estimate into the slab-operator bound \(C^{|w|} (|w| / r)^{\kappa |w|}\).

  • (ii) \(O (4)\)-covariance (VI.1). It shows that ground states are invariant under spatial translations, which makes \(M (w)\) well defined. Spatial displacements are rotated into time displacements, which are asymptotically invisible. It also makes \(\mathcal{A}\) commutative: exchanging the time axis with a spatial axis reverses the time order of two clusters inside a four-cluster limit.

  • (iii) Phase reflection \(M (w) = M (\vartheta w)\). Together with \(M (w)^{\ast} = M (\Theta w)\), this makes every phase value on real labels real. Proof sketch. Each detector is the cluster element \(c_E = \sum_i \lambda_i M (\text{word}_i)\) of a finite lattice episode \(E\), whose chessboard budget is \(\text{gap}_k\) (B18, from the corrected-field data). Then:

  • B16 (limit table). For fixed \(n\) and all sufficiently large translations, expand the product into finitely many monomials. Their points are generic: within each cluster this is part of the data, and between clusters each coordinate tie excludes at most one translation parameter. Lemma V.1.v (retention of null spaces). Assume the corrected-field data and detector bounds of V.1.u. If a table \(S'\) is symmetric, respects \(S\), and satisfies \(S' (w) = \chi_{\xi} (M (w))\) for every separated word and some fixed \(\xi\), then \(N_d (S') = N_d (S)\) for every \(d\). Proof sketch.

  • Comparison maps. Let \(F_d^{\text{free}}\) be the free-field space in the corrected-field data. Define \(U_k : F_d^{\text{free}} \to V_d\) by sending its chosen basis to the corrected labels. Compose \(U_k\), the quotient map for \(S'\), and the finite family of \(S'\)-probes to obtain \(f_k : F_d^{\text{free}} \to \mathbb{C}^m\).

  • Injectivity. V.1.t and V.1.u give one-point errors at most \(\text{gap}_k\) and two-point errors at most \(\text{gap}_k (1 + |s| + |s' |)\). The free singleton values converge and are bounded, and the rescaled test points converge. Thus \(f_k\) converges in norm to the injective free test map. Injectivity is open in this finite-dimensional operator space, so \(f_k\) is injective for large \(k\).

  • Conclusion. For all large \(k\), \(q_d U_k\) is also surjective by the spanning hypothesis. If \(v \in N_d (S')\), choose \(z \in F_d^{\text{free}}\) with \(q_d U_k z = q_d v\). Since \(S'\) respects \(S\), \(U_k z \in N_d (S')\), hence \(f_k z = 0\). Injectivity gives \(z = 0\), so \(q_d v = 0\). The reverse inclusion is the respect hypothesis.

Lemma V.1.x (one countable family; B14). There is a single sequence \((b_k)\) of positive words, depending only on the label register and the time axis, whose vectors have dense linear span in every OS Hilbert space realizing the positive-word Gram form of a symmetric smooth table. Proof sketch.

  • Parametrization. For fixed arity and degrees, positive words are parametrized by a separable space: positive separated configurations times label coefficients. Choose countable dense subsets, together with the empty word.

  • Continuity. Inner products of the vectors are values of the table at combined configurations, which are continuous in the points (smoothness) and in the coefficients (finite-dimensional multilinearity). So the vector map is continuous, and the closed span of the countable family contains all vectors.

Lemma V.1.y (almost every phase has a unique ground state).

  • Statement. In the setting of V.1.8, for each positive \(b\), \(P^{\xi}_0 |b \rangle_{\xi} = S^{\xi} (b) \hspace{0.17em} \Omega_{\xi}\) for \(\nu\)-almost every \(\xi\). This proves the uniqueness assertion of V.1.8.

Proof sketch.

  • The variance identity. Let \(\omega_{\xi} = \|P_0^{\xi} |b \rangle_{\xi} \|^2 = \lim_{n \to \infty} S^{\xi} (\Theta \tau_n b \cdot \tau_n b)\). For \(n \in \mathbb{N}\) these functions of \(\xi\) are continuous, nonnegative, decreasing in \(n\) and dominated by their value at \(n = 0\), which is integrable on compact \(X\). Their integrals are the corresponding quantities for \(S\). Dominated convergence gives \(\int \omega_{\xi} \hspace{0.17em} d \nu = \|P_0 |b \rangle \|^2 = \|M (b) \Omega \|^2 = \int | \chi_{\xi} (M (b)) |^2 d \nu\).

  • Almost-everywhere vanishing. Since \(\|P_0^{\xi} |b \rangle - S^{\xi} (b) \Omega_{\xi} \|^2 = \omega_{\xi} - |S^{\xi} (b) |^2 \geqslant 0\) has integral zero, it vanishes a.e.

  • Uniqueness. Apply this to the family \((b_k)\) of V.1.x; the union of the countably many null sets is null. Outside it, \(P_0^{\xi}\) maps a dense set into \(\mathbb{C} \Omega_{\xi}\), so \(\mathcal{H}_0^{\xi} =\mathbb{C} \Omega_{\xi}\). Since \(\nu (X) = 1\), such \(\xi\) exist; the choice is not canonical.

5. Where the difficulty lies

Distinctive arguments.

  • The decomposition uses the OS space of the limit correlations. Three properties require separate arguments:

    • spatial invariance of the ground states (V.1.h);

    • commutativity of \(\mathcal{A}\) (V.1.k);

    • reality of phase values (V.1.n).

  • Lattice input after the limit. It enters at three places only: the bench (V.1.7), phase reflection (V.1.n, through the grid hypothesis), and the detectors (V.1.u).

  • Phase reflection is the least standard argument. It is a variance-positivity statement for far-separated copies of a real lattice variable. It is equivalent to the claim that the abstract characters assign real values to real observables.

  • Detectors. They require quantitative UV input: one- and two-point chessboard deviations \(\text{gap}_k \to 0\) of corrected fields from free values. These estimates preserve the field null spaces in every phase.

Routine but long.

  • B02, B03, B09, B12, B14, B16, B18: smooth dependence on positions, label quotients, finite polynomial limits, separability.

  • SNAG, Paley, Heat (about 1,100 lines) reprove textbook theorems in an explicit form adapted to the library.

Unusual or worth checking.

  1. Selection. The selected phase comes from a measure-theoretic existence argument and is not canonical. No finite bound on the number of phases is asserted. V.3 later proves unique ground states for every phase.

  2. Sub-width in the bench. The bench gives the train bound only at a lattice-compatible width \(s \leqslant d\). The remaining width is absorbed by the contraction \(E_{d - s}\) (V.1.f).

  3. Which symmetries are used where.

    • Commutativity uses covariance only under the exchange of the time axis with one spatial axis. This is a lattice symmetry, already contained in the real-spin part of the hierarchy.

    • Spatial invariance of \(\mathcal{H}_0\) needs arbitrary hyperplane reflections, i.e. the continuum \(O (4)\) action of VI.1. In the Lean, both steps draw on that action.

    • Both use improper elements of \(O (4)\) (reflections), not only rotations.

  4. Scope of the certificate. Its unique-ground-state assertion concerns the time-axis chamber of the selected phase. It also records the hierarchy, field laws and null-space equality for every phase. The continuation linked to the selected Wightman realization uses that selected table.

  5. Smoothness hypothesis in B02. B02 needs the derivative identities of the table on which the slab family lives. For phases these come from B09, which uses the slab laws of \(S\), so the two applications of B02 are ordered.
