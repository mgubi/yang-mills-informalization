1. What this unit proves

The unit fixes the setting of the construction:

  • the periodic lattices and their symmetry group;

  • parallel transport and gauge transformations;

  • the subdivision of a torus into blocks and the schedule of lattice spacings;

  • a combinatorial measure of the size of a lattice set;

  • the gauge group in its defining representation, with its Lie algebra, Casimir constant, local logarithm and nearest-point retraction;

  • the Wilson measure and its invariance;

  • the continuum symmetry groups of \(\mathbb{R}^4\).

The results require no input theorem from another unit. Simplicity of \(\mathfrak{g}\) is assumed only in Proposition I.1.6(d)–(f); the other hypotheses are stated locally.

Conventions. \(G\) is a closed subgroup of \(U (N)\), with \(N \geqslant 1\) fixed. For an integer \(\ell \geqslant 1\), write \(\Lambda_{\ell} = (\mathbb{Z}/ \ell \mathbb{Z})^4\). Axes \(0, 1, 2\) are spatial and axis \(3\) is time; \(e_i\) are the unit vectors. A link \((x, i)\) joins \(x\) to \(x + e_i\). A configuration is a map \(U : \Lambda_{\ell} \times \{0, 1, 2, 3\} \to G\); the same transport and gauge definitions apply on the cover \(\mathbb{Z}^4\). The norm \(\| \cdot \|_F\) is the Frobenius norm on \(M_N (\mathbb{C})\); norms on real coordinate spaces are Euclidean unless marked otherwise.

Proposition I.1.1 (torus geometry). For \(y \in \mathbb{Z}^4\) let \([y]_{\ell} \in \Lambda_{\ell}\) be its reduction mod \(\ell\). For \(x \in \Lambda_{\ell}\) let \(r_{\ell} (x) \in \mathbb{Z}^4\) be the representative with coordinates in \((- \ell / 2, \ell / 2]\) (the centred lift; the tie \(x_i = \ell / 2\) lifts to \(+ \ell / 2\)). Then:

  • \([r_{\ell} (x)]_{\ell} = x\), and \(r_{\ell} (x)\) is the unique \(y\) with \([y]_{\ell} = x\) and \(- \ell < 2 y_i \leqslant \ell\) for all \(i\);

  • reduction is injective on this centred box, and \(| \Lambda_{\ell} | = \ell^4\).

Put \(|x|_{\ell} = \max_i |r_{\ell} (x)_i |\). The separation \(|x - y|_{\ell}\) is a metric on the torus:

  • it is symmetric and is zero exactly when \(x = y\);

  • it satisfies the triangle inequality;

  • it is invariant under translations and under the hyperoctahedral group \(B_4\) of Proposition I.1.2;

  • reduction does not increase distances: \(| [y]_{\ell} |_{\ell} \leqslant \max_i |y_i |\).

Proposition I.1.2 (lattice symmetries, transport, gauge transformations). Let \(B_4\) be the group of signed permutations of the four axes; it has order \(2^4 \cdot 4! = 384\). An element \(\rho = (\pi, \epsilon)\), with \(\epsilon_i \in \{\pm 1\}\), acts on \(\mathbb{Z}^4\) and on \(\Lambda_{\ell}\) by \((\rho x)_{\pi (i)} = \epsilon_i x_i\). On either lattice \(\Lambda\), an affine map is \(A = (\rho, b)\), \(Ax = \rho x + b\), with \(b \in \Lambda\).

A walk \(w\) is a finite word of steps \(\pm e_i\). The transport \(U (x ; w) \in G\) is the ordered product along \(w\) from \(x\): a forward step along the link \((y, i)\) contributes \(U (y, i)\), and a backward step from \(y\) contributes \(U (y - e_i, i)^{- 1}\).

The pullback \(A^{\ast} U\) is

\(\displaystyle (A^{\ast} U) (x, i) = \left\{ \begin{array}{ll} U (Ax, \pi (i)), & \epsilon_i = + 1,\\ U (Ax - e_{\pi (i)}, \pi (i))^{- 1}, & \epsilon_i = - 1, \end{array} \right.\)

that is, the transport of \(U\) along the image of the link \((x, i)\). Then:

  • (a) \(1^{\ast} = \text{id}\) and \(B^{\ast} A^{\ast} = (A \circ B)^{\ast}\), i.e. \((\rho', b')^{\ast} (\rho, b)^{\ast} = (\rho \rho', \rho b' + b)^{\ast}\). For \(A = (1, b)\) the pullback is the translation \((\tau_b U) (x, i) = U (x + b, i)\).

  • (b) \((A^{\ast} U) (x ; w) = U (Ax ; \rho w)\), where \(\rho w\) is the image word.

  • (c) For \(g : \Lambda \to G\) put \(U^g (x, i) = g (x) U (x, i) g (x + e_i)^{- 1}\). Then \(U^g (x ; w) = g (x) \hspace{0.17em} U (x ; w) \hspace{0.17em} g (x_{\text{end}})^{- 1}\); for a closed walk this is conjugation by \(g (x)\).

  • (d) (locality) If \(U\) and \(V\) agree on every link whose two endpoints are visited by \(w\) from \(x\), then \(U (x ; w) = V (x ; w)\).

  • (e) (plaquettes) A plaquette is \(p = (x ; i < j)\) with holonomy \(U_p = U (x ; e_i, e_j, - e_i, - e_j)\). Each \(A\) induces an injective map $A_\sharp $ of plaquettes, bijective on \(\Lambda_{\ell}\). It sends the axis pair to \(\{\pi (i), \pi (j)\}\) in increasing order and the base point to \(Ax - \sum_{k \in \{i, j\} : \hspace{0.17em} \epsilon_k = - 1} e_{\pi (k)}\). For every set \(M\) and every \(\phi : G \to M\) satisfying \(\phi (u^{- 1}) = \phi (u)\) and \(\phi (vuv^{- 1}) = \phi (u)\) for all \(u, v \in G\), one has \(\phi ((A^{\ast} U)_p) = \phi (U_{A_{\sharp} p})\).

  • (f) (periodic extension) For \(b \in \Lambda_{\ell}\) let \((E_b U) (y, i) = U ([y]_{\ell} + b, i)\) on \(\mathbb{Z}^4\). Then:

    • \((E_b U) (y ; w) = U ([y]_{\ell} + b ; w)\);

    • \(E_b ((\rho, a)^{\ast} U) = (\rho, 0)^{\ast} (E_{\rho b + a} U)\) for \(a \in \Lambda_{\ell}\);

    • \(E_b (U^g) = (E_b U)^{g ([\cdot]_{\ell} + b)}\);

    • if \(F\) depends only on links inside \(X \subset \mathbb{Z}^4\), then \(F \circ E_b\) depends only on links inside \([X]_{\ell} + b\).

A function \(F\) of configurations is gauge invariant if \(F (U^g) = F (U)\) for all \(g\). It is local on \(X\) if \(F (U) = F (V)\) whenever \(U, V\) agree on all links with both endpoints in \(X\). These are the notions of (Reg3)–(Reg4) in the main statement. Status: theorem. Proposition I.1.3 (schedule of spacings; subdivision and blocks).

(a) Schedule. A schedule consists of odd integers \(L_m \geqslant 3\) and a strictly increasing sequence of positive integers \(P_p\) with \(4 \mid P_p\), indexed by \(m, p \in \mathbb{N}\). Put

\(\displaystyle D_0 = 1, \quad D_{m + 1} = D_m L_m, \quad a_m = 1 / D_m, \quad \ell (m, p) = P_p D_m, \quad s_m = \log (1 / a_m) .\)

Then:

  • \(D_m\) is odd and strictly increasing;

  • \(a_0 = 1\), \(a_{m + 1} = a_m / L_m\), and \(a_m \downarrow 0\) strictly;

  • \(s_0 = 0\), \(s_{m + 1} = s_m + \log L_m\), and \(s_m \to \infty\);

  • \(4 \mid \ell (m, p)\) and \(a_m \ell (m, p) = P_p\);

  • the placement \(x \mapsto a_m \hspace{0.17em} r_{\ell (m, p)} (x)\) maps \(\Lambda_{\ell (m, p)}\) into \((- P_p / 2, P_p / 2]^4\).

For \(m \leqslant m'\), \(\Lambda_{\ell (m, p)} \to \Lambda_{\ell (m', p)}\) is a subdivision of odd ratio \(L_m \cdots L_{m' - 1}\) that preserves placements.

(b) Mesh inequality. If \(\log L_m / s_m \to 0\) as \(m \to \infty\) (the schedule is gentle), then there is \(c > 0\), depending on the schedule, with \(a_{m + 1} \geqslant c \hspace{0.17em} a_m^2\) for all \(m \in \mathbb{N}\). The ratio defining gentleness is considered for \(m \geqslant 1\), when \(s_m > 0\).

(c) Subdivision. Let \(L \geqslant 1\) be an integer and \(\ell' = \ell L\). Put \(\iota (x) = [L \hspace{0.17em} r_{\ell} (x)]_{\ell'}\). Then:

  • \(r_{\ell'} (\iota x) = L \hspace{0.17em} r_{\ell} (x)\);

  • \(\iota : \Lambda_{\ell} \to \Lambda_{\ell'}\) is an injective group homomorphism commuting with \(B_4\).

The residue map \(\text{res} : \Lambda_{\ell'} \to \Lambda_L\) (reduction mod \(L\)) is additive with \(\text{res} \circ \iota = 0\). The map \((x, y) \mapsto \iota (x) + [r_L (y)]_{\ell'}\) is a bijection \(\Lambda_{\ell} \times \Lambda_L \to \Lambda_{\ell'}\), with inverse \(z \mapsto (\text{par} \hspace{0.17em} z, \text{res} \hspace{0.17em} z)\); moreover \(\text{par} \circ \iota = \text{id}\).

(d) Odd ratio. If \(L\) is odd, then:

  • \(r_L\) commutes with \(B_4\) and \(\text{par} (\rho z) = \rho \hspace{0.17em} \text{par} (z)\);

  • the block \(\text{par}^{- 1} (x)\) is \(\iota (x) + [\{- \tfrac{L - 1}{2}, \ldots, \tfrac{L - 1}{2} \}^4]_{\ell'}\);

  • \(\text{par} (z + e_i) = \text{par} (z) + e_i\) if \(r_L (\text{res} \hspace{0.17em} z)_i = \tfrac{L - 1}{2}\), and \(\text{par} (z + e_i) = \text{par} (z)\) otherwise.

(e) Metric comparison. For every integer \(L \geqslant 1\), all \(x, x' \in \Lambda_{\ell}\) and all \(z, z' \in \Lambda_{\ell'}\):

  • \(|z - \iota \hspace{0.17em} \text{par} \hspace{0.17em} z|_{\ell'} \leqslant L / 2\);

  • \(L \hspace{0.17em} |x - x' |_{\ell} \leqslant | \iota x - \iota x' |_{\ell'}\);

  • \(L \hspace{0.17em} | \text{par} \hspace{0.17em} z - \text{par} \hspace{0.17em} z' |_{\ell} \leqslant |z - z' |_{\ell'} + L\) (foreshortening).

Proposition I.1.4 (cover numbers of lattice sets). For \(X \subset \Lambda_{\ell}\) and \(M \in \mathbb{N}\) let \(\text{halo}_M (X) = \{y : \exists x \in X, |x - y|_{\ell} \leqslant M\}\). Use the graph joining sites at sup-distance at most \(1\); diagonal moves are allowed. Connectivity means that every two vertices can be joined, so the empty set is allowed. A set \(D\) covers \(X\) at scale \(M\) if \(X \subset \text{halo}_M (D)\) and \(\text{halo}_M (D)\) is connected in this graph. Let \(\text{cov}_M (X)\) be the least \(|D|\) over such \(D\); it is finite since \(D = \Lambda_{\ell}\) covers. For all \(X, Y, D \subset \Lambda_{\ell}\) and \(M \in \mathbb{N}\), the following bounds hold independently of \(\ell\):

  • (a) \(X \subset Y\) implies \(\text{cov}_M (X) \leqslant \text{cov}_M (Y)\), and \(\text{cov}_M (X \cup Y) \leqslant \text{cov}_M (X) + \text{cov}_M (Y)\) if \(X \cap Y \neq \emptyset\).

  • (b) \(| \text{halo}_M (D) | \leqslant (2 M + 1)^4 |D|\) and \(|X| \leqslant (2 M + 1)^4 \hspace{0.17em} \text{cov}_M (X)\).

  • (c) \(\text{halo}_M (D)\) is connected in this graph if and only if any two points of \(D\) are joined by a chain in \(D\) with steps of sup-distance at most \(2 M + 1\).

  • (d) (diameter) For \(x, y \in X\): \(|x - y|_{\ell} \leqslant (4 M + 2) \hspace{0.17em} \text{cov}_M (X) + 2 M\).

  • (e) (change of scale) For \(b, k, m \in \mathbb{N}\) with \(b \geqslant 1\) and \(x \in \Lambda_{\ell}\), \(\text{cov}_b (\text{halo}_{kb} \{x\}) \leqslant (2 k + 1)^4\). If \(D\) covers \(X\) at scale \(mb\), then \(\text{cov}_b (X) \leqslant (2 m + 3)^4 |D|\).

  • (f) (coarsening) Let \(\text{par} : \Lambda_{\ell L} \to \Lambda_{\ell}\) be the parent map of a subdivision of integer ratio \(L \geqslant 1\). Let \(X \subset \Lambda_{\ell L}\) and \(M, B, s \in \mathbb{N}\) with \(B \geqslant 1\) and \(M \leqslant 2 B\). Then

\(\displaystyle L \cdot \text{cov}_B \left( \text{halo}_{sB} (\text{par} \hspace{0.17em} X) \right) \leqslant \left( L + 10 \hspace{0.17em} \text{cov}_M (X) \right) \hspace{0.17em} (2 s + 13)^4,\)

with the left side computed on the coarse torus. Thus a fine set of cover number \(k\) has coarse cover number \(\leqslant (2 s + 13)^4 (1 + 10 k / L)\).

Proposition I.1.5 (gauge group, plaquette cost, chord, Haar measure). \(G\) is compact and second countable, with its Borel structure. For \(u \in G\) put

\(\displaystyle \sigma (u) = 1 - \tfrac{1}{N} \text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} \text{tr} \hspace{0.17em} u, \qquad \chi (u) = \|u - 1\|_F / \sqrt{N} .\)

Then:

  • \(\chi^2 = 2 \sigma\), \(0 \leqslant \chi \leqslant 2\), \(0 \leqslant \sigma \leqslant 2\), and \(\sigma (1) = \chi (1) = 0\);

  • \(\chi (uv) \leqslant \chi (u) + \chi (v)\);

  • \(\sigma\) and \(\chi\) are continuous and invariant under \(u \mapsto u^{- 1}\) and \(u \mapsto vuv^{- 1}\).

The Frobenius norm is invariant under left and right multiplication by unitaries, and \(\|u\|_F = \sqrt{N}\). The normalized Haar measure \(\mu_G\) is invariant under left and right translations and under inversion; hence \(u \mapsto vuw\) preserves \(\mu_G\). Status: theorem. Proposition I.1.6 (Lie algebra, colours, Casimir constant, slope constant).

(a) \(\mathfrak{g}= \{X \in M_N (\mathbb{C}) : e^{tX} \in G \forall t \in \mathbb{R}\}\) is a closed real subspace of skew-Hermitian matrices. It is closed under \([X, Y] = XY - YX\) and under \(X \mapsto uXu^{- 1}\), \(u \in G\). Tangent criterion: if \(f : \mathbb{R} \to M_N (\mathbb{C})\) takes values in \(G\), \(f (0) = 1\), and \(f\) is differentiable at \(0\) with \(f' (0) = X\), then \(X \in \mathfrak{g}\).

(b) Colours. \(\langle X, Y \rangle_{\mathfrak{g}} = - \frac{1}{N} \text{tr} (XY)\) is real on \(\mathfrak{g}\) and is an \(\text{Ad} (G)\)-invariant inner product. Fix an orthonormal basis and write \(\mathfrak{g} \cong \mathbb{R}^d\), \(c \mapsto X_c\), \(d = \dim \mathfrak{g}\). Then:

  • \(\|X_c \|_F = \sqrt{N} \hspace{0.17em} |c|\);

  • if \(P_{\mathfrak{g}}\) is the orthogonal projection onto \(\mathfrak{g}\) for the real Frobenius inner product \(\text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} \text{tr} (XY^{\ast})\), define \(\Pi : M_N (\mathbb{C}) \to \mathbb{R}^d\) by \(X_{\Pi A} = P_{\mathfrak{g}} A\). Then \(\Pi X_c = c\) and \(| \Pi A| \leqslant \|A\|_F / \sqrt{N}\);

  • \(\text{Ad} (u) c = \Pi (uX_c u^{- 1})\) is an orthogonal representation of \(G\);

  • \(\text{ad} (c) c' = \Pi [X_c, X_{c'}]\) satisfies \(X_{\text{ad} (c) c'} = [X_c, X_{c'}]\);

  • \(c \mapsto \exp c := e^{X_c} \in G\) is continuous.

(c) \(G\) is simple if two conditions hold: there are \(X, Y \in \mathfrak{g}\) with \([X, Y] \neq 0\), and every real subspace \(I \subset \mathfrak{g}\) with \([\mathfrak{g}, I] \subset I\) is \(0\) or \(\mathfrak{g}\). This agrees with the simplicity hypothesis of the main theorem: its Lie algebra is characterized by the same exponential condition (GaugeGroup.coreSimple, unit VIII.1).

If \(G\) is simple, then:

  • (d) for every symmetric real bilinear form \(B\) on \(\mathbb{R}^d\) satisfying \(B (\text{ad} (x) y, z) = - B (y, \text{ad} (x) z)\) for all \(x, y, z\), there is \(c \in \mathbb{R}\) such that \(B (x, y) = c \langle x, y \rangle\) for all \(x, y\);

  • (e) \(- \text{tr}_{\mathbb{R}^d} (\text{ad} (x) \text{ad} (y)) = C_{\mathfrak{g}} \langle x, y \rangle\) for all \(x, y \in \mathbb{R}^d\), where \(C_{\mathfrak{g}} := - \frac{1}{d} \sum_{i = 1}^d \text{tr}_{\mathbb{R}^d} (\text{ad} (b_i)^2) > 0\) and \((b_i)_{i = 1}^d\) is the standard orthonormal basis;

  • (f) the slope constant \(\kappa := 11 C_{\mathfrak{g}} / (24 \pi^2)\) is positive.

Proposition I.1.8 (local logarithm, nearest point, curvature of a group element). Let \(\mathfrak{g}^{\perp}\) be the orthogonal complement of \(\mathfrak{g}\) in \(M_N (\mathbb{C})\) for \(\text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} \text{tr} (XY^{\ast})\).

(a) There are a map \(\log_G : M_N (\mathbb{C}) \to \mathbb{R}^d\), an open neighbourhood \(V\) of \(1\) in \(M_N (\mathbb{C})\) and an open neighbourhood \(W\) of \(0\) in \(\mathbb{R}^d\) such that:

  • \(\log_G\) is real-analytic on \(V\) and \(\log_G (1) = 0\);

  • \(\log_G (e^{X_c}) = c\) for all \(c \in W\);

  • \(\exp (\log_G u) = u\) for all \(u \in G \cap V\).

It is the first component of the local inverse of \((c, \nu) \mapsto e^{X_c} e^{\nu}\) on \(\mathbb{R}^d \times \mathfrak{g}^{\perp}\). Connectedness of \(G\) is not assumed, and nothing is claimed away from \(1\).

(b) \(u \in G\) is a nearest point to \(X \in M_N (\mathbb{C})\) if \(\|X - u\|_F \leqslant \|X - v\|_F\) for all \(v \in G\). Then:

  • nearest points exist;

  • for every \(v \in G\), \(u\) is nearest to \(X\) iff \(vu\) is nearest to \(vX\), iff \(uv\) is nearest to \(Xv\);

  • if \(u\) is nearest to \(X\), then \(u^{\ast} X - 1 \in \mathfrak{g}^{\perp}\);

  • there is \(\delta > 0\), depending only on \(G\), such that \(X\) has exactly one nearest point whenever \(\|X - v\|_F < \delta\) for some \(v \in G\).

On a neighbourhood of \(1\) the nearest point is \(\text{foc} (X) = \exp (c)\), where \((c, \nu)\) is the local inverse of \(\Psi (c, \nu) = e^{X_c} (1 + \nu)\) at \(X\). As a matrix-valued map, \(X \mapsto \text{foc} (X)\) is real-analytic at \(1\), with strict derivative the orthogonal projection \(P_{\mathfrak{g}}\) onto \(\mathfrak{g}\). Consequently, for every \(\varepsilon > 0\) there is \(\delta > 0\) such that, for all \(X, Y \in M_N (\mathbb{C})\) and \(u, v \in G\), the conditions \(\|X - 1\|_F, \|Y - 1\|_F < \delta\) and \(u, v\) nearest to \(X, Y\) imply

\(\displaystyle \|u - v - P_{\mathfrak{g}} (X - Y)\|_F \leqslant \varepsilon \|X - Y\|_F .\)

(c) The curvature of \(u \in G\) is \(F (u) = \Pi \left( \tfrac{1}{2} (u - u^{- 1}) \right) \in \mathbb{R}^d\), the projected anti-Hermitian part. It is continuous and satisfies:

  • \(F (1) = 0\) and \(F (u^{- 1}) = - F (u)\);

  • \(F (vuv^{- 1}) = \text{Ad} (v) F (u)\);

  • \(|F (u) | \leqslant \chi (u)\);

  • \(\frac{d}{dt} F (\exp (tc)) |_{t = 0} = c\).

Status: theorem. The neighbourhoods and \(\delta\) come from the inverse function theorem and compactness and are not explicit. Proposition I.1.9 (the Wilson measure on the torus). Let \(\mu_{\ell} = \mu_G^{\otimes (\Lambda_{\ell} \times \{0, \ldots, 3\})}\) be the product Haar measure on configurations. Let

\(\displaystyle E_{\ell} (U) = \sum_{x \in \Lambda_{\ell}} \sum_{i < j} \sigma (U_{(x ; i < j)})\)

be the Wilson energy: \(6 \ell^4\) terms, each unoriented plaquette counted once. For every \(\beta \in \mathbb{R}\) put

\(\displaystyle Z_{\ell} (\beta) = \int e^{- \beta E_{\ell}} \hspace{0.17em} d \mu_{\ell}, \qquad d\mathbb{P}_{\ell, \beta} = Z_{\ell} (\beta)^{- 1} e^{- \beta E_{\ell}} \hspace{0.17em} d \mu_{\ell} .\)

For integrable complex \(F\), write \(\langle F \rangle_{\ell, \beta} = \int F \hspace{0.17em} d\mathbb{P}_{\ell, \beta}\). Then:

  • \(E_{\ell}\) is continuous, with \(0 \leqslant E_{\ell} \leqslant 12 \ell^4\);

  • \(Z_{\ell} (\beta) > 0\) and \(\mathbb{P}_{\ell, \beta}\) is a probability measure;

  • every continuous complex function, and every bounded measurable complex function, is \(\mathbb{P}_{\ell, \beta}\)-integrable;

  • for integrable complex \(F\), \(\langle F \rangle_{\ell, \beta} = Z_{\ell} (\beta)^{- 1} \int e^{- \beta E_{\ell}} F \hspace{0.17em} d \mu_{\ell}\).

These are the energy and measure of Definitions 1.3–1.4 of the main statement, with \(h = \mu_G\); the identification is proved in VIII.1 (energy_eq, law_eq). Status: definition, with theorems. Proposition I.1.10 (invariance of the Wilson measure). For every \(\beta \in \mathbb{R}\), every lattice affine map \(A = (\rho, b)\) and every \(g : \Lambda_{\ell} \to G\), the maps \(U \mapsto A^{\ast} U\) and \(U \mapsto U^g\) preserve \(\mu_{\ell}\), \(E_{\ell}\) and \(\mathbb{P}_{\ell, \beta}\).

This covers translations, axis permutations, and reflections \(x_a \mapsto b_a - x_a\). When \(\ell\) is even, the reflection planes pass through sites for even \(b_a\) and bisect links for odd \(b_a\). Hence \(\langle F \circ A^{\ast} \rangle_{\ell, \beta} = \langle F \rangle_{\ell, \beta}\) for every integrable complex \(F\). 3. Strategy

The torus metric comes from the absolute values of centred representatives. Odd subdivision ratios give symmetric residue windows, so the parent map commutes with signed permutations even when the coarse torus has even side. The schedule separates the physical period from the mesh; gentle growth of the block factors gives the mesh inequality. Cover numbers measure the size of lattice sets by connected unions of balls. Sampling a path through a fine cover at intervals of \(LB\) yields the coarsening bound \(O (1 + k / L)\) for a set of fine cover number \(k\), with constants independent of the period.

Affine pullback assigns to each link the transport along its image. Reflections reverse links and shift their base points. The pullback therefore permutes link variables and inverts some of them, preserving product Haar measure. Image plaquette holonomies differ by conjugation and possibly inversion; the cost \(\sigma\) is invariant under both. Summing over the plaquette bijection proves invariance of the energy and hence of the Wilson measure.

For the closed matrix group, the tangent criterion follows by approximating \(e^X\) with \(f (1 / n)^n\). It gives the linear and bracket closure of \(\mathfrak{g}\). The inverse function theorem, together with isolation of \(\exp (\mathfrak{g}^{\perp})\) from \(G\) near \(1\), gives a local logarithm. Applying the same theorem to \(e^{X_c} (1 + \nu)\) identifies the nearest-point retraction and its derivative. Finally, the spectral theorem reduces an invariant symmetric form to an eigenspace; real simplicity forces that eigenspace to be all of \(\mathfrak{g}\). Applied to the negative Killing form, this gives \(C_{\mathfrak{g}} > 0\).

  • (d): let \(A\) be the symmetric operator with \(\langle Ax, y \rangle = B (x, y)\). Invariance of \(B\) and skewness of \(\text{ad}\) give \(A \hspace{0.17em} \text{ad}_x = \text{ad}_x A\). Nonabelianity gives \(d > 0\). Choose a real eigenvalue \(c\) of \(A\); its nonzero eigenspace is an \(\text{ad}\)-invariant subspace, hence an ideal. Simplicity makes it the whole space, so \(A = c \cdot \text{id}\).

  • (e): \(K (x, y) = - \text{tr} (\text{ad}_x \text{ad}_y)\) is symmetric and invariant, by \(\text{ad}_{[x, y]} = [\text{ad}_x, \text{ad}_y]\) and cyclicity, so \(K = c \langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle\). Taking the trace over an orthonormal basis gives \(c = C_{\mathfrak{g}}\).

  • Positivity: \(K (x, x) = \sum_i | \text{ad}_x b_i |^2\), which is \(> 0\) for some \(x\) because \(\mathfrak{g}\) is nonabelian. This forces \(C_{\mathfrak{g}} > 0\), and the positive factor \(11 / (24 \pi^2)\) gives \(\kappa > 0\).

Lemma I.1.t (local logarithm). Proposition I.1.8(a). Proof sketch.

  • \(\Phi (c, \nu) = e^{X_c} e^{\nu}\) on \(\mathbb{R}^d \times \mathfrak{g}^{\perp}\) is analytic, with \(D \Phi (0) (c, \nu) = X_c + \nu\) invertible, so it has a local analytic inverse.

  • Isolation: near \(0\), \(e^{\nu} \in G\) with \(\nu \in \mathfrak{g}^{\perp}\) forces \(\nu = 0\). So \(Y \in \mathfrak{g} \cap \mathfrak{g}^{\perp} = 0\), contradicting \(\|Y\|_F = 1\).

  • For \(u \in G\) near \(1\), write \(u = \Phi (c, \nu)\); then \(e^{\nu} = e^{- X_c} u \in G\) forces \(\nu = 0\).

Lemma I.1.u (nearest point). Proposition I.1.8(b). Proof sketch.

  • Existence by compactness; equivariance because left and right unitary multiplication are Frobenius isometries.

  • First-order condition: if \(1\) is nearest to \(X\), then \(t \mapsto \|e^{tY} - X\|_F^2\) is minimal at \(0\), so \(\langle X - 1, Y \rangle = 0\) for \(Y \in \mathfrak{g}\). The general case follows by left translation.

  • Identification near \(1\): for any choice \(b (X)\) of nearest points, \(\|b (X) - 1\|_F \leqslant 2 \|X - 1\|_F\). Hence \(\nu (X) = b (X)^{\ast} X - 1 \in \mathfrak{g}^{\perp}\) tends to \(0\), and \(X = \Psi (\log_G b (X), \nu (X))\) with both arguments near \(0\). Injectivity of the local diffeomorphism \(\Psi\) gives \(b (X) = \text{foc} (X)\). To obtain one neighbourhood valid for every nearest point, choose \(b (X)\) to differ from \(\text{foc} (X)\) whenever such a nearest point exists. The same argument forces this selection to equal \(\text{foc}\) on a neighbourhood, excluding all such exceptions there.

  • Uniqueness in a tube: translate by \(v^{- 1}\).

  • The derivative of \(\text{foc}\) at \(1\) is \(X_{(\cdot)} \circ \text{pr}_1 \circ D \Psi (0)^{- 1} = P_{\mathfrak{g}}\), and the approximation bound is the strict differentiability of \(\text{foc}\) at \(1\).

Lemma I.1.z (Lorentz group, boosts, cone, Poincaré group). Proposition I.1.11(b)–(d). Proof sketch.

  • The three defining conditions of \(\mathcal{L}\) are stable under products and inverses, and \(\det = 1\) gives Lebesgue invariance.

  • Boosts: the addition law \(B_e (r) B_e (r') = B_e (r + r')\) follows from the addition formulas for \(\cosh, \sinh\), and \(\textsf{m}\) is preserved by \(\cosh^2 - \sinh^2 = 1\). For the determinant: \(RB_e (r) R = B_e (- r)\) gives \(\det B_e (r) = \det B_e (- r) = \det B_e (r)^{- 1}\), and \(\det B_e (r) = \det B_e (r / 2)^2 > 0\), so \(\det = 1\). The cone is preserved by \(| \sinh r| \leqslant \cosh r\). Differentiating the boost formula at \(r = 0\) gives \(K_e\); continuity follows from \(\cosh\) and \(\sinh\).

  • Dual action: the identity \(\langle gx, g^{\vee} p \rangle = \langle x, p \rangle\) is polarization of \(\textsf{m} (x) = \langle x, Rx \rangle\).

  • Cone duality: Cauchy–Schwarz gives \(\langle x, p \rangle \geqslant x_t p_t - | \vec{x} | \hspace{0.17em} | \vec{p} | \geqslant 0\) for \(x, p \in \bar{V}_+\). Conversely, test against \(p = (- \vec{x} / | \vec{x} |, 1)\) when \(\vec{x} \ne 0\), and against \((0, 1)\) otherwise. If \(x \in V_+\) and \(p \in \bar{V}_+ \setminus \{0\}\), then \(p_t > 0\) makes the bound strict.

  • Half-space characterization: for \(x \in \bar{V}_+\), every \(g^{\vee} x\) remains in that cone and has nonnegative time coordinate. If \(x_t < 0\), take \(g = 1\). Otherwise, if \(x \not\in \bar{V}_+\), put \(e = \vec{x} / | \vec{x} |\), choose \(x_t / | \vec{x} | < s < 1\), and set \(r = \text{artanh} \hspace{0.17em} s > 0\) and \(g = B_e (r)\). Then \(g^{\vee} = B_e (- r)\) and \((g^{\vee} x)_t = \cosh r \hspace{0.17em} (x_t - s| \vec{x} |) < 0\).

  • The open cone is characterized by \(x_t > 0\) and \(\textsf{m} (x) > 0\), and is preserved by the cone and quadratic-form identities. A Euclidean rotation fixing time preserves \(| \vec{x} |\) and hence both cones and \(\textsf{m}\); determinant \(1\) places it in \(\mathcal{L}\). Affine composition gives the Poincaré group law. Evaluation at \(0\) recovers the translation, and subtracting it recovers the Lorentz map. Joint continuity follows from linear evaluation; the diagonal measure statement follows from preservation of each factor.

5. Where the difficulty lies

The local logarithm and nearest-point retraction require a closed-subgroup argument that also handles disconnected groups. The coarsening estimate must control arbitrary periodic sets uniformly in the torus side. The invariant-form argument uses simplicity over \(\mathbb{R}\); irreducibility of the defining representation is not assumed. Invariance of the Wilson law reduces to Haar invariance, but reflections require the correct shifts of link and plaquette base points.

The representation \(G \subset U (N)\) fixes the cost and the colour inner product, and hence \(C_{\mathfrak{g}}\) and \(\kappa\). Each positive-axis plaquette is counted once. The later running-coupling estimate says that the ratio of the level-\(m\) coupling to \(s_m\) tends to \(\kappa\), uniformly in the ultraviolet cutoff \(N' \geqslant m\) and the volume index \(p\), for a family satisfying the construction conditions (zeroUp_bound). Its normalization is \(\kappa = 2 b_0\), where \(b_0 = 11 C_{\mathfrak{g}} / (48 \pi^2)\) (b0_normalizes).

Even torus sides create tie points in the centred lift. Absolute values, and hence separation, remain invariant under reflections. Placements commute with signed permutations on the strict interior of the fundamental box. Odd subdivision ratios remove this ambiguity from the residue window, which gives equivariance of the parent map.
