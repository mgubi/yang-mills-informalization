Conventions. These are those of III.9.

  • Levels. Levels \(m = 0, 1, \ldots, N\) run from the coarsest (\(m = 0\), unit spacing) to the finest (\(m = N\), the Wilson lattice).

  • Steps and scales. The step \(m + 1 \to m\) blocks by an odd factor \(L_m \geqslant 3\). The scale is \(s_m = \sum_{i < m} \log L_i\). The level-\(m\) torus at volume index \(p\) has side \(n_p e^{s_m}\), where \(n_p\) is the side of the level-0 torus. III.9 writes \(L_p\) for \(n_p\); the letter \(L\) is kept here for block factors.

  • Slope. \(\kappa = 11 C_G / (24 \pi^2)\) (III.9.1), and \(\kappa > 0\) for simple \(G\).

  • Reference quantities (III.9.2). \(\hat{\beta}_m = \bar{\beta} + \kappa s_m\), \(M_m = e^{(\log \log \hat{\beta}_m)^2}\), the threshold \(t_m = \hat{\beta}_m^{- 1 / 2} M_m\), the cover size \(B_m = \lceil (\log \hat{\beta}_m)^2 \rceil\) and the band \(\mathfrak{B}_m = [\hat{\beta}_m / 2, \hspace{0.17em} 2 \hat{\beta}_m]\).

  • Patch radius. \(R_m = (J_{m + 1} + 3) L_m\) (Patch.radius).

The main results concern numerical choices and a conditional shooting construction:

  1. A long list of requirements can be met simultaneously by choosing the constants in one fixed order. Each requirement has the form "this inequality holds once the block factor, the coupling or the lattice is large enough, given the constants chosen before".

  2. There is a level schedule meeting every entrance condition.

  3. Given the one-step construction as a hypothesis, there is a trajectory of effective actions with \(\beta_{N, p, 0} = \bar{\beta}\) (shooting).

The terminology of sheets, prices and purchases records numerical bounds and their order of choice (glossary, family 1). The unit also contains a group of analytic lemmas of the Gaussian step, filed here by the dependency assignment (III.2.9).

1. What this unit proves

Proposition III.2.1 (the constants, in the order in which they are chosen). Let \(G\) be simple. The construction fixes the following data, in this order.

  1. Sheet (data chosen before the block factors). It consists of the following.

    • A kernel choice \(K\) with branches on every patch.

    • For every stage \(j \in \mathbb{N}\), a stage record \(X_j\) with:

      • a clip with relaxation \(r_j\);

      • derivative order \(k_j \geqslant 6\) and spatial power \(q_j \geqslant 2\);

      • support exponent \(a_j > 0\), width \(w_j > 0\) and path count \(\varpi_j > 0\);

      • drift allowance \(\omega_j \geqslant 0\) and column bound \(\gamma_j \geqslant 1\);

      • the constraint \(r_j w_j \geqslant 12\).

    • A first stage \(j_0 \geqslant 4\).

    • Floors \(f_j \in \mathbb{N}\).

    The sequences \(k_j\), \(q_j\), \(a_j\) are nondecreasing.

  2. Stage block factors \(\ell_j\). They are odd, satisfy \(\ell_j \geqslant \max (3, f_j)\), are nondecreasing in \(j\), and for \(j \geqslant j_0\)

    \(\displaystyle \frac{\omega_j}{\log \ell_j} \leqslant \min \left( \frac{\kappa}{4}, \frac{1}{j^2} \right), \qquad \frac{\log \mathcal{C}_j}{\log \ell_j} \leqslant \frac{1}{j^2}, \qquad \mathcal{C}_j := 1 + \varpi_j + w_j^{- 1} + \gamma_j + r_j + a_j .\)

  3. Smallness exponents \(\theta_j > 0\). They are nonincreasing, with \(\theta_j \leqslant 1 / j\) for \(j \geqslant j_0\).

  4. Delays. These are the following.

    • Entry couplings \(\beta^{\text{ent}}_j \in \mathbb{R}\).

    • Entry scales \(\sigma^{\text{ent}}_j (\bar{\beta})\). These are functions of the stage and of the barrier still to be chosen.

    • Volume shells \(S_j \in \mathbb{N}\) and growth exponents \(g_j \in \mathbb{N}\).

    Delays are ordered componentwise.

  5. Barrier \(\bar{\beta} > 1\). It satisfies \(\beta^{\text{ent}}_{j_0} \leqslant \bar{\beta}\) and \(x^{- 1 / 2} e^{(\log \log x)^2} \leqslant 1\) for all \(x \geqslant \bar{\beta}\).

  6. Wait. This consists of:

    • readiness scales \(\sigma^{\text{rdy}}_j\);

    • windows \(h_j : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{N}\) with \(h_j (s) / s \to 0\) as \(s \to \infty\) (for each fixed \(j\), not uniformly);

    • a minimal period \(n_{\min}\).

    Waits are ordered componentwise by readiness scales, windows and minimal period.

Items 2, 3 and 5 always exist:

  • item 2 exists for every sheet;

  • in item 3, \(\theta\) can be chosen below any prescribed positive sequence;

  • in item 5, \(\bar{\beta}\) can be chosen above any prescribed real number.

Item 4 has no further conditions. In item 6, only the stated sublinearity is required; the readiness scales are real and \(n_{\min} \in \mathbb{N}\).

Status: items 1–6 are definitions (specifications). There are three existence theorems. The existence of item 2 uses \(\kappa > 0\), hence the simplicity of \(G\). The later existence theorems III.2.4–III.2.7 meet specified requirements in this order. Proposition III.2.2 (a level schedule meeting all entrance conditions). Let \(G\) be simple and let data 1–6 of III.2.1 be given. Then there is a level system with the following data:

  • a stage index \(J_m\);

  • step block factors \(L_m = \ell_{J_{m + 1}}\);

  • level-0 sides \(n_p = 4 (M + p + 1)\) for some \(M \in \mathbb{N}\);

  • reference couplings \(\hat{\beta}_m = \bar{\beta} + \kappa s_m\).

It satisfies:

  • (a) identification: the kernel, stage records, \(\ell\), \(\theta\) and \(\bar{\beta}\) are those of III.2.1, and \(J_0 = J_1 = j_0\);

  • (b) entry: \(\beta^{\text{ent}}_{J_{m + 1}} \leqslant \hat{\beta}_m\) for all \(m\), and \(\sigma^{\text{ent}}_{J_{m + 1}} (\bar{\beta}) \leqslant s_m\) whenever \(J_{m + 1} > j_0\);

  • (c) volume: \(n_p \geqslant n_{\min}\), and \(S_j (1 + B_m + B_{m + 1})^{g_j} \leqslant n_p e^{s_m}\) for all \(m, p\), with \(j = J_{m + 1}\);

  • (d) windows: for every \(j \geqslant j_0\) there is a level \(k\) with \(\sigma^{\text{rdy}}_j \leqslant s_k\), \(h_j (s_k) \leqslant k\), and \(J_i = j\) for \(|i - k| \leqslant h_j (s_k)\);

  • (e) the scale conditions of III.9.2:

    • \(J\) is nondecreasing, \(J_{m + 1} \leqslant J_m + 1\) and \(J_m \to \infty\);

    • \(k_{J_m}\), \(q_{J_m}\), \(a_{J_m}\) are nondecreasing in \(m\);

    • \(\hat{\beta}_m > 1\) and \(t_m \leqslant 1\);

    • \(100 (B_m + 1) \leqslant n_p e^{s_m}\) and \(100 (R_m + 1) \leqslant n_p e^{s_{m + 1}}\);

    • \(0 < \theta_{J_m} \leqslant 1 / J_m\), and \(\theta\) is nonincreasing;

    • \(\omega_{J_{m + 1}} / \log L_m \leqslant J_{m + 1}^{- 2}\) and \(\log \mathcal{C}_{J_{m + 1}} / \log L_m \leqslant J_{m + 1}^{- 2}\);

    • \(\log L_m / s_m \to 0\);

  • (f) block control: at every step the kernel is well posed, symmetric under the lattice symmetries and gauge covariant.

The same construction also ensures that every positive integer divides \(n_p\) for infinitely many \(p\).

Proposition III.2.3 (shooting: a selected history from a recipe). Let \(G\) be simple. Fix a level system with

\(\displaystyle \omega_{J_{m + 1}} \leqslant \tfrac{\kappa}{4} \log L_m \qquad \text{for all } m.\)

This holds for every level system of III.2.2, by III.2.1(2).

Recipe. Let a recipe be given: path data \(P^{\mathcal{R}}_{N, m}\) and three predicates (a start test, a retention test at each level, a transition test for consecutive levels).

Admissible representations. A level-\(m\) representation \(\rho\) (III.9: path data, clip, activities, budget point, \(\beta\), \(v\)) is admissible for \((N, p)\) if:

  • its path data are \(P^{\mathcal{R}}_{N, m}\);

  • its budget point, clip and polymer bounds are those of level \(m\): width \(w_{J_m}\), path count \(\varpi_{J_m}\), loop cap \(2 M_m^{\theta_{J_m}}\), allowances \(\hat{\beta}_m^{- 11 / 10}\) and \(e^{- M_m^{1 / 5}}\);

  • it passes the retention test.

Continuous families. A family \(x \mapsto \rho_x\) on an interval is continuous if:

  • \(\beta\) and \(v\) are continuous in \(x\);

  • the address sets of the regular and forced activities do not depend on \(x\);

  • differences of regular activities tend to zero in the sum of budget masses of their derivative bounds, up to the budget order on the healthy domain; differences of forced activities tend to zero in the sum of budget masses of their uniform bounds over all configurations.

Hypotheses. Assume that for every \(N\) and \(p\):

  • (start) There is a continuous family \(\beta \mapsto \rho_{\beta}\), \(\beta \in \mathfrak{B}_N\), of admissible level-\(N\) representations. Each \(\rho_{\beta}\) has coupling \(\beta\), \(v = 0\), density equal to the Wilson weight at \(\beta\), and passes the start test.

  • (advance) Let \(m < N\) and \(l \leqslant u\), and let \(x \mapsto \rho'_x\), \(x \in [l, u]\), be a continuous family of admissible level-\((m + 1)\) representations with \(\beta (\rho'_x) \in \mathfrak{B}_{m + 1}\) for all \(x\). Then there is a continuous family \(x \mapsto \rho_x\) of admissible level-\(m\) representations such that, for every \(x\):

    • the density of \(\rho_x\) is the block-spin push-forward of the density of \(\rho'_x\) at coupling \(\beta (\rho'_x)\);

    • \(| \beta (\rho'_x) - \beta (\rho_x) - \kappa \log L_m | \leqslant \omega_{J_{m + 1}}\);

    • the transition test holds.

Conclusion. There are representations \(\rho_{N, p, m}\), \(0 \leqslant m \leqslant N\), for all \(N, p\), such that:

  • every \(\rho_{N, p, m}\) is admissible with \(\beta_{N, p, m} \in \mathfrak{B}_m\);

  • \(\rho_{N, p, N}\) is the Wilson weight with \(v = 0\);

  • consecutive levels are related as in (advance);

  • \(\beta_{N, p, 0} = \bar{\beta}\);

  • the path data at \((N, m)\) do not depend on \(p\).

The resulting history is selected in the sense of III.9.3. In particular it obeys the running law: for every \(\varepsilon > 0\) there is \(m_0\) such that \(| \beta_{N, p, m} - \hat{\beta}_m | \leqslant \varepsilon s_m\) for all \(m \geqslant m_0\), \(N \geqslant m\) and all \(p\).

Status: theorem, conditional on (start) and (advance).

    • (advance) from the one-step construction with coupling reset (Mallow.faces).

  • The drift hypothesis is derived from III.2.2 by Drifting.pay.

  • A second route, through the step program of III.1/III.9 (Directed.Program), is not used by the main theorem.

Proposition III.2.8 (purchases that persist under all later choices).

(a) Eventually paid properties. A property \(U\) of level systems is eventually paid if the following holds. For every sheet, block factors, smallness exponent and delay \(V\) there is a delay \(X \geqslant V\) such that every level system meeting the entrance conditions of any delay \(W \geqslant X\) (with any barrier and wait) satisfies \(U\). A conjunction of two eventually paid properties is eventually paid.

(b) Diagonal payment of constants. For every real array \(F (n, i)\), the following property is eventually paid. At every level \(m\), \(1 \leqslant 1 + \log \log \hat{\beta}_m\). For every \(n\) there is \(m_n\) such that

\(\displaystyle |F (n, i) | \leqslant 1 + \log \log \hat{\beta}_m \qquad \text{for all } m \geqslant m_n \text{ and all } i \leqslant J_{m + 1} .\)

Likewise, for every \(F : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{R}\), the property "\(F (J_{m + 1}) \leqslant \hat{\beta}_m\) for all \(m\)" is eventually paid.

(c) Countably many step requests. Fix a countable index set with an encoding in \(\mathbb{N}\), and let \((w_{\alpha})\) be a family of requests indexed by it. Each request consists of a floor \(\text{pre}_{\alpha} : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}\) and a cost function of a stage transition \((j, i)\), its block geometry and a stage record; at step \(m\) the record is \(X_{J_m}\). Then there is a single floor \(\text{pre}\), depending only on the family, with the following property. If \(\ell_j \geqslant \text{pre} (j)\) for all \(j\), then every delay can be raised so that every later level system satisfying the entrance conditions for the enlarged delay, with any barrier and wait, satisfies, for all \(\alpha\) and \(n\) with \(\text{code} (\alpha) \leqslant J_n\) and all \(m \geqslant n\):

  • \(\text{pre}_{\alpha} (J_{m + 1}) \leqslant L_m\);

  • the cost of step \(m\) is at most \(1 + \log (2 + \log \hat{\beta}_m)\).

(d) The treasury. For simple \(G\) and every kernel choice there is a plan. A plan fixes a naming of charges and, for every request, a dictionary of finite words and spectra. If the block factors exceed the plan's floor, then every delay can be raised so that every later level system satisfying its entrance conditions is treasury funded: it meets the step requests of the plan (as in (c)) and the inverse charge from level 0 on.

Proposition III.2.9 (analytic estimates used by the purchases). The row and cross norms are componentwise exponentially weighted \(\ell^1\) bounds on displacement kernels. Multilinear kernels use the corresponding sum over displacements and colour indices. The colour spaces are fixed and finite; constants may depend on them. A cross maps coarse fields to fine fields at block size \(L\). All vanishing orders refer to the Fourier symbol at zero momentum.

  • Pullback gain. For \(\sigma, \tau > 0\), \(C \in \mathbb{R}\) and \(d \in \mathbb{N}\), there are \(0 < \eta \leqslant \sigma\) and \(K \geqslant 0\) such that, for every positive odd integer \(L\) with \(\tau \leqslant L \eta\), the following holds. If \(\|A\|_{\tau} \leqslant C / L\), \(\| \nabla^s B\|_{\tau} \leqslant C / L^{1 + |s|}\) for every list of coordinate differences with \(|s| \leqslant d\), and \(R\) has \(\|R\|_{\sigma} \leqslant M\) with \(M \geqslant 0\) and vanishes to order \(d\), then \(\|A^{\textsf{T}} RB\|_{\tau} \leqslant KML^{2 - d}\). pullback_gain

  • Contraction of Hessian differences. For \(\sigma, \tau > 0\) and \(C \in \mathbb{R}\), there is \(N \in \mathbb{N}\) such that, for every positive odd \(L \geqslant N\), crosses \(A, B\) obeying the preceding bounds through order \(3\), and a row \(R\) on one-form components with \(M \geqslant 0\), \(\|R\|_{\sigma} \leqslant M\) and vanishing order \(3\), one has \(\|A^{\textsf{T}} RB\|_{\tau} \leqslant ML^{- 1 / 2}\). large_contraction

  • Soft transfer of multilinear kernels. For \(r, d \in \mathbb{N}\), \(\sigma, w > 0\) and \(C \geqslant 0\), there are \(0 < \eta \leqslant \sigma\) and \(K \geqslant 0\), fixed before \(L\), with the following property. Let \(F\) be a real \((r + 1)\)-linear coefficient kernel with width \(\sigma\) and bound \(B\), supported on tuples whose first site is the origin, and vanishing to order \(d\) in the independent momenta. Let \(L\) be positive and odd with \((r + 2) w \leqslant L \eta\), and let \(Q\) satisfy \(\| \nabla^i Q\|_{(r + 2) w} \leqslant C / L^{1 + |i|}\) for every list \(|i| \leqslant d\). 3. Strategy

Choose the stage records and floors first, then the block factors \(\ell_j\), post-\(L\) costs and smallness exponents, delays, the barrier \(\bar{\beta}\), and finally the wait. Increasing the appropriate threshold meets each requirement: the block factors dominate the stage constants, entry couplings pay later costs, and the wait provides comparison windows and large tori. Persistent requirements combine by taking maxima of floors and chaining delay and wait increases. This gives common constants for III.2.4–III.2.7.

The stage index \(J_m\) advances by at most one per level. It enters a stage only after its entry thresholds are met and stays there long enough for one prescribed comparison window. At each fixed stage the volume bill is bounded by a polynomial in \(\log s\), hence eventually by \(e^s\). Since \(s_m \geqslant m \log 3\), this pays every later volume demand. One period offset pays the finitely many steps of the first plateau.

For fixed \(N, p\), start with the Wilson family over \([\bar{\beta} + (\kappa / 2) s_N, \bar{\beta} + 2 \kappa s_N] \subset \mathfrak{B}_N\). At each step the drift bound sends the endpoint couplings strictly beyond the endpoints of the next window. A refined intermediate value argument selects a smaller parameter interval on which the new family stays in that window, so the next step applies. At level \(0\) the window is \(\{ \bar{\beta} \}\). Summing the drifts upward from there and using \(\omega_{J_{m + 1}} \leqslant \log L_m / J_{m + 1}^2\) gives the running law uniformly in depth and volume.

4.3 Shooting

Lemma 4.9 (running law from the transfer inequality). Consider a history on a level system of III.2.2 with \(\beta_{N, p, 0} = \bar{\beta}\) and \(| \beta_{N, p, i + 1} - \beta_{N, p, i} - \kappa \log L_i | \leqslant \omega_{J_{i + 1}}\) for all \(i < N\).   1. Since \(\hat{\beta}_{i + 1} - \hat{\beta}_i = \kappa \log L_i\) and \(\beta_{N, p, 0} = \hat{\beta}_0\), induction upward from level 0 gives \(| \beta_{N, p, i} - \hat{\beta}_i | \leqslant D_i := \sum_{k = 1}^i \omega_{J_k}\).

The bound is uniform in \(N \geqslant i\) and \(p\). Lean: trajectory.

4.5 The analytic lemmas

Lemma 4.20 (mechanisms behind III.2.9). Under the hypotheses of III.2.9, the estimates follow as follows.

  • Division: analytic division first gives complex coefficient packets on a smaller strip. Taking their real parts and using conjugation symmetry preserves the identity at real momenta and gives the stated real kernels.

  • Pullback gain: divide \(R\) into terms with \(d\) forward differences and move those differences onto \(B\). The two cross bounds contribute \(L^{- 2}\), the sum over fine sites contributes \(L^4\), and the differences contribute \(L^{- d}\), giving \(L^{2 - d}\).

  • Contraction: apply the gain with \(d = 3\) and choose \(N > \max (\tau / \eta, K^2)\). Then \(K / L \leqslant L^{- 1 / 2}\) for \(L \geqslant N\).

  • Multilinear transfer: divide in the independent momenta and distribute the \(d\) differences among the \(r + 1\) crosses. Their bounds contribute \(L^{- (r + 1 + d)}\); summing the free translation gives \(L^4\). The width reserve pays for symmetrization and recentering.

  • Odd-part cancellation: reflection cancels the first odd contribution. The rate calculus controls the remaining second derivative with every exponent strictly below \(2\). The second derivative of a function composed with \(y \mapsto - y\) equals its original second derivative at \(0\).

  • Local read-out: subtract the presentations. Equality of densities expresses the coefficient difference as the difference of the regular remainders on the healthy domain. The local projection kills nonwrapping supports. A wrapping support has an exponentially small contribution, bounded using the side condition; finitely many test functionals then control the coefficient norm.

5. Where the difficulty lies

  • Order and persistence. Each floor must be fixed before the block factors that pay it. Costs depending on the chosen factors can be paid by later entry thresholds. Delay and wait suppliers must remain valid under all subsequent increases; otherwise chaining them does not give a common set of constants.

  • Uniformity in depth and volume. The constants and schedule are common to every \(N, p\). The finite first plateau is handled by one period offset \(M\), giving \(n_p = 4 (M + p + 1)\). Later volume conditions follow from the exponential growth of the side length.

  • Continuity during shooting. Each new interval must keep every member of the family inside the next coupling band. The nested-interval argument preserves this condition while fixing the endpoint couplings. It requires continuity of the full representation family at each new step.

  • Two drift bounds. The bound \(\omega_j \leqslant (\kappa / 4) \log \ell_j\) gives the shooting gates. The bound \(\omega_j \leqslant \log \ell_j / j^2\) gives the running law. Both are paid after the stage constants are fixed.

  • Eventual payment. The entry thresholds can absorb any fixed real array, including one evaluated after the block factors are chosen. Each fixed row is paid eventually; a single level need not pay every row. The stronger all-stage condition for the zeroth post-\(L\) row is purchased separately.
