The reflected Gram form of the free Maxwell Wick algebra is strictly positive. It identifies the free field spaces with on-shell polynomial labels and supplies injective two-point tests (VII.3.1–VII.3.5). At degree four, rigidity gives finite position equations with invertible linearization and a leading comparison with lattice perturbation theory (VII.3.6). Independently, the trace anomaly excludes every finite Wick profile (VII.3.7). The lattice series used in the position comparison are defined in §4.7.

1. What this unit proves

Setting.

Write \(\text{Conf}_n = \{(x_1, \ldots, x_n) \in (\mathbb{R}^4)^n : x_i \ne x_j \text{ for } i \ne j\}\).

Coefficients and jets.

  • \(\mathfrak{g}\) is the Lie algebra of the gauge group, with its invariant inner product. \(\mathfrak{g}= 0\) is allowed in VII.3.1–VII.3.5.

  • For \(j \geqslant 0\), \(\text{Sol}_j\) is the real space of \(\mathfrak{g}\)-valued 2-forms \(S = (S_{\mu \nu})\) on \(\mathbb{R}^4\) that satisfy:

    • the coefficients are homogeneous polynomials of degree \(j\);

    • \(dS = 0\) and \(\delta S = 0\), where \(\delta\) is the codifferential (Sol, onShell).

  • Such \(S\) are harmonic (Sol.harmonic).

  • \(\text{Sol}_j\) is the space of on-shell \(j\)-jets: the \(j\)-th Taylor coefficient of a solution of the free Maxwell equations lies in \(\text{Sol}_j\).

  • \(S (x)\) denotes the polynomial 2-form.

  • Write \(T_j F = (j!)^{- 1} D^j F\) for a Taylor coefficient. The inner product \(\langle S, T \rangle\) sums the color inner products over both ordered form indices and all ordered derivative indices; there is no factor \(1 / 2\) for the antisymmetric form indices.

  • \(\text{Tow}_n := \bigoplus_{j < n} \text{Sol}_j\) (ProbeTower).

Wick polynomials.

  • Fix a basis of each \(\text{Sol}_j\). Put \(\mathcal{W}=\mathbb{C} [X_s]_{s \in \textsf{S}}\) with \(\textsf{S} = \{(j, a)\}\) (WickPol, Slot).

  • \(X_{(j, a)}\) is the \(a\)-th coordinate of the \(j\)-th on-shell Taylor coefficient of the free curvature at a point.

  • For \(p \in \text{Tow}_n\), \(\ell_p \in \mathcal{W}\) is the real linear polynomial \(A \mapsto \sum_{j < n} \langle p_j, A_j \rangle\) (digit, code).

Free moments.

  • \(C_{st} (x - y)\) is the free covariance of jet coordinate \(s\) at \(x\) with jet coordinate \(t\) at \(y\) (Wick.entry, legKernel). It is smooth off the diagonal and built from derivatives of \(\Gamma (z) = (4 \pi^2 |z|^2)^{- 1}\).

  • For \(x \in \text{Conf}_n\), the free moment \(\langle P_1 (x_1) \cdots P_n (x_n) \rangle_0\) is multilinear (Wick.moment). On monomials it is the sum, over perfect matchings of the variable occurrences with no pair inside one factor, of \(\prod_{\text{pairs}} C_{st} (x_i - x_k)\).

  • These are the Wick-ordered correlations of \(\dim \mathfrak{g}\) independent free Maxwell curvatures. Multiplication in \(\mathcal{W}\) therefore represents Wick multiplication at one insertion.

Reflected pairing.

  • For \(z \ne 0\), \(\mathcal{R}_z v = v - 2 \langle v, z \rangle |z|^{- 2} z\) is the reflection in \(z^{\perp}\) (face). Orthogonal transformations act on all tensor indices of jets and on polynomials by \((R \cdot P) (A) = P (R^{- 1} \cdot A)\) (pullSol).

  • \(\bar{P}\) conjugates the coefficients of \(P\) (barPol).

  • For \(x \ne y\) (duplex, aligned):

\(\displaystyle \langle P, Q \rangle_{x, y} := \left\langle P (x) \hspace{0.17em} (\mathcal{R}_{x - y} \bar{Q}) (y) \right\rangle_0 .\)

  • This is the local two-point Osterwalder–Schrader pairing for reflection in the perpendicular bisector of \([x, y]\), with the convention that the first argument is linear and the second conjugate-linear.

Tower Gram form.

  • On \(\text{Tow}_n\) the restriction to linear polynomials is \(B_{x - y} (p, q) := \langle \ell_p, \ell_q \rangle_{x, y}\). It is real and depends only on \(z = x - y\).

  • Explicitly, \(B_z (p, q) = \sum_{j, k < n} \text{scan}_z (p_j, q_k)\), where \(\text{scan}_z (P, Q)\) is the free covariance of \(\langle P, T_j F \rangle (z)\) with \(\langle \mathcal{R}_z Q, T_k F \rangle (0)\) (towerB, scan).

  • The identification of the two descriptions is crash_test together with join_crash.

Proposition VII.3.1 (strict reflected positivity of the free Wick algebra). Let \(x \ne y\) in \(\mathbb{R}^4\). (a) For every \(P \in \mathcal{W}\): \(\langle P, P \rangle_{x, y} > 0\) if and only if \(P \ne 0\). (b) \(\overline{\langle P, Q \rangle_{x, y}} = \langle Q, P \rangle_{x, y}\) for all \(P, Q \in \mathcal{W}\). (c) For every finite \(\mathbb{C}\)-linearly independent family \((V_i)\) in \(\mathcal{W}\), the matrix \((\langle V_j, V_i \rangle_{x, y})_{ij}\) is positive definite. No simplicity or positive-dimension assumption is needed, and the polynomial degree is unrestricted. Self-pairings are real, so the inequality in (a) is a real inequality. Status: theorem. Only (a) lies on the path to the main theorem; (b) and (c) are proved but not used. Lean: (a) reflected_positive; (b) reflected_symm; (c) reflected_pd.

Proposition VII.3.2 (tower Gram form and Fock structure). Let \(x \ne y\), \(z = x - y\), \(n \in \mathbb{N}\). (a) \(B_z\) is symmetric and positive definite on \(\text{Tow}_n\). More precisely, there is a block-unitriangular automorphism \(\sigma_z\) of \(\text{Tow}_n\) (Lemma VII.3.f) such that

\(\displaystyle B_z (\sigma_z p, \sigma_z q) = \sum_{j < n} \pi_j (z) \hspace{0.17em} \langle p_j, q_j \rangle, \qquad \pi_j (z) = \frac{(j + 1) (j + 2) \hspace{0.17em} 2^j}{\pi^2 \hspace{0.17em} |z|^{2 j + 4}} > 0.\)

(b) Let \(b_1, \ldots, b_N \in \text{Tow}_n\) satisfy \(B_z (b_i, b_k) = d_i \delta_{ik}\), with any \(d_i \in \mathbb{R}\). Let \(\Phi_b : \mathbb{C} [Y_1, \ldots, Y_N] \to \mathcal{W}\) be the algebra homomorphism with \(Y_i \mapsto \ell_{b_i}\) (encode). Then

\(\displaystyle \langle \Phi_b p, \Phi_b q \rangle_{x, y} = \sum_{\alpha} p_{\alpha} \hspace{0.17em} \overline{q_{\alpha}} \hspace{0.27em} \alpha ! \hspace{0.17em} d^{\alpha}, \qquad \alpha ! \hspace{0.17em} d^{\alpha} := \prod_i \alpha_i ! \hspace{0.17em} d_i^{\alpha_i} .\)

Proposition VII.3.3 (Wick recursion; characterization of the free moments). Let \(n \geqslant 1\), \(x \in \text{Conf}_n\), \(W \in \mathcal{W}^n\), \(1 \leqslant i \leqslant n\) and \(s \in \textsf{S}\). Let \(\partial_s^{y, y'}\) be the \(\mathbb{C}\)-linear derivation of \(\mathcal{W}\) with \(\partial_s^{y, y'} X_t = C_{st} (y - y')\). Then

\(\displaystyle \langle W_1 (x_1) \cdots (W_i X_s) (x_i) \cdots W_n (x_n) \rangle_0 = \sum_{k \ne i} \langle W_1 (x_1) \cdots (\partial_s^{x_i, x_k} W_k) (x_k) \cdots W_n (x_n) \rangle_0,\)

where on the right the factor at \(x_i\) is \(W_i\). Conversely, for any \(n \geqslant 0\) and \(x \in \text{Conf}_n\), a \(\mathbb{C}\)-multilinear map \(T : \mathcal{W}^n \to \mathbb{C}\) with \(T (1, \ldots, 1) = 1\) satisfying this recursion for every \(i, s, W\) is the free moment at \(x\). Consequences:

  • \(\langle \cdots \rangle_0\) is invariant under any simultaneous \(O (4)\) rotation of points and polynomials.

  • \(\langle \bar{P}_1 (x_1) \cdots \bar{P}_n (x_n) \rangle_0 = \overline{\langle P_1 (x_1) \cdots P_n (x_n) \rangle_0}\).

Status: theorem. Lean: Pair.remove, Pair.extractVar; characterization byAnnihilators; consequences wick_rotate, wick_bar.

Proposition VII.3.4 (the free table is faithful on on-shell labels).

Setting:

  • \(\text{Label}_d\) is the space of complex polynomials in radial potential jets that are invariant under the adjoint action of the gauge group and have engineering weight at most \(d\); an order-\(j\) slot has weight \(j + 2\) (II.1, III.16; Label).

  • \(\omega_d : \text{Label}_d \to \mathcal{W}\) evaluates a label on free on-shell jets (observable).

  • \(\text{sh}_d := \omega_d (\text{Label}_d)\) (shellSector).

  • The free table (freeTable, IV.1) has correlations \(\langle \omega (a_1) (x_1) \cdots \omega (a_n) (x_n) \rangle_0\).

  • Its field space \(E_d\) (the glossary's \(F_d\), for the free table) is the space of degree-\(d\) reference labels modulo null labels.

Statements: (a) For every \(d \geqslant 0\), let \(P \in \text{sh}_d\). Suppose \(\langle P (x_0) Q_1 (x_1) \cdots Q_n (x_n) \rangle_0 = 0\) for every \(n \geqslant 0\), every choice of degrees \(d_1, \ldots, d_n \geqslant 0\), all \(Q_i \in \text{sh}_{d_i}\) and all \(x \in \text{Conf}_{n + 1}\). Then \(P = 0\). Hence:

  • the null labels of the free table at degree \(d\) are exactly the labels with \(\omega_d (a) = 0\);

  • \(\omega_d\) induces a linear isomorphism \(E_d \cong \text{sh}_d\). Write \(\varpi_d : E_d \hookrightarrow \mathcal{W}\) for the resulting injection.

(b) For every basis \((b_i)_{i \in I}\) of \(E_d\) and every \(x \ne y\), the map \(v \mapsto \left( \langle \varpi v (x) \hspace{0.17em} \varpi b_i (y) \rangle_0 \right)_{i \in I}\) is injective.

(c) Fix a Wilson family \(C\), any table \(S\) on its register, and a pair satisfying \(x_{\mu} \ne y_{\mu}\) for each coordinate \(\mu\), for example \((x, y) = (0, (1, 1, 1, 1))\). For every \(d \geqslant 0\) there is a column system (IV.1) for \(E_d\). It consists of:

  • a complex basis fixed by conjugation, with the unit first, whose elements are images of frame labels with recorded weight and polynomial length;

  • the pair readings of (b) against this basis at \((x, y)\), which are injective.

Status: theorem. Part (a) enters the rank comparisons of III.11 and III.12; (c) supplies columns for III.11 and III.2. The embedding \(\varpi_d\) is used in VII.1. Proposition VII.3.5 (covariance of the free table). For every gauge group in the setting above, the free table has the following symmetries:

  • its correlations are invariant under the signed coordinate permutations, acting on points and labels;

  • conjugating the labels conjugates the correlations.

In addition, \(O (4)\) acts on each \(E_d\) by complex-linear isomorphisms satisfying \(\varpi_d (R \cdot v) = R \cdot (\varpi_d v)\). Status: theorem. The proof uses the \(O (4)\)-covariance of the free kernel, not any reconstruction theorem. Lean: table_spin, rideO, ride.

Proposition VII.3.6 (rigidity at degree four; position equations). Parts (a) and (c) assume that \(\mathfrak{g}\) is simple and nonabelian. Part (b) holds for any gauge group in the setting above.

Buffers. For a degree \(d\), a buffer (buffers) is a \(\mathbb{C}\)-linear map \(B : E_d \to E_d\) such that:

  • \(B\) kills the unit;

  • for each \(e < d\), \(B\) preserves the subspace of fields of degree \(\leqslant e\);

  • \(B\) commutes with conjugation and with the signed coordinate permutations.

Buffers form a finite-dimensional real space \(\text{Buf}_d\).

Stationarity. \(B\) is stationary if

\(\displaystyle \sum_{j = 1}^r \langle v_1 (x_1) \cdots (Bv_j) (x_j) \cdots v_r (x_r) \rangle_0 = 0 \quad \text{for all } r, v \in E_d^r, x \in \text{Conf}_r .\)

Equivalently, the change of identification \(v \mapsto (1 + tB) v\) of degree-\(d\) fields leaves all free correlations unchanged to first order in \(t\).

(a) Rigidity. Every stationary buffer of degree \(4\) vanishes. In fact \(B \in \text{Buf}_4\) vanishes as soon as both of the following hold, with \(e\) the unit vector of coordinate axis \(0\):

  • \(\langle Bv \rangle_0 = 0\) for all \(v\);

  • \(\langle (Bv) (e) \hspace{0.17em} w (0) \rangle_0 + \langle v (e) \hspace{0.17em} (Bw) (0) \rangle_0 = 0\) for all \(v, w\).

For \(d < 4\), \(\text{Buf}_d = 0\).

(b) Square systems. Suppose every stationary buffer of degree \(d\) vanishes. Then there are finitely many sections \((r_i, p_i, f_i)_{1 \leqslant i \leqslant k}\), with \(r_i \geqslant 0\), \(p_i \in E_d^{r_i}\) and \(f_i \in C_c^{\infty} (\text{Conf}_{r_i} ; \mathbb{C})\), and a real-linear map \(g : \mathbb{R}^k \to \mathbb{R}^m\) with \(m = \dim_{\mathbb{R}} \text{Buf}_d\), such that the map

\(\displaystyle B \longmapsto g \left( \text{Re} \int f_i (x) \sum_j \langle p_{i 1} (x_1) \cdots (Bp_{ij}) (x_j) \cdots \rangle_0 \hspace{0.17em} dx \right)_{i \leqslant k}\)

is an isomorphism \(\text{Buf}_d \to \mathbb{R}^m\).

  • This map is the derivative in the buffer direction, at the free data and \(B = 0\), of the position chart \((w, B) \mapsto g \left( \text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} w_i ((1 + B) p_i) \right)_i\). Here \(w_i\) ranges over complex-multilinear forms on \(E_d^{r_i}\).

  • The system is called square, and clean (all profiles are test functions).

  • For simple nonabelian \(\mathfrak{g}\) and \(d = 4\) such a system exists.

(c) Leading position comparison at degree four. Hypotheses:

  • \(b\) is a final record over the Wilson family, with limit table \(S\).

  • \(X = (r_k, U_{k, d}, \xi_k)\) is a scaling sequence: \(r_k > 0\) decreases strictly to \(0\), \(\xi_k > 0\), and \(U_{k, d} : E_d \to F_d (S)\) is a complex-linear isomorphism for every \(k, d\). Write \(U_k = U_{k, 4}\).

  • Along \(X\), the leading free-Maxwell comparison of VI.2 holds, including eventual compatibility of the frames with the unit, conjugation, signed permutations and degree inclusions.

  • \(Q\) is a clean square degree-four system. The map \(\varphi\) satisfies the four conditions in VII.3.r: a local analytic implicit solution, convergence of the order-zero lattice data, the formal position identities, and an actual leading comparison along \(X\).

Put \(B_k := \varphi (m_Q (k))\), where \(m_Q (k)\) are the tested rescaled correlations \(\int f (x) \langle U_k v_1 (r_k x_1) \cdots \rangle_S \hspace{0.17em} dx\) on basis fields. Then:

  • \(B_k \to 0\);

  • eventually \(1 + B_k\) is invertible, and the corrected frames \(U_k (1 + B_k)\) satisfy the position equations of \(Q\) exactly;

  • eventually \(U_k (1 + B_k)\) commutes with conjugation and signed permutations, maps the free unit to the unit of \(S\), and is compatible with the frames of degrees \(\leqslant 3\) under the inclusions \(E_d \subset E_4\);

  • for every clean degree-four system \(R\) and every integer \(L \geqslant 2\),

\(\displaystyle m_R^{B_k} (k) - \text{chart}_R (\pi_R^{(0)} (L, k), \varphi (\pi_Q^{(0)} (L, k))) \longrightarrow 0.\)

Here \(m_R^{B_k} (k)\) is the tested reading after replacing each field \(v\) by \(U_k (1 + B_k) v\), and \(\pi_R^{(0)} (L, k)\) is the array of order-zero lattice readings on basis fields at spacing \(L^{- k}\).

These conclusions, together with the comparisons for all clean systems described in VII.3.r, give clause (D15) of the detailed main theorem.

Proposition VII.3.7 (exclusion of finite Wick profiles; assigned to VII.2 and VIII.1).

Definitions:

  • \(G_{\Theta} (r)\) is the two-point function of the centered trace \(\Theta\) of the stress tensor in the selected phase, at the points \(\mp \frac{r}{2} e_{\text{t}}\) on the time axis (traceTwo).

  • A mode is \((\textsf{m}, j, q, h)\) with \(\textsf{m} \geqslant 0\), \(j, q \in \mathbb{N}\), and \(h : \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}\) continuous with \(h (tp) = t^j h (p)\) for every \(t > 0\) and \(p \in \mathbb{R}^3\).

  • For \(r > 0\), its integrated kernel is \(K_{\textsf{m}, q, h} (r) = \int_{\mathbb{R}^3} E^q \hspace{0.17em} h (p) \hspace{0.17em} e^{- rE} (2 E)^{- 1} dp\), with \(E = \sqrt{\textsf{m}^2 + |p|^2}\).

  • A Wick profile is a finite sum \(W = \sum_{\alpha} c_{\alpha} \prod_{\beta} K_{\alpha \beta}\) with \(c_{\alpha} \in \mathbb{C}\); empty products equal \(1\) (Mode, Mode.kernel, Track, wickProfile; statement.md, Definition 1.15).

(a) Suppose \(r^8 \log^2 (1 / r) \hspace{0.17em} G_{\Theta} (r) \to A\) as \(r \to 0^+\), with \(A > 0\). Then for every Wick profile \(W\) and every \(\delta > 0\) there is \(r \in (0, \delta)\) with \(G_{\Theta} (r) \ne W (r)\). Only this asymptotic law of \(G_{\Theta}\) is used. (b) Under the same asymptotic hypothesis, in the selected reconstructed theory, no finite sum of the corresponding mass-shell contraction integrals reproduces the Wightman two-point function of \(\Theta\) on every product \(\bar{f} \otimes g\) of Schwartz tests for which \(\psi_{f, g} = \overline{\hat{f}} \hspace{0.17em} \hat{g}\) is compactly supported, using the positive-sign Fourier convention. A product of mode kernels corresponds to testing \(\psi_{f, g}\) at the sum of their positive-energy on-shell momenta, with the product of their densities \(E^q h (p) / (2 E)\) (waveTest_product). Thus, for every proposed finite sum, some such pair \(f, g\) gives different readings.

With \(A = 3 \dim \mathfrak{g}/ \pi^4\) from VII.2, (a) is clause (T4) of the main theorem. Status: theorem. (a) is assigned to VII.2 and (b) to VIII.1. Lean: (a) Wick.euclidean_excludes, Wick.no_powerlog; public form publicTrace for Hierarchy.TraceTest. (b) Wick.polynomial_excludes. The asymptotic law and related polynomial-field exclusions occur in Headline.TraceExclusions.

The Euclidean conclusion concerns the single function \(G_{\Theta}\) along one axis. The excluded class allows arbitrary complex coefficients and continuous homogeneous numerators, but only finitely many modes and products. It does not include general continuous-mass generalized free fields or infinite Wick expansions.

3. Strategy

The reflected covariance couples different jet orders. Hobson's formula identifies the field of an order-\(j\) multipole with \(s_j\) times its Kelvin transform. Translating the polynomial multipole by the negative inverted separation and rescaling its Taylor components produces a triangular change of probes whose response has no Taylor terms below order \(j\) at the second point. The reflected covariance becomes diagonal, with positive pivots \(\pi_j\). Wick recursion extends this to polynomial monomials, with factorial norms \(\alpha !d^{\alpha}\).

Strict positivity detects every nonzero on-shell label. It identifies the free quotient and gives injective two-point columns. At degree four, the explicit quadratic basis reduces buffer rigidity to a coefficient calculation: two-point stationarity gives weighted antisymmetry, while signed permutations give symmetry or zero. Compactly supported tests convert this rigidity into finitely many equations with invertible derivative. The implicit function theorem solves them along the scaling sequence.

Independently, finite Wick profiles have expansions in integer powers of distance and nonnegative powers of its logarithm. Their real parts cannot have the trace field's leading term \(Ar^{- 8} (\log (1 / r))^{- 2}\). Comparison along \(r = 2^{- n}\) proves the contradiction. The spectral representation transfers equality of tested Wightman two-point functions to equality of the Euclidean profiles.

4.7 The formal lattice perturbation theory

Definitions VII.3.v. Fix a finite periodic four-dimensional lattice. The perturbation theory uses inversion-odd link potentials,

\(\displaystyle A (- x - e_{\mu}, \mu) = - A (x, \mu) .\)

Its definitions are:

Gauge-fixed linearization and Gaussian measure.

The links are \(U = \exp (A)\). The action \(S\) is the finite-regulator Wilson term plus the quadratic Feynman gauge-fixing term \(\tfrac{1}{4} \sum_x | \text{div} \hspace{0.17em} A (x) |^2\) (action).

The Haar Jacobian \(J\) and the normalized Faddeev–Popov determinant \(\text{FP}\) are smooth at \(0\), with \(J (0) = \text{FP} (0) = 1\). The latter is the determinant of the gauge-condition derivative on inversion-even site parameters fixed to zero at the root, divided by its flat value (Orb.FP). The matrix determinant, logarithmic determinant and inverse estimates come from the matrix calculus listed in the header.

The centered Gaussian probability has covariance equal to the inverse Hessian of this gauge-fixed action at \(0\) (Orb.data).

Formal series in the coupling.

Write \(S_j (A) = (j!)^{- 1} D^j S (0) [A, \ldots, A]\). After the rescaling \(A \mapsto gA\), the formal weight is

\(\displaystyle w (g, A) = \exp \hspace{-0.17em} \left( - \sum_{i \geqslant 1} g^i S_{i + 2} (A) \right) (J \hspace{0.17em} \text{FP}) (gA),\)

where the last factor denotes its formal Taylor series at \(g = 0\).

\(\mathbb{E}_0\) is the Gaussian expectation, taken coefficientwise.

The normalized expectation is \(\langle f \rangle_{\text{pert}} =\mathbb{E}_0 (wf) /\mathbb{E}_0 (w)\) (Orb.weight, Orb.expect, Orb.normalize).

Every coefficient is continuous with at most polynomial growth, hence Gaussian-integrable. Since \(w (0, A) = 1\), the denominator has constant coefficient \(1\) and is invertible as a formal series.

Lattice readers of labels. Substitute the lattice jet series plus \(iZ\) into each polynomial label, where \(Z\) is an independent centered real Gaussian jet with the prescribed covariance, and average in \(Z\) separately within each insertion. Multiply these averaged insertions and take the normalized formal expectation. The separate auxiliary averages implement Wick subtraction without auxiliary contractions between different insertions (Full.column, Full.amplitude).

Profiles. The profile is the lattice Riemann sum

\(\displaystyle \sum_o h^{4 r} f (\iota_h (o)) \hspace{0.17em} \text{amplitude} (o), \qquad h = L^{- m}, \text{side } 4 L^{m^2 + m + 1},\)

where \(o\) ranges over \(r\) lattice sites and \(\iota_h\) takes the chosen integer lifts relative to the quarter-side origin and multiplies them by \(h\) (Full.profile).

The order-zero profiles enter VII.3.r through the convergence theorem of VII.1. Their higher coefficients enter the formal position identities and the degree-four parity assertion assembled in VIII.1.

Orb.finite_agreement shows that retaining the interaction series, density series and insertions only through coupling order \(k\) preserves every normalized coefficient through order \(k\). The required action Taylor coefficients extend through order \(k + 2\).

5. Where the difficulty lies

The main calculation is the simultaneous diagonalization of all jet orders (VII.3.d–h). The Taylor normalization and the sum over ordered form indices determine the Kelvin constants and positive pivots; changing either convention changes the formulas. Recentring at the inverted separation eliminates all lower Taylor coefficients, so positivity follows from a triangular change of basis.

At degree four, the two-point form is indefinite before reflection. Rigidity uses its nonzero diagonal rates together with signed-permutation symmetry: equal reflection characters force symmetric coefficients and equal rates, while the two-point condition forces weighted antisymmetry. Full rotational equivariance of the buffer is unnecessary.

The position comparison is leading-order only. Its formal identities at every coupling order follow from the local implicit equation at a finite regulator; they give no all-order comparison with the interacting theory. The free quotient and its injective columns likewise give no interacting rank bound without the comparison estimates of III.11.

The non-freeness argument is independent of Gram positivity. Its analytic input is the power-log expansion of the massive Laplace moments, including the remainder estimates needed for integration. It excludes inverse-logarithmic leading behavior; logarithms with nonnegative powers already occur in free massive kernels.

7. What remains unexplained

  1. Image versus intrinsic invariant algebra. The statements use \(\text{sh}_d = \omega_d (\text{Label}_d)\) exactly. An intrinsic characterization as all gauge-invariant on-shell polynomials of weight at most \(d\) would require a separate surjectivity argument; no such characterization is needed here.

The estimates that turn these into interacting rank bounds and compatible interacting field spaces are inputs from III.11 and III.12; their proofs are outside this unit's verification.

  3. Continuum Wick subtraction. The auxiliary imaginary Gaussian average in VII.3.v is the exact lattice definition. Its comparison with continuum Wick-ordered correlations uses VII.1's order-zero profile theorem. The underlying regulator-removal and Wick-subtraction estimates are accepted as VII.1 inputs; their proofs are not checked here.

  4. Further non-freeness statements. The larger trace-exclusion package also contains exclusions involving embeddings into Maxwell Fock spaces and invariant averages. Their proofs are not covered by VII.3.7, which treats finite mass-shell contraction profiles and their tested two-point identities.
