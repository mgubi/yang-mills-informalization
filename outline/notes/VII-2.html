1. What this unit proves

The unit defines the centered trace \(\Theta\) and proves four results about it:

  • the trace anomaly at the level of fields: at scale \(r\), \(\Theta\) equals \(- \kappa_G \beta (r)^{- 1} \mathcal{O}+ o (\beta (r)^{- 1})\), where \(\beta (r) \sim \kappa_G \log (1 / r)\) is the running inverse coupling;

  • the resulting short-distance law \(r^8 \log^2 (1 / r) \hspace{0.17em} G_{\Theta} (r) \to 3 \dim \mathfrak{g}/ \pi^4\), and the corresponding law for the spectral measure;

  • the uniqueness (rank one) of the conserved stress tensor;

  • two exclusions of free-field descriptions.

Most of the lines prove the field-level anomaly, Proposition VII.2.4. That proof is a renormalization-group argument: a lattice Ward identity is transported through the block-spin steps, and a one-loop computation fixes the coefficient.

Proposition VII.2.1 (the centered trace). Let

\(\displaystyle \Theta := \sum_{\mu} T_{\mu \mu} - \left\langle \sum_{\mu} T_{\mu \mu} \right\rangle_{\xi_{\ast}} \boldsymbol{1} \in F_4 .\)

(a) \(\langle \Theta \rangle_{\xi_{\ast}} = 0\). \(\Theta\) is real, invariant under every \(K \in O (4)\) for the rotation action of VI.1, and fixed by the time-reflection adjoint. The same holds for the trace centered in any phase table respecting \(S\).

(b) In the reconstructed Wightman theory of the selected phase, the pinned field label \(\iota (\Theta)\) is fixed by the conjugation \(J\) and by the Lorentz group. Moreover \(\langle \Omega, \Phi_{\Theta} (f) \Omega \rangle = 0\) for every Schwartz \(f\).

(c) For every Schwartz test \(f\), as an identity on the common invariant field domain, \(\Phi_{\Theta} (f) = \Phi_{\sum_{\mu} T_{\mu \mu}} (f) - \left\langle \sum_{\mu} T_{\mu \mu} \right\rangle_{\xi_{\ast}} \left( \int f \right) \hspace{0.17em} \text{id}\).

(d) If \(r^8 \log^2 (1 / r) \hspace{0.17em} G_{\Theta} (r) \to A > 0\), then \(\iota (\Theta) \neq 0\).

3. Strategy

Physical picture and the approach taken. Classically the Yang–Mills stress tensor is traceless. The running coupling produces a nonzero trace after renormalization. It characterizes \(T\) by two properties: uniqueness (the conserved admissible tensors form a line, VII.2.6) and the Ward normalization. At each scale \(r_i\) it builds three lattice tensors \(T_i^{(B)} = \text{tub}_i (B)\) from the reference tensors \(B = L, \Delta, \Xi\): the free Maxwell stress \(L\), the pure-trace tensor \(\Delta_{\mu \nu} = \delta_{\mu \nu} \mathcal{O}\), and a third traceless tensor \(\Xi\).

The candidate is \(X_i = w_i T_i^{(L)} + c_{i, 0} T_i^{(\Xi)} + c_{i, 1} T_i^{(\Delta)}\), where \(w_i \sim \beta (r_i)\). 4.6 Exclusions

Lemma VII.2.s (power–logarithm expansions cannot carry the law). Suppose \(f = u + O (1)\) as \(s \downarrow 0\), where \(u\) is a finite real combination of \(s^a \log^k s\) with \(a \in \mathbb{Z}\) and \(k \in \mathbb{N}_0\), and suppose \(s^8 \log^2 (1 / s) \hspace{0.17em} f (s) \to A\). Then \(A = 0\). Proof sketch. The \(O (1)\) part contributes \(s^8 \log^2 (1 / s) \cdot O (1) \to 0\). Along \(s = 2^{- n}\), \(s^8 \log^2 (1 / s) \hspace{0.17em} u (s)\) is a combination of \(c_t \hspace{0.17em} \rho_t^{\hspace{0.17em} n} n^{k_t + 2}\) with \(\rho_t = 2^{- (8 + a_t)}\). Every term has a factor \(n^{k_t + 2}\) with \(k_t + 2 \geqslant 2\), so no term tends to a nonzero constant. Collect equal pairs \((a_t, k_t)\), discard zero coefficients, and order the remaining terms lexicographically by \((\rho_t, k_t + 2)\). If the leading term has \(\rho_t < 1\), all terms tend to \(0\). Otherwise the leading term diverges and dominates the others, so a finite limit is impossible unless its coefficient vanishes. Lemma VII.2.u (Fock sectors; proof of VII.2.8). Assume the positive short-distance law of VII.2.8 and a comparison \(U\) satisfying its relative-locality identity.

  1. Sector decomposition. By translation covariance, \(U \Phi_{\Theta} (\varphi) \Omega = \hat{\varphi} (p_{\text{tot}}) \hspace{0.17em} Y\) for fixed measurable sector amplitudes \(Y = (Y_{n, s})\), which need not form a square-integrable vector before smearing. Hence \(\mu_{\Theta}\) is the image of \(\sum_{n, s} |Y_{n, s} |^2 \hspace{0.17em} d (\text{photon measures})\) under total momentum, and \(\mu_{\Theta} \{p_0 \leqslant R\} = \sum_{n, s} \text{mass} (Y_{n, s} ; \{ \sum |k_j | \leqslant R\})\).

  2. Evaporation. The mass of \(Y_{n, s}\) on the box \(\{|k_j | \leqslant 2^J \text{ for all } j\}\) is at most \(\mu_{\Theta} \{p_0 \leqslant (n + 1) 2^J \}\). By the law, \(2^{- 8 J}\) times this tends to \(0\).

  3. Dilation polynomials. Relative locality gives equality of the forward and adjoint sector pairings when each test is supported spacelike to the origin. Approximate identities in time show that the Cauchy data of their difference are supported at the spatial origin. A distribution supported at one point has finite jet order. If \(D_j\) doubles the \(j\)th momentum, these jet bounds and mass-shell uniqueness give, for some \(K\), \(\prod_{a = 0}^{K - 1} (D_j - 2^{a - 1}) Y_{n, s} = 0\) in every slot. The roots are distinct and the \(D_j\) commute, so \(Y_{n, s}\) is a finite sum of joint eigencomponents.

  4. Finitely many sectors. A joint eigencomponent with slot degrees \(a_j\) has box mass proportional to \(R^{2 \sum_j a_j}\) at dyadic radii \(R\). Evaporation excludes \(\sum_j a_j \geqslant 4\). A component with any \(a_j = 0\) vanishes by local square integrability near zero momentum. Thus every nonzero component has \(a_j \geqslant 1\), so \(Y_{n, s} = 0\) for \(n \geqslant 4\). The remaining components have box mass at most \(C_{n, s} 2^{6 i}\) at radius \(2^i\).

  5. Contradiction. Summing over the finitely many sectors with \(n \leqslant 3\) gives \(\mu_{\Theta} \{p_0 \leqslant 2^k \} \leqslant D \hspace{0.17em} 2^{7 k}\). Multiply this bound by \(R^{- 8} \log^2 R\): it tends to zero. The spectral law instead gives a positive limit against a nonnegative compact test supported in \(\{0 < p_0 < 1, p^2 > 0\}\), a contradiction.

5. Where the difficulty lies

Hard. Three pieces carry the mathematics, and all three are needed for the exact constant.

  • The transport of the lattice Ward identity through the RG with fourth-order matching (VII.2.d–e).

  • The one-loop pole with coefficient exactly \(\kappa_G\) (VII.2.f, using III.7), and its conversion into a value by the mean-value argument (VII.2.g–h).

  • The control of lattice-to-continuum remainders to \(o (\hat{\beta}^{- p})\) for every \(p\) (VII.2.k). The trace must be controlled after multiplication by the inverse coupling. The separate coefficient errors have the slower exponential rates displayed in VII.2.k. The bounds on \(c_i\) grow like \(e^{\frac{1}{6} (\log \log \hat{\beta})^2}\) and are beaten by decay factors \(e^{- \frac{1}{5} (\log \log \hat{\beta})^2}\).

The identification of the explicit conserved tensor with \(T\) (VII.2.n) is short once the rank-one statement and the Ward normalization are available.

Routine.

  • Centering and the symmetries of \(\Theta\).

  • The passage from subsequential limits at rounded points to the full limit (monotonicity and the subsequence principle).

  • The Tauberian step.

  • The Wick exclusion.

The Fock exclusion also needs a distributional argument: relative locality yields point-supported Cauchy data, which force polynomial identities for momentum doubling. The growth comparison alone does not supply this step.

Unusual or worth checking.

  1. Logic of dependence. The law is derived from the gallery property, which is established while the ultraviolet run is chosen (III.2): the same Wilson family must carry the trace certificates. The exclusions are proved from the law, not independently. Their public form combines the two.

  2. The cancellation of \(b_0\) is a consistency check, not a tautology. The identities \(\kappa_G = 2 b_0\) and \(d_2 = - \kappa_G / 4\) are definitions. The content is:

    • \(c_{i, 1} \to - \kappa_G / 4\), where \(\kappa_G\) comes from the one-loop integral (VII.2.f);

    • \(\log (1 / r) / w \to 1 / \kappa_G\), where \(\kappa_G\) is the slope of the coupling schedule.

    Only because both are \(\kappa_G\) is the amplitude \(A_G = (4 d_2 / \kappa_G)^2 \cdot 3 \dim \mathfrak{g}/ \pi^4 = 3 \dim \mathfrak{g}/ \pi^4\), independent of \(C_A\). Had the one-loop slope differed from the schedule, the amplitude would differ by the square of their ratio.

  3. Geometry of the points. The law is first obtained at the generic configuration \((0, \frac{s}{2} (1, 1, 1, 1))\), as the lattice machinery requires distinct coordinates in each axis. It is then rotated to the time axis, which uses the full \(O (4)\) invariance of \(\Theta\) from VI.1.

  4. Precision. The payoff certificate is accurate to \(\hat{\beta}_k^{- 100}\), much more than the argument needs.

  5. Scope of the exclusions. The Wick class is exactly the public finite-profile class. The conclusion does not exclude continuous-mass generalized free fields. The Fock comparison is tested through vacuum matrix elements with coherent states; it gives an obstruction to relative locality with free photons under the stated embedding hypotheses.
