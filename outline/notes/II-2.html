This unit supplies the algebraic, combinatorial and numerical estimates used to multiply and sum polymer activities in Part III, especially in the Gaussian comparison of III.11. The activity format is defined in III.1; the Wilson family and construction record in II.1; reflection-positive cells in IV.1.

The results fall into five groups: marker algebra (II.2.1–II.2.4), polymer combinatorics and forced-activity fusion (II.2.5–II.2.8), switching (II.2.9), Gaussian polynomial calculus (II.2.10–II.2.11), and numerical scale comparisons (II.2.12–II.2.13). 1. What this unit proves

Conventions.

  • \(\Lambda\) is a four-dimensional periodic lattice.

  • A support is a finite subset of \(\Lambda\), and \(\text{dist}\) is the lattice separation.

  • For \(M \in \mathbb{N}\) and a support \(X\), the cover number \(\text{cov}_M (X)\) is the least cardinality of a set \(D \subseteq \Lambda\) such that the union \(B_M (D)\) of the balls of radius \(M\) around \(D\) contains \(X\) and is connected for nearest-neighbour steps (coverNumber, covers, I.1).

  • The parameters of the polymer norms are \(P = (r, q, e_P, B, t_P)\), with \(r, q, B \in \mathbb{N}\), \(r \geqslant 6\), \(q \geqslant 2\), \(B \geqslant 1\), \(e_P > 0\) and \(0 < t_P \leqslant 1\): smoothness order, power, support exponent, cover size and threshold. The mass of an envelope \(c\) on support \(X\) is \(e^{e_P \text{cov}_B (X)} c\).

  • Two facts from I.1 are used:

    • \(|X| \leqslant (2 M + 1)^4 \hspace{0.17em} \text{cov}_M (X)\) (Corona.pays);

    • \(\text{cov}_M (X \cup Y) \leqslant \text{cov}_M (X) + \text{cov}_M (Y)\) if \(X \cap Y \neq \emptyset\) (Corona.meets_sum).

A. Marker algebra

Proposition II.2.1 (marker algebra). Let \(V\) be a finite set and \(R\) a census on \(V\): a finite family of finite subsets of \(V\) that contains \(\emptyset\) and all singletons and is closed under taking subsets. Let \(\mathcal{A}_R\) be the space of functions \(a : R \to \mathbb{C}\), written \(a = \sum_{x \in R} a (x) \delta_x\), with product

\(\displaystyle (ab) (z) = \sum_{x, y \in R : x \cap y = \emptyset, x \cup y = z} a (x) \hspace{0.17em} b (y) \qquad (z \in R) .\)

Then:

  1. \(\mathcal{A}_R\) is a commutative unital \(\mathbb{C}\)-algebra with unit \(\delta_{\emptyset}\).

  2. \(\delta_x \delta_y = \delta_{x \cup y}\) if \(x \cap y = \emptyset\) and \(x \cup y \in R\), and \(\delta_x \delta_y = 0\) otherwise. So every marker \(\delta_{\{v\}}\) squares to zero, and products that leave \(R\) vanish.

  3. \((ab) (\emptyset) = a (\emptyset) \hspace{0.17em} b (\emptyset)\).

  4. If \(a (\emptyset) = 0\), then \(a^k (x) = 0\) whenever \(|x| < k\); in particular, \(a^k = 0\) for \(k > |V|\).

Status: theorem. Lean: Marker.Census, Poly, Poly.prod, ring structure, Poly.quiet_mul, Poly.high, Poly.stops.

Proposition II.2.2 (weighted \(\ell^1\) norm). For \(w : V \to (0, \infty)\) put \(w^x = \prod_{v \in x} w_v\) and \(\|a\|_w = \sum_{x \in R} w^x |a (x) |\). Then \(\|1\|_w = 1\), \(\|c \hspace{0.17em} \delta_x \|_w = w^x |c|\), and

\(\displaystyle \|ab\|_w \leqslant \|a\|_w \|b\|_w, \qquad | \prod_{i \in T} a_i |_w \leqslant \prod_{i \in T} \|a_i \|_w, \qquad \|a^k \|_w \leqslant \|a\|_w^k .\)

Set \(\text{Exp} (a) = \sum_{k = 0}^{|V|} a^k / k!\).

  • \(\| \text{Exp} (a)\|_w \leqslant e^{\|a\|_w}\) for every \(a\).

  • If a countable family satisfies \(\|f_i \|_w \leqslant b_i\) with \(\sum_i b_i < \infty\), then \(\sum_i f_i\) converges in \(\mathcal{A}_R\), \(\| \sum_i f_i \|_w \leqslant \sum_i b_i\), and coefficients of the sum are sums of coefficients.

  • \(\max_x |a (x) | \leqslant (\min_{x \in R} w^x)^{- 1} \|a\|_w\).

The multiplicative and exponential bounds have constant \(1\), independently of \(R\) and \(|V|\). The norm-comparison constant is used to establish convergence.

Status: theorem. Lean: Poly.toll, Poly.price, multiplication, multiple, powers, Poly.pass, pass_budget, budgeted, share, equivalent_norm.

Proposition II.2.3 (exponential and connected polynomial). Use the marker algebra and exponential of II.2.1–II.2.2.

(a) If \(a (\emptyset) = b (\emptyset) = 0\), then \(\text{Exp} (a + b) = \text{Exp} (a) \hspace{0.17em} \text{Exp} (b)\) and \(\text{Exp} (- a) \hspace{0.17em} \text{Exp} (a) = 1\). More generally, \(\text{Exp} (\sum_{i \in s} a_i) = \prod_{i \in s} \text{Exp} (a_i)\) for a finite family with all \(a_i (\emptyset) = 0\). The zero-constant-term hypotheses are necessary for these identities in general.

(b) If \(x \neq \emptyset\), then \(\text{Exp} (c \hspace{0.17em} \delta_x) = 1 + c \hspace{0.17em} \delta_x\). Hence, for a finite family of nonempty \(x_i \in R\) (not necessarily distinct),

\(\displaystyle \text{Exp} \left( \sum_{i \in s} c_i \delta_{x_i} \right) = \prod_{i \in s} (1 + c_i \delta_{x_i}) .\)

(c) If \(F (\emptyset) = 1\), there is exactly one \(C \in \mathcal{A}_R\) with \(C (\emptyset) = 0\) and \(\text{Exp} (C) = F\): the connected polynomial of \(F\). It is obtained by iteration:

  • \(C_0 = 0\) and \(C_{k + 1} = C_k + F - \text{Exp} (C_k)\);

  • \(F - \text{Exp} (C_k)\) vanishes on all marker sets with at most \(k\) elements, so \(C = C_{|V|}\);

  • each \(C_k\) is a polynomial, hence continuous, function of \(F\).

(d) If \(C (\emptyset) = 0\) and \(\text{Exp} (C) = F\), then \(F \hspace{0.17em} \text{Exp} (- C) \hspace{0.17em} P = P\) for every \(P\).

Status: theorem. Lean: Exponential.pass_add, pass_neg, pass_sum, Trajectories.pass_send, Trajectories.pass_sum, Expands, roll, rolls, roll_cont, Expands.uniq, connected_spec, correct.

Proposition II.2.4 (tagged products; repacking). Use positive weights \(w\) as in II.2.2.

(a) Let \(\tau : \iota \to R\) and let \(s \subseteq \iota\) be finite. Say that \(s\) fits if the sets \(\tau (i)\), \(i \in s\), are pairwise disjoint and their union lies in \(R\).

  • If \(s\) fits, then \(\prod_{i \in s} c_i \delta_{\tau (i)} = (\prod_{i \in s} c_i) \hspace{0.17em} \delta_{\bigcup_{i \in s} \tau (i)}\) and \(w^{\bigcup_{i \in s} \tau (i)} = \prod_{i \in s} w^{\tau (i)}\).

  • Conversely, if \(\prod_{i \in s} c_i \delta_{\tau (i)} \neq 0\), then \(s\) fits.

(b) Let:

  • \(\sigma : \iota \to \kappa\) assign a slot to each entry;

  • "admissible" be any property of finite sets of slots;

  • \(F : \iota \to \mathcal{A}_R\) satisfy \(\sum_i \|F_i \|_w < \infty\).

For finite \(s \subseteq \iota\) put \(\text{val} (s) = \prod_{i \in s} F_i\) if \(\sigma\) is injective on \(s\) and \(\sigma (s)\) is admissible, and \(\text{val} (s) = 0\) otherwise. Then \(\text{val}\) is summable over finite subsets of \(\iota\), and

\(\displaystyle \sum_s \text{val} (s) = \sum_{S \subseteq \kappa \text{ finite, admissible}} \prod_{k \in S} \sum_{i : \hspace{0.17em} \sigma (i) = k} F_i .\)

Status: theorem. Lean: Trajectories.Fits, tagged_prod, fits_of_tagged_prod, toll_union, Packets.summable_value, Packets.repack.

B. Polymer combinatorics

Proposition II.2.5 (connected components). Let \(X : \iota \to \{ \text{supports} \}\) and let \(s \subseteq \iota\) be finite.

  • Two labels of \(s\) are chained in \(s\) if they are joined by a sequence of labels of \(s\) whose successive supports intersect.

  • A spark is a nonempty \(s\) in which all labels are chained.

  • \(\text{Parts} (s)\) is the set of chain classes of \(s\), and \(X (p) = \bigcup_{i \in p} X_i\).

Then:

  1. The parts of \(s\) are sparks, pairwise disjoint, with union \(s\) and with pairwise disjoint supports \(X (p)\).

  2. If \(S\) is a finite set of sparks with pairwise disjoint supports, then \(\text{Parts} (\bigcup S) = S\).

  3. If \(s\) is a spark and \(i \in s\), every label of \(s \setminus \{i\}\) is chained in \(s \setminus \{i\}\) to a label whose support meets \(X_i\).

  4. If every label of \(s\) is chained in \(s\) to a label meeting a support \(Y\) (call \(s\) a pack for \(Y\)), then every part of \(s\) contains a label meeting \(Y\).

The same component statements hold when the supports are finite subsets of any set.

Status: theorem. Lean: Ac.Spark, Parts, reconstruct, separate, separates, excise, packHits, Collide.uncluttered.

Proposition II.2.6 (tree bound in cover-number weights). Let:

  • \(\iota\) be any set of labels and \(X : \iota \to \{ \text{supports} \}\);

  • \(M \in \mathbb{N}\), \(f : \iota \to [0, \infty)\), \(a \geqslant 0\) and \(\gamma \geqslant 0\) with \((2 M + 1)^4 \gamma \leqslant 1\).

Assume the hit condition with exponent \(a + 1\): for every site \(x\) the family \(\left( \boldsymbol{1}[x \in X_i] \hspace{0.17em} f_i \hspace{0.17em} e^{(a + 1) \text{cov}_M (X_i)} \right)_i\) is summable, with sum at most \(\gamma\). Then for every site \(x\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_{s \text{spark}, x \in X (s)} e^{a \hspace{0.17em} \text{cov}_M (X (s))} \prod_{i \in s} f_i \leqslant e^{\gamma} - 1.\)

The sum runs over finite sets of labels, so each label is used at most once, and the family is summable.

Status: theorem. Used by III.9–III.13, VII.2 and VII.6. Lean: Ac.Hit, Ac.roots, Ac.forest.

Proposition II.2.7 (unique recovery for two kinds of overlap). A kiln on a set \(I\) of labels consists of:

  • finite sets \(\text{men} (i) \subseteq V\) ("mentions");

  • finite sets \(\text{wall} (i) \subseteq W\) (\(W\) another set);

  • a set of dirty labels.

For finite \(s \subseteq I\):

  • a label \(i \in s\) is hot in \(s\) if it is joined to a dirty label of \(s\) by a chain in \(s\) of overlapping mentions;

  • the fuel of \(i\) in \(s\) is the subset of \(V \sqcup W\) consisting of its mentions in the \(V\) component and, only when \(i\) is hot, its walls in the \(W\) component;

  • \(s\) is a mole if it is a spark for fuel overlap;

  • the slabs of \(s\) are its parts for fuel overlap.

Then:

  1. Every slab \(p\) of \(s\) is a mole, and the fuel of its labels computed in \(p\) equals the fuel computed in \(s\). Distinct slabs have disjoint mention-unions. Distinct slabs that contain a dirty label have disjoint wall-unions. (In a mole, containing a dirty label is equivalent to all labels being hot.)

  2. Conversely, let \(S\) be a finite set of moles with pairwise disjoint mention-unions, and with pairwise disjoint wall-unions among the members that contain a dirty label. Then the slabs of \(\bigcup S\) are exactly the members of \(S\).

  3. By 1 and 2, \(s \mapsto \text{slabs} (s)\) and \(S \mapsto \bigcup S\) are inverse bijections between finite sets of labels and such finite sets of moles.

  4. Suppose the walls are supports and, inside a mole \(s\), overlapping mentions imply overlapping walls. Then \(s\) is a spark for the walls. Thus a family of moles can be estimated by II.2.6 whenever its wall supports and weights satisfy that proposition’s hypotheses.

Status: theorem. Used by III.11 and VII.4. Lean: Kiln, Kiln.Hot, fuel, Mole, good, fuel_comp, clubs_away, walls_away, takes_mole, Kiln.undo, Kiln.attends.

Proposition II.2.8 (fused gas of forced activities: definitions). Fix a gauge group \(G\) and a periodic lattice \(\Lambda\).

Data.

  • Pipes: regular addresses \((\text{reg}_i)\); forced addresses \((\text{ker}_i)\), each with a support, a nonempty inventory contained in it, and an extra test on configurations; nonempty "softer" supports \((Y_i)\), for \(i \in \mathbb{N}\).

  • Tap \(d\): three families of real-valued functions of the configuration, namely regular coefficients \(E_i\), forced coefficients \(K_i\) and logarithmic coefficients \(\ell_i\).

  • Labels are \(\mathbb{N} \sqcup \mathbb{N}\): left labels are forced kernels, right labels are logarithms.

    • The patch of a left label \(i\) is \(\text{supp} (\text{ker}_i)\); that of a right label \(i\) is \(Y_i\).

    • The reach of a left label is \(K_i (U)\); that of a right label is \(e^{\ell_i (U)} - 1\).

  • A pellet is a finite set of labels that contains at least one left label, is a spark for patch overlap, and whose left labels have pairwise disjoint kernel supports. Pellets form a countably infinite set.

Fused activities. Fix a activity parameter point \(P\) and a bijective enumeration \(F\) of the pellets. Eligibility is always tested at the threshold of \(P\). The fused activities keep the regular part \((\text{reg}_i, E_i)\). Their forced part is indexed by pellets:

  • address of \(F (i)\): support = union of the patches, inventory = union of the left inventories, extra test = all left kernels eligible;

  • value

\(\displaystyle \text{solid}_i (U) =\boldsymbol{1} [\text{the address of } F (i) \text{ is eligible at } U] \prod_{j \in F (i)} \text{reach}_j (U) .\)

The unit also defines the hypotheses under which the fused gas is used. Below, \(B\) is the cover size of \(P\).

  • Digest condition at \((d, P, \delta, b)\):

    1. the unfused activities \((\text{reg}, \text{ker}, E, K)\) are valid at \(P\) with regular and forced bounds \(\delta, b\);

    2. \(\ell_i (U) \neq 0\) only if \(Y_i\) meets the large-field set of \(U\);

    3. \(| \ell_i (U) | \leqslant B_i\) for every \(i, U\), with \(\sum_i B_i < \infty\);

    4. each \(\ell_i\) is gauge invariant and measurable, and has the same value on two configurations that agree on links internal to \(Y_i\) and have the same large-field flags on \(Y_i\);

    5. lattice affine maps permute the \(\ell_i\) covariantly.

  • Fusion bounds at \((d, P, \kappa, \gamma)\):

    1. \(\gamma, \kappa \geqslant 0\) and \((2 B + 1)^4 \gamma \leqslant 1\);

    2. there is a label weight \(f \geqslant 0\), independent of \(U\), with \(| \text{reach} | \leqslant e^{- \kappa} f\) on left labels and \(| \text{reach} | \leqslant f\) on right labels;

    3. \(f\) satisfies the hit condition of II.2.6 for the patches, with exponent (support exponent of \(P\)) \(+ 1\) and bound \(\gamma\).

  • For a parameter set \(T \subseteq \mathbb{R}\) and taps \(d_a\), \(a \in T\), the parametric conditions mean:

    1. for each label \(j\), \(a \mapsto \text{reach}_j (d_a)\) is continuous in the supremum over configurations, relative to \(T\);

    2. one common choice of \(\kappa, \gamma, f\) satisfies the reach and hit bounds for every \(a \in T\);

    3. the fused values are continuous in the norm \(\sum_i e^{e_P \text{cov}_B (S_i)} \sup_U |K_i (U) |\), where \(e_P\) is the support exponent and \(S_i\) the fused support.

Status: definitions, plus one theorem (an enumeration exists). The properties of the fused gas are the work items H01–H04, stated in YM/Bid/Kitchen.lean and proved in III.10:

  • H01 (under the digest condition and fusion bounds): \(\text{gas}_{\text{fused}} (U) = \text{gas}_{\text{unfused}} (U) \exp \sum_i \ell_i (U)\) for every configuration \(U\).

  • H02 (under the digest condition and fusion bounds): the fused forced activities satisfy the K-bound \(v\) whenever \(e^{- \kappa} (e^{\gamma} - 1) \leqslant v\).

  • H03 (under the digest condition): they form a forced family with the lattice moves.

  • H04: if the unfused representations form a continuous parameter curve, conditions 1 and 2 above imply that the fused representations form such a curve. The path data, cutoff and parameters \(P\) are fixed along the curve.

C. Switching

Proposition II.2.9 (switching). Let \(\iota\) be finite. For \(x \in \mathbb{R}\) and \(g : 2^{\iota} \to \mathbb{R}\) put \(\text{val}_x (g) (r) = \sum_{i \subseteq r} x^{|i|} g (i)\). This is the value at \(x\boldsymbol{1}_r \in \{0, x\}^{\iota}\) of the multilinear polynomial \(\sum_i g (i) \prod_{v \in i} X_v\).

(a) For \(x > 0\), \(\text{val}_x\) is a linear bijection of \(\mathbb{R}^{2^{\iota}}\).

(b) Let \((f_x)_{x \in [0, 1]}\) be functions on \(2^{\iota}\). Say \((g_x)\) models \(f\) if \(\text{val}_x (g_x) = f_x\) for \(x \in [0, 1]\) and \(g_x \to g_0\) as \(x \downarrow 0\). In that case \(\text{jet} (f) := \lim_{x \downarrow 0} \text{val}_x^{- 1} (f_x)\) exists and equals \(g_0\).

(c) A family \((f^u)_u\) is paid at cost \(c\) if there is \(b \geqslant 0\) on \(2^{\iota}\) with \(\sum_i b (i) \leqslant c\) such that, for each \(u\), some model \(g^u\) of \(f^u\) satisfies \(|g^u_x (i) | \leqslant b (i)\) for all \(x \in [0, 1]\). Then:

  • \(|f^u_x (r) | \leqslant c\) for every \(u\), \(r\) and \(x \in [0, 1]\);

  • paid families are closed under finite sums (costs add), finite products (costs multiply), scalar multiples (cost multiplied by the absolute value), and restriction by zero outside a set of parameters. Families of paid functions with summable costs may also be added, and their jets sum to the jet of the sum. Products of models obey the rule \(X_v^2 = xX_v\): \(\text{val}_x\) turns the product \((p \ast_x q) (k) = \sum_{i \cup j = k} x^{|i \cap j|} p (i) q (j)\) into the pointwise product;

  • a monomial \(u \mapsto c (u) \hspace{0.17em} \boldsymbol{1} [i \subseteq r] \hspace{0.17em} x^{|i|}\) with \(|c| \leqslant b\) is paid at cost \(b\).

(d) If \(f\) has a model and \(f_x (r) = f_x (r \setminus \{v\})\) for every \(r\) and \(x \in [0, 1]\), then \(\text{jet} (f) (i) = 0\) whenever \(v \in i\). For a paid family on a measurable parameter space, if \(u \mapsto f^u_x (r)\) is measurable for each fixed \(x \in [0, 1]\) and \(r\), then \(u \mapsto \text{jet} (f^u) (i)\) is measurable for every \(i\).

(e) For \(\rho : \iota \to \mathcal{A}_R\) put \(\text{snd}_{\rho} (f) = \sum_{i \subseteq \iota} \text{jet} (f) (i) \prod_{v \in i} \rho_v\).

  • If \((f^u)\) is paid at cost \(d\) and \(\| \rho_v \|_w \leqslant 1\) for all \(v\), then the coefficients of \(\text{snd}_{\rho} (f^u)\) are dominated, uniformly in \(u\), by a nonnegative array of \(w\)-norm at most \(d\).

  • If \(\rho_v (\emptyset) = 0\) for all \(v\), the coefficient of \(\text{snd}_{\rho} (f)\) on a singleton \(\{p\}\) is \(\sum_v \text{jet} (f) (\{v\}) \hspace{0.17em} \rho_v (\{p\})\).

Status: theorem. For square-zero \(\rho_v\), \(\text{snd}_{\rho}\) is multiplicative on functions with models. For paid families with summable costs, it commutes with sums; for paid \(f\), it sends \(e^f\) to the exponential series of \(\text{snd}_{\rho} (f)\) (mul_sounds, soundSum, soundExp). D. Gaussian polynomial calculus

Conventions.

  • \(\mathbb{C} [X] =\mathbb{C} [X_v : v \in V]\). The variable set \(V\) need not be finite; each polynomial uses finitely many variables.

  • \(\|p\|_1\) is the sum of the absolute values of the coefficients of \(p\).

  • For \(b \in \mathbb{C}^V\), \(\partial_b\) is the derivation with \(\partial_b X_v = b_v\).

  • A covariance is a symmetric \(Q : V \times V \to \mathbb{C}\); no positivity is assumed.

  • \(\pi (B, d) = (2 B (d + 1))^d\).

Proposition II.2.10 (algebraic Gaussian expectations). Let \(Q\) be a covariance. The operators \(X_v + \partial_{Q_{v \cdot}}\) on \(\mathbb{C} [X]\) commute; define \(\mathcal{G}_Q p = p (X + \partial_Q) \hspace{0.17em} 1\).

(a) \(\mathcal{G}_Q\) satisfies:

  • \(\mathcal{G}_Q 1 = 1\) and \(\mathcal{G}_Q (X_v p) = X_v \hspace{0.17em} \mathcal{G}_Q p + \partial_{Q_{v \cdot}} \mathcal{G}_Q p\);

  • \(\mathcal{G}_0 = \text{id}\) and \(\mathcal{G}_P \mathcal{G}_Q =\mathcal{G}_{P + Q}\);

  • \(\partial_b \mathcal{G}_Q =\mathcal{G}_Q \partial_b\).

(Equivalently \(\mathcal{G}_Q = \exp (\tfrac{1}{2} \sum_{v, w} Q_{vw} \partial_v \partial_w)\), so \(\mathcal{G}_{- M}\) is Wick ordering with respect to \(M\). On each polynomial the derivative sum and exponential series have only finitely many nonzero terms.)

(b) For \(m \in \mathbb{C}^V\) put \(\mathbb{E}_{Q, m} (p) = (\mathcal{G}_Q p) (m)\). This is a linear functional with \(\mathbb{E}_{Q, m} (1) = 1\), and it satisfies, for every finite set \(s\) and choice of variables \(z_j\), the Wick recursion

\(\displaystyle \mathbb{E}_{Q, m} \left( X_v \prod_{j \in s} X_{z_j} \right) = m_v \hspace{0.17em} \mathbb{E}_{Q, m} \left( \prod_{j \in s} X_{z_j} \right) + \sum_{j \in s} Q_{vz_j} \hspace{0.17em} \mathbb{E}_{Q, m} \left( \prod_{k \in s \setminus \{j\}} X_{z_k} \right) .\)

(c) Change of variables. For a matrix \(A = (A_{vz})\) with finitely supported rows, define the linear substitution \(p \mapsto p \circ A\) by \(X_v \mapsto \sum_z A_{vz} X_z\). If \(Q\) is a covariance and \(m\) a mean on the new variables, then:

  • \(\mathcal{G}_Q (p \circ A) = (\mathcal{G}_{AQA^T} p) \circ A\);

  • \(\mathbb{E}_{Q, m} (p \circ A) =\mathbb{E}_{AQA^T, Am} (p)\).

(d) Identification. Let \(\xi\) be a random vector in \(\mathbb{R}^V\) under a probability measure. Assume all polynomial moments of \(\xi\) are finite and \(\mathbb{E} [\xi_v \hspace{0.17em} q (\xi)] =\mathbb{E} [(\partial_{Q_{v \cdot}} q) (\xi)]\) for every polynomial \(q\) (Gaussian integration by parts). Then \(\mathbb{E} [q (m + \xi)] =\mathbb{E}_{Q, m} (q)\) for every \(m \in \mathbb{R}^V\).

(e) Bounds. Let \(B \geqslant 1\), \(|m_v | \leqslant B\), \(|Q_{vw} | \leqslant B\) and \(\deg p \leqslant d\). Then

\(\displaystyle |\mathbb{E}_{Q, m} (p) | \leqslant \|p\|_1 \hspace{0.17em} \pi (B, d) .\)

If \((Q', m')\) obeys the same bounds and each corresponding entry of the means and covariances differs by at most \(E \geqslant 0\), then \(|\mathbb{E}_{Q, m} (p) -\mathbb{E}_{Q', m'} (p) | \leqslant \|p\|_1 \hspace{0.17em} E \hspace{0.17em} \pi (B, d)\). 3. Strategy

Products of local activities are represented in a finite commutative algebra of square-zero markers. The allowed marker sets determine which products vanish. The exponential converts sums of nonconstant monomials into products, and a triangular correction constructs the unique connected polynomial whose exponential is a given polynomial with constant term \(1\). The weighted \(\ell^1\) norm controls these operations without a loss depending on the number of markers. Switching connects scalar functions of activity parameters to this algebra: for positive switch size \(x\), coefficients are recovered from values at \(0 / x\) configurations. A convergent model supplies their limit as \(x \downarrow 0\), while a uniform majorant controls that limit. The relation \(X_v^2 = xX_v\) then becomes the square-zero relation.

Connected-component decompositions reduce polymer sums to sums over packs attached to a support. A Kotecký–Preiss induction bounds the latter by \(e^{\text{cov}_M (Y)}\). Attaching \(X_i\) costs \(e^{\text{cov}_M (X_i)}\), paid by the extra unit in the hit exponent \(a + 1\). The geometric input is \(|Y| \leqslant (2 M + 1)^4 \text{cov}_M (Y)\). For two interactions, mentions always connect, while walls connect only hot labels. Hotness is unchanged on restriction to a component, so the component decomposition can be recovered uniquely. The forced-activity fusion uses ordinary patch components; its density identity and bounds are proved in III.10.

The Gaussian comparison in III.11 uses algebraic convolution on polynomials with complex symmetric covariances. Wick recursion gives coefficient-norm and moment bounds depending on the degree and entrywise bounds, independently of the number of variables. Gaussian integration by parts identifies this algebraic functional with an actual expectation when a measure is supplied. The numerical inequalities then absorb the errors and iteration losses. There are two iteration lengths: \(n (L, s) = O (y (s) / \log L)\) and \(H (L, s) = O (y (s)^2 / \log L)\) at fixed \(L\). The first gives losses \(\Pi (s)^{o (1)}\); the second requires the explicit lower bounds on \(L\) in II.2.13(d). The constants in those block-size inequalities are chosen before \(L\); all remaining constants must be fixed before the lower bound on \(s\) is chosen.

Lemma 4.13 (hotness is local).

Lemma 4.17 (triangular inversion). For \(x > 0\), \(\text{val}_x\) is injective. Lemma 4.22 (size of moments). II.2.10(e). Sketch:

5. Where the difficulty lies

The tree estimate must preserve the connected cover weight. Cover numbers are subadditive along intersecting unions, whereas a disconnected union may require an additional connecting region. The induction therefore uses connected components and spends exactly one extra cover exponent on each attachment. It counts finite sets of labels; it does not estimate Mayer coefficients with arbitrary repeated labels.

For two interactions, the graph itself depends on the selected set, through hotness. Unique recovery requires both locality of hotness and the asymmetric separation conditions: all components separate through mentions, while only components containing a dirty label separate through walls.

Switching requires convergence as well as a bound. Bounded coefficients alone need not have a limit as \(x \downarrow 0\). The model supplies convergence, and the common majorant allows sums and coefficient estimates to pass to the limit. The Gaussian bounds allow complex covariances without positivity; their constants grow like \(d^d\) up to exponential factors at fixed entry bounds. The degree is fixed before \(L\) and \(s\).

The numerical conditions establish feasibility for fixed parameters. They do not supply the analytic estimates to which those numbers are applied. The two iteration lengths in §3 must remain distinct.

The source contains duplicate exponential and component constructions. Its second trajectory logarithm and Candle.small are absent from the main theorem’s dependency set. The latter bounds a restarted threshold by \(3 h (\hat{\beta}) \beta^{- 1 / 2}\).
