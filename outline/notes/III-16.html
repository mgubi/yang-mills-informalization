The results fall into three groups.

  • (A) Traces of kernel products. A Hölder inequality and Cauchy's estimate give a dimension-free Lipschitz bound for moving insertions between time slices. Quadrature passes the bound from matrices to kernels; IV.1 uses it for equicontinuity.

  • (B) Analytic and Fourier estimates. Holomorphic symbols correspond to exponentially summable Laurent coefficients. Fourier and Bloch identities diagonalize periodized kernels, and a binomial series supplies a matrix square root. Taylor coefficient identities and several-variable analyticity support these arguments.

  • (C) Maxwell fields and labels. Radial-gauge potentials correspond to closed curvature jets. Restriction to the Maxwell shell defines the free contractions and Wick hierarchy. Gauge-invariant polynomial labels admit coherent complex bases with real coefficients.

1. What this unit proves

The contraction kernel and Wick hierarchy are definitions whose properties are proved here.

Notation for (A).

  • Matrices are complex \(\iota \times \iota\) matrices with \(\iota\) finite, and \(\| \cdot \|\) is the operator norm.

  • \(\|b\|_{2 q} := (\text{tr} ((b^{\ast} b)^q))^{1 / (2 q)}\) is the Schatten \(2 q\)-norm.

  • For \(A \succeq 0\) with spectral decomposition \(A = \sum_{\lambda} \lambda P_{\lambda}\), put \(A^z := \sum_{\lambda > 0} e^{z \log \lambda} P_{\lambda}\) for \(z \in \mathbb{C}\). This is entire in \(z\). It vanishes on \(\ker A\) for every \(z\), so \(A^0\) is the projection onto the range, and it equals the ordinary power \(A^m\) for every integer \(m \geqslant 1\).

  • Products \(\prod_{k = 1}^n\) are ordered by \(k\).

Proposition III.16.1 (integrating a positive-semidefinite kernel).

Hypotheses.

  • \(X\) is a compact metric space with a finite Borel measure \(\mu\), and \(\mathcal{A}, \mathcal{B}\) are sets.

  • \(K : \mathcal{A} \times \mathcal{A} \to \mathbb{C}\) is positive semidefinite: for every \(m\) and all \(a_1, \ldots, a_m \in \mathcal{A}\) (repetitions allowed), the matrix \((K (a_i, a_j))_{i, j}\) is Hermitian and positive semidefinite.

  • \(g : \mathcal{B} \times X \to \mathcal{A}\) is such that \((x, y) \mapsto K (g (b, x), g (c, y))\) is continuous on \(X \times X\) for all \(b, c \in \mathcal{B}\).

Conclusion. The kernel

\(\displaystyle \tilde{K} (b, c) = \int_X \hspace{-0.17em} \int_X K (g (b, x), g (c, y)) \hspace{0.17em} d \mu (y) \hspace{0.17em} d \mu (x)\)

is positive semidefinite on \(\mathcal{B}\).

Status: theorem. Lean: YM.Analysis.NNKernel.accumulate; the notion of positive semidefinite kernel is NNKernel.

Proposition III.16.2 (Hölder inequality for traces with complex exponents).

Hypotheses.

  • \(A \succeq 0\) and \(b_1, \ldots, b_n\) are complex \(\iota \times \iota\) matrices.

  • \(q, M \geqslant 1\) are integers, and \(Z_A := \text{tr} \hspace{0.17em} A^M > 0\).

  • \(v \in \mathbb{C}^n\) satisfies \(\text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} v_k \geqslant 0\) for all \(k\) and \(\frac{n}{2 q} + \frac{1}{M} \sum_k \text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} v_k = 1\).

Conclusion.

\(\displaystyle | \text{tr} \prod_{k = 1}^n b_k A^{v_k} | \leqslant \prod_k \|b_k \|_{2 q} \cdot Z_A^{\hspace{0.17em} 1 - n / (2 q)} .\)

For real \(v_k > 0\), the corresponding Schatten exponent is \(M / v_k\) and \(\|A^{v_k} \|_{M / v_k} = Z_A^{v_k / M}\). At \(v_k = 0\), the factor is the range projection, whose operator norm is \(1\) since \(Z_A > 0\). The bound has no dimension factor.

Status: theorem. Lean: YM.Holder.estimation. The left side is total and the right side is cost.

Proposition III.16.3 (Lipschitz bound in the gaps, matrices; key result).

Hypotheses.

  • \(A \succeq 0\) and \(b_1, \ldots, b_n\) are as in III.16.2.

  • \(q, w \geqslant 1\) are integers, \(M = 2 qw\), and \(\text{tr} \hspace{0.17em} A^M > 0\).

  • \(Y \in \mathbb{N}^n\) satisfies \(nw + \sum_k Y_k = M\).

  • For some \(i \ne j\) and some \(\ell \in \mathbb{N}\), the vector \(Y' \in \mathbb{N}^n\) moves \(\ell\) units from gap \(j\) to gap \(i\): \(Y'_i = Y_i + \ell\), \(Y'_j + \ell = Y_j\), and \(Y'_k = Y_k\) otherwise.

  • \(r > 0\), \(Y_k > 2 r\) for every \(k\), and \(\ell \leqslant r\).

Conclusion.

\(\displaystyle | \text{tr} \prod_k b_k A^{Y'_k} - \text{tr} \prod_k b_k A^{Y_k} | \leqslant \frac{\ell}{r} \hspace{0.17em} \prod_k \|b_k \|_{2 q} \hspace{0.17em} \left( \text{tr} \hspace{0.17em} A^M \right)^{1 - n / (2 q)} .\)

The condition \(i \ne j\) forces \(n \geqslant 2\).

Status: theorem. Lean: YM.Holder.finite_lip.

Proposition III.16.4 (Lipschitz bound in the gaps, kernels; key result).

Setting. \(X\) is a compact metric space with a finite Borel measure \(\mu\). For \(L \geqslant 1\) and continuous \(k_1, \ldots, k_L : X \times X \to \mathbb{C}\), write

\(\displaystyle \text{Tr} (k_1 \cdots k_L) = \int_{X^L} \prod_{l = 1}^L k_l (x_l, x_{l + 1}) \hspace{0.17em} d \mu (x_1) \cdots d \mu (x_L), \qquad x_{L + 1} = x_1,\)

and write \(k^{\ast} (x, y) = \overline{k (y, x)}\) for the adjoint kernel. Powers inside \(\text{Tr}\) denote repetitions in the cyclic word.

Hypotheses.

  • \(K\) is a continuous positive-semidefinite kernel, and \(B_1, \ldots, B_n\) are continuous kernels.

  • \(q, w \geqslant 1\), \(M = 2 qw\), and \(\text{Tr} (K^M) = Z\) with \(Z > 0\) real.

  • \(Y, Y', \ell, r\) are as in III.16.3.

  • \(c_1, \ldots, c_n\) are real numbers with \(| \text{Tr} ((B_k^{\ast} B_k)^q) |^{1 / (2 q)} \leqslant c_k \hspace{0.17em} Z^{1 / (2 q)}\) for every \(k\).

Conclusion.

\(\displaystyle | \text{Tr} (B_1 K^{Y'_1} \cdots B_n K^{Y'_n}) - \text{Tr} (B_1 K^{Y_1} \cdots B_n K^{Y_n}) | \leqslant Z \left( \prod_k c_k \right) \frac{\ell}{r} .\)

Status: theorem. Lean: YM.TimeKernel.passage, with closed (the cyclic integral), along (the word with gaps) and multiply (the column word \((B^{\ast} B)^q\)).

Proposition III.16.5 (holomorphy in a strip and exponentially weighted Laurent coefficients). Let \(\eta > 0\).

(a) Let \(u\) be holomorphic on \(S_{\eta}\) and \(2 \pi\)-periodic there: \(u (p + 2 \pi x) = u (p)\) for \(p \in S_{\eta}\) and \(x \in \mathbb{Z}^{\alpha}\). For \(p \in S_{\eta}\) let \(\hat{u}_p (x) = \int_{[0, 1]^{\alpha}} u (p + 2 \pi \theta) e^{- 2 \pi i \theta \cdot x} d \theta\), and put \(\hat{u} = \hat{u}_0\). Then:

  • the integral \(\int u (p + 2 \pi \theta) \hspace{0.17em} d \theta\) does not depend on \(p \in S_{\eta}\);

  • \(\hat{u}_p (x) = e^{ip \cdot x} \hat{u} (x)\).

If moreover \(|u| \leqslant K\) on \(S_{\eta}\), then:

  • \(| \hat{u}_p (x) | \leqslant K\);

  • for \(H \geqslant 0\) and \(b > 0\) with \(H + b < \eta\), \(\sum_x e^{H |x|_1} | \hat{u} (x) | \leqslant K (2 (1 + b) / b)^{\text{card} \hspace{0.17em} \alpha}\);

  • \(u (p) = \sum_x \hat{u} (x) e^{ip \cdot x}\) on \(S_{\eta}\), with absolute convergence.

(b) Let \(\sum_x e^{\eta |x|_1} |f (x) | \leqslant W\). Then:

  • \(| \hat{f} (p) | \leqslant W\) whenever \(| \text{Im} \hspace{0.17em} p_a | \leqslant \eta\) for all \(a\);

  • for \(0 < \sigma < \eta\), \(\hat{f}\) is holomorphic on \(S_{\sigma}\) with \(\|D \hat{f} (p)\| \leqslant W / (\eta - \sigma)\) (operator norm for the sup norm on \(\mathbb{C}^{\alpha}\)), so \(| \hat{f} (p) - \hat{f} (p') | \leqslant W \|p - p' \|_{\infty} / (\eta - \sigma)\) on \(S_{\sigma}\);

  • for real \(p\), the symbol of \(\text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} f\) equals \(\tfrac{1}{2} (\hat{f} (p) + \overline{\hat{f} (- p)})\).

Proposition III.16.6 (Fourier and Bloch decomposition on tori).

Setting.

  • All torus side lengths \(n, c, f, L\) are positive integers, and \(\Lambda_n = (\mathbb{Z}/ n\mathbb{Z})^4\). For \(p \in \Lambda_n\), represented in \(\{0, \ldots, n - 1\}^4\), let \(k_p = 2 \pi p / n\), and let \(F_n = (e^{ik_p \cdot x})_{x, p \in \Lambda_n}\).

  • \(\alpha, \beta\) are finite index sets, and the real kernel \(R = (R_{ab} (x))\), \(x \in \mathbb{Z}^4\), satisfies \(\sum_x |R_{ab} (x) | < \infty\) for every \(a \in \alpha\), \(b \in \beta\).

  • Its periodization on \(\Lambda_n\) is \(R_n ((y, a), (y', b)) = \sum_{x : \hspace{0.17em} y + x \equiv y'} R_{ab} (x)\), and its symbol is \(\hat{R} (k)_{ab} = \sum_x R_{ab} (x) e^{ik \cdot x}\).

(a) \(F_n F_n^{\ast} = F_n^{\ast} F_n = n^4 I\), and \(R_n (F_n \otimes I_{\beta}) = (F_n \otimes I_{\alpha}) \hspace{0.17em} \text{diag}_{p \in \Lambda_n} \hat{R} (k_p)\). This holds for every \(p\), including \(p = 0\).

(b) Let \(f = cL\). For \(p \in \Lambda_c\) and \(l \in \Lambda_L\) let \(k_{p, l} = 2 \pi (p + cl) / f\). These are the \(L^4\) fine momenta \(k\) with \(Lk \equiv 2 \pi p / c\) modulo \(2 \pi\), i.e. the aliases of the coarse momentum \(2 \pi p / c\). The fine Fourier matrix \(\Phi = (e^{ik_{p, l} \cdot y})_{y \in \Lambda_f, (p, l)}\) satisfies:

  • \(\Phi \Phi^{\ast} = f^4 I\);

  • \(R_f (\Phi \otimes I_{\beta}) = (\Phi \otimes I_{\alpha}) \hspace{0.17em} \text{diag}_{p \in \Lambda_c} \hspace{0.17em} \text{diag}_{l \in \Lambda_L} \hat{R} (k_{p, l})\).

(c) For every \(p \in \mathbb{C}^4\), the alias block \(E_L (p) = (e^{i (p + 2 \pi l) \cdot x / L})_{x, l \in \Lambda_L}\) satisfies \(E_L (\bar{p})^{\ast} E_L (p) = L^4 I\).

Proposition III.16.7 (analytic square root near a spectral band). Let \(m > 0\) and let the finite matrix index set be nonempty. For complex matrices \(X\) with \(\|X / m - 1\| < 1\), put \(\Phi_m (X) := \sqrt{m} \sum_{k \geqslant 0} \binom{1 / 2}{k} (X / m - 1)^k\).

  • \(\Phi_m\) is holomorphic on this ball.

  • \(\Phi_m\) is bounded on each smaller ball \(\|X / m - 1\| \leqslant r < 1\).

  • \(\Phi_m\) commutes with entrywise complex conjugation.

  • If \(X\) is Hermitian and \(v \leqslant X \leqslant m\) as quadratic forms, with \(v > 0\), then \(\Phi_m (X)\) is Hermitian, \(\Phi_m (X)^2 = X\), and \(\sqrt{v} \leqslant \Phi_m (X) \leqslant \sqrt{m}\).

Notation for (C).

  • Fix a compact matrix gauge group \(G\). Its colour space \(\mathfrak{g}\) is the real Lie algebra in coordinates orthonormal for \(- (\text{dim} \hspace{0.17em} \rho)^{- 1} \text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} \text{tr} (XY)\), where \(\rho\) is the fixed defining representation.

  • A homogeneous polynomial on spacetime is identified with its symmetric multilinear Taylor coefficient.

  • For \(j \geqslant 0\):

    • \(\mathcal{A}_j\) is the space of \(4\)-tuples \((A_l)\) of \(\mathfrak{g}\)-valued homogeneous polynomials of degree \(j + 1\) on \(\mathbb{R}^4\) with \(\sum_l x_l A_l (x) = 0\) (radial, or Fock–Schwinger, gauge);

    • \(\mathcal{C}_j\) is the space of \(\mathfrak{g}\)-valued homogeneous polynomials \(F_{il}\) of degree \(j\) with \(F_{il} = - F_{li}\) and \(\partial_a F_{bc} + \partial_b F_{ca} + \partial_c F_{ab} = 0\);

    • \(\mathcal{S}_j \subset \mathcal{C}_j\), the Maxwell shell, adds the condition \(\sum_a \partial_a F_{ab} = 0\).

Proposition III.16.8 (free Maxwell field).

(a) (Poincaré lemma in radial gauge.) The curl \(A \mapsto F_{il} = \partial_i A_l - \partial_l A_i\) is a linear isomorphism \(\mathcal{A}_j \to \mathcal{C}_j\). Its inverse is \(A_i (x) = (j + 2)^{- 1} \sum_k x_k F_{ki} (x)\).

(b) (Taylor coefficients of solutions.) Let \(U \subset \mathbb{R}^4\) be open, and let the \(\mathfrak{g}\)-valued field \(f = (f_{ab})\) be smooth and antisymmetric on \(U\) and satisfy the Bianchi and Maxwell equations there. Then for every \(x \in U\) and \(j \geqslant 0\), the \(j\)-th Taylor coefficient of \(f\) at \(x\) lies in \(\mathcal{S}_j\).

(c) (Free two-point function.) The function \(G_0 (x) = 1 / (4 \pi^2 |x|^2)\) is harmonic on \(\mathbb{R}^4 \setminus \{0\}\). Put

\(\displaystyle C_{ab, km} = \partial_a \psi_{b, km} - \partial_b \psi_{a, km}, \qquad \psi_{b, km} = \delta_{kb} \partial_m G_0 - \delta_{mb} \partial_k G_0 .\)

This is the field-strength two-point function \(\langle F_{ab} (x) F_{km} (0) \rangle\) of one colour component of the free field in Feynman gauge. Then:

  • for fixed \((k, m)\), \((a, b) \mapsto C_{ab, km}\) is smooth and antisymmetric on \(x \ne 0\) and solves the Bianchi and Maxwell equations there;

  • the same holds in \((k, m)\) for fixed \((a, b)\);

  • \(C_{ab, km} (x) = C_{km, ab} (x) = C_{ab, km} (- x)\).

(d) (One contraction between jets.) Let \(j, l \geqslant 0\), and let \(p\) and \(q\) be real linear forms on \(\mathcal{S}_j\) and \(\mathcal{S}_l\) (probes). Write \(c_j h (z) = D^j h (z) / j!\), and let \((e_c)\) be the orthonormal colour basis. Choose linear extensions \(P, Q\) of \(p, q\) to the unrestricted two-index multilinear tensors. Define the colour-valued field

\(\displaystyle R_{P, km} (z) = \sum_c P ((c_j (C_{ab, km} e_c) (z))_{a, b}) e_c,\)

and, for \(z \ne 0\), put

\(\displaystyle \kappa (p, q) (z) = (- 1)^l Q ((c_l R_{P, km} (z))_{k, m}) .\)

This definition is independent of both extensions. It is bilinear in \((p, q)\), smooth for \(z \ne 0\), and satisfies \(\kappa (p, q) (z) = \kappa (q, p) (- z)\).

For the coordinate probes \(p (F) = F_{ab} [u]_c\) and \(q (F) = F_{km} [v]_d\), with \(u \in (\mathbb{R}^4)^j\) and \(v \in (\mathbb{R}^4)^l\),

\(\displaystyle \kappa (p, q) (z) = \delta_{cd} (- 1)^l c_l (t \mapsto c_j C_{ab, km} (t) [u]) (z) [v] .\)

Equivalently, at \(z = x - y\) this is the mixed Taylor coefficient at \((x, y)\) of \(\delta_{cd} C_{ab, km} (x - y)\). Thus \(\kappa\) is the free covariance of the corresponding jet observables, with Taylor coefficients used at both endpoints.

Proposition III.16.9 (field labels).

Setting. Let \(d \geqslant 0\) be an integer.

  • Fix real bases of the spaces \(\mathcal{A}_j\).

  • \(\text{Pol}\) is the algebra of complex polynomials in the resulting coordinates of a jet sequence \((A_j)_{j \geqslant 0}\).

  • A coordinate from \(\mathcal{A}_j\) has weight \(j + 2\), and a monomial has the sum of the weights of its factors.

  • \(\text{Label}_d \subset \text{Pol}\) consists of the polynomials of weight \(\leqslant d\) that are invariant under the simultaneous adjoint action of \(G\) on all \(A_j\). Invariance is imposed on the whole polynomial.

(a) A polynomial that vanishes on all real jet sequences is zero.

(b) There are finitely many monomials of weight \(\leqslant d\), so \(\text{Label}_d\) is finite-dimensional.

(c) Every element of \(\text{Label}_d\) is the sum of its bihomogeneous components (weight, polynomial degree), both indices \(\leqslant d\).

  • Each component is again a label.

  • Taking components commutes with complex conjugation of coefficients and with the action of \(O (4)\) on jets.

  • For \(s \in O (4)\), set \((T_s A_j)_i (x) = \sum_k s_{ik} (A_j)_k (s^{- 1} x)\). The induced label action is \((s \cdot P) (A) = P (T_{s^{- 1}} A)\); it satisfies \((st) \cdot P = s \cdot (t \cdot P)\).

(d) For every integer \(d \geqslant 0\), \(\text{Label}_d\) has a complex basis (a frame) \(e_0 = 1, e_1, \ldots, e_{N_d}\) of bihomogeneous labels with real coefficients. These bases can be chosen simultaneously so that, under the natural inclusion, every basis vector at level \(d\) occurs at level \(d + 1\). No ordering beyond \(e_0 = 1\) is prescribed.

Proposition III.16.10 (free Wick hierarchy on labels).

Definitions. Let \(n \geqslant 0\), let \(d_1, \ldots, d_n \geqslant 0\) be integers, and let \(x_1, \ldots, x_n \in \mathbb{R}^4\) be pairwise distinct.

  • For Wick monomials in the coordinates of shell jets, the free moment is the sum, over all pairings of the factor occurrences in which partners sit at different points, of the products of the kernels \(\kappa (\cdot, \cdot) (x_i - x_{i'})\) of the paired coordinate forms. It is extended multilinearly to Wick polynomials.

  • The free correlation of labels \(P_k \in \text{Label}_{d_k}\) is the moment of their restrictions to on-shell jets, \(A_j = \text{curl}^{- 1} F_j\) with \(F_j \in \mathcal{S}_j\).

Properties.

  • The free correlation is smooth on the configuration space.

  • It vanishes if one label vanishes on all on-shell jets, i.e. lies in the equation ideal.

  • It is invariant under permuting the points together with the labels. More generally, embedding the index set into a larger configuration of distinct points preserves the correlation when each added point carries the label \(1\).

  • The moment with every insertion equal to \(1\) is \(1\), including at \(n = 0\). For constant insertions \(a_1, \ldots, a_n \in \mathbb{C}\), it is \(\prod_i a_i\).

3. Strategy

(A) Moving an insertion. Transfer-kernel correlations have the form \(\text{Tr} (B_1 K^{Y_1} \cdots B_n K^{Y_n}) / Z\). Moving one insertion transfers \(\ell\) units between two gaps. For finite matrices, continue the exponents along \(Y + z (e_i - e_j)\). The trace is entire, and the Hölder balance is unchanged. III.16.2 bounds it by a constant \(C\) on \(|z| \leqslant 2 r\); Cauchy's estimate gives \(|F' | \leqslant C / r\) on \(|z| \leqslant r\), hence a change of at most \(C \ell / r\). The dimension-free bound passes to kernels by sampling every factor and adjoint with the same partition. 4.1 Positive kernels and quadrature

Lemma 4.1 (algebra of positive-semidefinite kernels).

Statement.

  • Generators: the class contains the Gram kernels \(\overline{f (x)} f (y)\) and the constant \(1\).

  • Closure: it is closed under multiplication by constants \(c \geqslant 0\), finite sums, pointwise (Schur) products, nonnegative integer powers, pullbacks \(K (\varphi (x), \varphi (y))\), pointwise limits, and \(K \mapsto e^K\).

  • Criterion: a kernel is positive semidefinite if and only if it is Hermitian and \(\sum_{i, j} \bar{c}_i K (x_i, x_j) c_j \geqslant 0\) for all finite families.

  • Integrated form: on a compact metric space with a finite Borel measure, for continuous positive-semidefinite \(K\) and bounded measurable \(f\), \(\iint K (x, y) \overline{f (x)} f (y) \hspace{0.17em} d \mu \hspace{0.17em} d \mu \geqslant 0\).

Sketch.

  • Products follow from the Schur product theorem.

  • \(e^K\) is the limit of the partial sums \(\sum_{m < N} K^m / m!\).

  • Limits are handled by the quadratic-form criterion. Hermitian symmetry passes to the limit through the \(2 \times 2\) Gram matrices.

  • For the integrated form, replace \(K\) by its values at the tags of a fine partition (Lemma 4.2). The integral becomes \(\sum_{i, j} \overline{\int_{\text{cell } i} f} \hspace{0.17em} K (x_i, x_j) \int_{\text{cell } j} f \geqslant 0\), and dominated convergence finishes the proof.

Lemma 4.2 (quadrature).

Statement. Let \(X\) be a compact metric space with a finite Borel measure \(\mu\).

  • For each \(k \geqslant 0\) there is a finite measurable partition of \(X\) with assigned tags such that every point lies within \(1 / (k + 1)\) of its cell's tag. Empty cells and zero masses are allowed; a tag need not lie in its cell.

  • Let \(m_i = \mu (\text{cell } i)\). For every finite index set \(I\) and every continuous \(F : X^I \to E\) with \(E\) a Banach space, the Riemann sums \(\sum_{i \in \text{cells}^I} \prod_{j \in I} m_{i_j} \hspace{0.17em} F ((x_{i_j})_{j \in I})\) converge to \(\int F \hspace{0.17em} d \mu^{\otimes I}\).

Sketch.

  • The partitions: cover \(X\) by finitely many balls of radius \(1 / (k + 1)\) and disjointify.

  • The statement concerns each fixed \(F\) and finite product; it asserts no uniform rate over families of integrands.

4.2 The finite trace inequality

Lemma 4.4 (three-lines Hölder lemma).

Definition. A strip path is an entire map \(V : \mathbb{C} \to M_{\iota} (\mathbb{C})\) with:

  • \(\|V (t)\| \leqslant 1\) for \(0 \leqslant \text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} t \leqslant 1\);

  • trace norm \(\sup_{\|C\| \leqslant 1} | \text{tr} \hspace{0.17em} V (t) C| \leqslant 1\) for \(\text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} t = 1\).

Statement. If \(V_1, \ldots, V_n\) are strip paths and \(h_k \in \mathbb{C}\) satisfy \(\text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} h_k \in [0, 1]\) and \(\sum_k \text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} h_k = 1\), then \(| \text{tr} \hspace{0.17em} V_1 (h_1) \cdots V_n (h_n) | \leqslant 1\).

Sketch. Prove \(| \text{tr} (c \hspace{0.17em} V_1 (h_1) \cdots V_n (h_n)) | \leqslant 1\) for every contraction \(c\), by induction on \(n\). The case \(n = 0\) is excluded by the balance condition. In the induction step let \(t = \text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} h_1\).

  • Case \(t = 1\). The other factors are contractions. Apply the trace-norm bound of \(V_1\) after a cyclic permutation.

  • Case \(t < 1\). Put \(E (x) = \text{tr} \left( c \hspace{0.17em} V_1 (x + i \hspace{0.17em} \text{Im} \hspace{0.17em} h_1) \prod_{k \geqslant 2} V_k (x_k (x)) \right)\) with \(x_k (x) = \text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} h_k \hspace{0.17em} \frac{1 - x}{1 - t} + i \hspace{0.17em} \text{Im} \hspace{0.17em} h_k\).

    • \(E\) is entire and bounded on \(0 \leqslant \text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} x \leqslant 1\). The bound depends on \(\text{card} \hspace{0.17em} \iota\), but it is used only qualitatively.

    • On \(\text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} x = 0\), the first factor is a contraction absorbed into \(c\), and the remaining real parts sum to \(1\); the induction hypothesis gives \(|E| \leqslant 1\).

    • On \(\text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} x = 1\), the factors \(V_k (x_k (x))\) with \(k \geqslant 2\) are contractions, and the trace-norm bound of \(V_1\) gives \(|E| \leqslant 1\).

    • The three-lines theorem gives \(|E (t) | \leqslant 1\), and \(E (t)\) is the trace to be bounded.

Lemma 4.25 (Wick hierarchy; III.16.10).

Definitions.

  • Pairings are fixed-point-free involutions of the factor occurrences whose partners sit at different points; no self-contractions are allowed.

  • The moment is defined on the monomial basis and extended multilinearly.

  • Labels enter through the substitution \(A_j = \text{curl}^{- 1} F_j\), \(F_j \in \mathcal{S}_j\).

  • The equation ideal is the kernel of this substitution. It consists of the labels that vanish on every on-shell jet sequence.

Sketch of the properties.

  • Vanishing on the equation ideal: multilinearity.

  • Relabelling and insertion of \(1\): added unit monomials have no occurrences. An injection of the remaining indices induces bijections of occurrences and pairings. If the orientation of an edge reverses, the exchange identity for \(\kappa\) preserves its value. Extend from monomials by multilinearity.

  • Smoothness: each polynomial has finitely many monomials and each monomial finitely many pairings, so the result is a finite sum of products of smooth kernels off the diagonal. When all insertions are \(1\), there are no occurrences and exactly one empty pairing, whose product is \(1\).

Lemma 4.26 (= III.16.9).

Sketch.

  • (a) A complex polynomial that vanishes on \(\mathbb{R}^N\) is zero.

  • (b) A coordinate's exponent in a monomial of weight \(\leqslant d\) is at most \(d\), and only finitely many coordinates have weight \(\leqslant d\).

5. Where the difficulty lies

The analytic issue in III.16.3–III.16.4 is to retain a bound independent of matrix dimension while varying the exponents and passing to integral kernels. The three-lines argument uses dimension only for qualitative boundedness on the strip. Arbitrary insertions are allowed: their Schatten norms supply the entire insertion cost. Positivity and a positive cyclic trace are required of the transfer kernel, and quadrature is taken at fixed exponents and period.

The complex-power convention matters at zero: \(A^0\) is the range projection. The strict inequalities \(Y_k > 2 r\) ensure that the integer endpoints agree with ordinary positive powers. Fourier inversion loses strip width and the explicit factor \((2 (1 + b) / b)^4\); differentiating a Laurent sum costs \(1 / (\eta - \sigma)\). The Bloch identities retain all momenta, and at complex momentum the alias identity uses the adjoint evaluated at \(\bar{p}\).

For Maxwell jets, the conventions are Taylor coefficients \(D^j / j!\), real orthonormal colour coordinates, the normalization \((4 \pi^2)^{- 1}\), and the sign \((- 1)^l\) at the second endpoint. The equation subspace is defined by restriction to shell jets; no presentation by invariant generators is needed. The chosen label bases are coherent as sets of polynomials, rather than as prescribed ordered lists.
