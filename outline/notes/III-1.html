Conventions. \(N \geqslant 1\) and \(G \subset U (N)\) is a closed subgroup with probability Haar measure \(dg\). Its Lie algebra \(\mathfrak{g} \subset M_N (\mathbb{C})\) carries the real inner product \(\text{Re tr} (X^{\dagger} Y)\), and \(\Pi_{\mathfrak{g}}\) denotes the orthogonal projection onto \(\mathfrak{g}\). Matrix norms below are Frobenius norms. In path actions, Lie-algebra fields, Hessians and the six works, the physical inner product is \(N^{- 1} \text{Re tr} (X^{\dagger} Y)\); write \(|X| = \|X\| / \sqrt{N}\) for its norm. This is the coordinate normalization of Gauge.inner_frob_color. The curvature field is \(\text{curv} (u) = \Pi_{\mathfrak{g}} (u - u^{- 1}) / 2\), with \(| \text{curv} (u) | \leqslant \text{ch} (u)\). The chord is \(\text{ch} (u) = \|u - 1\| / \sqrt{N}\) and the price is \(\text{pr} (u) = 1 - \text{Re tr} \hspace{0.17em} u / N\). They satisfy \(\text{ch}^2 = 2 \hspace{0.17em} \text{pr}\), both are class functions, \(\text{ch} (u^{- 1}) = \text{ch} (u) \leqslant 2\), \(\text{ch} (uv) \leqslant \text{ch} (u) + \text{ch} (v)\), and \(\|u - v\|^2 = 2 N \hspace{0.17em} \text{pr} (uv^{- 1})\). The Wilson energy is \(\sum_p \text{pr} (U_p)\).

A step goes from the fine torus \(\Lambda_f = (\mathbb{Z}/ f)^4\) to the coarse torus \(\Lambda_c = (\mathbb{Z}/ c)^4\), with \(c \geqslant 1\), \(f = cL\) and \(L \geqslant 3\) odd. The coarse site \(x\) has root \(Lx\) and block \(Lx + B_{(L - 1) / 2}\), where \(B_r = \{- r, \ldots, r\}^4 \subset \mathbb{Z}^4\). Configurations are \(U \in G^{\text{links}}\), and \(\mu_f\) is the product of the probability Haar measures. The gauge action is \((k \cdot U)_{(y, i)} = k_y U_{(y, i)} k_{y + e_i}^{- 1}\). \(T_U (y ; w)\) is the parallel transport along a walk \(w\). \(T_U (y \to z ; o)\) is the transport along the coordinate route that changes the coordinates in the order given by \(o \in S_4\). For a coarse site \(x\), \(U^{(x)}\) is the periodic lift of \(U\) to \(\mathbb{Z}^4\), translated so that \(Lx\) sits at the origin. All patch constructions take place on \(\mathbb{Z}^4\), so a patch may be larger than the torus. For finite \(X \subset \mathbb{Z}^4\), \(E_X (U)\) is the sum of the chords of the links with both ends in \(X\), and \(F_X (U)\) is the sum of the chords of the plaquettes with all four corners in \(X\); \(F_X\) is gauge invariant. \(\mathfrak{B}_4\) is the group of signed permutations of the axes (384 elements). For \(s \in \mathfrak{B}_4\) and a point \(b\), \(s_b U\) is the pullback of \(U\) under \(z \mapsto b + s (z)\); a link reversed by \(s\) carries the inverse variable. Further, \(\kappa_{\text{slope}} = 11 C_A / (24 \pi^2)\), where \(C_A\) is the adjoint Casimir, and \(\text{temp} (x) = x^{- 1 / 2} e^{(\log \log x)^2}\) for \(x > 1\).

Definition (the block kernel). A patch is \(P = (L, J)\) with \(L \geqslant 3\) odd and \(J \geqslant 4\). Its radius is \(R = (J + 3) L\) and its points are those of \(B_R\), with centre \(0\). The kernel constants are \(K = (\kappa, \delta, \varepsilon, a)\), all positive: penalty, frame cutoff, predictor cutoff, axial factor. For a configuration \(W\) on \(\mathbb{Z}^4\) the following objects are defined.

  1. Frame tags and axial frames. A frame tag is \(t = (r, o) \in B_R \times S_4\). The axial frame of \(t\) is \(a^t_y = T_W (r \to y ; o)\).

  2. Minimizing branch. \(W \in M_{\delta}\) if \(\text{ch} \left( a^{(0, o)}_y \hspace{0.17em} T_W (y \to z ; o') \hspace{0.17em} (a^{(0, o)}_z)^{- 1} \right) < \delta\) for all \(o, o' \in S_4\) and \(y, z \in B_R\). In words: in each of the 24 axial gauges from the centre, every route transport in the patch is \(\delta\)-close to \(1\).

  3. Score. For \(h : B_R \to G\), \(S_W (h) = \text{avg}_{y, z \in B_R} \text{avg}_{o \in S_4} \hspace{0.17em} \text{pr} (h_y T_W (y \to z ; o) h_z^{- 1})\).

  4. Eligible tags. \(\mathcal{E} (W) = \{t : E_{B_R} (a^t \cdot W) \leqslant a (2 R + 1) F_{B_R} (W)\}\).

  5. Frame and tag mass. If \(W \in M_{\delta}\), the frame \(h^W\) is a minimizer of \(S_W\) subject to \(h_0 = 1\), and the tag mass is uniform, \(1 / (24| B_R |)\). Otherwise \(h^{W, t}_y = (a^t_0)^{- 1} a^t_y\) (the axial gauge from \(r\), normalized at the centre), with mass \(\boldsymbol{1}_{\mathcal{E} (W)} (t) / |\mathcal{E}(W) |\).

  6. Weight. \(w\) is the law on \(B_R\) of \(\xi_1 + \ldots + \xi_J\), where the \(\xi_k\) are i.i.d. uniform on \(B_{(L - 1) / 2}\). It is the \(J\)-fold convolution of the uniform weight on a centred block; it is \(\mathfrak{B}_4\)-invariant and supported in \(B_{J (L - 1) / 2}\).

  7. Connector. For a coarse link \(b = (x, i)\) and frame tags \((t_x)_{x \in \Lambda_c}\), set \(Y_y = h^{(x)}_y \hspace{0.17em} T_{U^{(x)}} (y ; L \text{ steps along } e_i) \hspace{0.17em} (h^{(x + e_i)}_y)^{- 1}\) for \(y \in B_R\). Here \(h^{(x)}\) is the frame of \(U^{(x)}\) with tag \(t_x\). This is the fine transport from \(Lx + y\) to \(L (x + e_i) + y\), written in the frames of the two patches.

  8. Prediction. Let \(\Phi_Y (u) = \sum_y w_y \hspace{0.17em} \text{pr} (Y_y u^{- 1})\). If \(\min \Phi_Y < \varepsilon\), then \(Q_b\) is a chosen minimizer, equivalently a nearest point of \(G\) to the matrix mean \(\sum_y w_y Y_y\) (an extrinsic, chordal mean); the bond tag then has uniform mass \(1 / |B_R |\). Otherwise \(Q_b = Y_{r_b}\), with the bond tag \(r_b\) drawn with probability \(w_{r_b}\).

  9. Kernel. The tag law is \(\lambda (U, t) = \prod_x m^{\text{fr}} (U^{(x)}, t_x) \prod_b m^{\text{pr}} (Y_b, r_b)\), and

\(\displaystyle \rho_{\beta} (U, V) = \sum_t \lambda (U, t) \prod_b Z_{\beta}^{- 1} e^{- \beta \kappa \hspace{0.17em} \text{pr} (V_b Q_b (U, t)^{- 1})}, \qquad Z_{\beta} = \int_G e^{- \beta \kappa \hspace{0.17em} \text{pr} (g)} \hspace{0.17em} dg.\)

The branch conditions for \((P, K, G)\) are:

  • (B1) for \(W \in M_{\delta}\) the constrained minimizer in item 5 is unique;

  • (B2) whenever \(\min \Phi_Y < \varepsilon\), the minimizer of \(\Phi_Y\) is unique;

  • (B3) \(\mathcal{E} (W) \ne \emptyset\) for every \(W\).

Compactness gives the chosen minimizers; (B1)–(B2) make the choices unique on the minimizing branches. Lean: Patch, KernelChoices, usesMin, score, eligible, frameMass, frame, weight, bondCost, predict, predictorMass, Branches, StepData, connector, prediction, likelihood, density.

1. What this unit proves

The block-spin kernel is normalized, Borel and covariant under gauge and lattice symmetries. Its branch cutoffs can be chosen independently of the patch size. The geometric estimates synchronize neighbouring frames and bound the predicted coarse curvature by \(L^{- 2}\) times the fine curvature in a surrounding box. Near the trivial configuration the prediction is analytic, with linearization \(\mathcal{M}+ d_0 \mathcal{F}\). Tree gauge reduces the step integral to the free links. The unit also defines effective densities, RG histories and the requirements used in the later energy and stationary-branch estimates.

Proposition III.1.1 (the block kernel is a Markov kernel). Assume (B1)–(B3). For every \(\beta \in \mathbb{R}\):

  • \(\rho_{\beta} \geqslant 0\);

  • \((U, V) \mapsto \rho_{\beta} (U, V)\) is Borel;

  • \(\sup \rho_{\beta} < \infty\);

  • \(\int \rho_{\beta} (U, V) \hspace{0.17em} d \mu_c (V) = 1\) for every \(U\).

Consequently, for every Banach-space-valued \(F \in L^1 (\mu_f)\) the push \((\mathcal{P}_{\beta} F) (V) = \int \rho_{\beta} (U, V) F (U) \hspace{0.17em} d \mu_f (U)\) is defined for every \(V\). Moreover \(\mathcal{P}_{\beta} F \in L^1 (\mu_c)\), \(\mathcal{P}_{\beta}\) is linear, and \(\int \mathcal{P}_{\beta} F \hspace{0.17em} d \mu_c = \int F \hspace{0.17em} d \mu_f\). This is the operation that takes level \(m + 1\) to level \(m\). Status: theorem. Positivity and integrability in \(V\) hold without (B1)–(B3). Lean: IsKernel, isKernel, push, push_integrable, push_integral, push_add, push_smul.

Proposition III.1.2 (exact covariance). Assume (B1)–(B3). For all \(\beta \in \mathbb{R}\), \(U\), \(V\):

  • (a) \(\rho_{\beta} (k \cdot U, (k \circ \text{embed}) \cdot V) = \rho_{\beta} (U, V)\) for every \(k : \Lambda_f \to G\), where \(\text{embed} (x) = Lx\);

  • (b) \(\rho_{\beta} (s_{Lb} U, s_b V) = \rho_{\beta} (U, V)\) for every \(s \in \mathfrak{B}_4\) and \(b \in \Lambda_c\);

  • (c) if the kernel constants of a calendar satisfy (B1)–(B3) for every patch, then every step of the calendar has block control (branch conditions together with (a) and (b)), and is a Markov kernel by III.1.1.

Status: theorem. Lean: covary, channel, Calendar.blocks_go, Calendar.BlockControl, Levels.transition_mass.

Proposition III.1.3 (branch constants depending only on \(G\)). Let \(d = d (G)\) be the constant of III.1.5. Let \(r_G > 0\) be such that every \(X \in M_N (\mathbb{C})\) at distance \(< r_G\) from \(G\) has a unique nearest point in \(G\). Put \(\delta_0 = d / \sqrt{N}\) and \(\varepsilon_0 = r_G^2 / (2 N)\). Then for every \(\kappa > 0\), \(\delta \in (0, \delta_0]\), \(\varepsilon \in (0, \varepsilon_0]\) and \(a \geqslant 16\), the constants \((\kappa, \delta, \varepsilon, a)\) satisfy (B1)–(B3) for every patch, that is, for every odd \(L \geqslant 3\) and every \(J \geqslant 4\). Condition (B3) holds in a stronger form: for every \(W\) and every ordering \(o\) there is a root \(r\) with \((r, o)\) eligible. Status: theorem. Proposition III.1.5 (least-squares synchronization: uniqueness up to a global left multiple). There is \(d = d (G) \in (0, 1 / 4]\) with the following property. Let \(n, \Lambda\) be finite nonempty sets. Let \(W : n \times n \times \Lambda \to G\) satisfy \(W_{xxk} = 1\) and \(W_{yx \sigma (k)} = W_{xyk}^{- 1}\) for some permutation \(\sigma\) of \(\Lambda\), and \(\|W_{xyk} - 1\| \leqslant d\) for all indices. Then any two global minimizers \(h, h'\) of \(\text{cost} (h) = \text{avg}_{x, y, k} \hspace{0.17em} \text{pr} (h_x W_{xyk} h_y^{- 1})\) over \(G^n\) satisfy \(h' = c \hspace{0.17em} h\) for a single \(c \in G\). The constant \(d\) does not depend on \(|n|\) or \(| \Lambda |\). Status: theorem. Lean: Branch.align, with Pairs.

Proposition III.1.6 (synchronization of the realized frame with any gauge). There is \(A = A (G) > 0\) with the following property. Let \(W\) be a configuration on \(\mathbb{Z}^4\), \(t\) a frame tag with \(m^{\text{fr}} (W, t) > 0\), \(h\) the realized frame, and \(H : \mathbb{Z}^4 \to G\) any gauge. Then there is \(c \in G\) with

\(\displaystyle \sum_{y \in B_R} \text{ch} (c^{- 1} h_y H_y^{- 1}) \leqslant A \hspace{0.17em} (2 R + 1) \left( E_{B_R} (H \cdot W) + a \hspace{0.17em} (2 R + 1) \hspace{0.17em} F_{B_R} (W) \right) .\)

Status: theorem. Lean: Pressure.synchronized.

Proposition III.1.7 (one gauge for a neighbourhood of blocks). Fix \(G\), \(K\) and \(j \geqslant 4\), and put \(\ell_j = 2 j + 15\). Then \(D = 16 \hspace{0.17em} \ell_j\) and \(C = A \hspace{0.17em} \ell_j (D + a \hspace{0.17em} \ell_j)\), with \(A\) from III.1.6, have the following property. Let \(S\) be any step with kernel constants \(K\) and \(J = j\) (any \(L\), any \(c\)). Let \(U\) be fine, \(x\) a coarse site, and \(t\) a tag that is usable at \(x\), meaning that \(m^{\text{fr}} (U^{(x + y)}, t_{x + y}) > 0\) for all \(y \in B_2\). Then there are a gauge \(H : \mathbb{Z}^4 \to G\) and pads \(p_y \in G\) (\(y \in B_2\)) with

\(\displaystyle E_{B_{R'}} (H \cdot U^{(x)}) \leqslant D \hspace{0.17em} L \hspace{0.17em} \text{port} (x, U), \qquad \sum_{i \in B_R} \text{ch} \left( p_y^{- 1} h^{(x + y)}_i \hspace{0.17em} H_{i + Ly}^{- 1} \right) \leqslant C \hspace{0.17em} L^2 \hspace{0.17em} \text{port} (x, U) \quad (y \in B_2) .\)

Here \(R' = R + 4 L = (J + 7) L\) and \(\text{port} (x, U) = F_{B_{R'}} (U^{(x)})\) is the fine curvature in the macro-box around \(x\). Every tag with \(\lambda (U, t) > 0\) is usable at every coarse site. Status: theorem. Lean: Pressure.planting, usable, wraps, port, macroR.

Proposition III.1.8 (predicted versus actual coarse curvature). For \(G\), \(K\), \(j\) let \(C_8 = 8 \max (1, \varepsilon^{- 1}) (2 C + D)\), with \(C, D\) from III.1.7. Let \(S\) be any step with constants \(K\) and \(J = j\). Then for all \(U\) and \(V\), every coarse site \(x\), every tag \(t\) usable at \(x\) and every plane \(p\):

\(\displaystyle \text{ch} (Q (U, t)_{(x, p)}) \leqslant \frac{C_8}{L^2} \hspace{0.17em} \text{port} (x, U), \qquad \text{ch} (V_{(x, p)}) \leqslant \text{fuel} (Q, V)_{(x, p)} + \frac{C_8}{L^2} \hspace{0.17em} \text{port} (x, U) .\)

Here \(Q_{(x, p)}\) denotes the plaquette holonomy, and \(\text{fuel} (Q, V)_{(x, p)} = \sum_{e \in \partial (x, p)} \text{ch} (V_e Q_e^{- 1})\) is the sum of the four wires of the plaquette, counted with multiplicity even when edges coincide on a short torus. The macro-box contains \(6 (2 R')^2 (2 R' + 1)^2\) internal plaquettes. Thus \(L^{- 4} \text{port}\) differs from their average chord by a factor bounded above and below in terms of \(J\) only. The bound therefore says that the coarse predicted plaquette is at most \(C (G, K, J) \hspace{0.17em} L^2\) times the average fine curvature: the area scaling of the non-abelian Stokes formula, with no further loss in \(L\). Status: theorem. Lean: Pressure.qdc, fuel, wire.

Proposition III.1.9 (effective densities and RG histories). The following definitions carry four elementary consequences.

(a) Budget points, flags, masks. A budget point is a tuple (integer power \(\geqslant 2\), integer order \(\geqslant 6\), support exponent \(> 0\), cover size \(B \geqslant 1\), threshold \(t \in (0, 1]\)). The flagged sites \(\text{flags}_t (U)\) are the sites touched by a plaquette of chord \(> t\). \(U\) is healthy on \(X\) if \(X \cap \text{flags}_t (U) = \emptyset\), and the masked function is \(\boldsymbol{1}_{\{ \text{healthy on } X\}} F\). A function is regular on \(X\) if it depends only on links in \(X\), is Borel and gauge invariant, and at each configuration healthy on \(X\) is \(C^q\) in exponential left increments, where \(q\) is the order; its ordered left derivatives through order \(q\) must exist. Its sup bounds control derivatives of orders \(0, \ldots, q\) in directions with \(|A_e | \leqslant t (1 + \text{dist} (e^-, o))^{\text{power}}\), where \(e^-\) is the base of the positive link. Theorem: a function that reads the links in \(X\) and the flags in \(X\) depends only on the links in \(\text{inspected} (X)\), the union of \(X\) and the corners of all plaquettes meeting \(X\).

(b) Activities. There are regular activities \(E_i\), with an address (a site \(o_i\) in a support \(X_i\)), and forced activities \(K_i\), with a support \(Y_i\), an inventory \(\emptyset \ne I_i \subset Y_i\) and an extra test. Validity at bounds \(\delta_E, w_K \geqslant 0\) means the following:

  • \(E_i\) is regular on \(X_i\) and \(E_i (1) = 0\);

  • if \(10 \hspace{0.17em} \text{dist} (y, o_i) < n\) for every \(y \in X_i\), where \(n\) is the torus side, the Hessian of \(E_i\) at the trivial configuration vanishes on affine directions \(A_{(y, \mu)} = a_{\mu} + \sum_{\nu} \widetilde{(y - o_i)}_{\nu} d_{\mu \nu}\), using the canonical centred lift \(\widetilde{(y - o_i)}\); equivalently, \(D^2 [E_i (\exp A)]_{A = 0} [A, B] = 0\) for every two such directions \(A, B\);

  • \(K_i\) and its extra test are gauge invariant, Borel, and depend only on links and flags in \(Y_i\); \(K_i\) vanishes unless \(Y_i \cap \text{flags}_t = I_i\) and the test holds;

  • covariance under every element of \(\mathfrak{B}_4\) and every base point (index permutations transporting addresses, inventories, tests and values);

  • there are nonnegative majorants \(e_i, k_i\), with \(e_i\) bounding the regular derivatives and \(|K_i | \leqslant k_i\), such that, for every site \(x\),

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i : o_i = x} e^{\sigma \hspace{0.17em} \text{cov}_B (X_i)} e_i \leqslant \delta_E, \qquad \sum_{i : x \in Y_i} e^{\sigma \hspace{0.17em} \text{cov}_B (Y_i)} k_i \leqslant w_K .\)

Here \(\sigma\) is the support exponent and \(\text{cov}_B (X)\) is the least number of radius-\(B\) balls with connected union covering \(X\); both series are required to converge.

The gas is \(\sum_b \prod_{i \in b} K_i (U)\), summed over finite families \(b\) with pairwise disjoint supports whose inventories cover \(\text{flags}_t (U)\) exactly. Theorem: the gas equals \(1\) when \(\text{flags}_t (U) = \emptyset\).

(c) Leading action. A clip has \(c_{\text{dir}} > 16 \hspace{0.17em} c_{\text{lin}} > 0\) and a relaxation length. Path data consist of row series \(\ell_p (U) = \sum_k r_{p, k} \hspace{0.17em} \text{slot}_k (U)\) (weighted parallel-transported curvatures) and loop, cubic and quartic atoms. Their bounds require exponentially weighted summability, invariance, the normalization \(c_{\text{dir}} \delta_{pq} + \sum_k \hat{s}_k (p) \hat{s}_k (q) = \delta_{pq}\) of the zero-momentum symbols (the sums of row coefficients at each plaquette orientation), and vanishing zeroth and first moments of the loop coefficients. The row term at a plaquette based at \(x\) is kept exactly when \(\text{ch} (U_q) \leqslant t \exp (\text{dist} (x, q^-) / r_{\text{rel}})\) for every plaquette \(q\), where \(r_{\text{rel}} > 0\) is the relaxation length and \(q^-\) its base. The per-plaquette term is \(\text{clip}_t (\text{ch} U_p) +\boldsymbol{1}_{\text{kept}} \tfrac{1}{2} | \ell_p (U) |^2\), where \(\text{clip}_t (s) = c_{\text{dir}} s^2 / 2\) for \(s \leqslant t\) and \(c_{\text{lin}} ts\) for \(s > t\). The atoms are masked by health on their path and plaquette footprints. The leading action \(\mathcal{S}\) is the \(\mathfrak{B}_4\)-average of \(\beta \hspace{0.17em} [\sum_p (\cdot) + \text{cubic} + \text{quartic}] + \text{loop}\). A representation \(\mathcal{R}= (\text{path data, clip, activities}, p, \beta, v)\) evaluates to \(\text{eval}_{\mathcal{R}} = \exp \left( v| \Lambda | -\mathcal{S}- \sum_i \boldsymbol{1}_{\text{healthy}} E_i \right) \cdot \text{gas}\). Theorem: if \(\mathcal{R}\) is lawful (path bounds and valid activities), then \(\text{eval}_{\mathcal{R}}\) is Borel and bounded on that finite torus.

(d) Histories.

  • A calendar holds torus sides \(n_{m, p}\) and factors \(L_m\), with \(n_{m + 1, p} = L_m n_{m, p}\) and \(\text{scale} (m) = \sum_{k < m} \log L_k\), the kernel constants, the barrier \(\bar{\beta} > 0\), stage constants, orders \(J_m \geqslant 4\) and exponents \(\theta\). It sets \(\hat{\beta}_m = \bar{\beta} + \kappa_{\text{slope}} \hspace{0.17em} \text{scale} (m)\), \(t_m = \text{temp} (\hat{\beta}_m)\) and cover size \(\lceil (\log \hat{\beta}_m)^2 \rceil\).

  • Step \(m\) goes from level \(m + 1\) to level \(m\), with patch \((L_m, J_{m + 1})\).

  • The fits conditions include: \(J_m\) nondecreasing with \(J_{m + 1} \leqslant J_m + 1\) and \(J_m \to \infty\); \(\hat{\beta}_m > 1\); \(t_m \leqslant 1\); the level-\(m\) torus side at least \(100 (\text{cover size}_m + 1)\) and the fine side of step \(m\) at least \(100 (R_m + 1)\); drift and logarithmic-cost bounds.

  • Levels means a calendar with its fits conditions and block control at every step.

  • A history assigns a representation \(\mathcal{R}_{N, p, m}\) to each mesh index \(N\), volume \(p\) and level \(m \leqslant N\). 3. Strategy

The step follows the block-spin method: fix a frame around each coarse site, transport across coarse links, average the connectors, and add independent Haar-normalized Wilson noise. Two rules define the frames on all configurations. When route holonomies in axial gauges are small, least-squares synchronization gives a unique frame normalized at the centre. Otherwise an axial frame is chosen uniformly among tags satisfying the curvature bound. The predictor likewise uses a unique nearest point to the weighted matrix mean when the minimum price is small, and a connector sampled with weight \(w\) otherwise. Uniqueness and symmetric sampling make these rules covariant. The tag probabilities sum to \(1\), and the noise has total mass \(1\), so the full kernel is normalized.

The branch cutoffs are independent of the number of patch sites. For frame uniqueness, normalize each minimizer by a nearest point to its centroid. Centroid differences then have zero tangential projection, making the feedback comparison a contraction. Predictor uniqueness follows from the tubular neighbourhood of \(G\). Eligible axial frames exist by induction over coordinate slabs: choose a layer of least curvature, fix its gauge, and compare the other layers by plaquette bounds.

For quantitative control, one axial gauge on the macro-box synchronizes the surrounding patch frames. Each frame defect costs \(O (L^2)\) times the curvature sum; the averaging weight contributes \(L^{- 4}\). Fine transports cost another \(L\) times a link sum of order \(L\), giving the same \(L^{- 2}\) coarse-curvature estimate. Near the trivial configuration, the analytic implicit function theorem gives the frame and predictor derivatives. Tree gauge then reduces integration to free links. These geometric and analytic results enter the later energy estimates and stationary-branch analysis.

5. Where the difficulty lies

The synchronization cutoff must be independent of the patch size. The feedback map alone has Lipschitz constant \(1\); normalizing the centroids removes its tangential average and gives the contraction factor \(d + \tfrac{1}{4} \leqslant \tfrac{1}{2}\).

The curvature estimate must hold on both branches. It combines clustering of arbitrary minimizers, route multiplicities and curvature-controlled axial gauges. Losing a power of \(L\) in any of these comparisons would spoil the area scaling in III.1.8.

Normalization and tree-gauge integration use finite sums and Haar invariance. Global regularity is more limited: the kernel is Borel, while analyticity is proved near the trivial configuration. The energy bound and stationary requirements in III.1.11–III.1.12 need the later units.
