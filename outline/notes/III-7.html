Conventions. Levels \(m = 0, \ldots, N\) run from the coarsest level (\(m = 0\)) to the Wilson lattice (\(m = N\)). Blocking from fine level \(m + 1\) to coarse level \(m\) uses an odd factor \(L_m\). The stage \(J_m\) is the index of the constants used at level \(m\); it satisfies \(J_{m + 1} \in \{J_m, J_m + 1\}\). In one step the fine torus \(\Lambda_f\) has side \(f = Lc\) and the coarse torus \(\Lambda_c\) has side \(c\); in general \(| \Lambda_n | = n^4\). Fields are Lie-algebra link variables \(x\), and configurations are \(U = e^x\). \(E_W (U) = \sum_p \left( 1 - d_{\text{rep}}^{- 1} \text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} \text{tr} \hspace{0.17em} U_p \right)\) is the Wilson energy in the fixed matrix representation of dimension \(d_{\text{rep}}\), \(C_G\) is the adjoint Casimir constant of I.1, and \(\kappa = 11 C_G / (24 \pi^2)\).

1. What this unit proves

At level \(m\) the effective action has the form \(\beta \hspace{0.17em} \text{main}_{\mathbf{c}, R} (U) + \text{loop}_R (U)\), plus polymer activities (III.10). Here \(R\) is a path datum: an exponentially weighted, explicit sum of gauge-covariant curvature monomials along lattice paths. This unit constructs the path data and the coupling shifts.

  • (A) At the finest level there is a path datum whose leading part has the same Taylor jet as \(E_W\) through order four (III.7.2).

  • (B) For one block-spin step, the coarse leading part can be chosen as the classical (tree-level) transform of the fine one through order four. The second-order Taylor coefficient of the one-loop term equals \(\alpha\) times the coarse quadratic form plus coarse two-leg atoms, up to an error exponentially small in the side of the torus. Moreover \(| \alpha + \kappa \log L|\) is bounded independently of \(L\) (III.7.4).

  • (C) The value \(\kappa = 11 C_G / (24 \pi^2)\) is computed, not assumed. The one-loop determinant of the background-field operator in Feynman gauge, regularized in the infrared at scale \(1 / L\), has \(F^2\)-coefficient \(- \kappa \log L + O (1)\) (III.7.3). Two results connect this determinant to the blocked one: an exact Faddeev–Popov identity (III.7.6) and uniform invertibility of the completed fluctuation operators (III.7.5, §4.6).

Iterated over all levels, (A)–(C) give the kinetic data satisfying the conditions required in III.9. The slope \(\kappa\), a defined constant in I.1 and III.9.1, is identified here with the one-loop coefficient of the blocked action.

Proposition III.7.1 (path data, beds, Fourier kernels).

  • Legs and atoms. A leg is a lattice walk \(\gamma\) from the origin of \(\mathbb{Z}^4\) together with a plaquette orientation \(P\). At base point \(x\) it evaluates \(U\) to \(\text{Ad} (U_{\gamma}) \hspace{0.17em} \mathcal{F} (U_{\partial p}) \in \mathfrak{g}\). Here \(p\) is the plaquette of orientation \(P\) at the end of \(\gamma\), \(U_{\gamma}\) is the transport along \(\gamma\), and \(\mathcal{F} (u)\) is the \(\mathfrak{g}\)-component of \((u - u^{- 1}) / 2\). The length of a leg is \(| \gamma | + 4\).

  • A \(k\)-atom is a \(k\)-tuple of legs with a coefficient tensor \(a : \{1, \ldots, \dim \mathfrak{g}\}^k \to \mathbb{R}\). It evaluates to \(\sum_b a (b) \prod_i (\text{leg}_i)_{b_i}\). It is invariant if this polynomial is \(\text{Ad}\)-invariant. Its exponential weight at width \(\sigma\) is \(\|a\|_1 e^{\sigma \cdot \text{length}}\).

  • Path datum \(R\). For each of the six plaquette orientations \(P\), a sequence of rows \((\lambda_{P, k}, \text{leg}_{P, k})\); and sequences of 2-atoms (loop atoms), 3-atoms and 4-atoms. The rows define \(\ell_R (U)_{(x, P)} = \sum_k \lambda_{P, k} \hspace{0.17em} \text{leg}_{P, k} (U, x)\).

  • Main term. For a clip \(\mathbf{c}\) (only \(c_{\text{dir}} > 0\) enters here), whenever the row and atom series converge absolutely:

\(\displaystyle \text{main}_{\mathbf{c}, R} (U) = \frac{1}{384} \sum_s \left[ \sum_p \left( c_{\text{dir}} \left( 1 - d_{\text{rep}}^{- 1} \text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} \text{tr} \hspace{0.17em} U_p \right) + \tfrac{1}{2} | \ell_R (U)_p |^2 \right) + \sum_x \sum_k (\mathcal{A}^{(3)}_k +\mathcal{A}^{(4)}_k) (U, x) \right]_{U \to sU} .\)

This is an average over the 384 signed permutations \(s\) of the axes. \(\text{loop}_R\) is the same average of the sum of all loop atoms. These are the unclipped analytic germs; the small-field clipping of the full action does not enter them.

  • Weight clauses \(R \in \text{Bounds} (\mathbf{c}; \sigma, C, \Lambda)\):

    • \(\sigma > 0\), and \(\sum_k | \lambda_{P, k} | e^{\sigma \hspace{0.17em} \text{len}} \leqslant C\) for each \(P\);

    • the sums of exponential weights are \(\leqslant C\) for the cubic and quartic atoms and \(\leqslant \Lambda\) for the loop atoms;

    • all atoms are invariant;

    • normalization at zero momentum: \(c_{\text{dir}} \delta_{PQ} + \sum_{P'} \hat{\ell}_{P' P} (0) \hat{\ell}_{P' Q} (0) = \delta_{PQ}\);

    • the moment conditions: for each pair of leg orientations and colours, the loop coefficients have total weight \(0\) and first moment \(0\) in the displacement between the two legs (with the required summability).

  • Bed \(B = (D, \tilde{D}, H, \tilde{H}, \rho)\): five translation-invariant kernels on plaquette fields (diagonal, its inverse, metric, its inverse, roots).

    • \(R\) is attached to \(B\) if its rows, summed by end plaquette and displacement, give \(\rho\).

    • \(\text{Floor} (B, \mathbf{c}, n ; \varepsilon, C)\) on \(\Lambda_n\) means: \(D \tilde{D} = 1\); \(H \tilde{H} = 1\); \(\Phi^{\textsf{T}} \Phi = H\) with \(\Phi = (\sqrt{c_{\text{dir}}} \hspace{0.17em} \boldsymbol{1}; \rho)\); \(\hat{H} (0) = 1\); \(H \hspace{0.17em} d_1 = D \hspace{0.17em} d_1\) on link fields; \(H, D\) symmetric with \(\varepsilon I \leqslant H, D \leqslant CI\) as quadratic forms.

    • A kernel is in \(\text{strip} (\sigma, C)\) if \(\sum_y e^{\sigma |y|_1} |K (y) | \leqslant C\) entrywise.

  • Fourier kernels. A \((t + 1)\)-leg kernel assigns a number \(\hat{K}_{b, a} (p_1, \ldots, p_t)\) to colours \(b\), directions \(a\) and momenta, with \(p_0 = - \sum p_j\).

    • \(\hat{K}\) is strip-analytic of width \(\sigma\) and size \(K\) if it is holomorphic, \(2 \pi\)-periodic in each scalar momentum coordinate, and bounded by \(K\) on \(| \text{Im} \hspace{0.17em} p_{j, a} | < \sigma\).

    • A function \(F\) on link fields on \(\Lambda_n\) is recorded by \(\hat{K}\) with error \(\epsilon\) if its complexified Taylor coefficient \(D^{t + 1} F (0) / (t + 1) !\), evaluated on canonical unit plane waves, differs from \(| \Lambda_n | \hat{K}_{b, a} (p)\) when the momenta sum to zero, and from \(0\) otherwise, by at most \(\epsilon\). All tuples of lattice momenta are tested.

    • \(F\) vanishes to order \(s\) along rays if \(F (tv) = O (t^s)\) as \(t \to 0\) for every \(v\), with the constant allowed to depend on \(v\).

  • \(F^2\)-coefficient. For a two-leg kernel, \(\alpha_b (\hat{K})\) is twice the real part of the coefficient of \(s^2\) in \(\hat{K}_{(b, b), (1, 1)} (s \hspace{0.17em} e_0)\). For the quadratic kernel of a bed whose metric has positive strip width and \(\hat{H} (0) = 1\), it equals \(1\), so \(\alpha\) is measured in units of the classical \(F^2\) term.

Hypotheses: sides \(n_i \to \infty\); a clip with \(c_{\text{dir}} \leqslant Z\); a bed \(B\) such that

  • \(\rho \in \text{strip} (\sigma_0, A)\);

  • \(\text{Floor} (B, \mathbf{c}, n ; a, b)\) holds for every side \(n\) and some \(a, b\);

  • the quadratic gauge form of \(B\) is invariant under signed permutations on each \(\Lambda_{n_i}\);

  • \(D = 1\) on each \(\Lambda_{n_i}\).

Conclusion: there is a path datum \(Q\) such that:

  1. the rows of \(Q\) are the entries of \(\rho\) placed on straight lattice routes, \(Q\) is attached to \(B\), and all loop coefficients vanish;

  2. \(Q \in \text{Bounds} (\mathbf{c}; \tau, k, \Lambda)\) for all \(0 < \tau \leqslant \sigma\), all \(k \geqslant \max (C, 6 Ae^{4 \sigma_0})\) and all \(\Lambda \geqslant 0\);

  3. for every \(i\) and every \(j \leqslant 4\), the functions \(x \mapsto \text{main}_{\mathbf{c}, Q} (e^x)\) and \(x \mapsto E_W (e^x)\) on \(\Lambda_{n_i}\) are real-analytic at \(0\) and have equal \(j\)-th derivatives there.

Proposition III.7.3 (the one-loop slope). Let \(G\) be simple. For \(\sigma, \varepsilon > 0\), \(C, Z \geqslant 0\), \(\tau > 0\) and \(A \geqslant 0\) there is \(D \geqslant 0\) with the following property.

Hypotheses:

  • \(L \geqslant 1\) is odd, \(\mu = 1 / L\), and \(n_i \to \infty\);

  • \(\mathbf{c}\) is a clip with \(c_{\text{dir}} \leqslant Z\);

  • \(B\) is a bed with \(H \in \text{strip} (\sigma, C)\), \(\text{Floor} (B, \mathbf{c}, n ; \varepsilon, C)\) for every \(n\), and gauge form invariant under signed permutations on each \(\Lambda_{n_i}\);

  • for any \(\Lambda \in \mathbb{R}\), a datum \(R \in \text{Bounds} (\mathbf{c}; \tau, A, \Lambda)\) is attached to \(B\).

For a link field \(x\) on \(\Lambda_{n_i}\) and \(U = e^x\) put

\(\displaystyle \Pi_{\mu} (x) = \tfrac{1}{2} \log \det (\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{c}, R} (U) + d_U d_U^{\ast} +\mathcal{M}_{\mu} (U)) - \log \det (d_U^{\ast} d_U +\mathfrak{m}_{\mu} (U)) .\)

Here:

  • \(\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{c}, R} (U)\) is the Hessian at \(y = 0\) of \(y \mapsto \text{main}_{\mathbf{c}, R} (e^y U)\);

  • \(d_U\) is the covariant difference \((d_U \phi)_e = \phi (e_-) - \text{Ad} (U_e) \phi (e_+)\) from adjoint site fields to link fields;

  • \(\mathfrak{m}_{\mu} (U) = \mu^2 (\mu^2 (d_U^{\ast} d_U + \mu^2)^{- 1})^9\);

  • \(\mathcal{M}_{\mu}\) applies \(\mathfrak{m}_{\mu}\) separately to the four link components, each regarded as a site field at the initial endpoint.

Conclusion: there are a strip-analytic two-leg kernel \(\hat{Y}\) and numbers \(e_i \to 0\) such that:

  • (a) \(\hat{Y}\) records the second-order Taylor coefficient of \(\Pi_{\mu}\) at \(0\) on \(\Lambda_{n_i}\) with error \(| \Lambda_{n_i} | \hspace{0.17em} e_i\), for every \(i\);

  • (b) \(| \alpha_b (\hat{Y}) + \kappa \log L| \leqslant D\) for every colour \(b\).

\(\Pi_{\mu}\) is the one-loop effective action of the fine leading part in the background \(U\), in Feynman background gauge: \(d_U d_U^{\ast}\) is the gauge-fixing term and \(d_U^{\ast} d_U\) the ghost operator. The infrared regulator of mass \(\mu = 1 / L\) stands in for the block-spin constraint. \(D\) does not depend on \(L\). The error \(e_i\) decays exponentially in \(n_i\), at an \(L\)-dependent rate.

Proposition III.7.4 (one renormalization step of the leading part). Fix a simple \(G\), kernel parameters \(K\) (penalty \(a > 0\)), a stage \(J\), and constants \(\varepsilon, w, \delta > 0\) and \(D, Z, C, Z' \geqslant 0\) for the fine and coarse beds and clips. There are \(A_{\ast} \geqslant 0\) and \(s > 0\) (output amplitude and width) such that, for every input width \(\tau > 0\) and amplitude \(A \geqslant 0\), there are:

  • \(d \geqslant 0\) (the drift) and \(\gamma > 0\) (a rate);

  • a minimal block factor \(u\);

  • functions \(\Theta_0 (L)\) and \(\Lambda_0 (L)\),

with the following property.

Hypotheses.

  • Steps. Coarse sides \(m_i \to \infty\); block-spin steps \(\Lambda_{Lm_i} \to \Lambda_{m_i}\) with one admissible patch of stage \(J\) and block factor \(L \geqslant u\), and kernel \(K\); each coarse side is greater than \(1\), and each fine side is at least \(100\) times the patch extent.

  • Clips and beds.

    • A fine clip \(\mathbf{p}\) with \(p_{\text{dir}} \leqslant Z\) and a coarse clip \(\mathbf{q}\) with \(q_{\text{dir}} \leqslant Z'\).

    • A fine bed \(B\) with \(H, \rho \in \text{strip} (w, D)\), \(\text{Floor} (B, \mathbf{p}, n ; \varepsilon, D)\) for every \(n\), and signed-permutation-invariant gauge form on the fine tori.

    • A coarse bed \(T\) with \(H_T, \rho_T \in \text{strip} (\delta, C)\), \(\text{Floor} (T, \mathbf{q}, n ; \varepsilon', C)\) for every \(n\) and some \(\varepsilon' \in \mathbb{R}\), and signed-permutation-invariant gauge form on the coarse tori.

    • A tower with interpolation kernel \(A_0\), satisfying the quadratic-form interpolation, curl-transport and diagonal-transport identities of III.1 on each step. For every list \(\nu\) of at most two fine-coordinate differences, \(\nabla^{\nu} A_0\) has strip width \(w\) and entrywise weighted sum at most \(D / L^{1 + | \nu |}\).

  • Fine datum. \(R \in \text{Bounds} (\mathbf{p}; \tau, A, \Lambda)\) attached to \(B\), with \(\Lambda \geqslant 1\).

  • Stationary branches. A common \(\eta > 0\) and, for each \(i\), an axial forest \(F_i\) and a branch \(g_i\) (coarse fields \(\to\) free fine variables) such that:

    • for every choice of block-kernel data, the second variation in the free variables at \((0, 0)\) is at least \(\eta \sum_e |v_e |^2\);

    • \(g_i (0) = 0\), \(g_i\) and the fine action are real-analytic at \(0\), and \(g_i\) is stationary for every choice of block-kernel data;

    • on every free edge, \(Dg_i (0) v\) is the free interpolant in the chosen axial gauge minus the derivative of the copied coarse configuration;

    • the second jet of the tree-level coarse action is the quadratic form of \(T\).

  • Chart. A local Haar chart ratio \(\textsf{J}\).

Conclusion. There are a coarse path datum \(Y\) and \(\alpha \in \mathbb{R}\) such that:

  1. the rows of \(Y\) are the straight-route rows of \(T\), and \(Y\) is attached to \(T\);

  2. \(Y \in \text{Bounds} (\mathbf{q}; \eta', A', \Lambda')\) whenever \(0 < \eta' \leqslant s\), \(A' \geqslant A_{\ast}\) and \(\Lambda' \geqslant \max (\Lambda_0 (L), \Lambda / 2)\);

  3. \(| \alpha + \kappa \log L| + 1 \leqslant d\);

  4. classical match: for every \(i\), every choice \(t\) of block-kernel data and every \(j \leqslant 4\), the \(j\)-th jets at \(0\) of

\(\displaystyle x \mapsto S_t (x, g_i (x)) \quad \text{and} \quad x \mapsto \text{main}_{\mathbf{q}, Y} (e^x)\)

coincide. Here \(S_t (x, z) = \text{main}_{\mathbf{p}, R} (U_{x, z}) + a \sum_e \left( 1 - d_{\text{rep}}^{- 1} \text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} \text{tr} (V_e \hspace{0.17em} \Pi_t (U_{x, z})_e^{- 1}) \right)\) is the fine leading part plus the block-spin penalty. \(U_{x, z}\) is the fine configuration lifted from \(V = e^x\) through the forest with free variables \(z\), and \(\Pi_t\) is the block predictor;

  1. finite bound: for every \(i\), every \(\Theta \geqslant \Theta_0 (L)\) and every \(c' \leqslant \gamma\), consider the one-loop function

\(\displaystyle \text{th}_{i, t} (x) = \text{loop}_R (U_{x, g_i (x)}) + \tfrac{1}{2} \log \det \text{Hess}_z S_t (x, g_i (x)) - \log \prod_e \textsf{J} (g_i (x)_e) .\)

It is analytic at \(0\), the Hessian determinant is positive near \(0\), and for all coarse lattice momenta \(p, p'\) and real plane waves \(w_{p, \xi} (x) = \text{Re} (e^{ip \cdot x} \xi)\) and \(w_{p', \zeta}\), with every direction-colour component of \(\xi, \zeta\) of modulus at most \(1\),

\(\displaystyle | D^2 \text{th}_{i, t} (0) [w_{p, \xi}, w_{p', \zeta}] - D^2 \left( \alpha \hspace{0.17em} \text{quad}_{\mathbf{q}, Y} + \text{loop}_Y \right) (0) [w_{p, \xi}, w_{p', \zeta}] | \leqslant | \Lambda_{m_i} | \hspace{0.17em} \Theta \hspace{0.17em} e^{- c' m_i} .\)

Here \(\text{quad}_{\mathbf{q}, Y}\) is the signed-permutation average of the Wilson-plaquette and row-square terms in \(\text{main}_{\mathbf{q}, Y}\), with the cubic and quartic atoms omitted. Its Hessian is the coarse quadratic form. The comparison function is also analytic at \(0\). The estimate holds for every \(t\).

On the constants. \(A_{\ast}\) and \(s\) are fixed before \((\tau, A)\). The drift is \(d = d_{\text{cmp}} + D_{\text{slope}} + 1\), where \(d_{\text{cmp}}\) is the comparison constant of §4.8 and \(D_{\text{slope}}\) is the constant of III.7.3. The construction gives \(\Lambda_0 (L) = a_0 L^4 + b_0 \log L + c_0\) and \(\Theta_0 (L) = b_1 L^4\), with nonnegative constants depending on the fixed input parameters; both are \(O (L^4)\).

Proposition III.7.5 (uniform inverse of the completed free operator). Let \(j\) be a stage, \(K\) kernel parameters, \(\sigma, \varepsilon > 0\) and \(\Omega \geqslant 0\). There is \(\eta > 0\), and for every block factor \(L\) a constant \(A_L \geqslant 0\), with the following property.

For every admissible patch of stage \(j\) and block factor \(L\), and every bed \(B\) with \(H \in \text{strip} (\sigma / L, \Omega)\) and \(\text{Floor} (B, \mathbf{c}, n ; \varepsilon, D)\) for all \(n\):

  • the inverse \(\mathcal{G}_B\) of the block-Bloch symbol of the completed free operator lies in \(\text{strip} (\eta, A_L)\) on the coarse lattice;

  • for every step with this patch (any coarse side \(c\), fine side \(cL\), any choice of block-kernel data), \(\mathcal{C}_B \hspace{0.17em} \mathcal{G}_B = 1\).

In the rooted coordinates, the joint variables are a fine link field \(v\) and a coarse site field \(h\). Before gauge completion, the quadratic form is

\(\displaystyle \langle dv, H \hspace{0.17em} dv \rangle + a \|- Qv + d_c h\|^2,\)

where \(Q\) is the linear block average and \(d_c\) the coarse gradient. The completed operator adds \(F_{1 / L}^{\textsf{T}} F_{1 / L}\), where \(F_{\mu} = d_{\hspace{-0.17em} 1}^{\ast} P_f +\mathfrak{m}_{\mu} X\), \(P_f\) projects onto fine links, and \(X\) is minus the derivative of the logarithm of the fine rooted frame. Here \(d_{\hspace{-0.17em} 1}\) is \(d_U\) at \(U = 1\). All matrices use physical counting bases. The decay rate \(\eta\) is uniform in \(L\); only the amplitude depends on \(L\).

Proposition III.7.6 (Faddeev–Popov identity for the one-loop term). Fix an admissible step, a clip \(\mathbf{p}\) and a datum \(R \in \text{Bounds} (\mathbf{p}; \sigma, A, \Lambda)\) for some \(\sigma > 0\) and \(A, \Lambda \in \mathbb{R}\). Let \(g (0) = 0\) be a real-analytic stationary branch for an axial forest, stationary for every choice of block-kernel data, with second variation \(\geqslant \eta \sum_e |v_e |^2\) (\(\eta > 0\)). Let \(\mu > 0\), let \(t\) be a choice of block-kernel data, and let \(\textsf{J}\) be a local Haar chart ratio. Then for coarse fields \(x\) near \(0\):

  • the completed Hessian \(\mathcal{P}_{\mu} (x)\) has positive determinant;

  • \(\text{th} (x) = \text{loop}_R (U_{x, g (x)}) + \tfrac{1}{2} \log \det \mathcal{P}_{\mu} (x) - \log \det \mathcal{G}_{\mu} (x)\).

\(\mathcal{P}_{\mu} (x)\) acts on all fine-link and coarse-frame directions. It is the Hessian in left exponential coordinates of the joint action

\(\displaystyle \mathscr{S}_t (V ; U, h) = \text{main}_{\mathbf{p}, R} (U) + a \sum_e \left( 1 - d_{\text{rep}}^{- 1} \text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} \text{tr} (V_e [\text{gauge}_h \Pi_t (U)]_e^{- 1}) \right),\)

3. Strategy

Classical part. At every level the quadratic part of the leading action is fixed by the free-field bed of that level. The cubic and quartic parts are curvature atoms, determined by Taylor agreement through order four with a given gauge-invariant function \(E\). At the top, \(E\) is the Wilson energy. At each step, \(E\) is the tree-level coarse action \(x \mapsto S_t (x, g (x))\). The atom-emission lemma (§4.3, Lemma 4.4) serves both cases. Its mechanism has four parts:

  1. The Taylor coefficients of a translation sum of local analytic functionals have Fourier kernels on \(\mathbb{Z}^4\) that are strip-analytic. Finite-volume coefficients agree with \(| \Lambda_n |\) times these kernels up to \(o (| \Lambda_n |)\) along any sequence of sides; this is how volume-uniformity enters.

  2. Gauge invariance makes the kernels transversal in each leg (linearized Ward identity).

  3. Transversal kernels with at least three legs factor through the curvature. This is the lattice Poincaré lemma, proved by contracting the Koszul complex of the differences \(e^{ip_j} - 1\), one axis at a time, with Hadamard division. A one-leg solve reduces the width from \(\sigma\) to \(\sigma / 4\) once, regardless of the four coordinate divisions within it. Successive leg solves cause further width losses, depending only on the fixed number of legs.

  4. The real, colour-invariant Laurent coefficients of the factored kernels are the coefficients of atoms, and exponential decay is the strip bound.

One-loop part.

  1. In the axial chart the one-loop term is \(\text{loop} + \tfrac{1}{2} \log \det - \log (\text{Haar Jacobian})\). An exact Faddeev–Popov identity turns it into \(\tfrac{1}{2} \log \det\) of a covariantly completed Hessian minus \(\log \det\) of a ghost operator.

  2. Its second-order Taylor coefficient is a two-leg kernel. Its \(F^2\)-coefficient \(\alpha\) is compared, with an \(L\)-independent error, to that of the regulated Feynman-gauge background-field determinant on the fine lattice.

  3. The latter is computed. The lattice integrand is the continuum bubble integrand \(h (k)\) of the vector loop (weight \(\tfrac{1}{2}\)) and the ghost loop (weight \(- 1\)), up to errors \(O (|k|^{- 3} + \mu^2 |k|^{- 6})\); its angular average is \(- \tfrac{11}{3} C_G |k|^{- 4}\). Integration over the shell \(1 / L < |k| \leqslant 1\) gives \(- \kappa \log L\), and the rest is bounded by absolute values.

  4. After subtracting \(\alpha\) times the bed's quadratic kernel, the remaining kernel vanishes to fourth order at zero momentum. It is therefore represented by loop atoms satisfying the two moment conditions, i.e. by irrelevant terms. Inherited fine loop atoms contribute with a factor \(L^{- 2}\).

Constants. The output width and cubic/quartic amplitude of a step are fixed before the input amplitude. The input enters the output only with factors \(L^{- 1}\) or \(L^{- 2}\), which a large \(L\) makes small. The strip widths and inverse bounds for the completed operators are uniform in \(L\) in block-scaled norms. The newly generated loop-atom amplitude is \(O (L^4)\). It is absorbed by \(\text{loopCap}_m\), which grows with \(\hat{\beta}_m\).

Relation to Balaban. The construction uses background-field renormalization with block-spin constraints. Here:

  • the effective action is carried by explicit, exponentially weighted path data;

  • the \(F^2\)-coefficient is obtained by comparison with a regulated covariant determinant plus a Fourier computation, with \(O (1)\) error, for an arbitrary simple compact gauge group.

4.2 The lattice Poincaré lemma in momentum space

Lemma 4.2 (one-leg solve). Let the momentum space have \(d\) scalar coordinates, including four selected coordinates, and let \(\delta\) contract differential forms with \(q_j (p) = e^{ip_j} - 1\) in those four coordinates. Let \(\sigma > 0\), \(A \geqslant 0\), and let \(f\) be a form of degree \(r \geqslant 0\) with the following properties:

  • each component is strip-analytic of width \(\sigma\) and size \(A\);

  • \(\delta f = 0\);

  • \(f\) vanishes to order \(s\) along rays;

  • on real momenta, \(f\) takes values in subspaces unchanged when any of the four selected momentum coordinates varies;

  • if \(r = 0\), \(f\) vanishes where the projected momentum is \(0\).

Then \(f = \delta B\), where \(B\) has degree \(r + 1\), is strip-analytic of width \(\sigma / 4\) and size \(4 A \hspace{0.17em} \pi_d (\sigma)\) with \(\pi_d (\sigma) = (4 / \sigma) (2 (1 + \sigma / 4) / (\sigma / 4))^d\), vanishes to order \(\max (s - 1, 0)\), and takes values in the same subspaces on real momenta. Sketch: Comb one axis at a time. \(f (p) - f (p) |_{p_j = 0} = q_j (p) D_j (p)\) by Hadamard division (div_var). \(D_j\) has width \(\sigma / 4\) and size \(A \pi_d (\sigma)\) by Cauchy estimates, while the restriction \(f|_{p_j = 0}\) keeps width \(\sigma\). After all four axes, no positive-degree component remains. In degree zero the remainder is \(f\) with all selected momenta set to zero, which vanishes by hypothesis. The factor \(4\) counts the axes; the width loss is a single one. For positive vanishing order, division by \(q_j \sim ip_j\) lowers it by one; order zero remains order zero. Lean: wall_lift.

5. Where the difficulty lies

  • The coefficient and its identification. The continuum bubble yields the factor \(11 / 3\) from the orbital and spin vertices and \(\text{tr} (\text{ad}_b^2) = - C_G\). Relating this calculation to the blocked determinant also requires the exact Faddeev–Popov identity, cancellation of the contact contribution to \(\alpha\), a comparison uniform in \(L\), and control of every Fourier alias.

  • Quantitative curvature factorization. The lattice Poincaré lemma must preserve exponential decay, colour invariance and the constraints already imposed on other legs. Each one-leg solve loses a factor four in strip width; the number of legs is fixed.

  • Uniformity in the volume. A single infinite-volume kernel or path datum works on every torus in the chosen sequence. Limits along increasingly fine momentum meshes transfer Ward identities and colour symmetries to that kernel. In the iteration, the sequence consists of the sides at one fixed level as the volume index varies.

  • Order of constants. The output width and cubic/quartic amplitude precede the input path amplitude. Contributions depending on the latter carry a factor \(L^{- 1}\); inherited loop atoms carry \(L^{- 2}\). The new loop amplitude is \(O (L^4)\), and the reference-coupling thresholds make the allowed loop amplitude large enough to absorb it.

  • Strength of the conclusion. The slope estimate has an \(O (1)\) error uniform in \(L\). This suffices for III.9 when \(\log L\) dominates that error. Polymer activities are absent from the argument.

  • The regulator. The scalar regulator is \(\mu^{20} (\Delta_U + \mu^2)^{- 9}\). Its componentwise extension to link fields respects gauge transformations and unsigned axis permutations; full signed-permutation symmetry is not asserted for this auxiliary determinant. The blocked action has the full lattice symmetry.

7. What remains unexplained

  1. Analytic estimates below the stated lemmas. The real quotient estimate and complex-strip perturbation estimate used in Lemma 4.10, and the analytic tadpole and bubble bounds used in Lemma 4.15, have not been checked through every subsidiary proof.   2. Choice of regulator power. The ninth power is part of the explicit definition, and the cited estimates use it. A minimal power, or a conceptual reason for choosing exactly nine, has not been established.

  3. Supply of the global hypotheses. III.4 supplies the free-field horizons and history conditions. The free/stationary connection explicitly constructs the two horizons with identical beds and interpolation kernels before applying the iteration. The discharge of these conditions in every downstream application has not been checked.

  4. Limits of path data. The projection results support the compactness argument in III.13: exponential weights give Hilbert-space parameters for curvature coefficients (fitting); coefficientwise convergence with uniform row bounds gives convergence of row-action jets (insideLine); compatible source jets and a common positive Hessian lower bound give continuity of stationary, tree-level and one-loop jets (interview). The complete compactness construction and passage through all its quantifiers belong to III.13 and remain outside this unit.
