Unit page · All units · Overview

Part: III, Ultraviolet construction (renormalization group). Lean: chapter 30, YM/FullVolume/. Size: 18,973 lines in the main-theorem closure.

Scope. This unit proves volume-uniform bounds and takes the ultraviolet limit at each fixed volume. It has three outputs:

  • (a) Bounds on lattice correlation functions of composite observables and of their finite differences at separated points. The bounds are uniform in the volume and eventually uniform in the cutoff.

  • (b) Convergence of these correlation functions as \(N \to \infty\) at each fixed volume, with smooth limits.

  • (c) Power-series bounds on their dependence on the renormalized coupling. The assembly uses these.

Notation.

  • \(N\) is the mesh index. The lattice spacing is \(a_N = 1 / D_N\), where \(D_N = \ell_0 \cdots \ell_{N - 1}\) is a product of odd block sizes.

  • \(p\) is the volume index. \(\Lambda_{N, p}\) is the torus of physical side \(L_p\), with \(L_p D_N\) sites per axis. The block size \(\ell_j\) and the physical period \(L_p\) are different quantities.

  • \(m, j, k\) are renormalization-group levels. \(\beta_{N, p}\) is the bare inverse coupling, \(\mathbb{E}_{N, p}\) the Wilson expectation, and \(\hat{\beta}_j\) the renormalized inverse coupling at level \(j\).

  • \(\text{Lab}_d\) is the finite-dimensional space of labels of degree \(d\). \(O_{N, p} (v ; o)\) is the lattice representative of \(v \in \text{Lab}_d\) at the site \(o\) (II.1).

  • \(\mathcal{N}_d \subset \text{Lab}_d\) is the equation ideal of the free Maxwell field, and \(F_d = \text{Lab}_d /\mathcal{N}_d\).

  • Eventually in the mesh means: there is \(N_0\) such that the statement holds for all \(N \geqslant N_0\) and all \(p\) (the filter mesh). In statements of the form “for all large \(m\), eventually in the mesh,” \(N_0\) may depend on \(m\).

  • A sequence \(w_{N, p}\) in a normed space has sub-power growth if for every \(\varepsilon > 0\), eventually in the mesh, \(\|w_{N, p} \| \leqslant a_N^{- \varepsilon}\) (Sluggish). The quantity flight \((\varepsilon)\) equals \(a_N^{- \varepsilon}\), because scale \((N) = \log a_N^{- 1}\).

1. What this unit proves

Definitions.

  • A trial \(F\) is a family of complex functions \(F_{N, p} (o)\) of the gauge field on \(\Lambda_{N, p}\), indexed by a site \(o\). It is local if, eventually in the mesh, it is translation covariant and each \(F_{N, p} (o)\) is gauge invariant, measurable, bounded and depending only on links within \(o + X\), for one fixed finite \(X \subset \mathbb{Z}^4\) (Local, Stencil).

  • Chessboard root at scale \(m\). For \(m \leqslant N\), \(T_m (o)\) is the tiling of \(\Lambda_{N, p}\) by cubes of physical side \(a_m\) with one cell centred at \(o\) (center). Let \(G\) be bounded, measurable and gauge invariant, localized in the central cell. Then \(R_m (G) = | \mathbb{E}_{N, p} \prod_c G^{(c)} |^{1 / \sharp \text{cells}}\). Here \(G^{(c)}\) is obtained by reflecting \(G\) into the cell \(c\) through the cell walls, and is complex conjugated after an odd number of reflections. The number of cells is \(\sharp \text{cells} = (L_p / a_m)^4\) (rootTest).

  • \(F\) is affordable at cost \(u (m, N, p)\) if \(F\) is local and the following holds for all large \(m\), eventually in the mesh: \(F_{N, p} (0)\) is localized in the central cell of \(T_m (0)\) and \(R_m (F_{N, p} (0)) \leqslant u (m, N, p)\).

    • \(F\) is owed at cost \(v (N, p)\) if it is affordable at a cost independent of \(m\).

    • \(F\) is funded if it is affordable at a cost \(u (m)\) independent of \((N, p)\).

    Lean: Affords, Owed, Funded.

  • Normalized differences: \((\Delta_{\mu} F)_{N, p} (o) = a_N^{- 1} (F_{N, p} (o + e_{\mu}) - F_{N, p} (o))\), and \(\Delta_l = \Delta_{\mu_1} \cdots \Delta_{\mu_k}\) for a list \(l = (\mu_1, \ldots, \mu_k)\) of axes (farther, hops).

  • A coin of degree \(d\) is a sequence \(x = (x_{N, p})\) in \(\text{Lab}_d\) whose inverse terminal coordinates have sub-power growth (Coin). Thus, if \(\tau_{d ; N, p}\) is the terminal coordinate isomorphism, \(\| \tau_{d ; N, p}^{- 1} x_{N, p} \| \leqslant a_N^{- \varepsilon}\) eventually for every \(\varepsilon > 0\). Under the consistency conditions on the family, bounded label sequences are coins. \(O (x)\) denotes the trial \(O_{N, p} (x_{N, p} ; \cdot)\).

  • A presentation is, for each \(d\) and each \((N, p)\), an idempotent linear map \(\pi_{N, p}\) on \(\text{Lab}_d\) (Presentation, IV.1). The kernel condition is an additional hypothesis.

  • For a real configuration \(x\), write \(\alpha_N (x)_{i \mu} = \lceil x_{i \mu} / a_N \rceil\) for its coordinatewise ceiling address (address). This is the right endpoint convention. Interpolation means multilinear interpolation on the corresponding cubes (interpolated).

Proposition III.12.1 (kernel bounds uniform in the volume). Let \(R\) be a run (§2) for a simple gauge group \(G\), with Wilson family \(C\). Fix \(n\), labels \(v_i \in \text{Lab}_{d_i}\) (\(1 \leqslant i \leqslant n\)), a finite list \(\lambda\) of pairs (slot \(i\), axis \(\mu\)), and \(r > 0\). Then there are \(B \geqslant 0\) and \(N_0\) with the following property. For all \(N \geqslant N_0\), \(p\) and integer configurations \(x\) satisfying \(a_N \hspace{0.17em} \text{dist} (x_i, x_j) \geqslant r\) for \(i \ne j\),

\(\displaystyle | \Delta_{\lambda} \hspace{0.17em} \mathbb{E}_{N, p} [O_{N, p} (v_1 ; x_1) \cdots O_{N, p} (v_n ; x_n)] | \leqslant B.\)

Here \(\Delta_{(i, \mu)}\) is the normalized forward difference in \(x_i\) along \(e_{\mu}\), and \(\text{dist}\) is the periodic \(\ell^{\infty}\) lattice distance. \(N_0\) and \(B\) may depend on \(R, n, (v_i), \lambda, r\), but not on \(p\). Status: theorem, for every run; the run supplies all inputs (§2). Lean: FixedBounds (statement), gKernel (the differenced kernel), genuine (theorem), funding (reduction to III.12.2).

Proposition III.12.2 (derivative closure of the chessboard budget). Let \(R\) be a run for a simple \(G\). Let \(w = (w_{N, p})\) be a sequence in \(\text{Lab}_d\) with \(\|w_{N, p} \| \leqslant K\) eventually in the mesh, for some \(K \geqslant 0\), and let \(l\) be a list of axes. Then \(\Delta_l O (w)\) is funded: for some \(u (m)\) and all large \(m\), eventually in the mesh it is localized in the central cell and \(R_m (\Delta_l O (w)_{N, p} (0)) \leqslant u (m)\). Status: theorem. Lean: Run.financed.

Proposition III.12.3 (closure lemma). Fix a degree \(d\). Let \(C\) be a Wilson family satisfying its consistency conditions and the following hypotheses:

  1. the centred budget (§2);

  2. the scale-selection data of §2;

  3. sound section data \(V_e\) and linear automorphisms \(W_e\) of the abstract labels, for every degree \(e\), satisfying the section, symmetry and exact local identities described in §2;

  4. a presentation \(\pi\) and the full section-comparison data of §2, including the quantitative comparison with \(V_e\), identification of \(\ker \pi_{N, p}\) with a dressed equation ideal, and a bound \(\sup_{N, p} \| \pi_{N, p} \| < \infty\) for each degree;

  5. degree-\(d\) detector rows satisfying the placement and bounded-lift conditions of Lemma 4.6 and the short-distance comparison of Lemma 4.8 (VitalRank).

Let \(F\) be a funded trial whose values \(F_{N, p} (o)\) are continuous functions of the gauge field, and let \(l\) be a list of axes. Let \(x\) be a coin of degree \(d\) such that \(O (x) - \Delta_l F\) is owed at a cost \(u_{N, p} \to 0\) in the mesh. Then \(\Delta_l F\) is funded. Status: theorem under the stated hypotheses. For a run, these follow from its ties, with the detector comparison obtained from its short-distance estimates (§2). Lean: improved, cap.

Proposition III.12.4 (uniform rank detection). Assume hypotheses 1–5 of III.12.3 and fix a list \(l\) of axes. For all large \(k\) and all sufficiently small \(b > 0\) there is \(Z \geqslant 0\) such that, eventually in the mesh,

\(\displaystyle \| \pi_{N, p} x\| \leqslant Z \hspace{0.17em} | \mathcal{P}_{k, b, l ; N, p} (\pi_{N, p} x) | \qquad \text{for all } x \in \text{Lab}_d .\)

Here \(\mathcal{P}_{k, b, l ; N, p} : \text{Lab}_d \to \mathbb{C}^J\) is the linear detector of Lemma 4.6: correlations with fixed partners, averaged successively in the directions of \(l\) over lengths between \(b\) and \(2 b\). Repeated axes give repeated averages. Status: theorem (conditional on 1–5). Lean: trained, probe.

Proposition III.12.5 (labels in the equation ideal give negligible observables). Assume the consistency conditions on \(C\) and hypotheses 2–4 of III.12.3. Let \(x\) be a coin of degree \(d\) with \(\pi_{N, p} x_{N, p} = 0\) for all \((N, p)\). Then \(O (x)\) is owed at cost \(a_N^{1 / 32}\): it is local, and for all large \(m\), eventually in the mesh, \(R_m (O (x)_{N, p} (0)) \leqslant a_N^{1 / 32}\). Status: theorem. 3. Strategy

The centred budget applies to primaries with bounded physical labels. A normalized difference carries the divergent factor \(a_N^{- 1}\), so it needs a separate argument. The local regularity estimates and the matching theorem of III.11 write \(\Delta_l O (w)\) as a degree-\((d + |l|)\) primary \(O (x)\) with an error of root cost \(a_N^{1 / 16}\). The inverse terminal coordinates of \(x\) have only sub-power control. To bound \(\pi x\), test against fixed partner observables and average in the differenced directions. Summation by parts bounds these tests by \((2 / b)^{|l|}\) times an undifferenced correlation bound. At short distances the detector approaches a free Maxwell matrix that separates the quotient by the equation ideal. Finite-dimensional stability then bounds \(\pi x\). The auxiliary equation sections have zero chessboard root by their distant-spectator identity; section comparison makes \(O (x - \pi x)\) negligible. Thus \(\Delta_l O (w)\) is funded, and the chessboard inequality bounds products at separated points (III.12.1–III.12.5).

For convergence, fix the volume and an intermediate level \(j\). Fixed-level convergence of the couplings, normalized activities and effective insertions makes the scalar-normalized level-\(j\) correlation Cauchy as the cutoff grows. The full correlation differs from it by at most \(2 F_0 e^{- B_j / 64}\) for all sufficiently large cutoffs, where \(F_0\) is independent of the cutoff and \(j\). Since \(B_j = \lceil (\log \hat{\beta}_j)^2 \rceil \to \infty\), the full correlations are Cauchy at configurations lying on a common lattice. Positional Lipschitz bounds extend convergence from this dense set to every non-collision configuration. Taylor bounds identify limits of differences with derivatives, giving smoothness and the smeared limits (III.12.7–III.12.8).

For dependence on the coupling, vary the level-0 value in a family parametrized by \(\bar{\beta} + ws\). After reducing the parameter interval, the lattice correlations have absolutely convergent power series whose coefficient norms grow polynomially in \(a_j^{- 1}\), uniformly in the cutoff (III.12.6). Their real level-to-level differences are \(O (a_j)\). A polynomial coefficient estimate after a further interval reduction makes the differences summable in coefficient norm. Fixed-level Cauchy convergence of the series then gives analytic continuum correlations on a complex disc. This uses the same fixed-level convergence input as the preceding paragraph, together with the additional analytic estimates.

4. Steps

4.1 Calculus of the chessboard budget

Throughout §§4.1–4.3, the family satisfies its consistency conditions. In §4.2, assume hypotheses 1–5 of III.12.3, with the same detector rows and presentation throughout.

Lemma 4.1 (closure properties). For trials \(F, H\) and costs \(u, v\):

  • \(\text{Aff} (F, u)\) and \(\text{Aff} (H, v)\) imply \(\text{Aff} (F \pm H, u + v)\).

  • \(\text{Aff} (F, u)\) implies \(\text{Aff} (cF, |c|u)\) for any \(c = c_{N, p} \in \mathbb{C}\).

  • \(\text{Aff} (0, 0)\), and finite sums behave the same way.

  • Affordability is monotone in the cost.

  • It is unchanged if \(F\) is modified off a tail of the mesh.

Hence funded trials form a vector space. Sketch: linear combinations preserve locality; the root is homogeneous and satisfies the chessboard triangle inequality. Lean: Affords.sub, Affords.scalar, Affords.enlarged, Affords.recurses, Funded.sub; negation, addition, the zero trial and finite sums are treated in the same file.

5. Where the difficulty lies

Matching controls only inverse terminal coordinates. Rank detection must bound the projected physical labels, while the equation-section comparison controls the projection kernel. These estimates precede every limit.

The estimates of III.12.1–III.12.5 are uniform in every admitted volume. The convergence results III.12.7–III.12.8 fix the volume first. Their thresholds may depend on the period, labels, compact spatial set, derivative order and test function. The parameter width in Lemma 4.18 may also depend on these data.

All positional limits are off collisions on the torus, including configurations with coordinate ties and configurations crossing the boundary of a period cell. Extension across collisions belongs to VII.4. Every derivative order or stencil is fixed before the cutoff threshold is chosen.

Sub-power factors are absorbed by the positive error exponents. Fixed-level convergence uses a positive lower bound on the scalar-normalized partition function; the additive action scalar need not converge.

7. What remains unexplained

  1. Local matching and equation sections. Lemma 4.14 imports the local-to-primary estimate of III.11; only its reduction to a coin and an \(a_N^{1 / 16}\) root bound is checked here. The construction of the auxiliary sections from the Maxwell equation ideal, and its relation to a lattice Schwinger–Dyson identity, remain unverified. Lemma 4.10 uses their exact distant-spectator identity, without identifying it with the Wilson action's naive equation of motion.

  2. One-step observable transfer. The capacity estimate used by Lemma 4.16 remains at statement level. Its coefficient and remainder constants require the one-step analysis.

  3. Analytic estimates. The intermediate weighted estimates in Lemma 4.17 and the identification of spatial derivatives of the analytic limit in Lemma 4.18 remain at statement level. The outer series-convergence argument is checked.

  4. Additional estimates in §4.4. The large-field and primary-step results and the two transfer comparisons retain their full hypotheses in III.8–III.10. Their proofs, and the detailed small-shift lawfulness estimate, are not checked here.

  5. Fixed-level convergence and tails. The analytic trajectory-convergence theorem and the multiscale remainder and omission estimates remain imported inputs; their applications are checked. The tail argument needs only \(B_j = \lceil (\log \hat{\beta}_j)^2 \rceil \to \infty\), not geometric decay in \(j\).
