The unit selects the construction run and proves a quantitative limit theorem for its Gaussian quadratic part.

  • The endpoint. For a compact matrix gauge group \(G\) with simple nonabelian Lie algebra and a penalty \(b > 0\), it selects a run: the Wilson family of the main theorem with its scale and extraction data. The run satisfies the funded condition of III.15 and carries the native component recipe.

  • The Gaussian limit. At each fixed stage, the order-zero quadratic kernel converges as the ultraviolet cutoff is removed, with a power rate in the ratio of lattice spacings (Proposition III.14.3). This is one of three components of the recipe.

Use in the main theorem. VIII.1 (continuum_with_position_tests) uses the run, its penalty, the pillars and the funded property. It discards the attached-recipe conjunct.

The selection theorem of III.2 accepts any nonempty family of demands. Here the demand is a recipe, with lower bounds on the block factors and numerical requests on the running coupling. The Gaussian limit is therefore an additional property of the selected run; VIII.1 does not invoke it separately. Replacing this selection by one with fewer demands is a separate question (§7).

1. What this unit proves

Conventions. We use the level systems and sufficiency condition of III.1. Throughout, \(G\) is a compact matrix gauge group; “simple” means that its real Lie algebra is simple and nonabelian.

  • Level system. A level system \(\mathcal{L}\) fixes stages \(i \in \mathbb{N}\) (finer as \(i\) grows), odd block factors \(L_i \geqslant 3\) for the step \(i + 1 \to i\), and spacings \(a_0 = 1\), \(a_{i + 1} = a_i / L_i\), with log-scales \(s_i = \log (1 / a_i)\). Its smoothing indices satisfy \(J_i \leqslant J_{i + 1} \leqslant J_i + 1\). Write \(\ell_j\) for the block factor assigned to smoothing index \(j\), so \(L_i = \ell_{J_{i + 1}}\). The level system also fixes the block-spin kernel with penalty \(b > 0\) and the reference inverse coupling \(\hat{\beta}_i = \bar{\beta} + s_G s_i > 1\), where \(s_G = 11 C_G / (24 \pi^2)\) (hatBeta, slopeConstant). The remaining compatibility conditions are those of III.1.

  • Cutoff. "Cutoff \(N\)" means the lattice of spacing \(a_N\), renormalized through stages \(N, N - 1, \ldots\).

  • Horizon. A horizon \(H\) assigns plaquette-metric and transfer data to each cutoff and stage. Write \(M^{\infty}_{N, i}\) for its translation-invariant metric kernel on \(\mathbb{Z}^4\), \(\hat{M}_{N, i} (\xi)\) for its Fourier symbol, and \(M^{\mathbb{T}}_{N, i}\) for its periodization. On a torus \(\mathbb{T}\), the Gaussian quadratic form on 1-forms has matrix \(\mathcal{K}^{\mathbb{T}}_{N, i} = d^{\top} M^{\mathbb{T}}_{N, i} d\).

  • Price map and sufficiency. A price map \(P = (\alpha_j, \Omega_j, \eta_j)_j\) specifies positive strip widths \(\alpha_j\), upper bounds \(\Omega_j\) and positive coercivity bounds \(\eta_j\). The full sufficiency condition of III.1 includes strip and derivative bounds for all metric and transfer data, inverse and factorization identities, symmetry and coercivity on every torus, the background-field and fluctuation identities between consecutive stages, and the bare terminal condition. In particular, a sufficient horizon satisfies \(\hat{M}_{N, i} (0) = 1\) and \(\mathcal{K}^{\mathbb{T}}_{N, N} = d^{\top} d\).

  • Position-space kernel. \(\mathcal{O}^H_{N, i} = d^{\top} M^{\infty}_{N, i} d\) is the quadratic kernel on the links of \(\mathbb{Z}^4\) (output). This is the Hessian convention: no factor \(1 / 2\) is included in \(\mathcal{O}\).

  • Kernel norms. Let \(x (u)\) be the base point of a link or plaquette \(u\). For link kernels, put \(\lVert F \rVert_{\sigma} = \sup_u \sup_{\phi} \sum_{y \in \mathbb{Z}^4} |F (u, (y, \phi)) | e^{\sigma |x (u) - y|_1}\). For plaquette kernels, put \(\lVert B \rVert^{\text{pl}}_{\sigma} = \sup_u \sum_{v \in \mathbb{Z}^4 \times \mathcal{F}} |B (u, v) | e^{\sigma |x (u) - x (v) |_1}\), where \(\mathcal{F}\) is the set of plaquette orientations. The latter sum includes all target orientations.

  • Age. \(\Lambda_{k, i} = 5 + \log \hat{\beta}_k + |s_i - s_k |\) is the age of stage \(i\) seen from a reporting scale \(k\).

Proposition III.14.1 (the construction run). Let \(G\) be simple and \(b > 0\). There exist a run \(R\), a campaign \(T\) for \(R\) and pillars \(W\) for \((R, T)\) such that:

  1. the penalty of the block-spin kernel of \(R\) equals \(b\);

  2. \(R\) satisfies the full funded condition of III.15 relative to the local-estimate package \(\Delta_{10}\). Its thirteen clauses (§6) include the prescribed local estimates, \(Q (i) \to \infty\), support exponent \(100\) at every stage, and the extraction, OPE and thermal conditions. The selection uses \(Q (i) = i + 2\);

  3. the native component recipe is attached to \(R\). There are a sheet \(s\), a price map \(q\), a recipe \(C\) for \(s\) (Proposition III.14.2) and floors \(n \geqslant \max (n_s, E_C)\), where \(n_s\) is the sheet's floor and \(E_C\) the combined recipe floor of Lemma 4.14, such that:

    • \(R\) is built on \(s\) with floors raised to \(n\), with the primary price map \(P_{s, q}\) of III.12;

    • both countable request families, before and after the block factors are chosen, satisfy the eventual bounds of Lemma 4.16; the recipe's post-length bill \(B_C\) also satisfies \(B_C (\ell, J_{m + 1}) \leqslant \hat{\beta}_m\) for every \(m\);

    • the realized recipe (Lemma 4.14) holds on \(R\)'s own levels and horizon.

Status: theorem. A run is fixed by choice as fundedRun.

Proposition III.14.2 (existence of the native component recipe). Let \(G\) be simple and let \(s\) be a sheet (the kernel choice and stage profile, fixed before any block factor). There exist a price map \(X\) (the secondary price map) and floors \(E^{\flat}, E^{\text{sw}}, E^{\text{du}} : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}\) with the following properties, for every level system \(\mathcal{L}\) with the kernel and stage profile of \(s\).

  • (flat representation) If \(L_i \geqslant E^{\flat} (J_{i + 1})\) for all \(i\), then for every \(e\) satisfying \(\Omega^X_{J_i} \leqslant \log \hat{\beta}_i\) for \(i \geqslant e\), there are kernels and constants satisfying clauses (a)–(c) of Proposition III.14.3 with this \(X\), simultaneously for every sufficient horizon.

  • (point-group invariance) If \(L_i \geqslant E^{\text{sw}} (J_{i + 1})\) for all \(i\), then for every horizon \(H\) sufficient for some price map, every \(i \leqslant N\) and every torus, the colour-summed bilinear form \(\sum_a \langle A^a, \mathcal{K}^{\mathbb{T}}_{N, i} B^a \rangle\) is invariant under simultaneous pullback of \(A, B\) by signed permutations of the axes fixing the origin. Pullback reverses the sign when a link orientation is reversed.

It therefore gives a minimizer of the Gaussian variational problem for the actual quadratic forms.

Proposition III.14.3 (ultraviolet rate for the Gaussian quadratic part). Let \(G\) be simple and \(s\) a sheet. There exist a price map \(X\) and a floor \(E : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}\) with the following property. Let \(\mathcal{L}\) be a level system with the kernel and stage profile of \(s\) and with \(L_i \geqslant E (J_{i + 1})\) for all \(i \geqslant 0\). Let \(e \in \mathbb{N}\) satisfy \(\Omega^X_{J_i} \leqslant \log \hat{\beta}_i\) for \(i \geqslant e\). Then there are kernels \(F_i\) (\(i \geqslant e\)) on the links of \(\mathbb{Z}^4\) and constants \(A, B \geqslant 0\), \(c > 0\), \(d, l \in \mathbb{N}\) such that the following holds for every horizon \(H\) of \(\mathcal{L}\) sufficient for some price map, every reporting scale \(k \in \mathbb{N}\) and every \(i \geqslant e\). Write \(w_j = \min (1, \alpha^X_j, \alpha^X_{j + 1})\).

  • (a) \(\lVert F_i \rVert_{w_{J_i}} \leqslant B \hspace{0.17em} \Lambda_{k, i}^{\hspace{0.17em} l}\);

  • (b) \(\mathcal{O}^H_{n, i} (u, v) \to F_i (u, v)\) as \(n \to \infty\), for all links \(u, v\);

  • (c) \(\lVert \mathcal{O}^H_{N, i} - F_i \rVert_{w_{J_i}} \leqslant A \hspace{0.17em} \Lambda_{k, i}^{\hspace{0.17em} d} \hspace{0.17em} (a_N / a_i)^c\) for all \(N \geqslant i\).

One may take \(c = 1 / 4\), \(d = l = 1\), \(A = 34 \sigma_0 |\mathcal{F}| (1 + s_G) / (1 - 3^{- 1 / 8})\) and \(B = \sigma_0 |\mathcal{F}| (1 + s_G)\), where \(\sigma_0 = |\mathcal{F}|^2 \lambda_d^2\) and \(\lambda_d\) is a width-one strip bound for the lattice exterior derivative. These constants depend only on \(G\) and the lattice, independently of the sheet, level system, entry stage and horizon.

Status: theorem. This is an order-zero estimate (see Mesh.lean).

Proposition III.14.4 (flat representations at all stages, abstract form). Let \(P\) be a price map, \(\theta > 0\), and \(w : \mathbb{N} \to (0, \infty)\). Let \((\mathcal{L}_x, H_x, r_x, N_x, n_x, k_x, Z_x)_{x \in X}\) be a family of level systems with horizons, integers \(r_x \leqslant N_x \leqslant n_x\), reporting scales \(k_x \in \mathbb{N}\) and offsets \(Z_x \geqslant 0\). Put \(\Lambda_x (i) = 2 + Z_x + |s_i - s_{k_x} |\), with the scale data understood to belong to \(\mathcal{L}_x\). Fix \(A_0 \geqslant 0\) and \(d_0 \in \mathbb{N}\), independent of \(x\). Assume:

  • each \(H_x\) is sufficient for \(P\), and its plaquette-transfer kernels at cutoff \(N_x\) and stages \(r_x \leqslant i < N_x\) are the canonical free kernels of III.3;

  • \(w_j \leqslant \alpha_j\) for all \(j\), and for \(r_x \leqslant i < N_x\):

    • \(w_{J_i} \leqslant \min (1, \alpha_{J_i}, \alpha_{J_{i + 1}})\);

    • \(2 w_{J_i} \leqslant L_i \hspace{0.17em} w_{J_{i + 1}}\);

    • \(q_i (w_{J_{i + 1}}) \leqslant L_i^{- \theta}\), where \(q_i\) is the one-step factor of Lemma 4.3;

  • \(2 |\mathcal{F}| \Omega_{J_{N_x}} \leqslant A_0 \Lambda_x (N_x)^{d_0}\) for every \(x\).

One may take \(c = \min (1, \theta / 2)\) and \(d = d_0\), with \(A\) depending only on \(A_0, d_0, \theta\).

Proposition III.14.5 (Schur identity for two cutoffs, and the flat step).

Identity. Fix one RG step: a fine torus \(\mathbb{T}_f\) with \(L\) times the side of the coarse torus \(\mathbb{T}_c\), the block average \(Q\) and penalty \(b > 0\). Say that \((W ; M, M')\) satisfies the background-field relations when

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{K} (M) \hspace{0.17em} W = Q^{\top} \mathcal{K} (M'), \qquad QW = 1 - b^{- 1} \mathcal{K} (M') .\)

If \(M\) is symmetric and positive semidefinite, these relations make \(WA'\) a minimizer of \(\langle A, \mathcal{K}(M) A \rangle + b |A' - QA|^2\), with minimum \(\langle A', \mathcal{K}(M') A' \rangle\). For the algebraic identity below, positivity is unnecessary: if \((W_1 ; M_1, M_1')\) and \((W_2 ; M_2, M_2')\) satisfy the relations and \(M_2, M_2'\) are symmetric, then

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{K} (M_2') -\mathcal{K} (M_1') = W_2^{\top} (\mathcal{K}(M_2) -\mathcal{K}(M_1)) W_1 .\)

Flat step. Let \(\tau > 0\), \(r \geqslant 0\), and let \(B\) be a neutral, translation-invariant plaquette kernel with \(\lVert B \rVert^{\text{pl}}_{\tau} \leqslant r\), whose periodization represents \(\mathcal{K} (M_2) -\mathcal{K} (M_1)\) on \(\mathbb{T}_f\). Suppose \(T, U\) are order-two strip-bounded curvature-transport kernels with constants \(q, p \geqslant 0\) in width \(\omega\). Assume \(T (x + Lv, y + v) = T (x, y)\) and the same identity for \(U\), that their periodizations \(C_2, C_1\) satisfy \(dW_2 = C_2 d\), \(dW_1 = C_1 d\), and that \(\sum_z U ((x, \varphi), (z, \psi))\) is independent of \(x\) for each pair of orientations. If \(0 \leqslant \sigma \leqslant \omega\) and \(2 \sigma \leqslant L \tau\), then

\(\displaystyle B' (y, z) = \sum_{x, w} T (x, y) B (x, w) U (w, z), \qquad \lVert B' \rVert^{\text{pl}}_{\sigma} \leqslant \frac{2 |\mathcal{F}|^2 qp}{\tau L} r.\)

The kernel \(B'\) is neutral and translation invariant, and its periodization represents \(\mathcal{K} (M_2') -\mathcal{K} (M_1')\) on \(\mathbb{T}_c\).

Sketch: transpose the second pair of background-field relations and substitute the first pair; the mixed \(b^{- 1} \mathcal{K} (M_2') \mathcal{K} (M_1')\) terms cancel. The intertwining identities then give the asserted curvature representative. Neutrality permits subtraction of the second transport kernel at the first base point, yielding the norm estimate in Lemma 4.3.

Status: theorem. No inverse of the curvature form is used. Preservation of neutrality requires the separate constant-row-sum condition.

Lean: Flat.poised (identity), Flat.toward (step), Packets.push_null, neutralCoin.

Proposition III.14.6 (rate lemma for fine-to-coarse recursions). For each \(x \in X\), fix a scale system with \(L_i \geqslant 3\), stages \(r_x \leqslant i \leqslant t_x\) and age \(\Lambda_x (i) = 2 + Z_x + |s_i - s_{k_x} |\), where \(Z_x \geqslant 0\). Fix \(\theta, c_0 > 0\), \(A_0 \geqslant 0\) and \(d_0 \in \mathbb{N}\), uniformly in \(x\). Suppose \(f_x (i), g_x (i) \geqslant 0\) on these stage ranges and

  • \(f_x (i) \leqslant L_i^{- \theta} f_x (i + 1) + g_x (i)\) for \(r_x \leqslant i < t_x\), and \(f_x (t_x) \leqslant g_x (t_x)\);

  • \(g_x (i) \leqslant A_0 \Lambda_x (i)^{d_0} (a_{t_x} / a_i)^{c_0}\) for \(r_x \leqslant i \leqslant t_x\).

Then \(f_x (i) \leqslant A \Lambda_x (i)^{d_0} (a_{t_x} / a_i)^{\mu}\) throughout each range, where \(\mu = \min (c_0, \theta / 2) > 0\). One may take \(A = A_0 (K + 1)^{d_0} / (1 - 3^{- \theta' / 2})\), with \(\theta' = \theta - \mu > 0\) and \(K = 2 (d_0 + 1) / \theta'\).

Status: theorem.

3. Strategy

The endpoint applies the selection theorem of III.2 to the demand for a recipe. First choose the sheet, primary price map, recipe and combined floor \(E_C\). Next choose the block factors \(\ell_j\) above that floor. Finally raise the coupling thresholds to pay the bill \(B_C (\ell, j)\) and the countable numerical requests. The selection retains the resulting inequalities on the run itself, so Lemma 4.15 realizes the recipe on that run's levels and horizon.

For the Gaussian estimate, compare two cutoffs \(N \leqslant n\) at a common stage \(i\). At stage \(N\), their metric difference \(M^{\infty}_{n, N} - M^{\infty}_{N, N}\) represents the quadratic-form difference and has zero row sums in each orientation, since both Fourier symbols equal the identity at zero momentum. The mixed Schur identity transports this representative through the two background-field maps. A transport kernel has row scale \(L^{- 2}\), column scale \(L^2\) and first-difference scale \(L^{- 3}\). Neutrality permits subtraction in one leg, gaining a factor \(L^{- 1}\); the distance introduced by subtraction is absorbed using half of the input exponential weight. The block-factor floor makes the one-step factor at most \(L_i^{- 1 / 2}\). A discrete Grönwall estimate absorbs polynomial growth in the age and gives the rate \((a_N / a_i)^{1 / 4}\). Passing from curvature representatives to link kernels and then taking a Cauchy limit gives Proposition III.14.3.

The argument carries representatives modulo kernels annihilated by \(d^{\top} (\cdot) d\), without inverting the degenerate curvature form. The plaquette metrics do have inverses, used in the transport kernels. The Gaussian quadratic forms are independent of the sufficient horizon, because the terminal form and the Schur recursion determine them. This permits the estimate to be proved on a shadow level system with altered clip parameters, where III.3 supplies canonical free transfer data, and then transferred to the original horizon.

4. Steps

4.1 Flat representations and their propagation

Definition 4.1 (flat representation). Let \(H\) be a horizon of \(\mathcal{L}\), \(N \leqslant n\), \(i \leqslant N\), and \(\sigma, p \geqslant 0\). A kernel \(K\) on plaquettes \(\mathbb{Z}^4 \times \mathcal{F}\) is a flat representation of \(\mathcal{K}_{n, i} -\mathcal{K}_{N, i}\) at stage \(i\), of width \(\sigma\) and cost \(p\), if:

  • \(\lVert K \rVert^{\text{pl}}_{\sigma} \leqslant p\);

  • \(\sum_{u \in \mathbb{Z}^4} K (x, (u, \varphi)) = 0\) for every \(x\) and every orientation \(\varphi\) (neutral);

  • \(K (x + u, y + u) = K (x, y)\);

  • for every torus \(\mathbb{T}\) of the level system at stage \(i\), the periodization \(K^{\mathbb{T}}\) satisfies \(d^{\top} K^{\mathbb{T}} d =\mathcal{K}^{\mathbb{T}}_{n, i} -\mathcal{K}^{\mathbb{T}}_{N, i}\).

Lean: Flat.FlatRep, Sigil, Folded, neutral, town.

Lemma 4.2 (the finest stage). Let \(H\) be sufficient for \(P\), \(N \leqslant n\), and \(0 < \sigma \leqslant \alpha_{J_N}\). Then \(M^{\infty}_{n, N} - M^{\infty}_{N, N}\) is a flat representation at stage \(N\) of width \(\sigma\) and cost \(2 |\mathcal{F}| \Omega_{J_N}\).

Sketch: each metric has plaquette norm \(\leqslant |\mathcal{F}| \Omega_{J_N}\) by its strip bound. The difference is neutral because \(\hat{M} (0) = 1\) for both metrics. The torus identity is \(\mathcal{K} (M_{n, N}) -\mathcal{K} (M_{N, N}) = d^{\top} (M_{n, N} - M_{N, N}) d\).

Lean: finest-stage lemma (private, World.lean), via turn_class; cost topTab.

Lemma 4.3 (one step to a coarser stage). Let \(H\) be sufficient for \(P\), with \(i < N \leqslant n\). Assume the plaquette-transfer kernel of \(H\) at \((N, i)\) is the canonical free kernel. Let \(K\) be a flat representation at stage \(i + 1\) of width \(u > 0\) and cost \(p \geqslant 0\), and let \(0 < \sigma \leqslant \min (1, \alpha_{J_i}, \alpha_{J_{i + 1}})\) with \(2 \sigma \leqslant L_i u\). Then

\(\displaystyle K' (y, z) = \sum_{x, w} T_{n, i} (x, y) \hspace{0.17em} K (x, w) \hspace{0.17em} T_{N, i} (w, z)\)

is a flat representation at stage \(i\) of width \(\sigma\) and cost \(q_i (u) \hspace{0.17em} p\), where:

  • \(T_{m, i} = (M^{\infty}_{m, i + 1})^{- 1} \mathcal{T}_{m, i} M^{\infty}_{m, i}\) is the curvature-transport kernel at cutoff \(m\), with \(\mathcal{T}_{m, i}\) the installed plaquette-transfer kernel and products denoting kernel composition;

  • \(q_i (u) = 2 |\mathcal{F}|^2 h_i^2 / (uL_i)\);

  • \(h_i = \exp (4) |\mathcal{F}|^2 \Omega_{J_{i + 1}}^2 \Omega_{J_i}\).

Sketch: the four properties come from four inputs.

  • Algebra. The torus identity follows from the Schur identity and from the intertwining \(dW_m = C_m d\) (inherited_precision). These give \(\mathcal{K}_{n, i} -\mathcal{K}_{N, i} = d^{\top} C_n^{\top} K^{\mathbb{T}} C_N d\), and \(C_n^{\top} K^{\mathbb{T}} C_N\) is the periodization of \(K'\) (town_hall).

  • Bounds on the transport kernels. Both are order-2 strip-bounded with constant \(h_i\) (response, allotment).

  • Neutrality. The free plaquette-transfer kernel has a zero-momentum symbol independent of the fine residue class, and therefore position-independent row sums. Composition with the translation-invariant metric kernels preserves this property for \(T_{N, i}\) (travels). Thus constants of each orientation go to constants and neutrality passes to \(K'\) (invariant).

  • Norm. Neutrality replaces \(T_{N, i} (w, z)\) by \(T_{N, i} (w, z) - T_{N, i} (\bar{w}, z)\), where \(\bar{w}\) has the base point of \(x\) and the orientation of \(w\). In the weighted row estimate this costs \(rh_i / L_i^3\), where \(r\) is the \(\ell^1\) distance between the base points of \(x, w\). The inequality \(re^{\sigma r / L_i} \leqslant (2 / u) e^{ur}\) for \(r \geqslant 0\) follows from \(2 \sigma \leqslant L_i u\). The column estimate for \(T_{n, i}\) contributes \(h_i L_i^2\); summing orientations gives the factor \(2 |\mathcal{F}|^2 h_i^2 / (uL_i)\).

4.2 Choice of widths and passage to position space

Lemma 4.6 (choice of widths). Let \(G\) be simple, \(X\) a price map and \(w_j = \min (1, \alpha^X_j, \alpha^X_{j + 1})\). Put

\(\displaystyle \beta_{j, j'} = 2 |\mathcal{F}|^2 h_{j, j'}^2 / w_j, \qquad h_{j, j'} = \exp (4) |\mathcal{F}|^2 (\Omega^X_j)^2 \Omega^X_{j'}, \qquad E^{\flat} (j) = \lceil 2 / w_j + \beta_{j, j}^2 + \beta_{j, j - 1}^2 \rceil .\)

  • \(2 w_{J_i} \leqslant L_i w_{J_{i + 1}}\);

  • \(q_i (w_{J_{i + 1}}) \leqslant L_i^{- 1 / 2}\);

  • \(\Omega^X_{J_j} \leqslant (1 + s_G) \Lambda_{k, j}\) for all \(k\) and all \(j \geqslant e\).

Sketch: since \(J_i \in \{J_{i + 1}, J_{i + 1} - 1\}\), the constant \(h_i\) is one of \(h_{j, j}, h_{j, j - 1}\) with \(j = J_{i + 1}\). Then \(q_i (w_j) = \beta / L_i \leqslant L_i^{- 1 / 2}\) because \(\beta^2 \leqslant L_i\). Next, \(2 w_{J_i} \leqslant 2 \leqslant L_i w_j\). For the age cap: \(\hat{\beta}\) is affine in \(s\), so \(\log \hat{\beta}_j \leqslant \log \hat{\beta}_k + s_G |s_j - s_k |\).

4.6 The packages behind the numerical floors

The combined recipe floor includes rail, and its post-length bill uses receipt/after. The second numerical component uses Yamen.need and Yamen.stamp.

The supporting estimates below supply finite widths, numerical floors and post-length bounds. The widths also enter the reciprocal-width requests of Lemma 4.17. These are summaries under the hypotheses of the linked results; the complete auxiliary statements are not reproduced here.

  • One-step transit and graded differences. harbor: for admissible parameters there are a constant \(A \geqslant 1\) and a growth function \(J\) of controlled degree bounding a one-step transit of paired marked kernel families. Supporting results: DocketBill, slushy_uniform, conquer, drive (graded differences of two kernel families with scale-independent constants).

  • Curvature-coefficient lifts with linear cost. Closed coefficients satisfying the required grading, symmetry and vanishing conditions admit curvature lifts, with reduced width and a bound linear in the input size. The bilinear case needs an additional zero-momentum condition: line_legs, doubleLift, arc, warehouse, arm, multi.

  • Formal Gaussian change of variables. Near-identity substitutions preserve the formal Gaussian functional after Jacobian correction. The supporting results include marked-variable vanishing and compatibility of connected logarithms: bottle, still, absorbed, impress.

  • Formal Ward identities and gauge variations. Change of variables with an insertion, a finite-torus Ward identity, and bounds on the response to adding gradients: stirred, welcomed, spark.

  • Representatives and evaluation. Gauge-equivalent parametrizations give equal block-integral germs and rescaled coefficients under the stated regularity conditions; the block-integral comparison also assumes equality of the quadratic germs. A related formula evaluates completed coefficients by averaging over signed lattice symmetries: blossom, switch_rep, hatch.

  • Marked-kernel receipts. Bounds on marked kernels including the exact leading term, with constants announced before the block factor: podium, baton.

  • Line inverse in cell coordinates. An algebraic inverse on the constrained subspace, followed by strip bounds and the finite-torus inverse identity: jointProjection, preheat, transcribe.

5. Where the difficulty lies

  • Degenerate quadratic forms. Gauge zero modes prevent inversion of \(d^{\top} Md\). The mixed Schur identity propagates curvature representatives without this inversion.

  • Neutrality. Zero row sums are required separately in every target orientation. Kinetic normalization gives the initial cancellation; the constant-row-sum identity for the free transfer kernel preserves it. Scaled translation covariance alone does not suffice.

  • The exponent. The width floor reduces the \(L^{- 1}\) gain to \(L^{- 1 / 2}\), and the recursion estimate uses half of the remaining exponent to absorb age growth. The resulting exponent \(1 / 4\) is not claimed to be optimal.

  • Order of choices. The flat floor is fixed from the price map before the block factors. It involves the constants at indices \(j, j - 1\) and widths at \(j, j + 1\). The post-length bill then enforces the delay condition through the coupling thresholds.

  • Scope. The estimate controls the Gaussian link kernel \(d^{\top} M^{\infty} d\). It gives no convergence rate for the undetermined part of the plaquette metric, positive-order coefficients or source kernels. The formal recipe is not an identification with physical marked observables.
