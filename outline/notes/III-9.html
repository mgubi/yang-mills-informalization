Conventions.

  • Levels. Levels \(m = 0, 1, \ldots, N\) run from the coarsest to the finest:

    • \(m = 0\) is the torus of side \(L_p\) at unit spacing; the sides \(L_p\) are multiples of \(4\) and strictly increase with \(p\);

    • \(m = N\) is the Wilson lattice.

  • Blocking. The step \(m + 1 \to m\) blocks by an odd factor \(L_m \geqslant 3\). The spacing at level \(m\) is \(a_m = (L_0 \cdots L_{m - 1})^{- 1}\), and the scale is \(s_m := \log (1 / a_m) = \sum_{i < m} \log L_i\). We write \(\Lambda_m\) for the level-\(m\) torus (at volume index \(p\)) and \(| \Lambda_m |\) for its number of sites.

  • Gauge group. \(G \subset U (n)\) is closed, and its Lie algebra \(\mathfrak{g}\) carries the inner product \(\langle X, Y \rangle = - \text{Retr} (XY) / n\).

  • Plaquettes. \(s (u) = 1 - \text{Retr} u / n\) is the plaquette function. \(F_p (U) \in \mathfrak{g}\) is the orthogonal projection of \(\tfrac{1}{2} (U_p - U_p^{- 1})\), and \(S_W = \sum_p s (U_p)\) is the Wilson action. Since \(s (e^X) = |X|^2 / 2 + O (|X|^4)\), the inverse coupling \(\beta\) equals \(1 / g_0^2\) in this normalization.

Effective actions. An effective action at level \(m\) is a representation \(\rho = (P, c, E, K, b, \beta, v)\). It evaluates to

\(\displaystyle \rho (U) = \exp \left( v| \Lambda_m | - \beta \hspace{0.17em} \mathcal{K}_P (U) -\mathcal{L}_P (U) -\mathcal{E}(U) \right) \hspace{0.17em} \mathcal{G} (U) .\)

  • The kinetic functional \(\mathcal{K}_P\). This is the \(\beta\)-coefficient of the leading terms of the path data \(P\):

    • a clipped plaquette term;

    • smeared curvature-squared terms;

    • cubic and quartic local polynomials in curvatures along lattice paths.

    These are averaged over the point group of the lattice, i.e. signed permutations of the axes.

  • The other factors. \(\mathcal{L}_P\) is the \(\beta\)-independent loop functional. \(\mathcal{E}\) is the sum of the regular activities multiplied by their small-field indicators. Their weighted norms are summed at each anchor. \(\mathcal{G}\) is the gas of forced activities, whose weighted uniform bounds are summed over supports containing each site. \(b = (k, q, a, B, t)\) records the derivative order \(k \geqslant 6\), spatial power \(q \geqslant 2\), support exponent \(a > 0\), cover radius \(B \geqslant 1\) and threshold \(0 < t \leqslant 1\) of the polymer norms.

  • Normalization. The zero-momentum quadratic symbol of the \(\beta\)-term is the identity on plaquette orientations. The loop kernel has vanishing zeroth and first moments. These conditions fix the normalization of \(\beta\) within the representation.

The block-spin step. \(T^{(m)}_{\beta'}\) denotes one step from level \(m + 1\) to level \(m\) at inverse coupling \(\beta'\):

\(\displaystyle (T^{(m)}_{\beta'} \rho') (V) = \int k_{\beta'} (V, U) \hspace{0.17em} \rho' (U) \hspace{0.17em} dU.\)

The kernel \(k_{\beta'}\) is a probability kernel (unit III.1). Densities are taken relative to product Haar probability measure and are not normalized.

1. What this unit proves

This unit tracks the scalar coefficient \(\beta\) of the normalized curvature-squared term through the blocking history. Its three results are:

  • (a) Start-up. With the start-up bounds, Wilson top path data and recipe acceptance, the Wilson weight at the finest level is admissible for every \(\beta\) in the band.

  • (b) One step. Given the early presentation, local bounds, numerical conditions and recipe acceptance stated below, one block-spin step followed by a reset of \(\beta\) maps a continuous admissible family at level \(m + 1\) to one at level \(m\). The coupling moves by \(\alpha_{N, m} + d\), where \(\alpha_{N, m}\) is the one-loop coefficient of the step and \(|d| \leqslant r_m \leqslant 1\).

  • (c) Thresholds. The delay-dependent numerical conditions hold beyond entry thresholds, provided the earlier constants satisfy the compatibility inequalities of III.9.7. These choices respect the order of constants in III.2.

Combined with the shooting argument of III.2 and the constructions of III.10 and III.13, this yields the selected history of III.9.3 and the law \(\beta_{N, p} / \log (1 / a_N) \to \kappa\) of the main theorem.

Proposition III.9.1 (the slope constant). Let \(\mathfrak{g}\) be simple and nonabelian, and let \((b_i)\) be an orthonormal basis of \((\mathfrak{g}, \langle \cdot, \cdot \rangle)\). Put

\(\displaystyle C_G = - \frac{1}{\dim \mathfrak{g}} \sum_i \text{tr}_{\mathfrak{g}} ((\text{ad} b_i)^2), \qquad \kappa = \frac{11 \hspace{0.17em} C_G}{24 \pi^2} .\)

Then \(\kappa > 0\).

Here \(C_G\) is the adjoint Casimir in the chosen inner product, and \(\kappa = 2 b_0\) in the conventional one-loop notation \(b_0 = 11 C_G / (48 \pi^2)\). For \(SU (N)\) in the defining representation, this normalization gives \(C_G = 2 N^2\) and \(\kappa = 11 N^2 / (12 \pi^2)\).

The invariant form \(- \text{tr} (\text{ad} X \hspace{0.17em} \text{ad} Y)\) is \(C_G \langle X, Y \rangle\). Its value at \((X, X)\) is \(\sum_i | [X, b_i] |^2\), and nonabelianness makes this positive for some \(X\). Thus \(C_G > 0\).

Status: definition; positivity is a theorem and uses simplicity. Inside this unit only two things are used: \(\kappa > 0\), and the identity \(\hat{\beta}_{m + 1} = \hat{\beta}_m + \kappa \log L_m\). The fact that the one-loop coefficient of the actual blocked action is \(- \kappa \log L + O (1)\) is proved in III.7, where \(11 / (24 \pi^2)\) is the value of an explicit momentum integral (§2).

Proposition III.9.2 (reference coupling and scale functions). Fix \(\kappa > 0\), \(\bar{\beta} > 1\) and an infinite sequence of odd block factors \(L_m \geqslant 3\). Define

\(\displaystyle \hat{\beta}_m = \bar{\beta} + \kappa s_m, \quad M_m = e^{(\log \log \hat{\beta}_m)^2}, \quad t_m = \hat{\beta}_m^{- 1 / 2} M_m, \quad \varepsilon_m = \hat{\beta}_m^{- 11 / 10}, \quad \tilde{\varepsilon}_m = e^{- M_m^{1 / 5}},\)

and set \(B_m = \lceil (\log \hat{\beta}_m)^2 \rceil\) and \(\mathfrak{B}_m = [\hat{\beta}_m / 2, \hspace{0.17em} 2 \hat{\beta}_m]\). These are, in order: the reference coupling, the threshold multiplier, the small-field threshold, the regular and forced allowances, the cover size and the band.

Then \(\hat{\beta}_{m + 1} = \hat{\beta}_m + \kappa \log L_m\), \(\hat{\beta}\) is nondecreasing, and \(s_m \geqslant m \log 3\), so \(\hat{\beta}_m \to \infty\).

The scale conditions are hypotheses on the scale sequence, not consequences of the definitions. They are:

  • Size conditions. \(\hat{\beta}_m > 1\) and \(t_m \leqslant 1\). For every volume index \(p\), the level-\(m\) side is at least \(100 (B_m + 1)\), and the level-\((m + 1)\) side is at least \(100 (R_m + 1)\), where \(R_m\) is the block-patch radius.

  • Stage index. The stage index satisfies \(J_m \geqslant 4\), is nondecreasing, \(J_{m + 1} \leqslant J_m + 1\), and \(J_m \to \infty\). The derivative orders, spatial powers and support exponents are nondecreasing along the levels. There are nondecreasing stage block factors \(\ell_j\) with \(L_m = \ell_{J_{m + 1}}\).

  • Smallness exponent. \(0 < \theta_{J_m} \leqslant 1 / J_m\), and \(\theta\) is antitone.

  • Gentle scales. \(\log L_m / s_m \to 0\).

  • Drift and logarithmic costs. With \(\omega_j\) the drift allowance of stage \(j\),

    \(\displaystyle \frac{\omega_{J_{m + 1}}}{\log L_m} \leqslant J_{m + 1}^{- 2}, \qquad \frac{\log (1 + \text{paths} + \text{width}^{- 1} + \text{columns} + \text{relaxation} + \text{support exponent})_{J_{m + 1}}}{\log L_m} \leqslant J_{m + 1}^{- 2} .\)

Status: definitions (III.1) and elementary theorems. The scale conditions are part of the scale sequence produced in III.2.

Proposition III.9.3 (selected histories). A history assigns to all \(N, p\) and \(m \leqslant N\) a level-\(m\) representation \(\rho_{N, p, m}\) with coupling \(\beta_{N, p, m}\). It is selected if the following hold.

  1. Common path data. The path data \(P_{N, m}\) of \(\rho_{N, p, m}\) do not depend on \(p\).

  2. Shape. \(\rho_{N, p, m}\) carries the level-\(m\) norm parameters and clip. It is lawful at level \(m\):

    • its path data obey the stage bounds, with loop cap \(2 M_m^{\theta_{J_m}}\);

    • its regular activities are local, gauge invariant, vanish at the flat field, and have zero Hessian on affine link directions whenever their supports do not wrap; their norm bound is \(\varepsilon_m\);

    • its forced activities satisfy the locality, eligibility and symmetry conditions of II.2, with norm bound \(\tilde{\varepsilon}_m\).

    The whole activity family transforms under translations and signed permutations of the axes.

    Moreover \(\beta_{N, p, m} \in \mathfrak{B}_m\).

  3. Start. \(v_{N, p, N} = 0\) and \(\rho_{N, p, N} (U) = e^{- \beta_{N, p, N} S_W (U)}\) for all \(U\).

  4. Transfer. For \(m < N\), \(\rho_{N, p, m} = T^{(m)}_{\beta_{N, p, m + 1}} \rho_{N, p, m + 1}\) at every configuration, and

    \(\displaystyle | \beta_{N, p, m + 1} - \beta_{N, p, m} - \kappa \log L_m | \leqslant \omega_{J_{m + 1}} .\)

  5. End point. \(\beta_{N, p, 0} = \bar{\beta}\).

  6. Running. For every \(\varepsilon > 0\) there is \(m_0\) with \(| \beta_{N, p, m} - \hat{\beta}_m | \leqslant \varepsilon s_m\) for all \(m \geqslant m_0\), \(N \geqslant m\) and all \(p\).

Consequences:

  • Partition function. For all \(m \leqslant N\), \(\int \rho_{N, p, m} \hspace{0.17em} d \text{Haar} = Z_W (\Lambda_{N, p} ; \beta_{N, p, N})\), the Wilson partition function.

  • Running law. \(\beta_{N, p, m} / s_m \to \kappa\) uniformly in \(N \geqslant m\) and \(p\), because \(s_m \to \infty\).

  • Bare coupling. The bare coupling of the Wilson family, \(\beta_{N, p} := \beta_{N, p, N}\), satisfies \(\sup_p | \beta_{N, p} / \log (1 / a_N) - \kappa | \to 0\). This is (Reg1) of the main theorem with \(b_{\ast} = \kappa = 2 b_0\). The continuum running-coupling comparison belongs to VII.5.

Proposition III.9.4 (one reset step along a curve). Fix a datum \(\mathfrak{D}\) consisting of three things:

  • levels satisfying the scale and kernel conditions of III.9.2 and §2, and meeting the entry thresholds of the fixed numerical choices;

  • a compatible horizon, a local Haar chart, positive post-block constants, and kinetic data \((\alpha_{N, m}, P_{N, m})\) satisfying the planned one-loop conditions (§2);

  • a recipe, with initial, retention and continuation tests.

Fix \(N, p\) and \(m < N\), let \(I = [l, u]\) with \(l \leqslant u\), and let \(x \mapsto \rho'_x\) (\(x \in I\)) be a continuous curve of admissible level-\((m + 1)\) representations.

  • Admissible means: path data \(P_{N, m + 1}\), the level-\((m + 1)\) norm parameters and clip, lawful with the level-\((m + 1)\) constants, and accepted by the recipe.

  • Continuous means: \(\beta\) and \(v\) are continuous; the regular and forced addresses, including supports, inventories and eligibility tests, are fixed; and the coefficients vary continuously in the weighted sum of regular sup-jet norms and forced uniform norms. Regular jets are measured only where their supports are small-field, at the prescribed derivative order and spatial power.

Write \(\beta'_x = \beta (\rho'_x)\) and \(H_x = T^{(m)}_{\beta'_x} \rho'_x\). Let \((c_m, r_m)\) be the reset constants of the stage pair \((J_{m + 1}, J_m)\), with \(c_m > 0\) and \(0 < r_m \leqslant 1\). Let \(g = g_{J_{m + 1}} > 0\) be the prescribed fine-stage forced-decay constant. Assume:

  • (P) Priced. \(\eta = \eta_{J_m}\) is a sensor constant (Lemma 4.7), and \(2 (c_m + 1) \varepsilon_m \leqslant \eta \hspace{0.17em} t_m^2 \hspace{0.17em} r_m\).

  • (V) Probe. \(V\) is a small-field configuration with \(\mathcal{K}_{P_{N, m}} (V) \geqslant | \Lambda_m | \eta t_m^2\). Such a \(V\) exists by (P).

  • (I) Income. There is a family \(F (x, d)\), \(x \in I\), \(|d| \leqslant r_m\), of level-\(m\) activities with fixed addresses, valid with \((\varepsilon_m, \tilde{\varepsilon}_m)\). Assume the catalogue conditions: each coefficient is continuous in its regular sup-jet norm or forced uniform norm, and every convergent parameter sequence admits a subsequence with common summable majorants in those weighted norms. Moreover \(H_x (1), H_x (V) > 0\), and

    \(\displaystyle | (\beta'_x + \alpha_{N, m}) \mathcal{K} (V) +\mathcal{L} (V) - \log \frac{H_x (1)}{H_x (V)} | \leqslant | \Lambda_m | c_m \varepsilon_m,\)
    \(\displaystyle |\mathcal{E}_{F (x, b)} (V) -\mathcal{E}_{F (x, a)} (V) | \leqslant | \Lambda_m | c_m \varepsilon_m |b - a| .\)

  • (R) Remainder. Put

    \(\displaystyle \text{shape}_{x, d} (U) = e^{- (\beta'_x + \alpha_{N, m} + d) \mathcal{K} (U) -\mathcal{L} (U) -\mathcal{E}_{F (x, d)} (U)} \hspace{0.17em} \mathcal{G}_{F (x, d)} (U) .\)

    Require:

    • Shape control: \(| \text{shape}_{x, a} (U) - H_x (U) / H_x (1) | \leqslant C_{x, U} \hspace{0.17em} |f (x, a) |\) for all \(U\) and \(|a| \leqslant r_m\), where \(f\) is the scalar residual of Lemma 4.9 and, for each \(x, U\), a finite \(C_{x, U} \geqslant 0\) works for every \(|a| \leqslant r_m\);

    • Forced bound: the forced activities are bounded by \(e^{- g \hspace{0.17em} M_{m + 1}^2}\);

    • Recipe: the recipe accepts every exact trial.

Conclusion. There is a continuous \(d : I \to [- r_m, r_m]\) with the following properties. Let \(\rho_x\) have path data \(P_{N, m}\), activities \(F (x, d (x))\), coupling \(\beta'_x + \alpha_{N, m} + d (x)\) and \(v_x = | \Lambda_m |^{- 1} \log H_x (1)\). Then:

  • \(x \mapsto \rho_x\) is a continuous curve;

  • \(\rho_x = H_x\) at every configuration;

  • \(\rho_x\) is admissible, its forced activities are bounded by \(e^{- gM_{m + 1}^2}\), and \(| \beta (\rho_x) - \beta'_x - \alpha_{N, m} | = |d (x) | \leqslant r_m \leqslant 1\).

Exact density transfer, recipe continuation, admissibility, the stronger forced bound and the error bound \(| \beta - \beta' - \alpha_{N, m} | \leqslant 1\) define a reset step. Without (R), the same \(d\) is the unique zero in \([- r_m, r_m]\) of the scalar residual. In that case \(\rho_x (1) = H_x (1)\) and \(\rho_x (V) = H_x (V)\).

Proposition III.9.5 (explicit reset and step program).

(a) Explicit correction. Fix the datum, input curve and reset constants of III.9.4, and assume \(G\) is simple and the input couplings lie in \(\mathfrak{B}_{m + 1}\). Suppose that, for every \(x \in I\), the blocked density has the early presentation

\(\displaystyle H_x (U) = \exp \left( \tau_x | \Lambda_m | - \beta'_x \mathcal{K}(U) -\mathcal{L}(U) - \sum_i \chi_{X_i} (U) E_{x, i} (U) \right) \hspace{0.17em} \mathcal{G}_x (U) \hspace{0.17em} e^{- \alpha_{N, m} \mathcal{S} (U)},\)

where \(\chi_X\) indicates that \(X\) is small-field and \(\mathcal{S}\) is the masked quadratic receipt of the coarse path data. Assume:

  • The early regular functions are local, gauge invariant and smooth through the required derivative order. Their sup-jet bounds \(h_{x, i} \geqslant 0\) satisfy, at every anchor \(o\),

    \(\displaystyle \sum_{i : o_i = o} e^{(a + 3) \hspace{0.17em} \text{cover}_{B_m} (X_i)} h_{x, i} \leqslant H_x^E,\)

    where \(a\) is the level-\(m\) support exponent. The early forced functions satisfy the forced-family conditions and a site-pinned bound \(w_x\) at support exponent \(a + 1\). The full family transforms under translations and the point group.

  • The addresses are fixed along \(I\), and the early functions satisfy the coefficient-continuity and summable-tail conditions used in III.9.4(I).

  • The reset is priced; the clip is compatible with the coarse norm parameters; and the width and cubic conditions hold. \(\displaystyle d (x) = \delta_x, \qquad \beta (\rho_x) = \beta'_x + \alpha_{N, m} + \delta_x, \qquad t_m^2 | \delta_x | \leqslant H_x^E \leqslant c_m \varepsilon_m / 4, \qquad v_x = \tau_x - | \Lambda_m |^{- 1} \sum_i E_{x, i} (1) .\)

    • forced activities \(e^{- \beta \hspace{0.17em} \text{charge} (D, U)}\), one for each connected component \(D\) of the large-field region for the padded adjacency relation.

  2. Residual bound. For every gauge group \(G\) and all orders \(k\) and spatial powers \(q\) there are \(C > 0\) and \(\delta \in (0, 1]\) with the following property. It is admissible for a given recipe if its prescribed top paths are \(P^{\text{top}}\), these paths satisfy the stage bounds, and its initial and retention tests accept the displayed curve.

  4. Installation. Suppose the old kinetic data satisfy the planned conditions, the Wilson top paths satisfy the stage bounds, and a revised horizon is supplied whose top quadratic datum is the Wilson one, whose lower data agree with the old horizon, and whose initial stationary guesses agree. Then the kinetic data can be replaced by data for that horizon with \(P_{N, N} = P^{\text{top}}\), the same \(\alpha\), and every planned condition preserved.

3. Strategy

The nonlinear step gives an early presentation of the blocked density. Its marginal coefficient has three contributions: the input coupling \(\beta'\), the one-loop shift \(\alpha_{N, m}\), and the isotropic Hessian coefficient \(\delta\) of the early regular functions. The reset sets \(\beta = \beta' + \alpha_{N, m} + \delta\). Subtracting flat values and quadratic projections, adding compensating path terms, and absorbing mask discrepancies into the forced gas preserves the full density. The output activity bounds and \(| \delta | \leqslant r_m \leqslant 1\), together with \(| \alpha_{N, m} + \kappa \log L_m | + 1 \leqslant \omega\), give the transfer inequality.

Two configurations identify the reset: the flat field and a small-field stripe \(V\) with \(\mathcal{K} (V) \geqslant | \Lambda | \eta t^2\). Matching at the flat field fixes \(v = | \Lambda |^{- 1} \log H (1)\). Matching the log-ratio at \(V\) gives a scalar equation \(f (x, d) = 0\). The pricing and activity-motion bounds make \(f\) increasing with slope at least \(\mathcal{K} (V) / 2\), and give \(|f (x, 0) | \leqslant \mathcal{K} (V) r / 2\). Its unique root lies in \([- r, r]\) and depends continuously on \(x\). Shape control gives equality at every configuration. For the reorganized activities, \(f (x, d) = (d - \delta_x) \mathcal{K} (V)\), so the correction is explicit.

The interval shooting chains these steps from the Wilson lattice down to level \(0\). At each level, the transfer inequality forces the two endpoint couplings to lie on opposite sides of \([\bar{\beta} + \kappa s_m / 2, \bar{\beta} + 2 \kappa s_m]\). Continuity selects a subinterval whose couplings stay within that window and attain its endpoints. At level \(0\), a surviving bare parameter gives \(\beta_0 = \bar{\beta}\). Summing the transfer errors gives the running law, uniformly in cutoff and volume. III.13 uses compatible finite-cutoff fixed points to obtain the centered history and its analytic continuations.

Lemma 4.16 (monotone thresholds). Let \(T_j (x)\) be properties, one for each stage \(j\), each holding for all large \(x\). Lemma 4.17 (eventual reset inequalities). Fix an allowed transition from fine stage \(j\) to coarse stage \(i\), its block factor \(L\), and all the constants in III.9.7. Write \(x = \hat{\beta}_m\), \(y = x + \kappa \log L\), \(M = e^{(\log \log x)^2}\) and \(q_i\) for the early-activity fraction. The conclusions are uniform for \(| \alpha | \leqslant \omega_j + \kappa \log L\) and \(\beta \in [y / 2, 2 y]\). Then all the delay-dependent reset conditions hold for sufficiently large \(x\).

Sketch: The sufficient pricing condition \(2 (c + 1) \leqslant \eta rM^2\) holds because \(M \to \infty\). Multiplication by \(x^{- 1}\) gives \(2 (c + 1) x^{- 1} \leqslant \eta rt^2\); since \(\varepsilon = x^{- 11 / 10} \leqslant x^{- 1}\) for \(x \geqslant 1\), this implies the required pricing inequality. The cubic condition follows from \(t^3 / \varepsilon = x^{- 2 / 5} M^3 \to 0\).

For the local reorganization, its regular bound has the form \(A_i H + B_i | \alpha | t^3\), with \(A_i, B_i\) fixed before \(L\). The choice \(H = q_i \varepsilon\), with \(A_i q_i \leqslant 1 / 64\), leaves room for an output bound \(\varepsilon / 32\). The forced estimate controls both the early forced terms and the added logarithmic factors, at support exponent \(a + 1\), where \(a\) is the coarse support exponent. The width condition is \(4 (a + 1) \leqslant \sigma B\), where \(\sigma\) is the path-decay width. It holds since \(B \to \infty\), as does clip compatibility. The corresponding forced output bound is \(e^{- R_{ji} (y, \beta)}\). If \(4 g_j \leqslant \text{rate}_{ji}\) and \(\beta \in [y / 2, 2 y]\), its definition gives \(R_{ji} (y, \beta) \geqslant g_j M (y)^2\); for large \(x\) it also gives the ordinary forced allowance at \(x\).

The inherited condition is

\(\displaystyle (f_{ji} / L + u_{ji} (x)) \varepsilon (y) +\mathcal{R}_{ji} (x, y, \beta) \leqslant q_i \varepsilon (x), \qquad \beta \in [y / 2, 2 y] .\)

Here \(\mathcal{R}_{ji}\) is the fixed step-estimator residual at exponent \(\theta_j\); it is \(o (\varepsilon (x))\) uniformly in this band when \(\theta_j \leqslant 1\), and \(u_{ji} (x) \to 0\). The pre-block inequality gives \(f_{ji} / L \leqslant q_i / 8\). Together with \(\varepsilon (y) / \varepsilon (x) \to 1\), these bounds prove the inequality. Lemma 4.16 combines the conditions in the order: inherited estimates, local reorganization, cubic estimate, reset pricing; the sensors were fixed before \(L\).

    \(\displaystyle \tilde{\varepsilon}_N C_d t^{- s_d} \leqslant \sqrt{\tilde{\varepsilon}_N},\)

Lemma 4.20 (shooting in the bare coupling). Suppose the step program holds and \(G\) is simple. Then for all \(N, p\) there is a trail \((\rho_{N, p, m})_{m \leqslant N}\) with the following properties:

  • it starts with a Wilson start-up \(e^{- \beta S_W}\);

  • each level is admissible, with coupling in \([\phi_m, \psi_m]\);

  • consecutive levels satisfy the transfer clause;

  • \(\beta_{N, p, 0} = \bar{\beta}\).

Choosing these trails for all \(N, p\) gives a history satisfying (1)–(5) of III.9.3. The common-path clause follows because every trail uses the same prescribed \(P_{N, m}\).

Sketch: Decreasing induction on \(m\) carries an interval \([l, u]\) of bare couplings and a continuous curve of trails.

  • At \(m = N\), restrict the start-up curve to \([\phi_N, \psi_N]\).

  • At each step, apply the step program, giving an output curve over the same interval. By Lemma 4.19 its couplings at \(l\) and \(u\) lie below \(\phi_m\) and above \(\psi_m\). A compactness and intermediate-value argument gives \([l', u'] \subset [l, u]\) whose image is \([\phi_m, \psi_m]\), with \(\phi_m, \psi_m\) attained at the ends.

  • At \(m = 0\) the left end gives \(\beta_0 = \bar{\beta}\).

Lemma 4.21 (running). For a history on levels satisfying the drift-smallness and stage-divergence conditions of III.9.2, clauses (4) and (5) of III.9.3 imply clause (6).

5. Where the difficulty lies

The early presentation with local bounds comes from III.10, the one-loop coefficient from III.7, and the compatible kinetic data from III.13. Given those inputs, the reset must preserve the full masked density, including failed-mask discrepancies and the forced gas. Two-point matching alone does not prove that identity. Lemma 4.13 supplies it and proves the output activity bounds.

The marginal coefficient is isolated by gauge symmetry, simplicity and lattice isotropy. It is a Hessian coefficient, so its quadratic energy carries a factor \(1 / 2\). The Wilson start-up has a uniform \(O (t^5)\) residual even when individual links are far from the identity. Continuity in the bare parameter must hold in the activity norms despite the hard configuration masks; shooting depends on this continuity.

The block factors grow. The planned one-loop condition implies \(\omega_{J_{m + 1}} \geqslant 1\), while the drift condition gives \(\log L_m \geqslant J_{m + 1}^2 \to \infty\). The gentle-scale condition requires \(\log L_m = o (s_m)\). The reset correction is small:

\(\displaystyle | \delta_x | \leqslant \frac{c_m}{4} \hat{\beta}_m^{- 1 / 10} e^{- 2 (\log \log \hat{\beta}_m)^2} .\)

The constants \(c_m\) depend on the stage pair, so this is a fixed-stage estimate. The separate one-loop error is bounded independently of \(L\) once its earlier parameters are fixed; no decay of that error in \(L\) is required. The argument gives \(\beta_m = \bar{\beta} + \kappa s_m + o (s_m)\) uniformly in the cutoff and volume. It does not give a bounded remainder or a two-loop expansion.

The coarsest coupling is prescribed exactly. Shooting chooses the bare coupling separately for each cutoff and volume and restricts the surviving parameter intervals to keep every level in its band. The uniqueness theorem in III.13 applies to its specified continuation class.
