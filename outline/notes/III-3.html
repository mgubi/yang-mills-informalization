Conventions.

  • Tori and forms. \(\Lambda_n = (\mathbb{Z}/ n\mathbb{Z})^4\), \(n \geqslant 1\). \(\Omega^r (\Lambda_n)\) is the space of real \(r\)-cochains, with the counting inner product. \(d_r : \Omega^r \to \Omega^{r + 1}\) is the exterior derivative, and \(d = d_1\). \(X^{\ast}\) denotes the transpose on real cochains and the Hermitian adjoint of a symbol at real momentum. Displacements use \(|y|_1\); real momenta use the Euclidean norm \(|p|\).

  • Levels. In mesh \(N\) the levels are \(i = N, \ldots, 0\). Level \(N\) is the bare lattice. Step \(i < N\) maps level \(i + 1\) (fine torus \(\Lambda_f\)) to level \(i\) (coarse torus \(\Lambda_c\)), with \(f = cL_i\) and \(L_i \geqslant 3\) odd. Level \(i\) has a stage index \(J (i) \geqslant 4\), and \(J (i) \leqslant J (i + 1) \leqslant J (i) + 1\).

  • Kernels. A kernel is a map \(R : \mathbb{Z}^4 \to \text{Hom} (\mathbb{R}^{\beta}, \mathbb{R}^{\alpha})\), where \(\alpha, \beta\) are sets of face types. It acts on every torus by periodisation: \((R_n \omega) (x) = \sum_{y \in \mathbb{Z}^4} R (y) \hspace{0.17em} \omega (x + y)\). Its symbol is \(\hat{R} (p) = \sum_y R (y) e^{ip \cdot y}\).

  • Cross kernels. A cross kernel of block size \(L\) also carries a fine residue \(r \in (\mathbb{Z}/ L)^4\) in its row index. It maps cochains on \(\Lambda_c\) to cochains on \(\Lambda_{cL}\), for every \(c\). Its displacements are measured on the coarse lattice.

  • Strip bound. \(R \in \mathcal{S} (\alpha, \Omega)\) means \(\sum_y e^{\alpha |y|_1} |R_{ab} (y) | \leqslant \Omega\) for every entry. This implies that \(\hat{R}\) is analytic and bounded on \(| \text{Im} \hspace{0.17em} p_a | < \alpha\). Conversely, a bounded periodic analytic symbol has such a weighted coefficient bound on each narrower strip.

  • Aliases and lattice momenta. For a coarse momentum \(p\), the fine aliases are \(q_{\ell} = (p + 2 \pi \ell) / L\), \(\ell \in (\mathbb{Z}/ L)^4\) (centred representatives). \(\tau (p) = (e^{ip_a} - 1)_{a = 1}^4\) is the lattice momentum, and \(\sigma_r (p)\) is the symbol of \(d_r\), so \(\sigma_0 = \tau\).

  • Complex cubes. Write \(\mathcal{C}_{\theta} = \{p \in \mathbb{C}^4 : | \text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} p_a | < \pi + \theta, | \text{Im} \hspace{0.17em} p_a | < \theta\}\) and \(\mathcal{C}_{\theta}^{\mathbb{R}} =\mathcal{C}_{\theta} \cap \mathbb{R}^4\). Absolute squares in the real-momentum formulas are continued holomorphically as \(g (p) g (- p)\) for real-coefficient filters, and \(| \tau (p) |^2\) as \(\sum_a (e^{ip_a} - 1) (e^{- ip_a} - 1)\).

  • Constants from the kernel choice. \(\kappa > 0\) is the penalty of the kernel choice \(K\) (the number \(b\) of the main theorem). \(c_i > 0\) is the direct coefficient of the clip of level \(i\), that is, the coefficient of the bare plaquette term in the quadratic part of the action.

1. What this unit proves

The unit computes the Gaussian part of the block-spin step of III.1 in closed form, for every history of steps. It proves three things about it:

  • exact algebraic identities on every torus, giving an orthogonal splitting of the quadratic form;

  • exponential decay, uniform in the block sizes, history length and torus, with constants fixed by the kernel choice and stage index, and, after repair, by the sheet's direct clip coefficients;

  • uniform ellipticity and hypercubic symmetry of the quadratic forms.

The constants are fixed before the block sizes (Proposition III.3.1). Positivity of the rolled kernel requires the lower bound \(L \geqslant b_j\).

The package also contains:

  • the free covariance of the curvature and its comparison with the continuum (III.3.6, used in III.11);

  • calculus lemmas used in the nonlinear step (III.3.7, used in III.10 and III.2).

The identities of one step. Fix a step with tori \(\Lambda_f, \Lambda_c\), \(f = cL\). Let \(Q : \Omega^1 (\Lambda_f) \to \Omega^1 (\Lambda_c)\) be the linearised block average of III.1: a weighted average, over a \(J\)-fold smoothing patch, of line integrals of the fine 1-form along \(L\)-link paths.

Metric data on a level is a quintuple \(\mathcal{B}= (D, D^{- 1}, H, H^{- 1}, R)\) of kernels on 2-forms: a diagonal multiplier, its inverse, a metric, its inverse, and a root. With the direct coefficient \(c\) of the level's clip, set

\(\displaystyle V = (\sqrt{c} \hspace{0.17em} 1, \hspace{0.17em} R) : \Omega^2 \to \Omega^2 \oplus \Omega^2 .\)

Then \(V^{\ast} V = c \hspace{0.17em} 1 + R^{\ast} R = H\) and \(\langle dA, H \hspace{0.17em} dA \rangle = |V \hspace{0.17em} dA|^2\).

Transfer kernels are \(\mathcal{U}= (W, T, M_f, M_n, O)\) with:

  • \(W : \Omega^1 (\Lambda_c) \to \Omega^1 (\Lambda_f)\) and \(T : \Omega^2 (\Lambda_c) \to \Omega^2 (\Lambda_f)\) (cross kernels);

  • \(M_f : (\Omega^2)^{\oplus 2} (\Lambda_c) \to (\Omega^2)^{\oplus 2} (\Lambda_f)\) (cross kernel);

  • \(M_n : (\Omega^2)^{\oplus 2} (\Lambda_c) \to \Omega^1 (\Lambda_c)\);

  • \(O : (\Omega^2)^{\oplus 2} (\Lambda_c) \to ((\Omega^2)^{\oplus 2} \oplus \Omega^3) (\Lambda_c)\).

With fine data \((\mathcal{B}, V)\) and coarse data \((\mathcal{B}', V')\), the identities are:

  • (D1) \(d^{\ast} H \hspace{0.17em} d \hspace{0.17em} W = Q^{\ast} d^{\ast} H' d\)

  • (D2) \(QW = 1 - \kappa^{- 1} d^{\ast} H' d\)

  • (D3) \(T^{\ast} d = dQ\)

  • (D4) \(T \hspace{0.17em} D' d = D \hspace{0.17em} d \hspace{0.17em} W\)

  • (R1) \(M_f^{\ast} M_f + M_n^{\ast} M_n + O^{\ast} O = 1\)

  • (R2) \(\sqrt{\kappa} \hspace{0.17em} M_n^{\ast} = V' d\)

  • (R3) \(M_f V' d = V \hspace{0.17em} d \hspace{0.17em} W\)

  • (R4) \(M_n V' d = \kappa^{- 1 / 2} d^{\ast} H' d\)

  • (R5) \(OV' d = 0\)

  • (R6) \(M_f^{\ast} V \hspace{0.17em} d = \sqrt{\kappa} \hspace{0.17em} M_n^{\ast} Q\)

  • (Δ) \(H' \left( {H'}^{- 1} - T^{\ast} H^{- 1} T - \kappa^{- 1} d \hspace{0.17em} d^{\ast} \right) H' = d_2^{\ast} P \hspace{0.17em} d_2\), for a 3-form kernel \(P\) on the coarse level.

What the identities mean:

  • (D1)–(D2). These say that \(A = WB\) is a critical point, hence a minimiser, of \(A \mapsto \langle dA, H \hspace{0.17em} dA \rangle + \kappa |QA - B|^2\), and that the minimum is \(\langle dB, H' dB \rangle\). So \(W\) is Balaban's background-field (minimiser) map, and \(H'\) is the effective metric of the coarse level. The value identity is III.1's action.

  • (D3)–(D4). (D3) makes \(T^{\ast}\) a 2-form average compatible with \(Q\). (D4) says that \(T\) carries the weighted coarse curvature \(D' dB\) to the weighted fine curvature \(D \hspace{0.17em} dWB\) of the background field.

  • (R1)–(R6): orthogonal splitting. For \(A \in \Omega^1 (\Lambda_f)\), \(B \in \Omega^1 (\Lambda_c)\) and \(Z = A - WB\), (R3), (R4) and (D2) give

    \(\displaystyle \left( V \hspace{0.17em} dA, \sqrt{\kappa} (B - QA) \right) = (M_f, M_n) V' dB + \left( V \hspace{0.17em} dZ, \hspace{0.17em} - \sqrt{\kappa} \hspace{0.17em} QZ \right) .\)

    The two summands are orthogonal by (R6), and \(| (M_f, M_n) V' dB| = |V' dB|\) by (R1) and (R5). Hence

    \(\displaystyle \langle dA, H \hspace{0.17em} dA \rangle + \kappa |QA - B|^2 = \langle dB, H' dB \rangle + \langle dZ, H \hspace{0.17em} dZ \rangle + \kappa |QZ|^2 .\)

  • (Δ). This is the same splitting for inverse metrics. It says \({H'}^{- 1} = T^{\ast} H^{- 1} T + \kappa^{- 1} dd^{\ast} {+ H'}^{- 1} d_2^{\ast} Pd_2 {H'}^{- 1}\), and the last term sees only \(d_2 {H'}^{- 1} \omega\).

Proposition III.3.1 (catalogue fixed before the block sizes). Let \(K\) be a kernel choice. There are numbers \(\alpha_j > 0\), \(\eta_j > 0\), \(\Omega_j \in \mathbb{R}\), \(\lambda_j \geqslant 0\) (amendments), \(\gamma_j > 0\) (clip bounds) and \(b_j \in \mathbb{N}\) (block thresholds), \(j \in \mathbb{N}\), depending only on \(K\), with the following properties.

(a) Metric data of a history. Let \(j \geqslant 4\), let the clip have direct coefficient \(c \leqslant \gamma_j\), and let \(v\) be a history of stage index \(j\). Then there is metric data \((D, D^{- 1}, H, H^{- 1}, R)\) such that:

  • \(D\) and \(D^{- 1}\) are the explicit kernels of \(v\) with penalty \(\kappa\), and \(H^{- 1} = D^{- 1} + \lambda_j (d_2 D^{- 1})^{\ast} (d_2 D^{- 1})\) (Lemmas 4.4–4.7);

  • all five kernels lie in \(\mathcal{S} (\alpha_j, \Omega_j)\);

  • on every torus: \(DD^{- 1} = HH^{- 1} = 1\), \(c \hspace{0.17em} 1 + R^{\ast} R = H\), \(\hat{H} (0) = 1\), \(Hd = Dd\), and \(H\) and \(D\) are symmetric;

  • on every torus: \(\eta_j | \omega |^2 \leqslant \langle \omega, H \omega \rangle \leqslant \Omega_j | \omega |^2\), and the same for \(D\);

  • for every gauge group \(G\), the form \(\langle A, d^{\ast} Hd \hspace{0.17em} A \rangle\) on \(\mathfrak{g}\)-valued 1-forms is invariant under the signed lattice symmetries;

  • \(D = 1\) if \(v\) is the empty history.

(b) One step. Let \(j, t \geqslant 4\) with \(t \in \{j, j - 1\}\), and let \(P\) be a flight of index \((j, t)\): odd block size \(L \geqslant 3\) and fine history of index \(j\). Then:

  • the explicit transfer kernels \(W, T\) of \(P\) satisfy \(\nabla_u W \in \mathcal{S} (\alpha_j, \Omega_j L^{- 1 - |u|})\) and \(\nabla_u T \in \mathcal{S} (\alpha_j, \Omega_j L^{- 2 - |u|})\), for every word \(u\) of at most \(j - 2\) fine forward differences;

  • the rolled 3-form kernel \(\mathcal{P}\) of \(P\), built with fine amendment \(\lambda_j\) and coarse amendment \(\lambda_t\) (Lemma 4.12), lies in \(\mathcal{S} (\alpha_j, \Omega_j)\);

  • if \(L \geqslant b_j\), then at every real momentum \(\hat{\mathcal{P}} (p)\) is Hermitian and \(\eta_j \leqslant \hat{\mathcal{P}} (p) \leqslant \Omega_j\).

  • (C1) \(c_i \leqslant \gamma_{J (i)}\) for every level \(i\);

  • (C2) \(b_{J (i + 1)} \leqslant L_i\) for every step \(i\).

Then, simultaneously for all \(N \geqslant 0\) and \(0 \leqslant i \leqslant N\), there are two families of metric data and transfer kernels: a pre-repair family \(\textsf{M} = (\mathcal{B}^{N, i}, \mathcal{U}^{N, i})\) and an output family \(\textsf{H} = (\mathcal{B}^{N, i}, \tilde{\mathcal{U}}^{N, i})\). Proposition III.3.6 (free covariance of the curvature). Call a real 2-form kernel \(f\) firm with constants \((\alpha, \Omega, \eta)\) if \(f \in \mathcal{S} (\alpha, \Omega)\), \(\hat{f} (0) = 1\), and \(\eta | \omega |^2 \leqslant \langle \omega, f_n \omega \rangle \leqslant \Omega | \omega |^2\) for every real cochain on every torus. Symmetry is not required. Fix \(\alpha, \eta > 0\) and \(\Omega \in \mathbb{R}\), and let \(U = \{p \in \mathbb{R}^4 \setminus \{0\}: |p_a | < 3 \pi / 2\}\). For \(p \in U\) define:

  • \(\Phi_f (p) = \sigma_1 (p)^{\ast} \hat{f} (p) \sigma_1 (p) + \sigma_0 (p) \sigma_0 (p)^{\ast}\), the symbol of \(d_1^{\ast} fd_1 + d_0 d_0^{\ast}\) on 1-forms;

  • \(G_f (p) = \sigma_1 (p) \Phi_f (p)^{- 1} \sigma_1 (p)^{\ast}\), the curvature covariance symbol when \(f\) is symmetric and the gauge-fixed potential has precision \(d_1^{\ast} fd_1 + d_0 d_0^{\ast}\);

  • \(G_{\infty} (p) = s (p) |p|^{- 2} s (p)^{\ast}\), where \(s (p)\) is wedging with \(ip\) on 1-forms: the continuum Feynman-gauge curvature symbol.

(a) There is \(b > 0\), depending only on \(\alpha, \Omega, \eta\), such that \(\text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} \langle x, \Phi_f (p) x \rangle \geqslant b |p|^2 |x|^2\) for every firm \(f\), \(p \in U\) and complex 1-form \(x\).

(b) For every \(n \in \mathbb{N}\) there is \(C_n \geqslant 0\), depending only on \(n, \alpha, \Omega, \eta\), such that, for all firm \(f\) and \(p \in U\), \(\|D^n G_f (p)\| \leqslant C_n |p|^{- n}\) and \(\|D^n (G_f - G_{\infty}) (p)\| \leqslant C_n |p|^{1 - n}\).

(c) If \(f\) is a scalar kernel with \(\sum_x e^{\alpha |x|_1} |f (x) | \leqslant \Omega\), then \(\hat{f}\) is \(C^{\infty}\) on \(\mathbb{R}^4\) and \(\|D^n \hat{f} (p)\| \leqslant A_n \Omega\) for every \(p\), where \(A_0 = 1\) and \(A_n = (n / \alpha)^n\) for \(n \geqslant 1\). Here \(D^n\) is the full \(n\)th Fréchet derivative, with its multilinear operator norm; this notation also applies to (b).

Status: theorems. Part (b) is used in III.11. Lean: tank_full, gem_quote, radial_prices, Firm, fuel, garnet, pearl.

Proposition III.3.7 (calculus lemmas for the nonlinear step). These do not concern free kernels; they are consumed in III.10 and III.2.

(a) Discrete Taylor expansion with scaled bounds. Let \(b, k \in \mathbb{N}\) with \(k \leqslant b\). There is \(C = C (b, k) \geqslant 1\) with the following property. Let \(L \geqslant 1\), \(M \geqslant 0\), and let \(p : \mathbb{Z}^4 \to E\) (\(E\) a real normed vector space) satisfy \(\| \nabla_u p (x)\| \leqslant ML^{- 1 - |u|} (1 + L^{- 1} |x|_1)^b\) for all words \(u\) of forward differences with \(|u| \leqslant k\). Then the Newton pieces \(P_i (x) = \sum_{| \nu | = i} \binom{x}{\nu} \nabla^{\nu} p (0)\), of degree at most \(i\), satisfy \(\|P_i (x)\| \leqslant CML^{- 1 - i} (1 + |x|_1)^b\) for \(i < k\), and the remainder satisfies \(\|p (x) - \sum_{i < k} P_i (x)\| \leqslant CML^{- 1 - k} (1 + |x|_1)^b\).

(b) Weighted Taylor coefficients. Let \(d \in \mathbb{N}\) and let \(f : E \to F\) be \(C^d\) at \(0\) between real normed vector spaces. Let \(p_v : E \to E\) (\(v \in V\) finite) be continuous linear maps with \(\sum_v p_v = 1\), and let \(Y_v \geqslant 1\) be integers. Suppose that for every \(z\) the function \(t \mapsto f (\sum_v t^{Y_v} p_v z)\) has vanishing derivatives of orders \(0, \ldots, d\) at \(0\). Then \(D^m f (0) (x_1, \ldots, x_m) = 0\) whenever there are integers \(k_i \geqslant 1\) with \(\sum_i k_i \leqslant d\) and \(p_v (x_i) = 0\) for every \(v\) with \(Y_v > k_i\).

(c) Moments of local gauge-invariant functions. Fix \(b, d \in \mathbb{N}\) with \(b \leqslant d\), a torus \(\Lambda_n\) with \(n \geqslant 2\), an anchor \(o\), and a finite support \(X\) satisfying \(10 \hspace{0.17em} \text{dist} (x, o) < n\) for every \(x \in X\), where \(\text{dist}\) is the torus \(\ell^{\infty}\) distance. Let \(u : \text{Config} (G, \Lambda_n) \to \mathbb{C}\) be gauge invariant and depend only on links whose two endpoints lie in \(X\), with \(u \circ \exp\) of class \(C^b\) at \(0\). For every radial polynomial connection jet \(J\) through weight \(d\), let \(U_{J, o} (t)\) be its link holonomies at scale \(t\), based at \(o\): a homogeneous connection component of degree \(r - 1\) has weight \(r\), \(2 \leqslant r \leqslant d\), and is multiplied by \(t^r\). 3. Strategy

The Gaussian block-spin problem is solved in Fourier space. Iterating the block average and its independent noise gives a diagonal 1-form covariance symbol. Minimising its precision over gauge directions gives the effective form \(d^{\ast} Dd\) by Lagrange's identity. The background-field map \(W\) and curvature transfer \(T\) are then explicit alias sums satisfying (D1)–(D4).

Uniform decay follows from the filters. A stage-\(J\) box filter gives \(\prod_a (1 + | \ell_a |)^{- J}\) alias decay, leaving \(J - 2\) fine differences summable. Along a history, successive dilation ratios decrease at least geometrically by \(1 / 3\), so the extra filter products and accumulated noise stay uniformly bounded. Before estimating inverse symbols, the principal-alias poles are cancelled. The remaining denominators are bounded below at real momenta and hence on a thinner complex cube. These constants depend on the kernel choice and stage, uniformly in history length and block sizes.

The inverse-metric defect factors through \(d_2\) as \({X'}^{\ast} CX'\), where \(X' = d_2 {D'}^{- 1}\) and \(C\) need not be positive. Amending the fine and coarse inverse metrics leaves their quadratic forms on potentials unchanged and replaces \(C\) by \(C + \lambda_t - \lambda_j S^{\ast} S\). The squared 3-form transfer satisfies \(S^{\ast} S = O (L^{- 2})\), including the sum over fine residues. Choose the coarse amendments first, then require \(\lambda_j B / L^2 \leqslant 1\). The resulting positive defect yields \(M_f, M_n, O\) and (R1)–(R6) through III.6 and the repair theorem of III.10. The repair constants also depend on the sheet's direct clip coefficients. Rigidity extends the schedule identities to all tori.

4.9 Calculus lemmas (III.3.7)

Lemma 4.23 (nonlinear calculus). The statements of III.3.7 hold under their respective hypotheses. Sketch:

  • (a) Split at \(|x|_1 = L\).

    • For \(|x|_1 \leqslant L\): localised Newton remainder estimate, \(\|p (x) - \sum_{i < k} P_i (x)\| \leqslant M' (1 + |x|_1)^k\) with \(M'\) the bound on \(k\)-th differences.

Rescaling \(z\) by an independent scalar separates the ordinary Taylor degrees, and polarization recovers the multilinear coefficients. All weighted components of weight \(\leqslant d\) of every Taylor coefficient vanish. A component of weight \(> d\) vanishes on the given inputs by the support condition.

  • (c) Locality restricts the logarithmic link fields to links with both endpoints in \(X\). Gauge invariance identifies \(u\) near the identity with its pullback by the map taking a site gauge and a radial connection jet to gauged logarithmic holonomies. Part (b) gives weighted vanishing for this pullback. 5. Where the difficulty lies

  • Uniformity. The algebraic identities must be accompanied by bounds uniform over histories of any length and over all block sizes, with the kernel choice and stage index fixed. The mechanism is in Lemmas 4.2–4.3:

    • geometric dilation along the history;

    • a lower bound on the principal alias, with an exponent linear in \(M / L\);

    • \(J\)-fold smoothing, which gives \(\prod_a (1 + | \ell_a |)^{- J}\) alias decay and therefore \(J - 2\) derivatives on \(W, T\).

  • Positivity is proved only at real momenta. \(| \tau |^{- 2}\) is never estimated on a strip. Every symbol is put in a filled form (Lemma 4.6) whose denominators are bounded below at real momenta, and inverses are taken on a thinner complex cube. The estimates use the Banach algebra of exponentially decaying kernels and its analytic symbols.

  • Unusual features.

    • The amendment \(\lambda_j\) (Lemma 4.7) changes the inverse metric. It modifies the metric only off exact 2-forms, so \(d^{\ast} Hd\) is unchanged, and it exists only to make the defect's 3-form factor positive.

    • \(L\) enters the catalogue only through \(\lambda_j B \leqslant L^2\).

    • The second table depends on the sheet through its direct clip coefficients.

    • The factorisation of the defect (Lemma 4.11) needs the identities on all tori, because of the harmonic forms.

    • For explicit families, universal_diagonal gives the diagonal identities directly. The output conditions alone give them by rigidity.

  • Units. Decay is in the \(\ell^1\) norm, on the lattice where the kernel acts. For \(W, T, M_f\) that is the coarse lattice, so the fine-lattice rate is \(\alpha / L\), with amplitudes \(L^{- 1}\), \(L^{- 2}\) and an extra \(L^{- 1}\) per fine difference. These are the scalings of Balaban's background field: smooth on scale \(L\).

  • Statement strength. III.3.2 is an existence statement whose output is a package of bounds and identities. Later units use (R1)–(R6), the floors and the strip bounds. The repaired family retains the pre-repair metric data and \(W, T\), so the defect identity and positivity remain available, although they are not fields of the output conditions.

7. What remains unexplained

  1. Gaussian interpretation and zero modes. Lemma 4.4 verifies the covariance recursion away from zero momentum. A measure-level derivation requires a stated treatment of gauge directions and harmonic modes; neither the auxiliary singular symbols nor III.1's action alone specifies that Gaussian measure. The identification with an iterated Feynman-gauge covariance remains probable.

  2. Change of Gaussian variables. The quadratic splitting in §1 follows from the identities. Its use as a change of integration variables, including null directions and determinants, belongs to III.4 and III.10 and is not established by this unit alone.

  3. Auxiliary spectral criterion. The finite-test hypotheses of the cited cutoff-projector criterion must be supplied at its point of use. Its statement does not assert that a trajectory satisfies them.

  4. Further proof detail. The analytic coefficient-extraction machinery underlying the strip bounds and the deeper positivity and square-root proofs F06–F07 are inputs here; their full proofs remain outside this account. The symbol-class calculations in Gem.lean and Weight.lean support the covariance comparison, but no position-space continuum limit or contact-term statement is asserted in III.3.6.
