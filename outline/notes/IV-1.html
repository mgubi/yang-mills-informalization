1. What this unit proves

Setting and notation. Let \(G\) be a compact matrix gauge group and \(C\) a Wilson family (II.2, III.13). It has:

  • lattice spacings \(a_N \downarrow 0\), with \(a_0 = 1\), \(a_{N + 1} = a_N / b_N\) and \(b_N \geqslant 3\) odd;

  • periods \(L_p \uparrow \infty\), each divisible by \(4\);

  • couplings \(\beta_{N, p}\);

  • the periodic lattice \(\Lambda_{N, p} = (\mathbb{Z}/ (L_p / a_N)\mathbb{Z})^4\), whose sites are placed in the box \((- L_p / 2, L_p / 2]^4\);

  • the normalized Wilson expectation \(\langle \cdot \rangle_{N, p}\).

Labels. Labels at degree \(d\) (polynomial weight at most \(d\)) form a finite-dimensional complex space \(\text{Label}_d\), given by coefficients in a real basis, so that \(v \mapsto \bar{v}\) is defined. We use the supremum norm on coefficient vectors. \(\text{Label}_d\) carries:

  • a representation \(\rho_d\) of the group \(H_4\) of signed coordinate permutations;

  • a unit label \(1_d\);

  • for each axis \(a\) the reflected label \(\theta_a v = \rho_d (s_a) \bar{v}\), where \(s_a\) reverses axis \(a\).

Observables and correlations. A label \(v \in \text{Label}_d\) has a renormalized lattice observable \(I_{N, p} (v, r)\) at each site \(r\), linear in \(v\). Covariance, reality and the unit identity are required for \(N \geqslant \text{ref} (d)\). Write \(\hat{r}\) for the physical position of a site in \((- L_p / 2, L_p / 2]^4\). For a label list \(v = (v_1, \ldots, v_n)\),

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{C}_{N, p} (v ; r) = \left\langle \prod_{i = 1}^n I_{N, p} (v_i, r_i) \right\rangle_{N, p}, \qquad \mathcal{C}_{N, p} (v ; t) = a_N^{4 n} \sum_{r \in \Lambda_{N, p}^n} t (\hat{r}) \hspace{0.17em} \mathcal{C}_{N, p} (v ; r) .\)

For a lifted tuple \(r \in (\mathbb{Z}^4)^n\), \(\mathcal{C}_{N, p} (v ; r)\) means evaluation after reduction modulo \(L_p / a_N\); its lifted physical position is \(a_N r\).

Configuration sets.

  • \(\text{Conf}_n\): configurations with \(x_i \ne x_j\) for \(i \ne j\).

  • \(\text{Gen}_n\): configurations with \(x_i^a \neq x_j^a\) for all \(i \ne j\) and all axes \(a\). This set is open and dense, and its complement is Lebesgue-null.

  • On the torus: \(\text{Conf}^{(p)}_n\) requires that for each \(i \ne j\) some \(x_i^a - x_j^a \not\in L_p \mathbb{Z}\); \(\text{Gen}^{(p)}_n\) requires all \(x_i^a - x_j^a \not\in L_p \mathbb{Z}\).

Test functions. \(\mathcal{D}_n = C_c^{\infty} (\text{Conf}_n)\). A test function \(t\) is admissible at volume \(p\) if \(\text{supp} t \subset (- L_p / 2, L_p / 2)^{4 n}\).

Extractions. An extraction \(f\) is a pair of strictly increasing sequences \((N_j)\) and \((p_k)\); a refinement \(f \circ g\) passes to subsequences of both. A property of \((N_j, p_k)\) holds eventually along ordered tails if it holds for \(k \geqslant k_0\) and then \(j \geqslant j_0 (k)\). Convergence along ordered tails means that, for each neighbourhood of the limit, membership holds eventually in this sense. The iterated limits in IV.1.1 (c) also require an actual spacing limit at every admissible fixed volume.

Tables. A table \(S\) assigns to each arity \(n\), degrees \(d_1, \ldots, d_n\) and point \(x\) a multilinear form \(S (\cdot \hspace{0.17em} ; x)\) on \(\text{Label}_{d_1} \times \ldots \times \text{Label}_{d_n}\). Its values off \(\text{Conf}_n\) play no role.

Standing hypotheses.

  • (H1) The family satisfies its specification (§2, input 1).

  • (H2) The fixed-volume centered budgets, the power budgets along ordered tails, reflection positivity and the chessboard inequalities hold along \(f\), with the quantifiers in §2, input 2.

Proposition IV.1.1 (subsequential raw limits). Assume (H1) and (H2) for an extraction \(f\). Then there are a refinement \(f \circ g = ((N_j), (p_k))\), a table \(S\) and tables \(P_p\) (\(p \in \mathbb{N}\)) such that the following hold for every \(n\) and every list of labels \(v\).

(a) (Spacing limit at fixed volume.) For every \(p\), \(P_p (v ; \cdot)\) is continuous on \(\text{Gen}^{(p)}_n\). For every compact \(K \subset \text{Gen}^{(p)}_n\) and \(\varepsilon > 0\), for all sufficiently large \(j\) and all lifted tuples \(r \in (\mathbb{Z}^4)^n\) with \(a_{N_j} r \in K\),

\(\displaystyle |\mathcal{C}_{N_j, p} (v ; r) - P_p (v ; a_{N_j} r) | < \varepsilon .\)

(b) (Volume limit.) \(S (v ; \cdot)\) is continuous on \(\text{Gen}_n\), and \(P_{p_k} (v ; \cdot) \to S (v ; \cdot)\) uniformly on compact subsets of \(\text{Gen}_n\).

(c) (Ordered smeared limit.) For every \(t \in \mathcal{D}_n\):

  • \(t\) is admissible at \(p_k\) for \(k\) large;

  • for every admissible \(p\) the limit \(V_p = \lim_j \mathcal{C}_{N_j, p} (v ; t)\) exists (in the proof, \(V_p = \int tP_p (v ; \cdot)\));

  • \(V_{p_k} \to \int t \hspace{0.17em} S (v ; \cdot)\).

(d) \(t \hspace{0.17em} S (v ; \cdot)\) is integrable for every \(t \in \mathcal{D}_n\), i.e. \(S\) is locally integrable on \(\text{Conf}_n\).

Status: theorem. (H2) holds for every \(f\) as soon as the volume-uniform budget of §2, input 2, holds (Wcmain.projects). Part (d) uses the uniform bound of Lemma IV.1.d.

Proposition IV.1.2 (limits of label derivatives). Let \(S\) be an ordered limit along \(f\) (IV.1.1 (c)) that is locally integrable (IV.1.1 (d)), with (H1) and (H2) along \(f\). Let \(\text{Null}_d (S)\) be the space of \(u \in \text{Label}_d\) with \(\int tS (\ldots, u, \ldots) = 0\) for every arity \(n\), slot, spectator label list and \(t \in \mathcal{D}_n\). Assume the relay data of §2, input 3:

  • projections \(\pi_d (N, p)\) on \(\text{Label}_d\) converging along ordered tails in operator norm to a projection \(\pi_d\);

  • the relay properties for every \(d\);

  • the dimension count \(\dim \text{Null}_d (S) \leqslant \dim \ker \pi_d\).

Then \(\ker \pi_d = \text{Null}_d (S)\). Moreover there are linear maps \(\kappa_{d, e} : \text{Label}_d \to \text{Label}_e\) (\(d \leqslant e\)) and \(\tau_{d, a} : \text{Label}_d \to \text{Label}_{d + 1}\) (\(a\) an axis) such that, for all \(n\), slots \(i\), labels \(v\) and \(t \in \mathcal{D}_n\),

\(\displaystyle \int t \hspace{0.17em} S (v_{i \to \kappa v_i} ; \cdot) = \int t \hspace{0.17em} S (v ; \cdot), \qquad \int t \hspace{0.17em} S (v_{i \to \tau_a v_i} ; \cdot) = - \int (\partial_{x_i^a} t) \hspace{0.17em} S (v ; \cdot) .\)

Here \(v_{i \to w}\) is \(v\) with the \(i\)-th label replaced by \(w\). In other words, \(\partial_{x_i^a} S (v ; \cdot) = S (v_{i \to \tau_a v_i} ; \cdot)\) in \(\mathcal{D}' (\text{Conf}_n)\).

Proposition IV.1.3 (smoothness of the limit). Let \(S\) be as in IV.1.1, along an extraction for which IV.1.2 applies. Then there is a table \(T\) with the following properties.

  • \(T = S\) on \(\text{Gen}_n\), and \(T (v ; \cdot) \in C^{\infty} (\text{Conf}_n)\) for every \(n\) and \(v\).

  • IV.1.1 (a)–(d) hold for \(T\) along the same extraction and with the same \(P_p\).

  • \(T\) satisfies the basic identities (E1) below.

  • The maps \(\kappa\) and \(\tau\) descend to linear maps \(\text{rise}_{d, e} : F_d \to F_e\) and \(\partial_{d, a} : F_d \to F_{d + 1}\) on the quotients \(F_d = \text{Label}_d / \text{Null}_d (T)\). They satisfy, pointwise on \(\text{Conf}_n\),

\(\displaystyle T (\ldots, \text{rise} \hspace{0.17em} u, \ldots) = T (\ldots, u, \ldots), \qquad T (\ldots, \partial_a u_i, \ldots) = \partial_{x_i^a} T (\ldots, u_i, \ldots) .\)

Such a pair of maps is a derivative plan with its inclusion and derivative identities.

On \(\text{Conf}_n\), the smooth ordered limit along a given extraction is unique (Order.determined).

Proposition IV.1.4 (the Euclidean hierarchy). Let \(T\) be a table and let \(f\) be an extraction. Assume (H1), IV.1.1 (a)–(c) hold for \(T\) and \(T (v ; \cdot) \in C^{\infty} (\text{Conf}_n)\) for every arity \(n\) and label list \(v\). Then \(T\) satisfies (E1)–(E5), with all points in \(\text{Conf}_n\):

  • (E1) Basic identities. \(T\) is invariant under simultaneous permutation of points and labels; \(T = 1\) for \(n = 0\); and \(T ((1_d, v) ; x) = T (v ; (x_2, \ldots, x_{n + 1}))\).

  • (E2) Real spin. \(T (\rho (s) v ; sx) = T (v ; x)\) for \(s \in H_4\), and \(T (\bar{v} ; x) = \overline{T (v ; x)}\).

  • (E3) Homogeneity. \(T (v ; x + s) = T (v ; x)\) for every \(s \in \mathbb{R}^4\).

  • (E4) Reflection positivity at points. Fix an axis \(a\) and words \(w_1, \ldots, w_m\), each a finite list of (point, label) pairs with distinct points and all \(x^a > 0\). Let \(\Theta_a w\) be the word in reverse order with \(x^a \mapsto - x^a\) and labels \(\theta_a\). Then for all \(c \in \mathbb{C}^m\) the following expression is real and nonnegative:

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i, j} \bar{c}_i c_j \hspace{0.17em} T (\Theta_a w_i \hspace{0.17em} w_j) \geqslant 0.\)

  • (E5) Smeared reflection positivity. The same holds for tests: replace \(w_i\) by pairs (labels \(v^{(i)}\), test \(t_i \in \mathcal{D}_{n_i}\) supported in \(\{x_k^a > 0 \forall k\}\)) and \(T (\Theta_a w_i w_j)\) by \(\iint \overline{t_i (y)} \hspace{0.17em} t_j (z) \hspace{0.17em} T (\Theta_a (v^{(i)}, y) \hspace{0.17em} (v^{(j)}, z))\). These integrals converge absolutely, and the Gram matrix is positive semidefinite.

If in addition (E7) (polynomial bounds on derivatives) holds, then \(T\) satisfies the hierarchy (E1)–(E7), where:

  • (E6) is smoothness on \(\text{Conf}_n\);

  • (E7) says: for every arity \(n\), degree list, label list \(v\) and order \(j \geqslant 0\), there are \(A \in \mathbb{N}\) and \(C \geqslant 0\) such that \(\|D^j_x T (v ; x)\| \leqslant Cr^{- A}\) whenever \(0 < r \leqslant 1\) and \(|x_i - x_{\ell} |_{\infty} \geqslant r\) for every \(i \ne \ell\). Here \(D^j\) is the iterated real Fréchet derivative with its operator norm on \((\mathbb{R}^4)^n\). The constants are independent of \(r\) and \(x\).

Status: theorem. (E6) and (E7) are hypotheses of the assembly. (E6) is supplied by IV.1.3; (E7) is supplied by IV.1.5 together with the promotion lemma B10 of V.1.

Proposition IV.1.5 (polynomial bound at short distance; D05). Assume (H1) and (H2) along \(f\). Let \(T (v ; \cdot)\) be smooth on \(\text{Conf}_n\) for every arity and label list, and suppose \(T\) satisfies IV.1.1 (a)–(b) along \(f\). Then for every arity \(n\), degree list \((d_i)\) and label list \(v\) there is \(C \geqslant 0\), independent of \(r\) and \(x\), such that

\(\displaystyle |T (v ; x) | \leqslant C \hspace{0.17em} r^{- A} \quad \text{whenever } 0 < r \leqslant 1 \text{ and } |x_i - x_{\ell} |_{\infty} \geqslant r \text{ for every } i \ne \ell, \qquad A = 4 n (1 + \max_i d_i) .\)

The maximum of an empty degree list is taken to be \(0\). The displayed exponent follows by choosing the largest degree in the proof.

Proposition IV.1.6 (the derivative plan respects the symmetries; D06). Let \(T\) be smooth on \(\text{Conf}_n\) for every arity and label list, permutation symmetric and with real spin, and let \((\text{rise}, \partial)\) be a derivative plan for \(T\) satisfying its inclusion and derivative identities. Use \(\rho_d\) also for the induced action on \(F_d\). For every \(d \leqslant e\), axis and \(s \in H_4\):

  • \(\text{rise}_{d, e}\) and every \(\partial_a\) commute with complex conjugation;

  • \(\text{rise}\) commutes with \(\rho (s)\) for every \(s \in H_4\);

  • \(\rho_{d + 1} (s) \hspace{0.17em} \partial_b = \sum_a s_{ab} \hspace{0.17em} \partial_a \hspace{0.17em} \rho_d (s)\), where \((s_{ab})\) is the signed permutation matrix of \(s\).

3. Strategy

Chessboard estimates bound correlations at distinct points uniformly in the cutoff and volume. To obtain equicontinuity, fix an axis and express a correlation as a cyclic trace of transfer kernels and observable kernels supported in slabs. Moving one insertion transfers length between two gaps. Finite-dimensional complex interpolation bounds the trace for complex gap shifts; Cauchy's estimate gives a Lipschitz bound. The required column moments are bounded by the same chessboard budgets. This argument needs separation in every coordinate. Two applications of Arzelà–Ascoli, with countable basis indices, give spacing limits at every fixed volume and then a volume limit on \(\text{Gen}_n\). Bounds on all of \(\text{Conf}_n\) supply domination for smeared limits.

Smoothness uses the label calculus. The relay estimates approximate each lattice forward difference quotient by a degree-raised observable. Summation by parts computes its weak limit, and chessboard-small errors have vanishing smeared limits. The projections and dimension count identify the null labels. A finite-dimensional compactness argument then replaces the varying approximating labels by fixed linear maps, giving distributional derivatives as kernels with raised labels. Iteration yields rapid Fourier decay after localization, hence a smooth representative on all distinct-point configurations.

The exact lattice symmetries pass first at generic grid points and then by continuity. Reflection positivity passes for joint tests supported in a half-space; normalized bump functions give its pointwise form. A two-scale choice of cells yields a polynomial bound at short distances, and the derivative identities promote it to bounds of every order. These arguments assemble the Euclidean hierarchy.

4.1 Reflection positivity and chessboard estimates

Lemma IV.1.a (reflection positivity of the Wilson measure). Let \(\Lambda = (\mathbb{Z}/ n\mathbb{Z})^4\) with \(n\) even. Let \(a\) be an axis, \(b \in \mathbb{Z}/ n\), and \(\Theta\) either the site reflection \(x^a \mapsto 2 b - x^a\) or the bond reflection \(x^a \mapsto 2 b - 1 - x^a\) (other coordinates fixed), acting on configurations by pull-back. Let \(\Lambda_+\) be the half-torus \(0 < (x^a - b) \bmod n < n / 2\) (site case) or \(0 \leqslant (x^a - b) \bmod n < n / 2\) (bond case), with residues in \(\{0, \ldots, n - 1\}\). For every \(\beta \geqslant 0\) and every bounded, measurable, gauge-invariant \(F\) depending only on links in \(\Lambda_+\),

\(\displaystyle \langle F \hspace{0.17em} \overline{F \circ \Theta} \rangle_{\beta} \geqslant 0.\)

Proof sketch. This is the Osterwalder–Seiler argument. Reparametrize configurations by the links in the two fixed planes, the links crossing them, and the two open halves; this map preserves the measure.

  • Site reflections. Given the plane links, the halves are independent and exchanged by \(\Theta\), so the expectation is a weighted integral of \(|\mathbb{E}(F \mid \text{plane links}) |^2\) with nonnegative weights.

  • Bond reflections. Integrating the crossing links gives for each plane the one-step kernel \(K_{\beta} (X, Y)\). It is positive semidefinite because \(\text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} \text{tr} (XY^{\ast})\) is a sum of Gram kernels, and exponentials, products and averages of positive semidefinite kernels stay positive semidefinite (Schur). The product of the two boundary kernels is positive semidefinite, so its quadratic integral is nonnegative.

Lemma IV.1.b (chessboard estimate). Let \(\mathcal{T}\) be a tiling of \(\Lambda\) into \(M^4\) congruent cells (\(M\) even), with site or bond seams. For a cell \(c\) let \(\theta_c\) be the composition of seam reflections carrying the base cell to \(c\). For \(F\) interior to the base cell (bounded, measurable, gauge invariant, depending only on links inside it) put \(\|F\|_{\mathcal{T}} = | \langle \prod_c \text{copy}_c F \rangle_{\beta} |^{1 / M^4}\). If \(\beta \geqslant 0\), then for interior \(F_c\)

\(\displaystyle | \left\langle \prod_c \text{copy}_c F_c \right\rangle_{\beta} | \leqslant \prod_c \|F_c \|_{\mathcal{T}},\)

and \(\| \cdot \|_{\mathcal{T}}\) is subadditive. By (H1) this holds for every \((N, p)\) of the family.

Proof sketch. This is the Fröhlich–Israel–Lieb–Simon argument.

  • Fold. For an assignment \(A\) of functions to cells, put \(\Phi (A) = | \langle \prod_c \text{copy}_c A_c \rangle_{\beta} |\). Cauchy–Schwarz for a band cut gives \(\Phi (\text{assign} (f, g))^2 \leqslant \Phi (\text{assign} (f, f)) \Phi (\text{assign} (g, g))\), where the two assignments supply the two halves.

  • Maximization. Among assignments of finitely many functions to cells, the maximum of \(| \langle \prod \rangle |\) is attained by a constant assignment, because a fold-closed property satisfied by some assignment is satisfied by a constant one.

  • Normalization. Apply this to \(F_c / (\|F_c \|+ \epsilon)\) and let \(\epsilon \to 0\).

  • Subadditivity. Expand \(\prod_c (F + F')\) into \(2^{M^4}\) assignments and apply the estimate to each.

Lemma IV.1.e (Lipschitz bound for traces; proved in III.16). Let \(X\) be a compact metric space with a finite Borel measure, \(K\) a continuous positive semidefinite kernel, and \(B_1, \ldots, B_n\) continuous kernels, with \(n \geqslant 2\). Let \(q, w \geqslant 1\) be integers, put \(M = 2 qw\), and assume \(Z = \text{Tr} \hspace{0.17em} K^M > 0\). Take gaps \(Y, Y' \in \mathbb{N}^n\) with \(nw + \sum_k Y_k = M\). Suppose \(Y'\) transfers an integer \(\ell \geqslant 0\) units from one gap to a different gap, \(Y_k > 2 r\) for every \(k\), and \(0 \leqslant \ell \leqslant r\) with \(r > 0\). If

\(\displaystyle \|B_k \|_{2 q} := (\text{Tr} (B_k^{\ast} B_k)^q)^{1 / (2 q)} \leqslant c_k Z^{1 / (2 q)},\)

then

\(\displaystyle | \text{Tr} \prod_k B_k K^{Y'_k} - \text{Tr} \prod_k B_k K^{Y_k} | \leqslant Z \left( \prod_k c_k \right) \frac{\ell}{r} .\)

Proof sketch. Approximate the cyclic integrals by finite matrices \((A, b_k)\) using one quadrature for all kernels and their adjoints. It agrees with \(A^m\) at positive integers \(m\). The trace with gap vector \(W (z) = Y + z (e_i - e_j)\) is entire. On \(|z| \leqslant 2 r\), all real gap parts are nonnegative and

\(\displaystyle \frac{n}{2 q} + \sum_k \frac{\text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} W_k (z)}{M} = 1.\)

Writing \(Z_A = \text{Tr} \hspace{0.17em} A^M\), complex interpolation gives the bound \(\prod_k \|b_k \|_{2 q} \hspace{0.17em} Z_A^{1 - n / (2 q)}\). Cauchy's estimate bounds the derivative on \(|z| \leqslant r\) by this quantity divided by \(r\). Integrate from \(0\) to \(\ell\) and pass to the kernel limit; the partition traces and column moments converge under the common quadrature.

4.7 Euclidean axioms, bounds and assembly

Lemma IV.1.t (symmetries and basic identities). If \(T\) is smooth on \(\text{Conf}_n\) and is a point limit (IV.1.1 (a)–(b)) along \(f\), and (H1) holds, then \(T\) satisfies (E1)–(E3).

Proof sketch.

  • Grid convergence. For a point \(x\) of the level-\(k\) grid \(a_k \mathbb{Z}^{4 n}\) with \(x \in \text{Gen}_n\), use its reduction in \(\Lambda_{N, p}\) for \(N \geqslant k\) and sufficiently large \(p\). The lattice correlations there converge to \(T (v ; x)\) along ordered tails.

  • Lattice identities. Permutation symmetry, \(H_4\)-covariance about the origin, translations by grid vectors, reality and the unit label hold exactly on the lattice, by (H1) and the invariance of the Wilson measure.

  • Extension. Equality on the dense set of generic grid points extends to \(\text{Gen}_n\) by continuity, and then to \(\text{Conf}_n\), since both sides are continuous there and \(\text{Gen}_n\) has full measure. Real translations are approximated by grid translations.

Lemma IV.1.u (reflection positivity of the limit). If \(T\) is smooth, symmetric and an ordered limit along \(f\), and (H1) holds, then (E5) holds; (E5) implies (E4).

Proof sketch.

  • Lattice side. For a test supported in \(\{x^a > 0\}\), take \(p\) large enough that it is admissible. The smeared lattice observable \(a_N^{4 n} \sum_r t (\hat{r}) \prod I (v_k, r_k)\) is then, for \(N\) large, interior to the half-torus \(0 < x^a < L_p / 2\). Its reflection through the site plane \(x^a = 0\) carries labels \(\theta_a\), because \(I (\theta_a v, \Theta x) = \overline{I (v, x) \circ \Theta}\). The lattice Gram matrix of such observables is the smeared lattice correlation of \(\overline{t_i} \otimes t_j\) pulled back by reflection of the first configuration. Symmetry permits reversal of its entries. The resulting joint test has compact support in \(\text{Conf}_{n_i + n_j}\), since the two configurations lie strictly on opposite sides of the plane. Lemma IV.1.a makes the lattice Gram matrix positive semidefinite.

  • Limit. Smoothness gives absolute integrability on the compact joint supports. Pass to the ordered limit using (c).

  • (E4) from (E5). Use bump functions concentrating at the points, together with continuity of \(T\).

Lemma IV.1.v (the polynomial bound). Under the hypotheses of IV.1.5, its stated exponent \(4 n (1 + \max_i d_i)\) is valid.

Proof sketch. Set \(D = 1 + \max_i d_i\). The centered power budgets for all transformed labels can be bounded by \(Ba_k^{- D}\) for every \(k \geqslant M\), with fixed \(B \geqslant 0\) and \(M\).

  • Choose the first \(m \geqslant M\) with \(a_m < r / 2\), and the first \(k \geqslant M\) with \(a_k < a_m / [6 (n + 1)]\). The scale inequality, including the possible endpoint \(m = M\) or \(k = M\), gives \(a_m \geqslant c (r / 2)^2\) and then \(a_k \geqslant c' r^4\).

  • Lemma IV.1.d bounds the lattice correlations by \((Ba_k^{- D})^n\). Pass first to the spacing limit and then to the volume limit at generic points with separation \(> r / 2\).

  • Continuity and density extend the bound to every \(r\)-separated configuration. Since \(a_k \geqslant c' r^4\), the bound is at most \(Cr^{- 4 Dn}\), with \(C\) independent of positions and \(r\).

5. Where the difficulty lies

The main estimates are the transfer-kernel Lipschitz bound and the control of uncentered chessboard roots. Complex interpolation and Cauchy's estimate turn reflection positivity into equicontinuity without prior derivative bounds. Translation log-convexity allows the centered budgets to control observables at arbitrary interior positions.

The regularity argument depends on more than compactness: the relay estimates supply degree-raised approximants to differences, and the rank comparison identifies precisely which labels vanish. The normalized remainder estimate in Lemma IV.1.p is needed to obtain fixed derivative labels. Fourier regularity, passage of symmetries by density, and positivity of limiting Gram matrices then use standard arguments.

Three distinctions matter:

  • Equicontinuity is proved on \(\text{Gen}_n\), whereas bounds and the smooth representative extend to \(\text{Conf}_n\).

  • The maps \(\tau_{d, a}\) are chosen by compactness. Their kernel identities determine them only modulo \(\text{Null}_{d + 1}\); the induced maps on \(F_d\) are intrinsic.

  • The hierarchy here has signed coordinate symmetries and reflection positivity at coordinate planes. Translation invariance moves those planes. Continuous rotation symmetry is a later result.
