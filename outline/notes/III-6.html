1. What this unit proves

Setting. The definitions are those of III.1. All lattice sums use counting measure.

Lattices, group, distance.

  • One renormalization step goes from a fine torus \(\Lambda_f\) to a coarse torus \(\Lambda_c\), with an odd block factor \(L \geqslant 3\) and \(f = Lc\), in dimension \(4\). The patch order satisfies \(J \geqslant 4\).

  • \(G\) is the gauge group and \(\mathfrak{g}\) its Lie algebra, normed in physical colour coordinates.

  • \(\text{chord} (U) = \|U - 1\| / c_G\) and \(\text{price} (U) = \tfrac{1}{2} \text{chord} (U)^2\). With this normalization \(\text{price} (e^{tX}) = \tfrac{1}{2} t^2 |X|^2 + o (t^2)\); the price is the Wilson plaquette function up to a constant.

The step datum \(S\). It consists of:

  • a block-spin kernel, with penalty \(\kappa > 0\), from the kernel choices \(K\);

  • a patch, with order \(J\) and macro-radius \(R_S = (J + 7) L\);

  • a finite set of tags (frame and path choices; StepData.Choices);

  • for each tag \(k\), a predictor \(U \mapsto Q_k (U)\) from fine configurations to coarse configurations.

\(P\) denotes the derivative of the predictor at the identity. It acts on fine \(\mathfrak{g}\)-valued \(1\)-forms; it is the linearized block average plus a gradient correction at the block roots. \(\bar{Q}\) denotes the linearized block average alone.

"Larger". \(S\) is larger (Larger) if three conditions hold:

  • the minimizing frame and predictor are unique whenever their minimizing branches are selected, and the fallback set of eligible axial frames is nonempty; see Branches;

  • \(100 R_S \leqslant f\);

  • \(c > 1\).

Forests and the axis chart.

  • A forest \(H\) is a spanning forest of the fine torus, made of one tree in each block, rooted at the block centre, with tree height at most \(4 L\) (Forest). Its bonds are the tree bonds, all internal to blocks; the remaining bonds are the free bonds \(H^{\text{fr}}\).

  • The replica \(\mathcal{R} (V)\) of a coarse configuration \(V\) puts \(V_{(i, \mu)}\) on every fine bond that leaves block \(i\) in direction \(\mu\), and \(1\) on bonds internal to a block. Its linearization is \(\iota\).

  • The axis chart has coordinates \(x \in \mathfrak{g}^{\text{coarse bonds}}\) and \(a \in \mathfrak{g}^{H^{\text{fr}}}\). It describes the fine field

\(\displaystyle U^H (x, a)_e = e^{a_e} \hspace{0.17em} \mathcal{R} (e^x)_e \quad (e \in H^{\text{fr}}), \qquad U^H (x, a)_e = 1 \quad (e \text{a tree bond}) .\)

Clip and path datum.

  • A clip \(p\) carries the Wilson coefficient \(c_p > 0\).

  • A path datum \(W\) (PathData) consists of:

    • a series \(\ell_W (U)_{\pi} = \sum_i w_i \hspace{0.17em} \text{slot}_i (U)\) of parallel-transported plaquette variables, attached to each plaquette \(\pi\);

    • loop atoms, cubic atoms and quartic atoms: \(\text{Ad}\)-invariant polynomials of degree \(2, 3, 4\) in such slot values.

  • Bounds \((p, \sigma, C, \Lambda)\) for \(W\) (Bounds) mean four things:

    • the coefficients are summable with weight \(e^{\sigma \cdot \text{length}}\), with sums at most \(C\) (cubic, quartic, \(\ell\)-rows) and \(\Lambda\) (loop atoms);

    • the atoms are \(\text{Ad}\)-invariant;

    • if \(r_{ij}\) is the sum of row-\(i\) coefficients whose plaquette type is \(j\), then \(c_p \delta_{ij} + \sum_k r_{ki} r_{kj} = \delta_{ij}\);

    • for each pair of plaquette types and colour indices, the loop coefficients have zero sum and zero first moment in every component of the relative slot offset; these moment series are summable.

The tested action.

  • The main term (mainTerm) is the average over the finite group \(\mathcal{S}\) of signed coordinate permutations, acting by pullback \(s^{\ast} U\):

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{M}_p (W ; U) = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|} \sum_{s \in \mathcal{S}} \left[ \sum_{\pi} \left( c_p \hspace{0.17em} \text{price} ((s^{\ast} U)_{\pi}) + \tfrac{1}{2} | \ell_W (s^{\ast} U)_{\pi} |^2 \right) + \sum_x \sum_i (\text{cub}_i + \text{quart}_i) (s^{\ast} U)_x \right] .\)

  • The tested action for tag \(k\) (tested) is

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{T}^H_k (x, a) =\mathcal{M}_p (W ; U^H (x, a)) + \kappa \sum_b \text{price} \left( e^{x_b} \hspace{0.17em} Q_k (U^H (x, a))_b^{- 1} \right),\)

where \(b\) runs over the coarse bonds. The second term is the block-spin penalty.

Beds, towers and the quadratic identities.

  • A bed \(B\) (Bed) is a family of translation-invariant kernels on plaquette \(2\)-forms: a metric \(M_B\), an inverse kernel, a diagonal kernel \(D_B\), a dual diagonal kernel, and roots \(R_B\). Set \(K_B = d^{\top} M_B d\), which acts on \(1\)-forms.

  • A floor \((a, A)\) for \(B\) (Floor) requires:

    • the root identity \(M_B = c_p \hspace{0.17em} 1 + R_B^{\top} R_B\);

    • inverse identities for the metric and diagonal kernels;

    • the Fourier-symbol normalization \(\hat{M}_B (0) = I\) and \(M_B d = D_B d\) on the specified torus;

    • symmetry of \(M_B\) and \(D_B\);

    • the bounds \(a | \omega |^2 \leqslant \langle \omega, M_B \omega \rangle \leqslant A | \omega |^2\), and the same bounds for \(D_B\).

  • \(W\) is attached to \(B\) if the coefficients of \(\ell_W\), summed by offset and plaquette type, are the entries of \(R_B\). \(B\) is sworn if \(\langle dA, M_B dA \rangle\) is \(\mathcal{S}\)-invariant.

  • Towers \(T\) for the pair of beds \((B, C)\) (Towers.Diagonal) provide a linear map \(\mathcal{W}\) from coarse \(1\)-forms to fine \(1\)-forms. Its first clause, Inputs, is

\(\displaystyle K_B \mathcal{W}= \bar{Q}^{\top} K_C, \qquad \bar{Q} \mathcal{W}= 1 - \kappa^{- 1} K_C .\)

  • \(\hat{\mathcal{W}}_H =\mathcal{W}+ d_0 \chi_H\) is the linearized gauge transform of \(\mathcal{W}\) that vanishes on tree bonds (extracted).

  • \(\text{rel}_H (x) = (\hat{\mathcal{W}}_H x - \iota x) |_{H^{\text{fr}}}\) (relative).

Critical branch. A critical branch for \((H, p, W)\) (Critical) is a map \(\rho\) from coarse fields to \(\mathfrak{g}^{H^{\text{fr}}}\) such that:

  • \(\rho (0) = 0\) and \(\rho\) is real-analytic at \(0\);

  • for every tag \(k\), \(\mathcal{T}^H_k\) is jointly analytic at \(0\);

  • for every tag \(k\), \(D_a \mathcal{T}^H_k (x, \rho (x)) = 0\) for \(x\) near \(0\).

Chart ratio and thermal function.

  • A local chart ratio \(\mathscr{J}\) (LocalRatio) is a density \(J_{\mathscr{J}}\) on a ball \(\{|X| < r_{\mathscr{J}} \} \subset \mathfrak{g}\), where \(r_{\mathscr{J}} > 0\) and \(s_{\mathscr{J}} > 0\) is a scale. It satisfies:

    • \(J_{\mathscr{J}}\) is measurable, positive and real-analytic on the ball, with \(J_{\mathscr{J}} (0) = 1\);

    • \(\exp\) is injective on the ball and its image is open;

    • the pushforward of \(s_{\mathscr{J}} J_{\mathscr{J}} (X) \hspace{0.17em} dX\) restricted to the ball is probability Haar measure restricted to its exponential image.

    Only these density properties enter the forest comparison.

  • The thermal function of a branch (thermal) is

\(\displaystyle \Theta^H_k (x) = \text{loop}_W (U^H (x, \rho (x))) + \tfrac{1}{2} \log \det \text{Hess}_a \mathcal{T}^H_k (x, \rho (x)) - \sum_{e \in H^{\text{fr}}} \log J_{\mathscr{J}} (\rho (x)_e) .\)

The Hessian is taken in the orthonormal colour basis. For a branch with a positive vertical gap, the determinant and chart densities are positive near \(0\).

Proposition III.6.1 (second-order jet of the tested action on the tree slice; F03). Let \(S\) be larger, \(H\) a forest and \(B\) a bed with a floor \((a, A)\) on \(\Lambda_f\) for the clip \(p\). Let \(W\) be a path datum with bounds \((p, \sigma, C, \Lambda)\), attached to \(B\), with \(B\) sworn on \(\Lambda_f\). Assume that the main term of \(W\) is analytic along analytic families through the identity (true by III.6.2). Then for every tag \(k\), \(\mathcal{T}^H_k\) is real-analytic at \(0\), with \(\mathcal{T}^H_k (0) = 0\) and \(D\mathcal{T}^H_k (0) = 0\). Moreover, for all \((x, a), (x', a')\),

\(\displaystyle D^2 \mathcal{T}^H_k (0) [(x, a), (x', a')] = \langle ds, \hspace{0.17em} M_B \hspace{0.17em} ds' \rangle + \kappa \hspace{0.17em} \langle x - Ps, \hspace{0.27em} x' - Ps' \rangle, \qquad s = \iota x + a, s' = \iota x' + a' .\)

Here \(a\) is extended by \(0\) to tree bonds, and the pairings are summed over plaquettes or coarse bonds and over colours. Status: theorem. Only the well-posedness clause of "larger" is used. Lean: Bid.F03, with conclusions Prismatic and Comptroller; proof Work.F03.answer.

Proposition III.6.2 (analyticity of the main term; F02). Let \(W\) have bounds \((p, \sigma, C, \Lambda)\). Let \(X\) be a finite-dimensional real normed space and \(x_0 \in X\). Let \(y \mapsto A (y)\) be a family of fine configurations with \(A (x_0) = 1\) whose link matrices are real-analytic at \(x_0\). Then \(y \mapsto \mathcal{M}_p (W ; A (y))\) is real-analytic at \(x_0\). The proof does not use \(A (x_0) = 1\): the main term is analytic along analytic families at every point. Status: theorem. It supplies the hypothesis Fires of III.6.1. Lean: Bid.F02, Work.F02.answer.

Proposition III.6.3 (gap of the vertical Hessian; F01). Assume the shell comparison E02 (§2). Fix \(G\), \(L\), \(J\), kernel choices \(K\) with penalty \(\kappa\), and \(a > 0\). Then there is \(\eta > 0\) with the following property. Take sides \(c, f\); a larger step datum \(S\) with kernel choices \(K\), block factor \(L\) and order \(J\); a forest \(H\); a clip \(p\); a bed \(B\) with floor \((a, A)\) for some \(A\); and a path datum \(W\) such that every \(\mathcal{T}^H_k\) is analytic at \(0\) with the value, gradient and Hessian specified in III.6.1. Then for every tag \(r\) and every \(v \in \mathfrak{g}^{H^{\text{fr}}}\),

\(\displaystyle \eta \sum_{e \in H^{\text{fr}}} |v_e |^2 \leqslant D_a^2 \mathcal{T}^H_r (0, 0) [v, v] .\)

One may take

\(\displaystyle \eta = \frac{\min (a, \kappa)}{1 + \beta + \gamma}, \qquad \beta = 2 C_{\text{E02}}^2 \hspace{0.17em} L^4 \cdot 4 \cdot 6 \hspace{0.17em} |X|^2, \qquad \gamma = 2 L^4,\)

where:

  • \(C_{\text{E02}} = C_{\text{E02}} (G, L, J, K)\) is the constant of E02;

  • \(X = \{- R_S, \ldots, R_S \}^4 \subset \mathbb{Z}^4\), where \(R_S = (J + 7) L\), so \(|X| = (2 R_S + 1)^4\);

  • the factors \(L^4\), \(4\) and \(6\) are the numbers of fine sites per block, of axes and of plaquette orientations.

So \(\eta\) depends only on \((G, L, J, K, a)\). It does not depend on \(c\), \(f\), \(H\), \(B\) beyond its floor, or \(W\). Status: theorem, conditional on E02 in its statement. E02 is proved in III.10 (Work.E02.answer), and the combination is unconditional in III.6.6. Lean: Bid.F01, with conclusion PriceGap; proof Work.F01.answer.

Proposition III.6.4 (existence, tangent and value of the critical branch; F04). Let \(S\) be larger, \(H\) a forest, \(B, C\) beds, \(p\) a clip, \(W\) a path datum, \(a > 0\), and \(T\) towers for \((S, B, C)\) satisfying the diagonal identities. Assume:

  • every \(\mathcal{T}^H_k\) is analytic at \(0\);

  • every \(\mathcal{T}^H_k\) has the value, gradient and Hessian specified in III.6.1 with bed \(B\);

  • the gap of III.6.3 holds with constant \(a\);

  • there are a clip \(q\) and real constants \(v, u\) for which \(C\) has floor \((v, u)\) on \(\Lambda_c\).

Then there is a critical branch \(\rho\) for \((H, p, W)\) such that:

  • (ii) \(D \rho (0) = \text{rel}_H\), built from the tower map \(\mathcal{W}\);

  • (iii) for every tag \(k\), \(x \mapsto \mathcal{T}^H_k (x, \rho (x))\) is analytic at \(0\), and its second derivative at \(0\) is \(\langle dx, M_C \hspace{0.17em} dx' \rangle\). Equivalently, its \(2\)-jet is that of \(\tfrac{1}{2} \langle dx, M_C dx \rangle\).

The floor of \(C\) is unused in the proof. Of the diagonal identities, only the clause \(K_B \mathcal{W}= \bar{Q}^{\top} K_C\), \(\bar{Q} \mathcal{W}= 1 - \kappa^{- 1} K_C\) is used. Status: theorem. It gives a germ only: no control of the size of the neighbourhood. Lean: Bid.F04, with conclusions Critical, Guessed and HeatG; proof Work.F04.answer.

Proposition III.6.5 (independence of the gauge-fixing forest; F05). Let \(S\) be larger, \(H, H'\) forests, and \(W\) a path datum with bounds \((p, \sigma, C, \Lambda)\). Let \(\rho, \rho'\) be critical branches for \(H\) and \(H'\). Assume positive gap constants \(a, b\) for \(H, H'\), respectively, and let \(\mathscr{J}\) be a local chart ratio. Then for every tag \(k\), for \(x\) near \(0\):

  • (i) \(\mathcal{T}^H_k (x, \rho (x)) =\mathcal{T}^{H'}_k (x, \rho' (x))\);

  • (ii) \(\Theta^H_k (x) = \Theta^{H'}_k (x)\).

The gap for \(H\) is unused in the proof. The statement of (ii) allows an additive constant; the proof gives \(0\). Status: theorem. Lean: Bid.F05, Work.F05.answer, Work.F05.same_thermal.

Proposition III.6.7 (the branch on a volume-independent ball).

(a) Uniform implicit function theorem. Let \(\iota, \tau\) be finite sets and let \(\ell : \iota \sqcup \tau \to \Lambda\) assign their coordinates to sites of a four-dimensional torus, with at most \(K_0\) coordinates per site. Equip the coordinate spaces with the sup norm. Fix \(C_0, a_0, r, m > 0\) and an integer \(n \geqslant 3\). Suppose \(f : \mathbb{R}^{\iota \sqcup \tau} \to \mathbb{R}\) is real-analytic at every point \(z\) of \(\{\|z\|_{\infty} < r\}\) and, for \(1 \leqslant k \leqslant n\), every coordinate tuple \(q_1, \ldots, q_k\) and every \(j \leqslant k\),

\(\displaystyle | \partial_{q_1} \cdots \partial_{q_k} f (z) | \leqslant C_0 \exp \hspace{-0.17em} \left( - a_0 \sum_{i = 1}^k \text{dist} (\ell (q_j), \ell (q_i)) \right) .\)

Here distance is the periodic max-distance. Assume \(\nabla_{\tau} f (0) = 0\) and \(D^2_{\tau \tau} f (0) \geqslant mI\) in counting coordinates. Then there are positive radii \(r_{\text{hor}} \leqslant r_{\text{zone}} \leqslant r_{\text{safe}} < r\), depending only on \((K_0, C_0, a_0, r, m)\), and a map \(g : \mathbb{R}^{\iota} \to \mathbb{R}^{\tau}\) with \(g (0) = 0\), such that whenever \(\|x\|_{\infty} < r_{\text{hor}}\):

  • \(g\) is analytic at \(x\);

  • \(\|g (x)\|_{\infty} \leqslant r_{\text{zone}} / 2\);

  • \(g (x)\) is the unique critical point of \(f (x, \cdot)\) in \(\{\|v\|_{\infty} \leqslant r_{\text{zone}} \}\).

(b) Application to the tested action. Fix \(G\), real constants \(\mu, C\), a patch order \(J\), kernel choices \(K\), and an integer \(n \geqslant 3\). There is a choice of auxiliary constants, made before \(L\), with the following property. For every clip \(p\), real \(\Lambda\), larger step \(S\) of order \(J\) with kernel choices \(K\), forest \(H\) and path datum \(W\) with bounds \((p, \mu, C, \Lambda)\), part (a) applies to the tested action in individual real colour components. It gives a critical branch \(g\) common to all tags: for each tag \(t\) and \(\|x\|_{\infty} < r_{\text{hor}}\), \(g (x)\) is the unique critical point of \(\mathcal{T}^H_t (x, \cdot)\) in the closed sup-ball of radius \(r_{\text{zone}}\). The radii depend on the auxiliary constants, \(p\) and \(L\), but not on \(c, f, H, \Lambda\) or \(W\) beyond its bounds. Coarse coordinates are located at their fine block roots when measuring decay. Status: theorem. Lean: (a) Receipt.throughout with hypotheses Receipt and radii reserveX. (b) Before.graph, Before.Service, pre_order.

Proposition III.6.8 (derivatives along the branch). Fix an integer \(N \geqslant 0\), the data and hypotheses of III.6.7(b), auxiliary constants chosen at order \(N + 3\), and any branch \(g\) satisfying its analytic, size and uniqueness conclusions. Then for every tag the following hold, with constants depending only on the corresponding bounds and radii.

  • (a) At every point of the closed joint sup-ball of radius \(r_{\text{safe}} \geqslant r_{\text{zone}}\), the vertical Hessian, as a matrix function of all variables, has a block package of order \(N\):

    • it is analytic and \(\geqslant m / 2\);

    • its entries and their derivatives up to order \(N + 1\) are localized;

    • for every principal sub-block and every shift \(t \geqslant 0\), the shifted sub-block is invertible;

    • the inverse of the shifted sub-block is exponentially localized with constant \(O ((1 + t)^{- 1})\), has localized derivatives up to order \(N + 1\), and has \(\ell^2\) norm \(\leqslant (m / 2 + t)^{- 1}\);

    • \(\log \det\) of the shifted sub-block is analytic and its derivatives of orders \(1, \ldots, N + 1\) are localized, with bounds proportional to \((1 + t)^{- 1}\).

  • (b) On the coarse sup-ball of radius \(r_{\text{hor}}\), the map \(g\) and the graph \(x \mapsto (x, g (x))\) have localized derivatives of orders \(1, \ldots, N + 2\).

  • (c) The classical action \(x \mapsto \mathcal{T}^H_t (x, g (x))\) has localized derivatives of orders \(1, \ldots, N + 3\).

  • (d) The Hessian along the branch, \(x \mapsto \text{Hess}_a \mathcal{T}^H_t (x, g (x))\), has a block package of order \(N\).

For a matrix function \(A (z)\), localization of its coefficients means a bound

\(\displaystyle | \partial_{q_1} \cdots \partial_{q_k} A_{ij} (z) | \leqslant C_k \exp \hspace{-0.17em} \left[ - a_k \left( \text{dist} (i, j) + \sum_{\nu} \text{dist} (i, q_{\nu}) \right) \right],\)

with coordinates replaced by their assigned sites. For positive-order derivatives of a map such as \(g\), omit \(j\); for a scalar function use the bound in III.6.7(a). No volume-independent bound on the value of the classical action or of \(\log \det\) is asserted.

Status: theorem under the hypotheses of III.6.7(b). Lean: Counter.service, Counter, BlockPack.

Proposition III.6.9 (one level of the iteration). Assume the following.

  • The schedule hypotheses of III.2: constants issued before \(L\), warm-up, and a paid schedule.

  • A horizon of beds \(M_{N, m}\) and towers with the prescribed strip and derivative bounds, floors on every torus, diagonal and stack identities, and identity diagonal at the cutoff (Suffices).

  • All beds are sworn at the periods used. The towers are harmonic: the zero-momentum coefficient of their 2-form component is independent of the fine residue within a block.

  • A gap quote, i.e. III.6.6 at every level, which the Lean supplies from Iteration.post.

  • A local chart ratio.

  • The fresh-data hypotheses. For each admissible input datum and each family of branches satisfying (i), these hypotheses supply the fresh cubic, quartic and loop atoms and their bounds, with the classical and thermal matchings each verified on a chosen forest and tag per period. These choices may differ between the two matchings. It also supplies the cutoff cubic and quartic atoms.

Let \(m < N\) and let \(T\) be a path datum funded at level \(m + 1\) (Work item). That is, the rows of \(T\) enumerate the root-kernel coefficients of \(M_{N, m + 1}\) using the fixed coordinate paths to each offset; \(T\) has the level-\((m + 1)\) bounds; and the sums of absolute cubic and quartic coefficients weighted by exponential path length are each at most the level cap. This row convention is stronger than attachment alone. Then there exist a path datum \(R\) funded at level \(m\), branches \(\rho_{p, F}\) for every period \(p\) and every forest \(F\), and a number \(\alpha\) such that:

  • (i) each \(\rho_{p, F}\) satisfies the conclusions of III.6.6 with the level-\((m + 1)\) gap, its tower map, and the bed \(M_{N, m}\);

  • (ii) \(| \alpha + \kappa_G \log L_m | + 1 \leqslant \text{drift}_{m + 1}\), where \(\kappa_G\) is the one-loop slope constant of \(G\);

  • (iii) for every \(p\) and \(F\), \(x \mapsto \mathcal{T}^F_k (x, \rho_{p, F} (x))\) (built with \(T\) and the clip of level \(m + 1\)) has the same jets of orders \(0, \ldots, 4\) at \(0\) as \(x \mapsto \mathcal{M}_q (R ; e^x)\) (clip \(q\) of level \(m\));

  • (iv) for every \(p\) and \(F\), the thermal function is analytic and the Hessian determinant is positive near \(0\). Moreover, for any two real plane waves \(w_1, w_2\), each the real part of a complex lattice plane wave whose coefficient in every axis and colour has modulus at most \(1\),

\(\displaystyle | D^2 \Theta^F_k (0) [w_1, w_2] - D^2 \left( \alpha \hspace{0.17em} \mathcal{M}^{(2)}_q (R) + \text{loop}_R \right) (0) [w_1, w_2] | \leqslant | \Lambda_c | \hspace{0.17em} \Gamma \hspace{0.17em} \lambda_{m + 1} \hspace{0.17em} e^{- \gamma c} .\)

Here \(\mathcal{M}^{(2)}_q (R ; x)\) denotes the symmetrized Wilson-plus-row-square term evaluated on \(e^x\):

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{M}^{(2)}_q (R ; x) = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|} \sum_{s \in \mathcal{S}} \sum_{\pi} \left[ c_q \hspace{0.17em} \text{price} ((s^{\ast} e^x)_{\pi}) + \tfrac{1}{2} | \ell_R (s^{\ast} e^x)_{\pi} |^2 \right] .\)

It includes all higher-order dependence on \(x\) in these terms; it is not their degree-two Taylor polynomial. The constants \(\Gamma, \gamma > 0\) are chosen after \(L\). All conclusions hold for every tag \(k\), and the same \(R, \alpha\) serve every period and forest.

A companion statement, Iteration.peak, gives a funded path datum at the cutoff level \(N\) whose main term has the jets of order \(\leqslant 4\) of the Wilson energy. 3. Strategy

At fixed coarse field, the renormalization step expands the fine-field integral around a stationary background. Gauge fixing removes the blockwise gauge directions. A positive vertical Hessian then gives an analytic branch, and the tower identities identify its tangent and the quadratic part of its classical value. The one-loop contribution is the Hessian determinant corrected by the Haar density and the loop atoms.

The construction uses the penalty \(\kappa \sum_b \text{price} (e^{x_b} Q_k (U)_b^{- 1})\) and fixes the fine field on a rooted forest inside each block. The shell comparison bounds the free links by curvature and predictor response. After linearization and summation, it gives a positive vertical Hessian with a lower bound independent of the torus volume. The analytic implicit function theorem produces the branch germ. A change of forest preserves both the stationary value and the thermal function: the squared Jacobian in the Hessian determinant cancels the Haar-density ratio.

For a branch on a ball independent of the volume, exponential decay of the inverse Hessian gives a uniform sup-norm bound. A Newton contraction produces the branch on that ball; differentiating the stationarity and resolvent identities controls its localized derivatives. Finally, the iteration combines inherited atoms with fresh classical and one-loop data supplied by the hypotheses of III.6.9. This is a penalized background-field construction in the spirit of Bałaban's method. It establishes local stationarity and quantitative control near the identity.

Lemma 4.1 (holomorphic extension of the main term). Let \(W\) have bounds \((p, \sigma, C, \Lambda)\) and let \(U\) be any configuration on a finite torus. The \(\ell_W\)-series and the sums of cubic and quartic atoms have holomorphic extensions near the matrix pair \((U, U^{\ast})\). Their summands are uniformly summable on the ambient ball of radius \(R_{\sigma} = e^{\sigma / 2} - 1\), using the maximum of the linkwise Frobenius norms. Sketch: Treat the link and inverse-link matrices as independent complex variables. A word of length \(l\) has norm at most \((1 + R_{\sigma})^l \|1\|\). Lemma 4.18 (Combes–Thomas bound). Let \(S\) be a real symmetric matrix on a finite index set assigned to sites of a four-dimensional torus, with multiplicity at most \(K_0\). Suppose \(a_0, M > 0\), \(C_0 \geqslant 0\), \(|S_{ij} | \leqslant C_0 e^{- a_0 \text{dist} (i, j)}\), and \(S \geqslant MI\). Then

\(\displaystyle | (S^{- 1})_{ij} | \leqslant \frac{2}{M} e^{- r_{\text{CT}} \text{dist} (i, j)}, \qquad r_{\text{CT}} = \min \left( \frac{a_0}{4}, \frac{M}{2 (1 + 4 C_0 K_0 c (a_0 / 2) / a_0)} \right) .\)

Here the site labels are understood in the distances, and

\(\displaystyle c (\alpha) = \frac{16 \cdot 6! \hspace{0.17em} e^{2 \alpha}}{\alpha^6}, \qquad \sum_y e^{- \alpha \text{dist} (x, y)} \leqslant c (\alpha) \quad (\alpha > 0),\)

uniformly over the torus side. Sketch: Conjugate \(S\) by the diagonal weight \(e^{r_{\text{CT}} \text{dist} (\cdot, j)}\). The difference has both absolute row and column sums at most \(M / 2\). Its quadratic form is therefore bounded by \((M / 2) \|v\|_2^2\), so the conjugated matrix retains coercivity \(M / 2\), although it need not be symmetric. Applying this inequality to the tilted \(j\)-th inverse column gives the displayed entry bound.

5. Where the difficulty lies

The main estimate is the volume-independent lower bound on the vertical Hessian. Curvature alone leaves gauge and flat directions; the forest condition and predictor penalty together control every free link. The denominator of the gap contains a term proportional to \(C_{\text{E02}}^2 L^4 |X|^2\), and \(|X| = (2 (J + 7) L + 1)^4\). The gap is chosen after \(L\); no lower bound uniform in \(L\) is asserted.

A spectral gap gives an \(\ell^2\) inverse bound. To obtain a sup-norm radius independent of the volume, one also needs the exponential entry bounds and their summability. The uniform construction uses a direct shell estimate and the positive Wilson coefficient, so it needs no bed or attachment hypothesis. It reuses the linearization and counting estimates of F01, while supplying its own gap and radii.

Forest independence requires both the stationary change-of-coordinates formula for the Hessian and preservation of product Haar measure. Their Jacobians cancel exactly, including the Haar normalization scales. The proof needs only the gap for the target forest. Other unused hypotheses are the coarse floor in F04, the identity base value in F02, and the size clauses of “larger” in F03–F05.

The conclusions concern a branch near the identity and uniqueness in a specified sup-ball. Global constrained minimization is not asserted. The one-loop coefficient and the classical remainders remain inputs to the iteration through the fresh-data hypotheses in III.6.9.
