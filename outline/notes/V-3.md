## 1. What this unit proves

**Setting.** Fix a Wilson family, an extraction and the limit table $S=S^{\rm mix}$, together with a record [`Final`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/Spec/Closed.lean#L64-L73) for them. The unit uses the following parts of it.

- The Euclidean axioms of $S^{\rm mix}$ (IV.1).
- The phase decomposition of V.1:
  - a compact metric space $X$ with a Borel probability measure $\nu$ of full support;
  - the commutative C$^*$-algebra $\mathfrak A$ generated by the time-slab operators of $S^{\rm mix}$, compressed to the ground space $\mathcal D$ of the Osterwalder–Schrader (OS) space $\mathcal H^{\rm OS}$ of $S^{\rm mix}$;
  - continuous characters $\chi_\xi$ of $\mathfrak A$, indexed bijectively by $X$, with $\xi\mapsto\chi_\xi(M)$ continuous for every $M\in\mathfrak A$ and $\int_X\chi_\xi(M)\,d\nu=\langle\omega,M\omega\rangle$;
  - spatial translations acting trivially on $\mathcal D$;
  - tables $S^\xi$ satisfying the Euclidean axioms, with $S^\xi(w)=\chi_\xi(M_w)$ for every word $w$ of pairwise distinct points. Here $M_w\in\mathfrak A$ is the compressed slab operator of a time translate of $w$.
- The relativistic theory $\mathfrak W_{\xi_*}$ of V.2 at the selected phase $\xi_*$, and the analytic continuations of $S^{\rm mix}$ and $S^{\xi_*}$.
- The field algebra of VI.1 (derivatives, rotations, the Euclidean stress tensor $T^{\rm E}_{ab}\in F_4$), and the Euclidean Ward identities in every phase.

**Notation.**

- **OS side.** For each $\xi$, $\mathcal H^{\rm OS}_\xi$ is an OS space of $S^\xi$ for the time axis. It carries the transfer semigroup $e^{-sH^{\rm OS}_\xi}$ and the Euclidean vacuum $\omega_\xi$. Its ground space is $\mathcal D_\xi=\{v:\ e^{-sH^{\rm OS}_\xi}v=v\ \text{for all } s\ge0\}$.
- **Wightman theory.** $\mathfrak W_\xi=(\mathcal H_\xi,\mathfrak D_\xi,\Omega_\xi,\phi_\xi,U_\xi,P_\xi)$ consists of:
  - a Hilbert space $\mathcal H_\xi$ and a dense common invariant domain $\mathfrak D_\xi$ spanned by word vectors;
  - a unit vacuum $\Omega_\xi$;
  - fields $\phi_\xi(a,f)$ for labels $a\in\mathbb F$ (the common label space generated by the $F_d$) and $f\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R^4)$;
  - a strongly continuous unitary Poincaré representation $U_\xi$ and an energy–momentum spectral measure $P_\xi$.
- **Time and energy.** Time is the fourth coordinate. $H_\xi$ generates the time translations, and $P^0_\xi=P_\xi(\{p^4=0\})$.
- **Words.** A *finite sum of words* is $A=\sum_kc_k\,\phi(a_{k1},f_{k1})\cdots\phi(a_{kn_k},f_{kn_k})$. It is *compactly supported* if every $f_{kj}\in C^\infty_c(\mathbb R^4)$. $A^\dagger$ is its formal adjoint (conjugate coefficients and tests, apply the star operation to labels, and reverse each word). The spectral measure of $A\Omega_\xi$ is $\mu^\xi_A(B)=\langle A\Omega_\xi,P_\xi(B)A\Omega_\xi\rangle$.
- **Continuations.** $\mathsf F^\xi_n(a;z,\vec x)$ is the analytic continuation of $S^\xi$ to complex times with $\mathrm{Im}z_1<\dots<\mathrm{Im}z_n$. $W^\xi_n(a;g)$, for $g\in\mathcal S((\mathbb R^4)^n)$, are its boundary distributions. $W^{\rm mix}_n$ are those of the continuation of $S^{\rm mix}$ (V.2).

**Proposition V.3.1 (reconstruction in every phase).** There is a family indexed by $X$ with the following data for each $\xi\in X$:

- an OS space $\mathcal H^{\rm OS}_\xi$ of $S^\xi$ with its time-slab operators;
- a continuation $\mathsf F^\xi$ of $S^\xi$ with boundary distributions $W^\xi$;
- a Wightman theory $\mathfrak W_\xi$ on the label space $\mathbb F$, with the same star operation and the same Poincaré action on labels as $\mathfrak W_{\xi_*}$;
- an isometry $J_\xi:\mathcal H_\xi\to\mathcal H^{\rm OS}_\xi$ with $J_\xi\Omega_\xi=\omega_\xi$. It intertwines the space–time translations and sends word vectors to the boundary values of the corresponding Euclidean vectors at imaginary times (Lemma V.3.b).

At $\xi=\xi_*$ these are the data of V.1 and V.2. For **every** $\xi\in X$, with no exceptional set, the following hold.

1. *Wightman axioms.*
   - The vacuum expectation values of products of fields are $W^\xi$.
   - The Hilbert-space adjoint of $\phi_\xi(a,f)$ extends $\phi_\xi(a^*,\bar f)$.
   - Fields with spacelike separated supports commute on $\mathfrak D_\xi$.
   - $U_\xi$ implements Poincaré covariance.
   - The joint spectrum of the translations lies in the closed forward cone $\overline V_+$.
   - For every nonzero label $a$, there is a test function $f$ for which $\phi_\xi(a,f)\ne0$ on $\mathfrak D_\xi$.
   - Every translation-invariant vector is a multiple of $\Omega_\xi$.
2. *Surjectivity.* $J_\xi$ is unitary.
3. *Euclidean vacuum.* $\mathcal D_\xi=\mathbb C\omega_\xi$.
4. *Zero energy.* For every $x\in\mathcal H_\xi$, $P^0_\xi x=x$ if and only if $x\in\mathbb C\Omega_\xi$.
5. *Low-energy bound.* For every compactly supported finite sum of words $A$ there is $M\ge0$ such that $\mu^\xi_A(\{0<p^4\le u\})\le Mu^2$ for all $\xi\in X$ and $u\in(0,1]$.
6. *Heat decay.* For every such $A$ there is $M\ge0$ such that, for all $\xi\in X$ and $s\ge1$,
$$0\le\big\langle A\Omega_\xi,(e^{-sH_\xi}-P^0_\xi)A\Omega_\xi\big\rangle\le Ms^{-2}.$$

7. *Irreducibility.* For a bounded complex-linear operator $B$ on $\mathcal H_\xi$, the following are equivalent: $B\in\mathbb C\,1$; and, for every $a$, $f$ and $u,v\in\mathfrak D_\xi$, one has $u\in\mathrm{dom}\,\phi_\xi(a,f)^*$ and $\langle\phi_\xi(a,f)^*u,Bv\rangle=\langle u,B\phi_\xi(a,f)v\rangle$.
8. *Stress tensor.* The conclusions (T0)–(T6) of Proposition V.3.y hold for $\mathfrak W_\xi$.
9. *Dependence on the phase.*
   - For all $n$, $a\in\mathbb F^n$ and $g\in\mathcal S((\mathbb R^4)^n)$, the map $\xi\mapsto W^\xi_n(a;g)$ is continuous and $\int_XW^\xi_n(a;g)\,d\nu(\xi)=W^{\rm mix}_n(a;g)$.
   - For every word $w$ of pairwise distinct points, $\int_XS^\xi(w)\,d\nu(\xi)=S^{\rm mix}(w)$.

In item 6, $e^{-sH_\xi}$ is defined as $J_\xi^*e^{-sH^{\rm OS}_\xi}J_\xi$. Since $J_\xi$ intertwines the spectral projections (V.3.b), this equals $\int e^{-sp^4}\,dP_\xi(p)$. The projection subtracted in item 6 is the full zero-energy projection. Items 5 and 6 are therefore proved before item 4 and independently of it.

## 3. Strategy

V.2 reconstructs a relativistic theory at a selected phase $\xi_*$. The same construction applies to every $S^\xi$ before vacuum uniqueness is known. The norm of each phase’s slab operator is bounded by the corresponding norm for $S^{\rm mix}$ (V.3.a): repeated reflection gives a power bound, and the fixed norm of the character disappears on taking roots. This gives polynomial continuation bounds uniform in $\xi$. Character continuity at imaginary times propagates to complex times by Vitali’s theorem, then to boundary distributions by a Taylor formula with an integrable remainder. Integration over phases commutes with both passages (V.3.d–V.3.h).

Vacuum uniqueness holds almost everywhere by the phase decomposition (V.3.c). To extend it to every phase, fix a compactly supported finite sum of words $A$ and set
$$r_\xi(s)=\langle A\Omega_\xi,e^{-sH_\xi}A\Omega_\xi\rangle-|\langle\Omega_\xi,A\Omega_\xi\rangle|^2.$$
Locality makes the vacuum commutator vanish at large spatial separation for $|t|\le1$. Its time-averaged odd part is the spatial Fourier transform of a positive measure, weighted by $(1-\cos E)/E$. A momentum bump of radius $u$ bounds its shell mass by $O(u^2)$: the bump contributes $u^3$, while the weight contributes $u$. Dyadic summation and a Laplace transform give decay $Ms^{-2}$, uniformly in $\xi$, after subtracting the full zero-energy projection (V.3.k–V.3.o). Almost everywhere this is the vacuum projection. Since $r_\xi(s)$ is continuous, the same estimate holds everywhere by full support of $\nu$. Letting $s\to\infty$ identifies the zero-energy projection with the vacuum projection at every phase (V.3.q–V.3.r). This needs no continuity of the zero-energy projection itself.

Separately, positive-height Euclidean vectors are images of Wightman vectors with exponentially weighted frequency tests, so $J_\xi$ is onto (V.3.i–V.3.j). Vacuum uniqueness therefore holds on both sides. A Liouville argument gives irreducibility. Finally, continuation of the Euclidean Ward identities gives the local stress-charge commutator (V.3.u–V.3.y). The removal of the exceptional set uses uniform low-energy control from locality, rather than a mass gap.

### 4.1 Slab bounds and the family of reconstructions

**Lemma V.3.a (slab operators of a phase are dominated).** Fix $\xi\in X$. Let $\mathcal K$ be any OS space of $S^\xi$ for the time axis, and $\Pi_\xi$ any family of slab operators on it. Then for every thickness $d>0$ and every word $w$ of pairwise distinct points in the slab $(0,d)$,
$$\|\Pi_\xi(d,w)\|\le\|\Pi(d,w)\|,$$
where $\Pi$ are the slab operators of $S^{\rm mix}$ on $\mathcal H^{\rm OS}$. Here $\Pi(d,w)$ maps the OS vector of a positive-time word $v$ to that of $w$ followed by $v$ translated by $d$.

*Sketch.*

1. Let $u=\Pi(d,w)$ and $D>\|u\|$. For a positive-time word $v$, let $X(v)$ be the slab operator of $v$ restricted to the ground space $\mathcal D$.
2. The compression of the doubled word $\theta v\,v$ is $X(v)^*X(v)\in\mathfrak A$. Let $r_n$ be $v$ with $n$ reflected pairs of copies of $w$ inserted. Then $X(r_n)=(u^*u)^nX(v)$.
3. Hence the diagonal Gram entry of $r_n$ in $S^\xi$ satisfies
$$|S^\xi(\theta r_n\,r_n)|=|\chi_\xi(X(r_n)^*X(r_n))|\le\|\chi_\xi\|\,\|X(v)\|^2D^{4n}.$$

4. The power argument (iterated Cauchy–Schwarz on the span of OS vectors) turns this into an operator on $\mathcal K$ of norm at most $D$ that inserts $w$. The fixed factor $\|\chi_\xi\|\,\|X(v)\|^2$ disappears in the limit.
5. Slab operators are determined by their action on OS vectors, so this operator is $\Pi_\xi(d,w)$. Finally let $D\downarrow\|u\|$.

### 4.3 Surjectivity of the boundary isometry

**Lemma V.3.i (Euclidean vectors at positive heights are in the range).** Fix $n\ge1$, $\xi\in X$, labels $a\in\mathbb F^n$, heights $0<y_1<\dots<y_n$ and $p\in(0,1)$ with $4p<y_1$ and $4p<y_{j+1}-y_j$ for $1\le j<n$.

- Let $B_{\xi,p}(a)$ be the centered one-point slab operator of thickness $2p$. For $\mathrm{Im}z>\ell$ put
$$R_{\xi,\ell}(z,\vec x)=e^{-(\mathrm{Im}z-\ell)H^{\rm OS}_\xi}e^{i\mathrm{Re}zH^{\rm OS}_\xi}U^{\rm OS}_\xi(\vec x).$$

For a real configuration $q$, write $z_j=q_j^4+iy_j$ and define
$$\Psi^\xi_p(a;q+iy)=R_{\xi,p}(z_1,\vec q_1)B_{\xi,p}(a_1)\left(\prod_{j=2}^n R_{\xi,2p}(z_j-z_{j-1},\vec q_j-\vec q_{j-1})B_{\xi,p}(a_j)\right)\omega_\xi,$$
where the factors are ordered by increasing $j$ from left to right; the product is empty when $n=1$.

- Let $h\in C^\infty_c((\mathbb R^4)^n)$ and use the convention $g(q)=\widehat h(q)=\int e^{-i\sum_j p_j\cdot q_j}h(p)\,dp$. Set $g_y=\widehat{e^{-\sum_jy_jp_j^4}h}$. Then
$$\int g(q)\,\Psi^\xi_p(a;q+iy)\,dq=J_\xi\,\Phi_\xi(a;g_y).$$
Here $\Phi_\xi(a;\cdot)$ is the joint-test extension from V.3.b. Compact support of $h$ ensures that the exponential multiplier preserves the Schwartz class.

- Consequently every $\Psi^\xi_p(a;q+iy)$ lies in $\mathrm{ran}\,J_\xi$.

*Sketch.*

1. Call the two sides $v$ and $J_\xi\Phi$. Their squared norms and their inner product, $\|v\|^2$, $\langle v,J_\xi\Phi\rangle$ and $\|J_\xi\Phi\|^2$, are each expressed through partial boundary values of $W^\xi$ at heights $y$.
2. On tests of compact frequency support these partial boundary values equal $W^\xi$ tested against the damped test functions, so the three numbers coincide and $\|v-J_\xi\Phi\|=0$.
3. Density of compact-frequency tests and continuity in $g$ extend the membership in $\mathrm{ran}\,J_\xi$ to all Schwartz $g$.
4. Testing against all $g$, and using continuity in $q$, gives the pointwise statement. The range is closed since $J_\xi$ is an isometry.

### 4.4 Low-energy bound and heat decay

**Lemma V.3.k (time-averaged commutator).** Let $\rho,\sigma$ be finite positive measures on $\mathbb R^4$ supported in $\overline V_+$, with $\rho(\mathbb R^4)+\sigma(\mathbb R^4)\le M$. Put
$$\hat\rho(\vec x,t)=\int e^{i(\vec p\cdot\vec x+p^4t)}\,d\rho(p),\qquad a=\hat\rho(\cdot)-\hat\sigma(-\,\cdot),\qquad D(\vec x)=\int_0^1\frac{a(\vec x,t)-a(\vec x,-t)}{2i}\,dt .$$
Then $|D|\le M$. Write $\mathcal F f(x)=\int e^{-i\vec p\cdot x}f(\vec p)\,d\vec p$, so $c_3=(2\pi)^3$. If moreover $D(\vec x)=0$ for $|\vec x|>R$, then for $0<u\le1$
$$\rho(\{u/2<p^4\le u\})\le c(R,M)\,u^2,\qquad c(R,M)=16\pi\,|B_1|\,|B_R|\,M/c_3 .$$

*Sketch.*

1. By Fubini, $D$ is the spatial Fourier transform of $\rho^\sharp+\sigma^\sharp$. Here $\rho^\sharp$ is the image under $p\mapsto\vec p$ of $\pi(p^4)\,d\rho(p)$, with $\pi(E)=\int_0^1\sin(Et)\,dt$, equal to $(1-\cos E)/E$ for $E>0$ and to $0$ at $E=0$, and $\sigma^\sharp$ is the analogous image of $\sigma$ under $p\mapsto-\vec p$.
2. On $\overline V_+$ one has $\pi(p^4)\ge0$ at all energies, and $\pi(E)\ge E/\pi$ for $0\le E\le1$.
3. Test $D$ against $\mathcal F f$, where $f$ is a smooth spatial-momentum bump with $0\le f\le1$, equal to $1$ on the ball of radius $u$ and supported in the ball of radius $2u$. Then $|\mathcal F f|\le|B_{2u}|=8|B_1|u^3$. Since $D$ vanishes outside $B_R$, the pairing is at most $8|B_1||B_R|M\,u^3$.
4. The pairing equals $c_3\big(\int f\,d\rho^\sharp+\int f\,d\sigma^\sharp\big)$, which is at least $c_3\,\tfrac{u}{2\pi}\,\rho(\{u/2<p^4\le u\})$. On that shell $|\vec p|\le p^4\le u$, so $f=1$ there, and $\pi(p^4)\ge u/(2\pi)$.
5. Thus the exponent $2$ is $3$ (spatial dimension, from the volume of the momentum ball) minus $1$ (first-order vanishing of the weight).

### 4.6 Irreducibility

**Proposition V.3.t (trivial weak commutant).** Item 7 of V.3.1.

*Sketch.* The weak commutant is first rewritten with $\phi_\xi(a^*,\bar f)$ in place of the Hilbert adjoint, since the former is the restriction of the latter to $\mathfrak D_\xi$. The converse direction is the adjoint relation. Now let $B$ be weakly central and write $\Omega=\Omega_\xi$.

1. For word vectors $u=\phi(w)\Omega$ and $v=\phi(w^\dagger)\Omega$, and $\mathrm{Im}z\ge0$, set
$$p(z)=\langle J_\xi u,e^{izH^{\rm OS}_\xi}J_\xi B\Omega\rangle,\qquad q(z)=\langle J_\xi B^*\Omega,e^{izH^{\rm OS}_\xi}J_\xi v\rangle .$$
Both are bounded, continuous, and holomorphic for $\mathrm{Im}z>0$. On the real axis $p(t)=\langle u,U_\xi(t)B\Omega\rangle$, and at $z=is$ one has $p(is)=\langle u,e^{-sH_\xi}B\Omega\rangle$.

2. Covariance and weak centrality give $p(t)=q(-t)$ for real $t$.
3. Gluing $p$ on the upper half-plane to $q(-\,\cdot)$ on the lower one gives a bounded continuous function, holomorphic off $\mathbb R$. It is entire by Morera's theorem and constant by Liouville's theorem.
4. Hence $\langle u,e^{-sH_\xi}B\Omega\rangle=\langle u,B\Omega\rangle$ for all $s\ge0$. Letting $s\to\infty$ and using V.3.2 gives $B\Omega=a\Omega$ with $a=\langle\Omega,B\Omega\rangle$.
5. Weak centrality then gives $\langle\phi(c)\Omega,B\phi(b)\Omega\rangle=\langle\phi(b^\dagger)\phi(c)\Omega,B\Omega\rangle=a\langle\phi(c)\Omega,\phi(b)\Omega\rangle$ for all words, so $B=a\,1$.

The proof uses V.3.2 and the reconstruction data of V.3.b. It requires neither surjectivity of $J_\xi$ nor invariance of $\mathfrak D_\xi$ under $B$.

## 5. Where the difficulty lies

The delicate points are the common bounds near the analytic boundary, the recovery of positive-height Euclidean vectors from joint frequency tests, and the joint current limits in the stress-tensor argument. The last passage uses equal continuation germs only at spacelike separation; the current itself is spatially integrated. It gives a charge commutator for every admissible time profile, without defining a stress field at a fixed real time.

The low-energy estimate needs only locality, the spectrum condition and the two vector-norm bounds. Its positive weight avoids differentiating spectral integrals or imposing generator-domain bounds. The exponent $2$ is compatible with the massless scalar measure, since $\int_{|\vec p|\le u}d^3p/|\vec p|\sim u^2$. Thus this estimate does not imply a mass gap. For removal of the exceptional set, any decay rate tending to zero uniformly in $\xi$ would suffice.

The remaining integrations, dyadic summation and assembly are routine once their uniform bounds have been established.

*What looks unusual.*

- Item 8 quantifies over all admissible time profiles and spatial cutoffs. Admissible profiles and cutoffs exist ([`Stress.accepts`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/EveryPhase/Charge.lean#L24-L35), [`Stress.reservable`](https://github.com/yang-mills-group/OpenAI-YM-20261028/blob/3456e5e75cf8d4cd558955a5c7638fba2d68f464/YM/EveryPhase/Charge.lean#L42-L59)); this existence assertion is separate from the commutator conclusion. The main theorem does not use it or the auxiliary results of Remark V.3.3.
- The reconstruction agrees with V.2 at $\xi_*$. The subsequent proof of vacuum uniqueness applies to every phase.
- The Euclidean half of item 9 restates V.1, and the public clause (R12) is derived from it.
- Nothing here or in the main theorem decides whether $X$ is a single point. Each $\mathfrak W_\xi$ is irreducible, while $S^{\rm mix}$ is the $\nu$-mixture of the $S^\xi$.
