Overview. The unit has three independent parts, plus some record types.

  • (A) Pair-step pricing (DIRECTED_RECIPE). Two numerical facts used by the RG step of III.9 and III.10. A hard family of polymer activities is merged with the Mayer expansion of the exponential of soft local terms. The merged family keeps a weighted polymer bound and carries an explicit Gaussian large-field factor (III.15.1–2).

  • (B) Derivative bounds for local observables (SCHEDULE/Wages). On the small-field region, a transported curvature slot has left derivatives of every order \(\leqslant K\) bounded by a constant times \(t\). A \(k\)-leg monomial in slots has them bounded by a constant times \(t^k\). Here \(t\) is the small-field threshold (III.15.3–4).

  • (C) Launch data for the remainder comparison (RemainderComparison). For connected-activity targets with rates below \(- \tfrac{3}{8}\), a sufficiently large block side permits the numerical conditions of a comparison step. After the auxiliary data are fixed, one chooses the comparison functions and then an entry coupling above which those conditions hold (III.15.5–7).

  • (D) Records and numerical definitions. These include the construction-run bounds, the additional-expense and readout records, and the RG05 readout prices. Their scope is described in §4.6.

Conventions.

  • \(x\) denotes the coarse renormalized inverse coupling \(\hat{\beta}\).

  • \(\beta_F (x, L) = x + b_G \log L\) is the inverse coupling at the next finer scale (YM.Pressure.betaF); \(b_G\) is the slope constant of \(G\), positive when \(G\) is simple.

For \(\beta > 0\) and \(x > 1\), use the scale functions

\(\displaystyle g (\beta) = \beta^{- 1 / 2}, \qquad p (x) = e^{(\log \log x)^2}, \qquad t (x) = g (x) \hspace{0.17em} p (x), \qquad \mathcal{B} (x) = \lceil (\log x)^2 \rceil .\)

  • \(t\) is the small-field threshold (YM.Pressure.temp, equal to YM.Next.Scheduling.Instrument.t for the plain instrument).

  • \(\mathcal{B}\) is the cluster radius (YM.Pressure.radiusB, the same formula as YM.Next.Scheduling.Clock.cover).

  • The band of \(y\) is \([y / 4, 4 y]\) (YM.Next.Scheduling.inBand). On it, \(g (y) / 2 \leqslant g (\beta) \leqslant 2 g (y)\) (YM.Next.Scheduling.band_gap).

Growth classes, at fixed \(L\), as \(x \to \infty\):

  • Rate \(\leqslant a\) (YM.ConnectedActivityE6.Rate): for every \(v > a\), eventually \(|w (x) | \leqslant \beta_F (x, L)^v\).

  • Reserve (YM.RemainderComparison.Reserve): \(|w (x) | \leqslant M (x)^k\) eventually for some \(k \in \mathbb{N}\), where \(M (x) = p (\beta_F (x, L))\) (YM.Push.AbsoluteSums.Delay.fineMonitor). Every reserve has rate at most \(0\).

  • Gear \(a\) (YM.RemainderComparison.Gear): \(\beta_F^{- a} w\) is a reserve, equivalently \(|w (x) | \leqslant \beta_F (x, L)^a M (x)^k\) eventually for some \(k \in \mathbb{N}\). This implies rate at most \(a\).

Weighted hit bound (YM.Ac.Hit): for supports \(X_i\), weights \(f_i \geqslant 0\), scale \(M\) and exponent \(a\), write \(\text{Hit}_M (f ; a) \leqslant \gamma\) if

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i : \hspace{0.17em} z \in X_i} f_i \hspace{0.17em} e^{aN_M (X_i)} \leqslant \gamma \quad \text{for every site } z,\)

where \(N_M (X)\) is the minimal number of \(M\)-balls with connected union covering \(X\).

The quote is \(\text{quote} (\kappa, \gamma) = e^{- \kappa} (e^{\gamma} - 1)\) (YM.Bid.quote).

In the code, a price or bill is a numerical bound; “financed” means that the bound satisfies the specified growth conditions.

Part A: pair-step pricing

Proposition III.15.1 (pair-step rates). Let \(\rho > 0\), \(C \geqslant 0\), \(r > \rho\), \(d \in \mathbb{N}\), \(e \in \mathbb{R}\) and \(q > 0\). For \(y > 1\) and \(\beta > 0\) put

\(\displaystyle s (y, \beta) = \frac{t (y)}{g (\beta)} = p (y) \sqrt{\beta / y}, \quad \text{mild} (y) = C (1 +\mathcal{B}(y))^d p (y)^e g (y)^q,\)
\(\displaystyle \text{strong} (y, \beta) = Ce^{- rs^2}, \quad \kappa (y, \beta) = \rho s^2 .\)

For all sufficiently large \(y > 1\), the following holds for every \(\beta \in [y / 4, 4 y]\) and every integer \(0 \leqslant B \leqslant \mathcal{B} (y)\). With \(a = \text{strong} (y, \beta)\), \(b = \text{mild} (y)\) and \(\gamma = e^{\kappa} a + 2 b\):

\(\displaystyle \kappa \geqslant 0, \quad a \geqslant 0, \quad 0 \leqslant b \leqslant 1, \quad (2 B + 1)^4 \gamma \leqslant 1, \quad \text{quote} (\kappa, \gamma) \leqslant e^{- \kappa} .\)

These are the rate conditions with target \(w = e^{- \kappa}\). The threshold in \(y\) depends only on \((\rho, C, r, d, e, q)\). Status: theorem. Proposition III.15.2 (merging a hard and a soft family). Fix a parameter point \(P\) with derivative order \(K \geqslant 6\), direction power \(P_0 \geqslant 2\), cluster scale \(B_P \geqslant 1\), support exponent \(\sigma > 0\) and threshold \(0 < t_P \leqslant 1\). Consider a countably indexed regular family \(E_i\), a hard family of forced activities \(F_i\) with supports \(X_i\), and a soft family of real local exponential corrections \(\ell_i\) with nonempty supports \(X'_i\). Assume:

  1. The regular terms satisfy the regular-family conditions at \(P\): regularity on their supports, vanishing at the flat field, and vanishing affine Hessian on nonwrapping supports. The forced terms and their eligibility tests are gauge invariant, measurable and determined by the links and final flags on their supports; a nonzero forced term satisfies its eligibility test. The regular and forced families admit the prescribed translation/reflection permutations, including the support and inventory transformations. Each \(\ell_i\) is gauge invariant, measurable and determined by the links and final flags on \(X'_i\); it vanishes on configurations small-field on \(X'_i\). The soft family is also covariant under translations and reflections.

  2. The regular family has bound \(\delta\): it admits nonnegative bounds on all left derivatives through order \(K\) on its small-field supports, in the directions specified by \(P\), whose weighted sum at each anchor is at most \(\delta\).

  3. For every \(i\) and every configuration \(U\), \(|F_i (U) | \leqslant p_i\) and \(| \ell_i (U) | \leqslant q_i\), with \(p_i, q_i \geqslant 0\).

  4. \(\text{Hit}_{B_P} (p ; \sigma + 1) \leqslant a\) on \((X_i)\) and \(\text{Hit}_{B_P} (q ; \sigma + 1) \leqslant b\) on \((X'_i)\).

  5. The rate inequalities of III.15.1 hold with \(B = B_P\), with the final inequality replaced by \(\text{quote} (\kappa, e^{\kappa} a + 2 b) \leqslant w\).

Then the fusion input condition with \((\delta, w)\) holds. Explicitly:

  • the original regular and forced families retain bounds \(\delta\) and \(a\), respectively; the soft corrections admit a summable uniform majorant;

  • the merged family \((F_i) \sqcup (e^{\ell_i} - 1)\), with weights \(e^{\kappa} p_i\) and \(2 q_i\), satisfies \(|F_i | \leqslant e^{- \kappa} \cdot e^{\kappa} p_i\) and \(|e^{\ell_i} - 1| \leqslant 2 q_i\);

  • that family has \(\text{Hit}_{B_P} (\cdot \hspace{0.17em} ; \sigma + 1) \leqslant \gamma := e^{\kappa} a + 2 b\), with \((2 B_P + 1)^4 \gamma \leqslant 1\);

  • \(\text{quote} (\kappa, \gamma) \leqslant w\).

Status: theorem. The only analytic input is \(|e^z - 1| \leqslant 2 |z|\) for \(|z| \leqslant 1\). Through the fusion lemmas of III.10 (Work.H01–H03, used in YM.Progress.DirectedRecipe.Ingredients.gates), the conclusion gives the fused polymer family with norm bound \(\leqslant w\). With \(w = e^{- \kappa}\) from III.15.1, this is the Gaussian large-field factor \(\exp (- \rho \hspace{0.17em} (\beta / y) \hspace{0.17em} p (y)^2)\). Part B: derivative bounds for local observables

A slot \(s\) is a lattice walk \(\omega\) from the origin, followed by a plaquette \(\pi\) (two axes) at the endpoint of \(\omega\) (YM.RG.Slot). Its length is \(|s| = | \omega | + 4\), and its value at a site \(o\) is the transported curvature

\(\displaystyle s (U, o) = \text{Ad} (U_{\omega}) \hspace{0.17em} \mathcal{C} (U_{\pi}) \in \mathfrak{g},\)

where \(\mathcal{C}\) is the colour projection of the anti-Hermitian part.

A \(k\)-leg atom \(a\) has slots \(s_1, \ldots, s_k\) and a real coefficient tensor \(c_b\) indexed by colour multi-indices \(b \in \{1, \ldots, \dim \mathfrak{g}\}^k\) (YM.RG.Atom). Its value is \(a (U, o) = \sum_b c_b \prod_i s_i (U, o)^{b_i}\), its size is \(|a| = \sum_b |c_b |\), and its length is \(\text{len} (a) = \sum_i |s_i |\).

Fix a parameter point with derivative order \(K \geqslant 6\), power \(P \geqslant 2\) and threshold \(t \in (0, 1]\). Directions are colour-valued increments \(A\) of the left derivative \(\partial_A\). A direction is admissible at \(o\) if

\(\displaystyle \|A_e \| \leqslant t \hspace{0.17em} (1 + \text{dist} (e, o))^P \quad \text{for every link } e.\)

Here \(\text{dist} (e, o)\) is the periodic maximum distance from the basepoint of \(e\) to \(o\) (YM.RG.BudgetPoint.dirBound).

A configuration is small-field on \(X\) (healthy, YM.RG.Healthy) if every plaquette touching a site of \(X\) has chord distance \(\leqslant t\) from the identity. Let \(\kappa_G > 0\) be the colour scale of \(G\) and \(\|1\|\) the Frobenius norm of the identity matrix.

Proposition III.15.3 (slot derivative bound). Let \(\ell \in \mathbb{N}\) and \(s\) be a slot with \(|s| \leqslant \ell\), let \(S\) be a finite set of points of \(\mathbb{Z}^4\) containing the stencil of \(s\), and let \(o\) be a site. Then \(U \mapsto s (U, o)\) is \(K\) times left-differentiable. Let \(U\) be small-field on \(o + S\) (wrapped on the torus), and let \(A_1, \ldots, A_m\) be admissible directions at \(o\) with \(m \leqslant K\). Then

\(\displaystyle | \partial_{A_1} \cdots \partial_{A_m} s (U, o) | \leqslant \text{slotCost} (P, K, \ell) \hspace{0.17em} t,\)
\(\displaystyle \text{slotCost} (P, K, \ell) = (1 + \kappa_G^{- 1} \|1\|) \hspace{0.17em} \text{margin} (P, \ell)^K, \qquad \text{margin} (P, \ell) = 2 (\kappa_G + 1) \hspace{0.17em} 2^P (\ell + 1)^{P + 1} .\)

The constant depends on \(P, K, \ell, G\) only, not on \(t\), \(o\) or the volume. At \(m = 0\) the stronger order-zero bound is \(\|s (U, o)\| \leqslant t\). Status: theorem. Used in: III.9 (YM.AnalyticBridge.VerifiedReset.Low.plain_bill) and III.15.4.

Proposition III.15.4 (bound for \(k\)-leg atoms). Let \(k \in \mathbb{N}\), let \(a\) be a \(k\)-leg atom, \(o\) a site, and \(U\) small-field on the footprint \(o + \text{stencil} (a)\). Then \(U \mapsto a (U, o)\) is \(K\) times left-differentiable, and every left derivative of order \(m \leqslant K\) in admissible directions at \(o\) is bounded by

\(\displaystyle \text{price} (a) = |a| \hspace{0.17em} 2^K \left( 2^K \hspace{0.17em} \text{slotCost} (P, K, \text{len} (a)) \hspace{0.17em} t \right)^k = \text{start} (G, k, P, K) \hspace{0.27em} t^k \hspace{0.27em} |a| \hspace{0.27em} (\text{len} (a) + 1)^{(P + 1) Kk},\)

where \(\text{start} (G, k, P, K) = 2^K (2^K \text{slotCost} (P, K, 0))^k\). Status: theorem. Lean: YM.Next.Scheduling.Wages.fin, YM.Next.Scheduling.Wages.price, YM.Next.Scheduling.Wages.scan, YM.Next.Scheduling.Wages.startup. Used in: III.9 (YM.AnalyticBridge.VerifiedReset.High.paid) and III.10.

Part C: launch data for the remainder comparison

A connected-activity target \(Q\) (YM.ConnectedActivityE6.Target, III.10) carries:

  • a rate \(\rho_Q \in (- \tfrac{1}{2}, 0)\);

  • an exponent \(\text{e}_Q \geqslant 0\);

  • small parameters \(\varepsilon_Q, \delta_Q > 0\) with \(4 \varepsilon_Q\) below the top support exponent;

  • a halo \(h_Q \geqslant 500\);

  • a chart table with its local-ratio property. The target also requires \(8 \varepsilon_Q\) below both input chart widths.

Its relative exponent is \(\text{rel}_Q = \text{e}_Q + 2 \varepsilon_Q + \delta_Q\).

Stage pairs \((j, i)\) are those with \(i \in \{j, j - 1\}\) (YM.Push.PriceCompiler.StepStages). The stage profile \(P_j\) has support exponent \(\sigma_j\).

  • tolerances \(h_{ji} > 0\);

  • libraries \(C_{ji}\) (of the order of \(P_i\));

  • mark numbers \(r_{ji}\).

Then there are thresholds \(f : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}\) and constants \(C'_{ji} \geqslant 1\) such that:

More precisely, take an RG step of side \(L\), fine and coarse parameter points \(B, P\), and supports \(X, Y\) such that \(Y\) lies in the \(r_{ji} \mathcal{B} (x)\)-neighbourhood of the coarse image of \(X\). For every \(u \geqslant 0\) satisfying the local analytic smallness condition for the return fee and every \(\tau \geqslant 0\),

    \(\displaystyle e^{(\sigma_i + 2) N_{B_P} (Y)} \hspace{0.17em} \tau \hspace{0.17em} \text{returnFee} \leqslant C'_{ji} L^{- 5} e^{\sigma_j N_{B_B} (X)} \tau .\)

3. Strategy

Part A separates the numerical loss from the fusion argument. On the coupling band, the hard bound is \(Ce^{- rs^2}\) with \(s \geqslant p (y) / 2\). Retaining \(e^{- \rho s^2}\) leaves \(Ce^{- (r - \rho) s^2}\) to absorb the support-counting factor. The soft bound decays because its negative power of \(y\) dominates its powers of \(p (y)\) and \(\mathcal{B} (y)\). The Mayer expansion then combines the hard activities with \(e^{\ell_i} - 1\); their weighted bounds add to \(e^{\kappa} a + 2 b\). Both families may be supported on large fields. The terminology “hard” and “soft” distinguishes their estimates.

Part B represents a transported curvature slot as the projection of the single group word \(\omega \cdot \partial \pi \cdot \omega^{- 1}\). Unitarity bounds each differentiated product without an exponential loss in path length. Each derivative contributes one direction norm, at most \(t (1 + 2 \ell)^P\), and has at most \(2 \ell\) possible link occurrences. For positive derivative order, \(t^m \leqslant t\); at order zero, the small-field plaquette bound gives the factor \(t\). The Leibniz rule gives \(t^k\) for a product of \(k\) slots, with a polynomial loss in total path length.

Part C first chooses a block-side threshold and then, for each permitted side and each fixed set of auxiliary data, chooses comparison functions and an entry coupling. The connected-activity bounds have rate \(\rho_Q\); the extra tree weight \(\beta_F^{3 / 8}\) still leaves negative rate. A marked input family of size \(\delta \leqslant \beta_F^{- 11 / 10}\) gives an anchored output bound \((C' / L + o (1)) \delta\), subject to the support and local analytic conditions in Lemma 4.16. Clusters with at least one marked occurrence and \(m\) further occurrences outside the inserted leaves gain an additional factor \(S' (x)^m\) of negative rate. At each fixed expansion order, the tail bounds are small enough to absorb any prescribed power of \(g (\beta)\). Finally, finitely many requests at each stage are met by enlarging the entry coupling and spatial-size thresholds.

4.5 The Chron chain (Part C)

The following conventions specify the conditional estimates in this chain. An admissible step record consists of step data with the prescribed patch, a stamped tube, an input representation \(R\), and its connected-activity hypotheses at \((W, x, \mu, L, Q)\). A marked input \((E, w, \delta)\) is a set \(E\) of regular input indices with derivative bounds \(w_i \geqslant 0\), zero outside \(E\), satisfying

\(\displaystyle \sum_{i : \hspace{0.17em} o_i = z} e^{\sigma_R N_{B_R} (X_i)} w_i \leqslant \delta \qquad \text{for every fine site } z.\)

Here \(o_i, X_i\) are the input anchors and supports, and the derivative bounds use the input parameter point. In particular, \(\delta \geqslant 0\).

For a finite family of inserted leaves with supports \(Z_{\alpha}\), nonnegative coefficients \(l_{\alpha}\), attached root sets \(A_{\alpha}\) and leaf weights \(\omega_{\alpha}\), the leaf-scale condition with parameters \((n, \sigma, Y)\) requires \(Y \geqslant 0\), at most \(n\) roots over each coarse site, and

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\alpha : \hspace{0.17em} v \in A_{\alpha}} e^{\sigma N_{B_R} (Z_{\alpha})} \omega_{\alpha} \leqslant Y \qquad \text{for every root } v.\)

The weights \(\omega_{\alpha}\) include the leaf norm and \(l_{\alpha}\), and each positive weight must have an attached root lying in its leaf support. The radius condition at \(\sigma\) is \((\text{rel}_Q + 1) 10 c_{h_Q + 5} / L + 2 \varepsilon_Q \leqslant \sigma\).

A cluster together with chosen leaf insertions has \(N\) remaining occurrences: the sum, over its vertices, of the numbers of input occurrences outside those insertions. It is marked if at least one remaining occurrence comes from \(E\). Thus \(N \geqslant 1\) for a marked cluster, but the other remaining occurrences need not be marked. The insertion count is the total number of leaves, counted with multiplicity. These are the two counts used below.

5. Where the difficulty lies

The active outputs III.15.1–4 require uniform estimates in the coupling band and the volume. In III.15.1, the threshold must work for every \(\beta\) in the band and every \(B \leqslant \mathcal{B} (y)\). In III.15.3–4, the constants depend on derivative order and path length but not on \(t\) or the torus side. The single-word representation and unitarity give that independence.

Part C adds the conditional comparison bounds to the selected run, but those bounds are discarded in the final construction-run theorem. Its main estimates concern an inherited marked family: the anchored bound \((C' / L + o (1)) \delta\) and the geometric gain per additional remaining occurrence. Their application requires the leaf-scale, radius, quotation and local analytic conditions. The gain counts all remaining occurrences, with at least one marked occurrence.

The exponent \(\tfrac{3}{8}\) is the tree-weight exponent inherited from III.10. Requiring \(\rho_Q < - \tfrac{3}{8}\) makes the relevant tree-sum majorant decay; the run's choice \(\rho_Q < - \tfrac{7}{16}\) leaves a margin greater than \(\tfrac{1}{16}\). The large-side threshold is independent of the later auxiliary data. The comparison functions and entry-coupling threshold may depend on those data, and the estimates then hold for all larger \(x\).

7. What remains unexplained

  1. Scope of Part C. The selected-run theorem retains its comparison data as an additional conjunct; the final construction-run theorem discards that conjunct. A written proof using only the final run properties can omit these comparison conclusions. An all-order expansion using them would also have to supply the coefficient-matching hypotheses; existence of the comparison functions alone does not supply that match.

  2. Local analytic conditions. The quotation bound and Lemmas 4.12, 4.16 and 4.19 retain the source's support-enlargement, library-compatibility and analytic-smallness conditions. Their full numerical definitions, and the local analytic, readiness, compatibility, resonance and tolerance conditions in III.15.6, remain to be written out. In particular, the local analytic condition attached to a funded step has not been translated into an independent mathematical statement.

  3. Provider proofs. The assembly proofs in §4 have been read. The deeper proofs of the local singleton estimate, weighted-cluster bounds, full-bill estimates and analytic expansion estimates have not all been checked here. The contraction and occurrence-count statements are consequences of the cited conditional estimates, not unconditional bounds for arbitrary local terms.

  4. Historical interpretation. The estimate \(L^{- 5}\) per fine site and its conversion to \(L^{- 1}\) per coarse block are explicit. Identifying it with a particular dimension-five operator is not justified by those inequalities alone. The reason for the choice \(\tfrac{3}{8}\) belongs to III.10's tree estimates.

  5. Other fragments. The construction-run, additional-expense and readout record statements have been read. The RG05 readout price formulas and their proofs, and the remaining RG12 and R2I fragments, are not covered by the detailed verification. The thermodynamic fragment also includes eventual numerical estimates and a delay-enlargement lemma; it is not solely a collection of definitions.
