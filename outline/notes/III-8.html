The unit has two independent halves.

  • Part A, connected activities. Bounds at fixed order for a recursive heat expansion, organised by powers of the coupling \(g\), the heat time \(t\) and the field. For finite index sets and symmetric inputs, its unmarked coefficients satisfy the formal equation for \(\log \mathbb{E}_{t \Gamma} e^V\). A second recursion treats one marked insertion. Both admit polynomial bounds in an input size \(W\) and Lipschitz bounds for differences, uniform in the index set and geometry.

  • Part B, backward dependence cones. The pull-back of a coarse observable through the block-spin kernel of III.1 is its conditional expectation given the fine field. It is local in an explicit fine window, and pull-backs of observables on disjoint coarse sets multiply exactly. Along a history the radius of the window grows by an explicit affine map whose increments, in physical units, decay geometrically.

1. What this unit proves

Part A

Conventions.

  • \(I\) is an arbitrary set (of cells), \(d\) is a pseudometric on \(I\), and \(\sigma \geqslant 0\).

  • A rank-\(n\) tensor is a function \(F : I^n \to \mathbb{R}\) with ordered slots and no symmetry factors. For a tuple \(x \in I^n\), \(\text{diam}_d (x) = \max (0, \max_{i, j} d (x_i, x_j))\), with value \(0\) for the empty tuple.

  • The rooted norm is

\(\displaystyle \lVert F \rVert_{\sigma} = \max_{1 \leqslant i \leqslant n} \sup_{z \in I} \sum_{x \in I^n, x_i = z} |F (x) | e^{\sigma \text{diam}_d (x)} \in [0, \infty] .\)

For \(n = 0\) set \(\lVert F \rVert_{\sigma} = 0\): a rooted bound imposes no condition on the scalar coefficient. Suprema over empty index sets also have value \(0\).

  • A propagator is any \(\Gamma : I \times I \to \mathbb{R}\); symmetry is not assumed. It is measured by its weighted row and column sums,

\(\displaystyle [\Gamma]_{\sigma} = \max \left( \sup_u \sum_v | \Gamma (u, v) |e^{\sigma d (u, v)}, \sup_v \sum_u | \Gamma (u, v) |e^{\sigma d (u, v)} \right) .\)

  • A pair \((F, F')\) is \((W, \delta)\)-controlled if \(\lVert F \rVert_{\sigma} \leqslant W\), \(\lVert F' \rVert_{\sigma} \leqslant W\) and \(\lVert F - F' \rVert_{\sigma} \leqslant W \delta\). The same definition applies to a pair of propagators, with \([\cdot]_{\sigma}\) in place of \(\lVert \cdot \rVert_{\sigma}\).

  • A polynomial majorant is a function \(P\) with \(0 \leqslant P (x) \leqslant Cx^t\) for all \(x \geqslant 1\), for some \(C \geqslant 0\) and \(t \in \mathbb{N}\). The class is closed under sums and products.

Formal potentials. A formal potential is a family \(V = (V_{e, n})_{e, n \geqslant 0}\) of rank-\(n\) tensors; \(V_{e, n}\) is the coefficient of \(g^e\) with \(n\) field variables. In Gaussian applications the variables may include spectators, on which \(\Gamma\) vanishes. \(V\) is eligible if \(V_{0, n} = 0\) for all \(n\), and \(V_{e, n} \ne 0\) implies \(n \leqslant e + 2\) and \(e + n\) even. Eligibility does not require symmetry.

Heat coefficients. The heat coefficients \(V^{(j)} = V^{(j)} (\Gamma, V)\) are defined by \(V^{(0)} = V\) and

\(\displaystyle V^{(j + 1)}_{e, n} = \frac{1}{j + 1} \left[ \binom{n + 2}{2} \Delta_{\Gamma} V^{(j)}_{e, n + 2} + \sum_{c = 0}^n \sum_{i = 0}^j \sum_{a = 0}^e \frac{(c + 1) (n - c + 1)}{2} V^{(i)}_{a, c + 1} \star_{\Gamma} V^{(j - i)}_{e - a, n - c + 1} \right] . \qquad (\text{A1})\)

Here \(\Delta_{\Gamma}\) (contraction) and \(\star_{\Gamma}\) (join) are defined in Step A1. For infinite \(I\), the recursions assign value \(0\) to a sum that is not summable. The bounds below justify the required sums absolutely for positive-rank unmarked outputs and for all marked outputs; they make no convergence assertion for unmarked scalar contractions. The formal heat-equation interpretation for finite \(I\) and symmetric \(V, \Gamma\) is given in A1.

Proposition III.8.1 (bounds at fixed order for the connected heat coefficients). Fix \(j, e, n \in \mathbb{N}\) and put

\(\displaystyle T_j (e, n) = \{(a, r) : 1 \leqslant a < e, 1 \leqslant r \leqslant a + 2\} \cup \{(e, r) : 1 \leqslant e, n < r \leqslant e + 2\},\)

enlarged by \((e, n)\) when \(j = 0\), \(e \geqslant 1\) and \(1 \leqslant n \leqslant e + 2\). There is a polynomial majorant \(P\) with the following property. Let \(I\) be a set, \(d\) a pseudometric on it, \(\sigma \geqslant 0\), \(\delta \geqslant 0\), \(W \geqslant 1\), \(V, V'\) eligible and \(\Gamma, \Gamma'\) propagators. Suppose \((\Gamma, \Gamma')\) is \((W, \delta)\)-controlled, and so is \((V_{a, r}, V'_{a, r})\) for every \((a, r) \in T_j (e, n)\). Then \((V^{(j)}_{e, n} (\Gamma, V), V^{(j)}_{e, n} (\Gamma', V'))\) is \((P (W), \delta)\)-controlled.

  • \(P\) depends on \((j, e, n)\) only. It does not depend on \(I\), \(d\), \(\sigma\) or \(\delta\), hence not on the number of cells, the period, the scale or the stage at which it is applied.

  • For \(j \geqslant 1\), inputs at the same order \(e\) have strictly higher rank.

  • For \(n = 0\) the conclusion is empty.

Status: theorem. Lean: YM.RG08.Steam.navigation, input range taps, norms Held, Wire, Cot, WireCot, majorants Wage.

Proposition III.8.2 (support; the heat-time series is finite). Let \(V\) be eligible and \(\Gamma\) arbitrary.

  • Every \(V^{(j)}\) is eligible.

  • \(V^{(j)}_{e, n} \ne 0\) implies \(n + 2 j \leqslant 3 e\).

  • For \(j \geqslant 1\), \(V^{(j)}\) is symmetric under permutations of its slots.

Hence, at fixed \((e, n)\), the heat-time series is zero if \(n > 3 e\) and otherwise is a polynomial in \(t\) of degree at most \(\lfloor (3 e - n) / 2 \rfloor\). For every real \(t\) the complete series is

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{E}_t (V)_{e, n} = \sum_{j = 0}^{3 e} t^j V^{(j)}_{e, n} = V_{e, n} +\mathcal{R}_t (V)_{e, n}, \qquad \mathcal{R}_t (V)_{e, n} = \sum_{j = 1}^{3 e} t^j V^{(j)}_{e, n},\)

and \(\mathcal{E}_t (V)\) is eligible. Status: theorem. Lean: preserves, live, off, coal, ember, cinder, smoulder, appropriate.

Proposition III.8.3 (expansion with one marked insertion).

Test functions and their norms. Let \(\mathcal{S}\) be the real vector space of smooth real-valued functions on \(\mathbb{R}^4\) whose derivatives of every order have polynomial growth. For \(k \in \mathbb{N}\) and \(\sigma > 0\), using the Euclidean norm \(|y|\), put

\(\displaystyle \lVert f \rVert_{k, \sigma} = \max_{| \alpha | \leqslant k} \sup_{y \in \mathbb{R}^4} | \partial^{\alpha} f (y) | e^{- \sigma |y|},\)

and, for a real-linear functional \(\lambda\) on \(\mathcal{S}\), \(\lVert \lambda \rVert^{\ast}_{k, \sigma} = \sup \{| \lambda (f) | : \lVert f \rVert_{k, \sigma} \leqslant 1\}\).

Marked tensors and the marked norm. A marked rank-\(n\) tensor assigns to each \(x \in I^n\) a real-linear functional \(M (x)\) on \(\mathcal{S}\). Let \(h : I \to [0, \infty)\) be 1-Lipschitz for \(d\) (a height, for instance a distance to the insertion), and set \(h (x) = \max (0, \max_i h (x_i))\) for a tuple \(x\). The marked norm is the unrooted weighted \(\ell^1\) norm

\(\displaystyle \lVert M \rVert_h = \sum_{x \in I^n} \lVert M (x) \rVert^{\ast}_{k, \sigma} e^{\sigma h (x)} .\)

Unlike the rooted norm, it gives a genuine bound for \(n = 0\). A pair \((M, M')\) is \((U, \delta)\)-controlled if \(\lVert M \rVert_h \leqslant U\), \(\lVert M' \rVert_h \leqslant U\) and \(\lVert M - M' \rVert_h \leqslant U \delta\).

Marked heat coefficients. For a marked series \(F = (F_{e, n})\) they are \(M^{(0)} = F\) and

\(\displaystyle M^{(j + 1)}_{e, n} = \frac{1}{j + 1} \left[ \binom{n + 2}{2} \Delta_{\Gamma} M^{(j)}_{e, n + 2} + \sum_{c = 0}^n \sum_{i = 0}^j \sum_{a = 0}^e (c + 1) (n - c + 1) M^{(i)}_{a, c + 1} \star_{\Gamma} V^{(j - i)}_{e - a, n - c + 1} \right] .\)

The cross term has twice the coefficient of its unmarked counterpart. The recursive outputs are initially maps on test functions; the bounds below prove that they are real-linear. Their interpretation as a normalised Gaussian expectation is discussed in §7.

(i) Fixed-order bound. Fix \(j, e, n\). There is a polynomial majorant \(P\) with the following property, for all \(I, d, h\) as above, \(\sigma > 0\), \(k \in \mathbb{N}\), \(\delta \geqslant 0\), \(W \geqslant 1\), \(U \geqslant 0\), eligible \(V, V'\), propagators \(\Gamma, \Gamma'\) and marked series \(F, F'\). Assume:

  • \((\Gamma, \Gamma')\) is \((W, \delta)\)-controlled;

  • the unmarked inputs \((V_{a, r}, V'_{a, r})\) with \(1 \leqslant a \leqslant e\) and \(1 \leqslant r \leqslant a + 2\) are \((W, \delta)\)-controlled;

  • the marked inputs \((F_{a, r}, F'_{a, r})\) are \((U, \delta)\)-controlled for: \(a < e\) (any \(r\)); or \(a = e\) and \(r > n\); or \(j = 0\) and \((a, r) = (e, n)\).

Then \((M^{(j)}_{e, n} (\Gamma, V, F), M^{(j)}_{e, n} (\Gamma', V', F'))\) is \((P (W) U, \delta)\)-controlled, with real-linear output values. The majorant depends only on \((j, e, n)\); in particular it is independent of \(k, \sigma\) and the height \(h\).

(ii) Support. Let \(l \in \mathbb{N}\). If \(V\) is eligible and \(F_{a, r} \ne 0\) implies \(r \leqslant l + a\) for every \(a, r\), then \(M^{(j)}_{e, n} \ne 0\) implies \(n + 2 j \leqslant l + 3 e\) and \(n \leqslant l + e\).

(iii) Full positive-time part. Fix \(l, e, n\). There is a polynomial majorant \(P\) such that the pair

\(\displaystyle \sum_{j = 1}^{l + 3 e} t^j M^{(j)}_{e, n} (\Gamma, V, F), \qquad \sum_{j = 1}^{l + 3 e} t^j M^{(j)}_{e, n} (\Gamma', V', F')\)

is \((P (W) U, \delta)\)-controlled for every real \(t\) with \(|t| \leqslant W\). The hypotheses are those of (i), with marked inputs required only for \(a < e\), or \(a = e\) and \(r > n\). By (ii), this is the whole positive-time part when both \(F_{a, r}\) and \(F'_{a, r}\) vanish for \(r > l + a\), for every \(a, r\).

Status: theorem. This is the form in which the main theorem uses Part A (in III.14). Lean: journey, full, registry. Test space: Pearl. Norms: Sings, Dyad, purse, beam, Loom, fits.

Part B

Setting.

  • The gauge group is as in I.1, with its Haar probability measure.

  • A block-spin step (III.1) maps a fine periodic lattice to a coarse one with odd block factor \(L \geqslant 3\). The coarse site \(x\) sits at the fine site \(\iota x\).

  • The kernel is built from patches: boxes of radius \(\rho = (J + 3) L\) around the points \(\iota x\), where the stage satisfies \(J \geqslant 4\).

  • \(k_{\beta} (U, V)\) is the conditional density of the coarse configuration \(V\) given the fine configuration \(U\), with respect to the product Haar probability measure.

  • The pull-back of a bounded measurable complex function \(F\) of the coarse configuration is

\(\displaystyle (\mathcal{B}F) (U) = \int k_{\beta} (U, V) F (V) dV.\)

  • For \(r \in \mathbb{N}\), \(\Lambda_r (x)\) is the periodic \(\ell^{\infty}\) box of radius \(r\) about \(x\); periodic images are allowed. All lattice radii below are nonnegative integers.

  • \(F\) is local on \(X\) if \(F (V)\) depends only on the links of \(V\) with both endpoints in \(X\).

  • \(\mathcal{I} (X)\) is the class of observables that are gauge invariant, measurable, bounded and local on \(X\) (IV.1).

Patch hypothesis (III.1). The minimising frame and the best predictor are unique whenever their respective branches are used. The set of admissible fallback frame tags is nonempty for every cover configuration.

Proposition III.8.4 (one step: the pull-back is local). Assume the patch hypothesis, and let \(\beta \in \mathbb{R}\).

(i) Let \(F\) be a bounded measurable complex function local on a coarse set \(X\), and let \(Y\) be a set of fine sites containing \(\Lambda_{10 \rho} (\iota x)\) for every \(x \in X\). Then \(\mathcal{B}F\) is local on \(Y\).

(ii) Assume moreover that the density is gauge covariant, \(k_{\beta} (U^g, V^{g \circ \iota}) = k_{\beta} (U, V)\) for every fine gauge transformation \(g\). If \(F \in \mathcal{I} (\Lambda_R (o))\), then \(\mathcal{B}F \in \mathcal{I} (\Lambda_{LR + 10 \rho} (\iota o))\), with the same sup bound.

Status: theorem. III.1 supplies these kernel properties for its chosen kernel parameters: the patch hypothesis is Next.Foundations.branches. Gauge covariance follows from StepData.covary. For each step of a history, both are fields of the block-control data built in Calendar.blocks_go. Lean: back_local, back_cylinder.

Proposition III.8.5 (exact factorisation). Assume the patch hypothesis.

  • If \(F, G\) are bounded, measurable and local on disjoint coarse sets \(X, Y\), then \(\mathcal{B} (FG) =\mathcal{B}F \cdot \mathcal{B}G\) for every \(\beta\).

  • In particular \(\mathcal{B} \prod_{i \in l} F_i = \prod_{i \in l} \mathcal{B}F_i\) when \(F_i \in \mathcal{I} (C_i)\) for distinct cells \(C_i\) of a tiling.

  • Along a history with the kernel properties stated below, fix \(N, p\) and let \(h \leqslant k \leqslant m\). Let \(l\) be a finite set of level-\(h\) sites and \(F_i \in \mathcal{I} (\Lambda_r (\iota_{h \to k} i))\) at level \(k\) for \(i \in l\). If \(\text{room} (h, k, r)\) holds, then \(\mathcal{B}_{k \to m} \prod_{i \in l} F_i = \prod_{i \in l} \mathcal{B}_{k \to m} F_i\).

  • The same holds for the product of the tile copies of one \(F \in \mathcal{I} (\Lambda_r (0))\) (translated, reflected, and complex-conjugated on odd tiles), the copies on the right being taken at level \(m\).

Status: theorem. It is an identity, not an estimate, and the fine windows of \(\mathcal{B}F\) and \(\mathcal{B}G\) may overlap. Lean: back_factors, back_cells, backTo_cells, backTo_copies.

History.

  • Levels \(m = 0, 1, 2, \ldots\) get finer as \(m\) grows. Level \(m\) has spacing \(a_m\).

  • The step from level \(m + 1\) to level \(m\) has block factor \(L_m\) (odd, \(\geqslant 3\)), so \(a_{m + 1} = a_m / L_m\). Its patch radius is \(\rho_m = (J_{m + 1} + 3) L_m\), where \(J_m \geqslant 4\) and \(J_{m + 1} \leqslant J_m + 1\). The inverse couplings are \(\beta_{N, p, m}\).

  • Every step satisfies the patch hypothesis, gauge covariance and covariance under lattice translations and signed permutations of the axes. These are part of the history data supplied by III.1.

  • \(\mathcal{B}_m\) is the pull-back through this step at \(\beta_{N, p, m + 1}\). Put \(\mathcal{B}_{k \to m} =\mathcal{B}_{m - 1} \circ \ldots \circ \mathcal{B}_k\) (with \(\mathcal{B}_{k \to k} = \text{id}\)), and let \(\mathcal{B}^{\ast}_{k \to m}\) be the composite of the push-forwards of III.1 from level \(m\) to level \(k\).

  • For a level-\(h\) site \(i\) and \(m \geqslant h\), \(\iota_{h \to m} i\) is its image at level \(m\).

  • The cone radius is \(r_{k \to k} (r) = r\), \(r_{k \to m + 1} (r) = L_m r_{k \to m} (r) + 10 (J_{m + 1} + 3) L_m\).

  • The physical budget is \(\tau_m = 30 a_m (J_m + 5)\).

  • \(\text{room} (h, k, r)\) is the condition \(2 (a_k r + \tau_k) < a_h\).

Proposition III.8.6 (pull-back along the history). For all \(N, p\) and \(k \leqslant m\):

(i) \(\mathcal{B}_{k \to m}\) maps bounded measurable functions to bounded measurable functions, with \(\sup |\mathcal{B}_{k \to m} F| \leqslant \sup |F|\). It is linear, fixes constants and commutes with complex conjugation.

(ii) For every bounded measurable complex \(F\) at level \(k\) and every integrable complex \(g\) at level \(m\), \(\int (\mathcal{B}^{\ast}_{k \to m} g) F = \int g\mathcal{B}_{k \to m} F\) and \(\int \mathcal{B}^{\ast}_{k \to m} g = \int g\). All integrals here use the Haar probability measures at their respective levels; the push-forward is integrable.

(iii) Let \(h \leqslant k\) and \(i\) a level-\(h\) site. If \(F \in \mathcal{I} (\Lambda_r (\iota_{h \to k} i))\), then \(\mathcal{B}_{k \to m} F \in \mathcal{I} (\Lambda_{r_{k \to m} (r)} (\iota_{h \to m} i))\).

(iv) On bounded measurable functions, \(\mathcal{B}_{k \to m}\) commutes with signed permutations of the axes followed by translation by \(\iota_{h \to \cdot} i\), for \(h \leqslant k\) and each level-\(h\) site \(i\), and with the copy maps of the centred level-\(h\) tiling.

Status: theorem. The hypotheses on the kernels are supplied by the history. Lean: backTo, pushTo, backTo_bound, backTo_env, backTo_add, backTo_const, backTo_star, pushTo_dual, pushTo_integral, backTo_local, backTo_orient, backTo_copy.

Proposition III.8.7 (size of the cone in physical units). For the history above and all nonnegative integer levels and radii:

(i) \(\tau_{m + 1} \leqslant \frac{2}{3} \tau_m\); hence \(\tau_m \leqslant (2 / 3)^m \tau_0 \to 0\).

(ii) \(a_m r_{k \to m} (r) + \tau_m \leqslant a_k r + \tau_k\) for \(k \leqslant m\). Thus an observable of lattice radius \(r\) at level \(k\) has, at every finer level, a backward cone of physical radius at most \(a_k r + \tau_k\), uniformly in \(m\), \(N\) and \(p\).

(iii) If \(h \leqslant k\) and \(\text{room} (h, k, r)\) holds, then for every \(m \geqslant k\) and every level-\(h\) site \(i\), the box \(\Lambda_{r_{k \to m} (r)} (\iota_{h \to m} i)\) lies in the cell of \(i\) of the centred level-\(h\) tiling at level \(m\). That cell has \(L_h \cdots L_{m - 1}\) sites per side, i.e. physical side \(a_h\).

(iv) For fixed \(h\) and \(r\), \(\text{room} (h, k, r)\) holds for all sufficiently large \(k \geqslant h\).

3. Strategy

Part A. Each step of (A1) either contracts two slots of one coefficient or joins two coefficients by a propagator line. The rooted norm absorbs the internal sums without changing \(\sigma\): the output diameter is bounded by the input diameters plus the line length, so the exponential weights multiply. For a marked coefficient, the height inequality plays the same role, with the marked factor summed over all its slots.

Induction on heat order gives a polynomial majorant in \(W\). Telescoping the inputs gives the corresponding difference bound with the same \(\delta\). Every primitive vertex has positive coupling order and at most three times that order in slots; every propagator removes two slots. This gives \(n + 2 j \leqslant 3 e\) and finiteness in heat time at each coupling order. Positive heat order uses only lower coupling orders and higher ranks at the same coupling order.

Part B. Given the fine field, the kernel first draws independent site tags, then independent predictor tags on bonds, and finally independent coarse links conditional on these tags. Integrating an observable local on \(X\) removes all unused bonds and site tags. The remaining factors read the fine field within radius \(10 \rho\) of \(\iota X\). Observables on disjoint coarse site sets use disjoint tags and bonds, so their pull-backs multiply. Under iteration the radius grows by \(R \mapsto L_m R + 10 \rho_m\). In physical units the added radius is at most \(\tau_m / 3\), while \(\tau_{m + 1} \leqslant 2 \tau_m / 3\). The total radius therefore stays within \(a_k r + \tau_k\). When this fits in one level-\(h\) cell, different cells remain separate at every intermediate step, allowing the product formula to iterate.

4. Steps

A. Connected heat coefficients

A1. Operations and the recursion.

Definitions. For \(F\) of rank \(m + 2\),

\(\displaystyle (\Delta_{\Gamma} F) (x) = \text{Sym} \sum_{u, v \in I} \Gamma (u, v) F (u, v, x) .\)

For \(F\) of rank \(p + 1\) and \(G\) of rank \(q + 1\), the join has rank \(p + q\):

\(\displaystyle (F \star_{\Gamma} G) (x, y) = \text{Sym} \sum_{u, v \in I} F (u, x) \Gamma (u, v) G (v, y) .\)

\(\text{Sym}\) averages over permutations of the slots.

Combinatorial factors. On a finite index set, write \(W = \sum_n \langle W_n, \phi^{\otimes n} \rangle\) with symmetric coefficient tensors and no slot factorials. For symmetric \(\Gamma\), the differential operators \(\tfrac{1}{2} \sum_{u, v} \Gamma (u, v) \partial_u \partial_v W\) and \(\tfrac{1}{2} \sum_{u, v} \Gamma (u, v) \partial_u W \hspace{0.17em} \partial_v W\) give the factors \(\binom{n + 2}{2}\) and \((c + 1) (n - c + 1) / 2\). For nonsymmetric tensors (A1) remains a definition, without this identification.

Vanishing terms and symmetry. For eligible \(V\) the pair terms with \(a = 0\) or \(a = e\) vanish, because \(V^{(i)}_{0, \cdot} = 0\). Every \(V^{(j)}\) with \(j \geqslant 1\) is symmetric, since every term is symmetrised.

Formal heat equation. For finite \(I\), eligible \(V\) symmetric in every rank (waxed), and symmetric \(\Gamma\), III.14 proves:

  • the generating series solves the formal Hamilton–Jacobi equation (Glyph.cloudy);

  • its exponential solves the linear heat equation (Cloud.Clockwise.linear);

  • summation at time \(t\) gives \(\mathcal{E}_t (V)\) (Glyph.breaktime).

These identities support the formal cumulant interpretation. They do not assert convergence of a Gaussian integral or of the series in \(g\). The norm estimates use only the recursion.

A2. Support (Proposition III.8.2). Since \(\Delta_{\Gamma} 0 = 0\) and \(0 \star_{\Gamma} G = F \star_{\Gamma} 0 = 0\), a nonzero \(V^{(j + 1)}_{e, n}\) has a nonzero predecessor: either \(V^{(j)}_{e, n + 2} \ne 0\), or both \(V^{(i)}_{a, c + 1}\) and \(V^{(j - i)}_{e - a, n - c + 1}\) are nonzero for some \(c \leqslant n\), \(i \leqslant j\), \(a \leqslant e\). Strong induction on \(j\) gives \(n + 2 j \leqslant 3 e\):

  • at \(j = 0\), \(n \leqslant e + 2 \leqslant 3 e\) because \(e \geqslant 1\);

  • the contraction step uses \((n + 2) + 2 j \leqslant 3 e\);

  • the pair step adds \((c + 1) + 2 i \leqslant 3 a\) and \((n - c + 1) + 2 (j - i) \leqslant 3 (e - a)\).

Parity and \(n \leqslant e + 2\) propagate the same way. Splitting off \(j = 0\) gives \(\mathcal{E}_t = V +\mathcal{R}_t\). Lean: predecessors, preserves, live, off, smoulder, appropriate.

A3. The two contraction estimates. Let \(\sigma \geqslant 0\), let the input rooted norms and \([\Gamma]_{\sigma}\) be finite, and require positive output rank. Then

\(\displaystyle \lVert \Delta_{\Gamma} F \rVert_{\sigma} \leqslant \lVert F \rVert_{\sigma} \sup | \Gamma | \leqslant \lVert F \rVert_{\sigma} [\Gamma]_{\sigma}, \qquad \lVert F \star_{\Gamma} G \rVert_{\sigma} \leqslant \lVert F \rVert_{\sigma} [\Gamma]_{\sigma} \lVert G \rVert_{\sigma} .\)

Symmetrisation. \(\text{Sym}\) does not increase the rooted norm: it permutes the slots and preserves diameters.

Contraction. Fix a slot of \(x\). Then \(\text{diam} (x) \leqslant \text{diam} (u, v, x)\) and \(| \Gamma (u, v) | \leqslant \sup | \Gamma |\), and the sum over \((u, v, x)\) with that slot fixed is a rooted sum of \(F\).

Join. The key inequality is

\(\displaystyle \text{diam} (x, y) \leqslant \text{diam} (u, x) + d (u, v) + \text{diam} (v, y) .\)

With a slot of \(x\) fixed, sum in three stages:

  1. over \(y\) at fixed \(v\): at most \(\lVert G \rVert\), rooted at \(v\);

  2. over \(v\): at most the row sum of \(\Gamma\);

  3. over \((u, x)\): at most \(\lVert F \rVert\).

A fixed slot of \(y\) is treated symmetrically, with column sums.

Differences. Absolute convergence permits telescoping: \(\Delta_{\Gamma} F - \Delta_{\Gamma'} F' = \Delta_{\Gamma} (F - F') + \Delta_{\Gamma - \Gamma'} F'\), and the join gives three terms. If the tensor pairs are \((P, \delta)\)- and \((Q, \delta)\)-controlled and \((\Gamma, \Gamma')\) is \((B, \delta)\)-controlled, with \(P, Q, B, \delta \geqslant 0\), the contraction pair is \((2 PB, \delta)\)-controlled and the join pair is \((3 PBQ, \delta)\)-controlled. At output rank \(0\) these controlled-pair conclusions are vacuous; telescoping need not be valid for divergent scalar sums.

A4. Proof of Proposition III.8.1. Strong induction on \(j\), for all \((e, n)\) at once.

\(\displaystyle P_{j + 1, e, n} (x) = \frac{1}{j + 1} \left[ \binom{n + 2}{2} 2 xP_{j, e, n + 2} (x) + \sum_{c = 0}^n \sum_{i = 0}^j \sum_{a = 0}^e \frac{(c + 1) (n - c + 1)}{2} 3 xP_{i, a, c + 1} (x) P_{j - i, e - a, n - c + 1} (x) \right] .\)

A5. Full correction and change of index set (outside the main-theorem closure).

For each slot \(i\), choose a kernel \(K_i : I \times J \to \mathbb{R}\), a nonnegative weight \(E_i\), and \(b_i \geqslant 0\) bounding both weighted row and column sums of \(|K_i | e^{E_i}\). A6. Marked expansion (Proposition III.8.3).

Operations. For \(M\) marked and \(G\) unmarked, the contraction \(\Delta_{\Gamma} M\) is as in A1, and the join is \((M \star_{\Gamma} G) (x, y) = \text{Sym} \sum_{u, v} \Gamma (u, v) G (v, y) M (u, x)\).

Estimates.

\(\displaystyle \lVert \Delta_{\Gamma} M \rVert_h \leqslant \sup | \Gamma | \lVert M \rVert_h, \qquad \lVert M \star_{\Gamma} G \rVert_h \leqslant \lVert M \rVert_h [\Gamma]_{\sigma} \lVert G \rVert_{\sigma} .\)

The first holds because \(h (x) \leqslant h (u, v, x)\). The second follows from \(h (x, y) \leqslant h (u, x) + d (u, v) + \text{diam}_d (v, y)\), which uses that \(h\) is 1-Lipschitz. Sum the marked factor over all its slots, the propagator by its row bound, and the unmarked factor by its rooted bound at \(v\). With finite input bounds, the sum of the dual norms is finite. Evaluation at each test function is therefore absolutely summable and preserves linearity, including at output rank \(0\). The difference constants are \(2\) and \(3\), as in A3.

Induction for (i). Induct on \(j\) as in A4.

  • The contraction term uses the case \((j ; e, n + 2)\).

  • Pair terms with \(a = e\) vanish, because the unmarked factor then has order \(0\).

  • For \(a < e\), the marked factor \(M^{(i)}_{a, c + 1}\) is bounded by the induction hypothesis, which needs marked inputs of order at most \(a < e\). The unmarked factor \(V^{(j - i)}_{e - a, n - c + 1}\) is bounded by III.8.1, whose inputs lie in \(\{1 \leqslant a' \leqslant e, 1 \leqslant r \leqslant a' + 2\}\).

This gives (i) with output \(P (W) U\).

(ii) and (iii). For (ii), a nonzero contraction has a marked predecessor of rank \(n + 2\). A nonzero join has marked and unmarked predecessors whose ranks sum to \(n + 2\) and whose coupling orders sum to \(e\). Add the marked induction bounds to the unmarked bounds of III.8.2 to obtain \(n + 2 j \leqslant l + 3 e\) and \(n \leqslant l + e\). For (iii), sum (i) over \(1 \leqslant j \leqslant l + 3 e\), using \(|t|^j \leqslant W^j\); no bound for the initial rank-\(n\), order-\(e\) marked coefficient is needed.

Consistency. Evaluating a marked tensor at a fixed test function turns its contraction and join into those of A1.

B. Backward dependence cones

B1. Product structure of the kernel. Summing the bond tags out of the definition in III.1 gives

\(\displaystyle k_{\beta} (U, V) = \sum_t \prod_x q_x (U, t_x) \prod_b p_b (U, t ; V_b) .\)

  • \(t = (t_x)\) runs over the site (frame) tags, and \(q_x (U, \cdot)\) is the frame mass at \(x\).

  • \(p_b (U, t ; \cdot)\) is a mixture, over predictor tags \(r\) with weights summing to one, of normalised penalty densities centred at the predictions \(Q_r (Y_b)\), which are built from the connector \(Y_b\) of the bond:

\(\displaystyle p_b (U, t ; v) = \sum_r w_r (Y_b) Z_{\beta}^{- 1} \exp (- \beta \kappa \text{price} (vQ_r (Y_b)^{- 1})) .\)

Here \(\kappa > 0\) is the kernel penalty of III.1, called \(b\) in the main theorem.

Each \(p_b\) has Haar integral \(1\). The identity \(\sum_r q_x (U, r) = 1\) uses the patch hypothesis (the fallback set is nonempty). Hence

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{B}F (U) = \sum_t \left( \prod_x q_x (U, t_x) \right) S_F (U, t), \qquad S_F (U, t) = \int \prod_b p_b (U, t ; V_b) F (V) dV.\)

Given \(U\), the site tags are independent; given \(U\) and the tags, the coarse links are independent. Adjacent bonds share a tag, so the links are not independent given \(U\) alone. Lean: density_parts, noiseLink, perLink, perSite, noiseLink_mass, perLink_mass, site_unit, parts_back.

B2. Read sets.

(a) Frames. The frame and the frame mass at \(x\) depend only on \(U\) on links inside \(\Lambda_{10 \rho} (\iota x)\). There are two branches:

  • if a small-field test on transports in the patch passes, the frame is the minimiser, normalised at the centre, of an averaged price of transports between patch points, and the mass is uniform over all tags;

  • otherwise the frame is the axial gauge built from the tag, normalised at the centre, and the mass is uniform over admissible tags (those whose axial cost is bounded by a multiple of the plaquette cost in the patch).

All transports run along coordinate routes between points of \(\Lambda_{\rho}\), of length at most \(8 \rho\), so they stay in \(\Lambda_{9 \rho}\). The minimiser is selected by a global choice. Its uniqueness (patch hypothesis) is what forces two configurations that agree on the window to select the same frame.

(b) Connectors. The connector of the bond from \(x\) to \(x + e_{\mu}\) depends only on the tags at both ends and on \(U\) in \(\Lambda_{10 \rho} (\iota x) \cup \Lambda_{10 \rho} (\iota (x + e_{\mu}))\). It is built from the two frames and from straight transports of length \(L \leqslant \rho\).

B3. Locality (Proposition III.8.4). Two elementary facts:

  • (P1) if a function reads only the coordinates in a set \(s\), its integral against a product of probability densities does not change when the densities outside \(s\) are changed;

  • (P2) the same holds for sums over products of probability vectors.

Proof of (i). If \(F\) is local on \(X\), it reads only bonds with both ends in \(X\). By (P1) and B2(b), \(S_F (U, t)\) depends only on \(t|_X\) and on \(U\) in the windows around \(\iota X\). By (P2), the tags off \(X\) sum out. By B2(a), \(q_x\) for \(x \in X\) reads \(U\) only in \(\Lambda_{10 \rho} (\iota x)\).

Proof of (ii). If \(y \in \Lambda_R (o)\) then \(\iota y \in \Lambda_{LR} (\iota o)\), and \(\Lambda_{10 \rho}\) of a point of \(\Lambda_{LR} (\iota o)\) lies in \(\Lambda_{LR + 10 \rho} (\iota o)\). Gauge invariance follows from the covariance of \(k_{\beta}\) and the invariance of Haar measure. The sup bound and measurability hold because \(k_{\beta}\) is a probability kernel.

B7. Two consequences used downstream.

(a) Replicated differences (used by III.11). Fix \(N, p\) and \(h \leqslant k \leqslant N\). Let \(O\) be a bounded measurable complex function at level \(N\), let \(F \in \mathcal{I} (\Lambda_w (0))\) at level \(k\) with \(\text{room} (h, k, w)\), and let \(s\) be a finite set of level-\(h\) sites. Then

\(\displaystyle Z_N \left\langle \prod_{i \in s} \text{copy}_i (\mathcal{B}_{k \to N} F - O) \right\rangle_N = \sum_{l \subseteq s} \int \mathcal{B}^{\ast}_{k \to N} \left[ e^{- S_N} \prod_{i \in s \setminus l} (- \text{copy}_i O) \right] \prod_{i \in l} \text{copy}_i F.\)

Here \(S_N = \beta_{N, p, N} E_N\) is the Wilson action, \(Z_N = \int e^{- S_N} \hspace{0.17em} dU\), and \(\langle Q \rangle_N = Z_N^{- 1} \int e^{- S_N} Q \hspace{0.17em} dU\). The copies on each side use the level-\(h\) tiling at the level of their argument; the integrals on the right are at level \(k\). Expand the product, commute the copies with subtraction, then apply III.8.5 and the duality in III.8.6(ii). The old factors stay together inside one push-forward; no independence of \(O\) is required.

(b) Chessboard telescoping (used by III.13, VII.2). Fix \(h \leqslant N\), \(p\) and a real coupling \(\beta\) for which the chessboard inequality of IV.1 holds; the Wilson law has this property for \(\beta \geqslant 0\). Let \(T\) be the centred level-\(h\) tiling at level \(N\). For a central-cell observable \(Q\), define

\(\displaystyle \text{root}_T (Q) = | \left\langle \prod_{a \in T} \text{copy}_a Q \right\rangle_{\beta} |^{1 / (\sharp T)},\)

where \(\sharp T\) is the number of cells. Let the index set be finite, let \(a_i\) be distinct level-\(h\) sites, and let \(O_i, Z_i \in \mathcal{I} (T_0)\), where \(T_0\) is the central cell. Put \(b_i = \iota_{h \to N} a_i\) and let \(\tau_{b_i}\) act on configurations by lattice translation. If real bounds \(c_i, e_i\) satisfy \(\text{root}_T (O_i) \leqslant c_i\) and \(\text{root}_T (Z_i - O_i) \leqslant e_i\), then

\(\displaystyle | \left\langle \prod_i (Z_i \circ \tau_{b_i}) \right\rangle_{\beta} - \left\langle \prod_i (O_i \circ \tau_{b_i}) \right\rangle_{\beta} | \leqslant \prod_i (e_i + c_i) - \prod_i c_i .\)

Expand \(\prod_i (O_i + (Z_i - O_i))\) over subsets. The empty-subset term cancels, and the chessboard estimate bounds each remaining term by the corresponding product of \(c_i\) and \(e_i\). To apply it to literal translates, undo the reflection in each copy and, on odd cells, conjugate its source. These operations preserve the central-cell class and its root. Summing the nonempty-subset bounds gives the right-hand side.

5. Where the difficulty lies

  • Input ranges. At positive heat order, inputs of coupling order \(e\) have strictly higher rank. The purely contracting contribution to \(V^{(j)}_{e, n}\) starts at \(V_{e, n + 2 j}\). This permits downward induction in rank at fixed coupling order. The bound is uniform in geometry, but its polynomial may depend on \((j, e, n)\); no resummation in \(g\) is proved.

  • Scalar terms. The unmarked rooted norm gives no scalar estimate. The marked norm sums all slots and does control rank \(0\), including absolute convergence of its contractions. The same \(\sigma > 0\) occurs in the line, diameter, height and test-function weights.

  • Locality of a selected minimiser. Agreement on the read window makes the minimisation problems identical. Uniqueness then makes their selected frames equal. The read radius \(10 \rho\) also includes fallback tests and connectors; the coordinate routes used by frames lie in \(\Lambda_{9 \rho}\).

  • Conditional factorisation. Disjoint coarse site sets use disjoint tags and bonds. Their pull-backs may nevertheless read overlapping fine windows. Factorisation therefore concerns the conditional kernel, not the fine Gibbs law. Iteration requires separation at every intermediate level, supplied by the cell-containment bound.

  • Physical size. The lattice radius grows by \(R \mapsto L_m R + 10 \rho_m\), while its physical radius stays below \(a_k r + 30 a_k (J_k + 5)\). For fixed \(h, r\), this bound eventually fits inside a level-\(h\) cell.
