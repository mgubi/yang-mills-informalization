1. What this unit proves

Setting. Fix a Wilson family, an extraction and the limit table \(S = S^{\text{mix}}\), together with a record Final for them. The unit uses the following parts of it.

  • The Euclidean axioms of \(S^{\text{mix}}\) (IV.1).

  • The phase decomposition of V.1:

    • a compact metric space \(X\) with a Borel probability measure \(\nu\) of full support;

    • the commutative C\(^{\ast}\)-algebra \(\mathfrak{A}\) generated by the time-slab operators of \(S^{\text{mix}}\), compressed to the ground space \(\mathcal{D}\) of the Osterwalder–Schrader (OS) space \(\mathcal{H}^{\text{OS}}\) of \(S^{\text{mix}}\);

    • continuous characters \(\chi_{\xi}\) of \(\mathfrak{A}\), indexed bijectively by \(X\), with \(\xi \mapsto \chi_{\xi} (M)\) continuous for every \(M \in \mathfrak{A}\) and \(\int_X \chi_{\xi} (M) \hspace{0.17em} d \nu = \langle \omega, M \omega \rangle\);

    • spatial translations acting trivially on \(\mathcal{D}\);

    • tables \(S^{\xi}\) satisfying the Euclidean axioms, with \(S^{\xi} (w) = \chi_{\xi} (M_w)\) for every word \(w\) of pairwise distinct points. Here \(M_w \in \mathfrak{A}\) is the compressed slab operator of a time translate of \(w\).

  • The relativistic theory \(\mathfrak{W}_{\xi_{\ast}}\) of V.2 at the selected phase \(\xi_{\ast}\), and the analytic continuations of \(S^{\text{mix}}\) and \(S^{\xi_{\ast}}\).

  • The field algebra of VI.1 (derivatives, rotations, the Euclidean stress tensor \(T^{\text{E}}_{ab} \in F_4\)), and the Euclidean Ward identities in every phase.

Notation.

  • OS side. For each \(\xi\), \(\mathcal{H}^{\text{OS}}_{\xi}\) is an OS space of \(S^{\xi}\) for the time axis. It carries the transfer semigroup \(e^{- sH^{\text{OS}}_{\xi}}\) and the Euclidean vacuum \(\omega_{\xi}\). Its ground space is \(\mathcal{D}_{\xi} = \{v : e^{- sH^{\text{OS}}_{\xi}} v = v \text{for all } s \geqslant 0\}\).

  • Wightman theory. \(\mathfrak{W}_{\xi} = (\mathcal{H}_{\xi}, \mathfrak{D}_{\xi}, \Omega_{\xi}, \phi_{\xi}, U_{\xi}, P_{\xi})\) consists of:

    • a Hilbert space \(\mathcal{H}_{\xi}\) and a dense common invariant domain \(\mathfrak{D}_{\xi}\) spanned by word vectors;

    • a unit vacuum \(\Omega_{\xi}\);

    • fields \(\phi_{\xi} (a, f)\) for labels \(a \in \mathbb{F}\) (the common label space generated by the \(F_d\)) and \(f \in \mathcal{S} (\mathbb{R}^4)\);

    • a strongly continuous unitary Poincaré representation \(U_{\xi}\) and an energy–momentum spectral measure \(P_{\xi}\).

  • Time and energy. Time is the fourth coordinate. \(H_{\xi}\) generates the time translations, and \(P^0_{\xi} = P_{\xi} (\{p^4 = 0\})\).

  • Words. A finite sum of words is \(A = \sum_k c_k \hspace{0.17em} \phi (a_{k 1}, f_{k 1}) \cdots \phi (a_{kn_k}, f_{kn_k})\). It is compactly supported if every \(f_{kj} \in C^{\infty}_c (\mathbb{R}^4)\). \(A^{\dagger}\) is its formal adjoint (conjugate coefficients and tests, apply the star operation to labels, and reverse each word). The spectral measure of \(A \Omega_{\xi}\) is \(\mu^{\xi}_A (B) = \langle A \Omega_{\xi}, P_{\xi} (B) A \Omega_{\xi} \rangle\).

  • Continuations. \(\textsf{F}^{\xi}_n (a ; z, \vec{x})\) is the analytic continuation of \(S^{\xi}\) to complex times with \(\text{Im} z_1 < \ldots < \text{Im} z_n\). \(W^{\xi}_n (a ; g)\), for \(g \in \mathcal{S} ((\mathbb{R}^4)^n)\), are its boundary distributions. \(W^{\text{mix}}_n\) are those of the continuation of \(S^{\text{mix}}\) (V.2).

Proposition V.3.1 (reconstruction in every phase). There is a family indexed by \(X\) with the following data for each \(\xi \in X\):

  • an OS space \(\mathcal{H}^{\text{OS}}_{\xi}\) of \(S^{\xi}\) with its time-slab operators;

  • a continuation \(\textsf{F}^{\xi}\) of \(S^{\xi}\) with boundary distributions \(W^{\xi}\);

  • a Wightman theory \(\mathfrak{W}_{\xi}\) on the label space \(\mathbb{F}\), with the same star operation and the same Poincaré action on labels as \(\mathfrak{W}_{\xi_{\ast}}\);

  • an isometry \(J_{\xi} : \mathcal{H}_{\xi} \to \mathcal{H}^{\text{OS}}_{\xi}\) with \(J_{\xi} \Omega_{\xi} = \omega_{\xi}\). It intertwines the space–time translations and sends word vectors to the boundary values of the corresponding Euclidean vectors at imaginary times (Lemma V.3.b).

At \(\xi = \xi_{\ast}\) these are the data of V.1 and V.2. For every \(\xi \in X\), with no exceptional set, the following hold.

  1. Wightman axioms.

    • The vacuum expectation values of products of fields are \(W^{\xi}\).

    • The Hilbert-space adjoint of \(\phi_{\xi} (a, f)\) extends \(\phi_{\xi} (a^{\ast}, \bar{f})\).

    • Fields with spacelike separated supports commute on \(\mathfrak{D}_{\xi}\).

    • \(U_{\xi}\) implements Poincaré covariance.

    • The joint spectrum of the translations lies in the closed forward cone \(\bar{V}_+\).

    • For every nonzero label \(a\), there is a test function \(f\) for which \(\phi_{\xi} (a, f) \ne 0\) on \(\mathfrak{D}_{\xi}\).

    • Every translation-invariant vector is a multiple of \(\Omega_{\xi}\).

  2. Surjectivity. \(J_{\xi}\) is unitary.

  3. Euclidean vacuum. \(\mathcal{D}_{\xi} =\mathbb{C} \omega_{\xi}\).

  4. Zero energy. For every \(x \in \mathcal{H}_{\xi}\), \(P^0_{\xi} x = x\) if and only if \(x \in \mathbb{C} \Omega_{\xi}\).

  5. Low-energy bound. For every compactly supported finite sum of words \(A\) there is \(M \geqslant 0\) such that \(\mu^{\xi}_A (\{0 < p^4 \leqslant u\}) \leqslant Mu^2\) for all \(\xi \in X\) and \(u \in (0, 1]\).

  6. Heat decay. For every such \(A\) there is \(M \geqslant 0\) such that, for all \(\xi \in X\) and \(s \geqslant 1\),

\(\displaystyle 0 \leqslant \langle A \Omega_{\xi}, (e^{- sH_{\xi}} - P^0_{\xi}) A \Omega_{\xi} \rangle \leqslant Ms^{- 2} .\)

  1. Irreducibility. For a bounded complex-linear operator \(B\) on \(\mathcal{H}_{\xi}\), the following are equivalent: \(B \in \mathbb{C} \hspace{0.17em} 1\); and, for every \(a\), \(f\) and \(u, v \in \mathfrak{D}_{\xi}\), one has \(u \in \text{dom} \hspace{0.17em} \phi_{\xi} (a, f)^{\ast}\) and \(\langle \phi_{\xi} (a, f)^{\ast} u, Bv \rangle = \langle u, B \phi_{\xi} (a, f) v \rangle\).

  2. Stress tensor. The conclusions (T0)–(T6) of Proposition V.3.y hold for \(\mathfrak{W}_{\xi}\).

  3. Dependence on the phase.

    • For all \(n\), \(a \in \mathbb{F}^n\) and \(g \in \mathcal{S} ((\mathbb{R}^4)^n)\), the map \(\xi \mapsto W^{\xi}_n (a ; g)\) is continuous and \(\int_X W^{\xi}_n (a ; g) \hspace{0.17em} d \nu (\xi) = W^{\text{mix}}_n (a ; g)\).

    • For every word \(w\) of pairwise distinct points, \(\int_X S^{\xi} (w) \hspace{0.17em} d \nu (\xi) = S^{\text{mix}} (w)\).

In item 6, \(e^{- sH_{\xi}}\) is defined as \(J_{\xi}^{\ast} e^{- sH^{\text{OS}}_{\xi}} J_{\xi}\). Since \(J_{\xi}\) intertwines the spectral projections (V.3.b), this equals \(\int e^{- sp^4} \hspace{0.17em} dP_{\xi} (p)\). The projection subtracted in item 6 is the full zero-energy projection. Items 5 and 6 are therefore proved before item 4 and independently of it.

3. Strategy

V.2 reconstructs a relativistic theory at a selected phase \(\xi_{\ast}\). The same construction applies to every \(S^{\xi}\) before vacuum uniqueness is known. The norm of each phase’s slab operator is bounded by the corresponding norm for \(S^{\text{mix}}\) (V.3.a): repeated reflection gives a power bound, and the fixed norm of the character disappears on taking roots. This gives polynomial continuation bounds uniform in \(\xi\). Character continuity at imaginary times propagates to complex times by Vitali’s theorem, then to boundary distributions by a Taylor formula with an integrable remainder. Integration over phases commutes with both passages (V.3.d–V.3.h).

Vacuum uniqueness holds almost everywhere by the phase decomposition (V.3.c). To extend it to every phase, fix a compactly supported finite sum of words \(A\) and set

\(\displaystyle r_{\xi} (s) = \langle A \Omega_{\xi}, e^{- sH_{\xi}} A \Omega_{\xi} \rangle - | \langle \Omega_{\xi}, A \Omega_{\xi} \rangle |^2 .\)

Locality makes the vacuum commutator vanish at large spatial separation for \(|t| \leqslant 1\). Its time-averaged odd part is the spatial Fourier transform of a positive measure, weighted by \((1 - \cos E) / E\). A momentum bump of radius \(u\) bounds its shell mass by \(O (u^2)\): the bump contributes \(u^3\), while the weight contributes \(u\). Dyadic summation and a Laplace transform give decay \(Ms^{- 2}\), uniformly in \(\xi\), after subtracting the full zero-energy projection (V.3.k–V.3.o). Almost everywhere this is the vacuum projection. Since \(r_{\xi} (s)\) is continuous, the same estimate holds everywhere by full support of \(\nu\). Letting \(s \to \infty\) identifies the zero-energy projection with the vacuum projection at every phase (V.3.q–V.3.r). This needs no continuity of the zero-energy projection itself.

Separately, positive-height Euclidean vectors are images of Wightman vectors with exponentially weighted frequency tests, so \(J_{\xi}\) is onto (V.3.i–V.3.j). Vacuum uniqueness therefore holds on both sides. A Liouville argument gives irreducibility. Finally, continuation of the Euclidean Ward identities gives the local stress-charge commutator (V.3.u–V.3.y). The removal of the exceptional set uses uniform low-energy control from locality, rather than a mass gap.

4. Steps

4.1 Slab bounds and the family of reconstructions

Lemma V.3.a (slab operators of a phase are dominated). Fix \(\xi \in X\). Let \(\mathcal{K}\) be any OS space of \(S^{\xi}\) for the time axis, and \(\Pi_{\xi}\) any family of slab operators on it. Then for every thickness \(d > 0\) and every word \(w\) of pairwise distinct points in the slab \((0, d)\),

\(\displaystyle \| \Pi_{\xi} (d, w)\| \leqslant \| \Pi (d, w)\|,\)

where \(\Pi\) are the slab operators of \(S^{\text{mix}}\) on \(\mathcal{H}^{\text{OS}}\). Here \(\Pi (d, w)\) maps the OS vector of a positive-time word \(v\) to that of \(w\) followed by \(v\) translated by \(d\).

Sketch.

  1. Let \(u = \Pi (d, w)\) and \(D > \|u\|\). For a positive-time word \(v\), let \(X (v)\) be the slab operator of \(v\) restricted to the ground space \(\mathcal{D}\).

  2. The compression of the doubled word \(\theta v \hspace{0.17em} v\) is \(X (v)^{\ast} X (v) \in \mathfrak{A}\). Let \(r_n\) be \(v\) with \(n\) reflected pairs of copies of \(w\) inserted. Then \(X (r_n) = (u^{\ast} u)^n X (v)\).

  3. Hence the diagonal Gram entry of \(r_n\) in \(S^{\xi}\) satisfies

\(\displaystyle |S^{\xi} (\theta r_n \hspace{0.17em} r_n) | = | \chi_{\xi} (X (r_n)^{\ast} X (r_n)) | \leqslant \| \chi_{\xi} \| \hspace{0.17em} \|X (v)\|^2 D^{4 n} .\)

  1. The power argument (iterated Cauchy–Schwarz on the span of OS vectors) turns this into an operator on \(\mathcal{K}\) of norm at most \(D\) that inserts \(w\). The fixed factor \(\| \chi_{\xi} \| \hspace{0.17em} \|X (v)\|^2\) disappears in the limit.

  2. Slab operators are determined by their action on OS vectors, so this operator is \(\Pi_{\xi} (d, w)\). Finally let \(D \downarrow \|u\|\).

Lemma V.3.b (the family of reconstructions). For every \(\xi\) there are:

  • (i) an OS space \(\mathcal{H}^{\text{OS}}_{\xi}\) of \(S^{\xi}\) with slab operators \(\Pi_{\xi}\) satisfying the slab laws (copy bounds, smoothness in the positions, derivative rule), with the copy constants of \(S^{\text{mix}}\), hence uniformly in \(\xi\);

  • (ii) a continuation \(\textsf{F}^{\xi}\) of \(S^{\xi}\) with non-tangential boundary values \(W^{\xi}\);

  • (iii) a Wightman theory \(\mathfrak{W}_{\xi}\) with all properties of V.3.1(1) except uniqueness of the vacuum, with the star operation and label action of \(\mathfrak{W}_{\xi_{\ast}}\);

  • (iv) an isometry \(J_{\xi}\) with \(J_{\xi} \Omega_{\xi} = \omega_{\xi}\) and \(J_{\xi} U_{\xi} (a) = U^{\text{OS}}_{\xi} (a) J_{\xi}\) for \(a \in \mathbb{R}^4\), where \(U^{\text{OS}}_{\xi}\) is the OS translation group. It is uniquely determined by the following boundary property: Euclidean word vectors at positive imaginary heights, smeared with a joint Schwartz test \(g\), converge in Hilbert norm to \(J_{\xi} \Phi_{\xi} (a ; g)\) as the heights and slab widths tend to zero with the positive margins of V.3.i. Here \(\Phi_{\xi}\) is the continuous extension of word vectors to joint tests.

At \(\xi_{\ast}\) these are the data of V.1–V.2. Moreover:

  • \(J_{\xi} P_{\xi} (B) = P^{\text{OS}}_{\xi} (B) J_{\xi}\) for every Borel set \(B \subset \mathbb{R}^4\), so \(\mu^{\xi}_x\) is the spectral measure of \(J_{\xi} x\);

  • if \(\mathcal{D}_{\xi} =\mathbb{C} \omega_{\xi}\), then \(\mathfrak{W}_{\xi}\) satisfies all of V.3.1(1).

Sketch.

  • For \(\xi \ne \xi_{\ast}\), the OS space is built from \(S^{\xi}\) and the slab operators are those of V.3.a.

  • Their copy bounds are those of \(S^{\text{mix}}\), by V.3.a; the remaining slab laws follow from the copy bounds.

  • The continuation, the compatibility data and a theory without vacuum uniqueness come from the reconstruction machinery of V.2. The boundary property in (iv) determines the isometry on a dense span of word vectors.

  • At \(\xi_{\ast}\) the existing data are taken, so the construction does not alter V.2.

  • The intertwining of the spectral projections follows from intertwining the translations, by comparing characteristic functions.

  • The last assertion holds because a translation-invariant \(x\) has \(J_{\xi} x \in \mathcal{D}_{\xi}\) and \(J_{\xi}\) is injective.

Lemma V.3.c (unique Euclidean vacuum for almost every phase; from V.1). For \(\nu\)-almost every \(\xi\), \(\mathcal{D}_{\xi} =\mathbb{C} \omega_{\xi}\).

Sketch (a variance identity). For a positive-time word \(b\), let \(\Psi_{\xi} (b) \in \mathcal{H}^{\text{OS}}_{\xi}\) be its OS vector and \(Q_{\xi}\) the projection onto \(\mathcal{D}_{\xi}\).

  1. \(\|Q_{\xi} \Psi_{\xi} (b)\|^2 = \lim_{s \to \infty} S^{\xi} (\theta b_s \hspace{0.17em} b_s)\), where \(b_s\) is \(b\) translated by \(s\) in time. The terms are character values, continuous in \(\xi\), nonincreasing in \(s\) and dominated by the value at \(s = 0\).

  2. By dominated convergence and the barycenter identity, \(\int \|Q_{\xi} \Psi_{\xi} (b)\|^2 d \nu = \|Q \Psi (b)\|^2\).

  3. On the other hand \(S^{\xi} (b) = \chi_{\xi} (M_b)\) and \(M_b \omega = Q \Psi (b)\). Multiplicativity of characters gives \(\int |S^{\xi} (b) |^2 d \nu = \int \chi_{\xi} (M_b^{\ast} M_b) \hspace{0.17em} d \nu = \|Q \Psi (b)\|^2\).

  4. Hence the nonnegative function \(\|Q_{\xi} \Psi_{\xi} (b)\|^2 - |S^{\xi} (b) |^2 = \|Q_{\xi} \Psi_{\xi} (b) - S^{\xi} (b) \omega_{\xi} \|^2\) has integral zero, so \(Q_{\xi} \Psi_{\xi} (b) \in \mathbb{C} \omega_{\xi}\) almost everywhere.

  5. Let \(b\) run over a countable family of words whose OS vectors are total in every OS space (Work.B14.universal).

4.2 Continuity in the phase and barycenter

Lemma V.3.d (polynomial bound, uniform in the phase). For every \(n\) and \(a \in \mathbb{F}^n\) there are \(m \in \mathbb{N}\) and \(C_0 \geqslant 0\) with the following property. For all \(\xi\), all real configurations \(x\), and all heights \(y\) with \(y_j - y_i \geqslant k\) for \(i < j\), where \(k \in (0, 1]\),

\(\displaystyle | \textsf{F}^{\xi}_n (a ; \hspace{0.17em} t (x) + iy, \hspace{0.17em} \vec{x}) | \leqslant C_0 \hspace{0.17em} k^{- m} .\)

Sketch.

  • \(\textsf{F}^{\xi}_n\) at such points is a matrix element, between \(\omega_{\xi}\) and \(\omega_{\xi}\), of a product of contractions (complex-time evolutions, spatial translations) and one-point slab operators of thickness of order \(k\).

  • Each one-point slab operator is bounded by the corresponding one for \(S^{\text{mix}}\) (V.3.a), which is polynomial in \(1 / k\) (input (ii) of §2).

  • Choose slab half-width \(p = k / 8\). With the constants of §2(ii), take \(m = \sum_j N_{a_j}\) and \(C_0 = \prod_j (Q_{a_j} 8^{N_{a_j}})\).

Lemma V.3.e (strict heights). For all \(n\), \(a\), real \(x\) and \(y_1 < \ldots < y_n\), the map \(\xi \mapsto \textsf{F}^{\xi}_n (a ; t (x) + iy, \vec{x})\) is continuous and its \(\nu\)-integral is \(\textsf{F}^{\text{mix}}_n (a ; t (x) + iy, \vec{x})\).

Lemma V.3.k (time-averaged commutator). Let \(\rho, \sigma\) be finite positive measures on \(\mathbb{R}^4\) supported in \(\bar{V}_+\), with \(\rho (\mathbb{R}^4) + \sigma (\mathbb{R}^4) \leqslant M\). Put

\(\displaystyle \hat{\rho} (\vec{x}, t) = \int e^{i (\vec{p} \cdot \vec{x} + p^4 t)} \hspace{0.17em} d \rho (p), \qquad a = \hat{\rho} (\cdot) - \hat{\sigma} (- \hspace{0.17em} \cdot), \qquad D (\vec{x}) = \int_0^1 \frac{a (\vec{x}, t) - a (\vec{x}, - t)}{2 i} \hspace{0.17em} dt.\)

Then \(|D| \leqslant M\). Write \(\mathcal{F}f (x) = \int e^{- i \vec{p} \cdot x} f (\vec{p}) \hspace{0.17em} d \vec{p}\), so \(c_3 = (2 \pi)^3\). If moreover \(D (\vec{x}) = 0\) for \(| \vec{x} | > R\), then for \(0 < u \leqslant 1\)

\(\displaystyle \rho (\{u / 2 < p^4 \leqslant u\}) \leqslant c (R, M) \hspace{0.17em} u^2, \qquad c (R, M) = 16 \pi \hspace{0.17em} |B_1 | \hspace{0.17em} |B_R | \hspace{0.17em} M / c_3 .\)

Sketch.

  1. By Fubini, \(D\) is the spatial Fourier transform of \(\rho^{\sharp} + \sigma^{\sharp}\). Here \(\rho^{\sharp}\) is the image under \(p \mapsto \vec{p}\) of \(\pi (p^4) \hspace{0.17em} d \rho (p)\), with \(\pi (E) = \int_0^1 \sin (Et) \hspace{0.17em} dt\), equal to \((1 - \cos E) / E\) for \(E > 0\) and to \(0\) at \(E = 0\), and \(\sigma^{\sharp}\) is the analogous image of \(\sigma\) under \(p \mapsto - \vec{p}\).

  2. On \(\bar{V}_+\) one has \(\pi (p^4) \geqslant 0\) at all energies, and \(\pi (E) \geqslant E / \pi\) for \(0 \leqslant E \leqslant 1\).

  3. Test \(D\) against \(\mathcal{F}f\), where \(f\) is a smooth spatial-momentum bump with \(0 \leqslant f \leqslant 1\), equal to \(1\) on the ball of radius \(u\) and supported in the ball of radius \(2 u\). Then \(|\mathcal{F}f| \leqslant |B_{2 u} | = 8 |B_1 | u^3\). Since \(D\) vanishes outside \(B_R\), the pairing is at most \(8 |B_1 | |B_R | M \hspace{0.17em} u^3\).

On that shell \(| \vec{p} | \leqslant p^4 \leqslant u\), so \(f = 1\) there, and \(\pi (p^4) \geqslant u / (2 \pi)\).

  5. Thus the exponent \(2\) is \(3\) (spatial dimension, from the volume of the momentum ball) minus \(1\) (first-order vanishing of the weight).

5. Where the difficulty lies

The delicate points are the common bounds near the analytic boundary, the recovery of positive-height Euclidean vectors from joint frequency tests, and the joint current limits in the stress-tensor argument. The last passage uses equal continuation germs only at spacelike separation; the current itself is spatially integrated. It gives a charge commutator for every admissible time profile, without defining a stress field at a fixed real time.

The low-energy estimate needs only locality, the spectrum condition and the two vector-norm bounds. Its positive weight avoids differentiating spectral integrals or imposing generator-domain bounds. The exponent \(2\) is compatible with the massless scalar measure, since \(\int_{| \vec{p} | \leqslant u} d^3 p / | \vec{p} | \sim u^2\). Thus this estimate does not imply a mass gap. For removal of the exceptional set, any decay rate tending to zero uniformly in \(\xi\) would suffice.

The remaining integrations, dyadic summation and assembly are routine once their uniform bounds have been established.

What looks unusual.

  • Item 8 quantifies over all admissible time profiles and spatial cutoffs. Admissible profiles and cutoffs exist (Stress.accepts, Stress.reservable); this existence assertion is separate from the commutator conclusion. The main theorem does not use it or the auxiliary results of Remark V.3.3.

  • The reconstruction agrees with V.2 at \(\xi_{\ast}\). The subsequent proof of vacuum uniqueness applies to every phase.

  • The Euclidean half of item 9 restates V.1, and the public clause (R12) is derived from it.

  • Nothing here or in the main theorem decides whether \(X\) is a single point. Each \(\mathfrak{W}_{\xi}\) is irreducible, while \(S^{\text{mix}}\) is the \(\nu\)-mixture of the \(S^{\xi}\).
