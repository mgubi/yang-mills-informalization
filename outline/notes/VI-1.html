Conventions.

  • Table. \(S\) is the limit table of IV.1. For labels \(v_i \in F_{d_i}\) and \(x \in \text{Conf}_n\) (pairwise distinct points of \(\mathbb{R}^4\)), \(S (v_1, \ldots, v_n ; x_1, \ldots, x_n)\) is a correlation kernel, smooth on \(\text{Conf}_n\).

  • Label spaces. \(F_d\) is the finite-dimensional complex space of labels of degree \(\leqslant d\) modulo null labels. It has inclusions \(\iota = \iota_{d \to e}\), a unit \(\boldsymbol{1}_d\), the action \(M_{\sigma}\) of the signed permutation matrices \(\sigma \in B_4 \subset O (4)\), and an antilinear conjugation \(\Gamma\).

  • Euclidean axioms:

    • permutation symmetry, normalisation of the empty correlation, deletion of unit insertions, and translation invariance;

    • covariance under \(B_4\) and \(\Gamma\);

    • reflection positivity in the coordinate hyperplanes, for point words and for smeared tests;

    • smoothness on configuration space;

    • for each fixed arity, labels and derivative order \(j\), constants \(C \geqslant 0\) and \(A \in \mathbb{N}\) such that \(\|D^j S (x)\| \leqslant Cr^{- A}\) whenever \(0 < r \leqslant 1\) and the points are separated by at least \(r\) in the maximum norm. The constants are uniform in the positions.

  • Differential plan. Complex linear maps \(\partial_a : F_d \to F_{d + 1}\) satisfying three laws:

    • the derivative law \(S (\ldots, \partial_a v_k, \ldots ; x) = \partial_{x_k^a} S (\ldots, v_k, \ldots ; x)\);

    • the inclusion law \(S (\ldots, \iota v_k, \ldots) = S (\ldots, v_k, \ldots)\);

    • orientation: \(\Gamma\) and \(M_{\sigma}\) commute with the inclusions, \(\partial\) commutes with \(\Gamma\), and \(M_{\sigma} \partial_b = \sum_a \sigma_{ab} \hspace{0.17em} \partial_a M_{\sigma}\). Commutation of \(\partial\) with the inclusions follows from the inclusion and derivative laws.

  • Rotations. \(\mathfrak{so} (4)\) is the space of real skew \(4 \times 4\) matrices with \([K, K'] = KK' - K' K\). \(K^{(ba)} = E_{ba} - E_{ab}\) generates the rotation field \(x_a e_b - x_b e_a\).

  • Spin rows. A system of spin rows is a family of \(\mathbb{R}\)-linear maps \(T : \mathfrak{so} (4) \to \text{End}_{\mathbb{C}} F_d\).

  • Angular sum. For spin rows \(T\) and maps \(P_b : F_d \to F_{d + 1}\),

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{W}_K [T, P] (q ; x) = \sum_{i = 1}^n \left[ S (\ldots, T_K q_i, \ldots ; x) + \sum_b (Kx_i)_b \hspace{0.17em} S (\ldots, P_b q_i, \ldots ; x) \right], \qquad \mathcal{W}_K [T] := \mathcal{W}_K [T, \partial] .\)

By the derivative law, \(\mathcal{W}_K [T] = 0\) says \(\frac{d}{dt} |_0 S (q ; e^{tK} x) = - \sum_i S (\ldots, T_K q_i, \ldots ; x)\). This is infinitesimal rotation covariance with spin part \(T\).

  • Lorentz group. Axes are numbered \(0, 1, 2, 3\); axis \(3\) is time and \(0, 1, 2\) are spatial. Write \(e_t = e_3\). The axis \(0\) used for the continuation in §4.4 has no distinguished physical role.

  • All identities hold at separated points. No contact term is evaluated.

1. What this unit proves

Inputs.

  • The table and its differential plan.

  • An unnormalised stress \(J = (J_{ab})\), \(J_{ab} \in F_4\), with complex linear raw translation maps \(p_a : F_d \to F_{d + 1}\) and real linear raw spin rows \(Y : \mathfrak{so} (4) \to \text{End} \hspace{0.17em} F_d\). These satisfy the shell identities below with unknown right-hand sides.

  • Free-Maxwell comparison data: a free table with its differential plan \(\partial^{\text{free}}\), rotation action \(R^{\text{free}}\) with generators \(\mathcal{L}^{\text{free}}_K\), stress \(J^{\text{free}}\) and Ward constant \(\kappa \ne 0\).

  • A weak scaling comparison of \(S\) with the free table.

The table and differential plan come from IV.1. The stress, shell maps and comparison data come from VI.2, using ultraviolet inputs as specified in §2. Every result below assumes the Euclidean axioms for \(S\), unless it is a statement solely about groups or operators.

Outputs. Given the differential plan and the comparison inputs, the unit constructs:

  • an action of \(O (4)\) on every \(F_d\) whose generators are \(c^{- 1} Y\);

  • \(O (4)\)-covariance of \(S\);

  • a conserved, symmetric, real, \(O (4)\)-tensorial stress \(J'\) satisfying the Ward identity (13) of the assessment and its rotation analogue;

  • a Lorentz action on the labels, compatible with the rotation action.

Shells and stress laws. A window for \(x = (x_0, \ldots, x_n) \in \text{Conf}_{n + 1}\) is a real function \(\chi \in C^{\infty}_c (\mathbb{R}^4)\) that equals \(1\) near \(x_0\) and whose closed support excludes \(x_1, \ldots, x_n\). For \(B \in F_d\) and tags \(q\) at \(x_1, \ldots, x_n\), the two shells are

\(\displaystyle \Phi^{\text{tr}}_{\chi} (b) = - \sum_a \int \partial_a \chi (y) \hspace{0.17em} S (J_{ab} (y), B (x_0), q) \hspace{0.17em} dy, \qquad \Phi^{\text{rot}}_{\chi} (K) = - \sum_{a, b} \int \partial_a \chi (y) \hspace{0.17em} (K (y - x_0))_b \hspace{0.17em} S (J_{ab} (y), B (x_0), q) \hspace{0.17em} dy.\)

The integrands vanish near all insertions. Given maps \(P_b : F_d \to F_{d + 1}\) and \(A : \mathfrak{so} (4) \to \text{End} \hspace{0.17em} F_d\), the triple \((J ; P, A)\) satisfies the stress laws if

\(\displaystyle \Phi^{\text{tr}}_{\chi} (b) = S (P_b B (x_0), q) \qquad \text{and} \qquad \Phi^{\text{rot}}_{\chi} (K) = S (A_K B (x_0), q)\)

for all windows, all \(B\) and all \(q\) (Table.StressLaws, shellGrad, shellSpin, Window).

Raw hypotheses (R) (Climb.Pre):

  • \(J\) is conserved: symmetric, \(\Gamma\)-real, and \(\sum_a \partial_a J_{ab} = 0\) in \(F_5\) (Conserved);

  • \(J\) is raw-tensorial: \(M_{\sigma} J_{ab} = \sum_{ij} \sigma_{ia} \sigma_{jb} J_{ij}\) for \(\sigma \in B_4\) (RawTensor);

  • \((J ; p, Y)\) satisfies the stress laws.

3. Strategy

Conservation and the raw shell laws make the maps \(p\) and \(Y\) compatible with inclusions, conjugation and signed permutations. Moving the center inside a fixed window gives their commutators with derivatives. To obtain a global rotation identity on a coordinate line, test the stress against a compact vector field that equals the rotation field near the insertions. Its cutoff has widths \(\lambda^{100}\) in the rotation plane, \(\lambda^{150}\) in the line direction if that direction is transverse to the plane, and \(\lambda^{300}\) in the remaining directions. Two derivative terms cancel by symmetry of the stress. Reflection positivity and spectral estimates make the remaining exterior flux \(O (\lambda^{- 1})\), so the collinear angular sum vanishes. Translation along the line then gives the transverse translation sums.

The free comparison identifies \(p = c \partial\): finitely many collinear tests have a one-dimensional kernel in the free vector-map space, and this rank condition persists along a sufficiently accurate scaling system. Dividing by \(c\) gives the collinear Ward identity for \(D = c^{- 1} Y\). Its derivatives determine the transverse Taylor series of each two-point function from an explicit analytic comparison function built from radial correlations. Positivity turns the resulting bounds on Gram-kernel jets into factorial bounds for derivatives of the corresponding OS vectors. Correlations are therefore analytic when one insertion moves while remaining extreme in some coordinate. The Ward identity propagates along finite chains of these moves; density gives it at every separated configuration.

Differentiating the global Ward identity twice gives the \(\mathfrak{so} (4)\) brackets, after row erasure is transported from the free theory. A relative-Casimir polynomial and McWeeny iteration produce exact intertwiners with the free rotation generators. Transporting the free action through them gives \(O (4)\) actions on each degree. The Ward identity implies covariance, which in turn gives compatibility with inclusions, the unit and derivatives. Haar averaging of \(c^{- 1} J\) gives the tensorial stress \(J'\) without changing its Ward laws. The free Lorentz action is transported through the same intertwiners; uniqueness for the boost ODEs makes it compatible across degrees.

4.1 Local structure of the raw shell maps (A01–A03)

Lemma VI.1.a (A01: compatibility). Assume (R), the derivative law and the inclusion law. Then:

  • \(p\) lies in the colour space (Climb.ColorLaw): \(p_i \boldsymbol{1}= 0\); \(p\) commutes with \(\iota\) and \(\Gamma\); \(M_{\sigma} p_i = \sum_j \sigma_{ji} \hspace{0.17em} p_j M_{\sigma}\).

Lemma VI.1.s (A12: the intertwiner projection). There is a real polynomial \(z\) such that \(P = z (\Omega (C, C))\) satisfies:

Lemma VI.1.t (approximate to exact intertwiners).

  • (i) Purification. In a Banach algebra, let \(Z_n \to z\) with \(z^2 = z\). There are \(C > 0\) and \(n_0\) such that, for \(n \geqslant n_0\), the iterates of \(\varphi (x) = 3 x^2 - 2 x^3\) starting at \(Z_n\) converge to idempotents \(P_n \to z\), with \(\|P_n - Z_n \| \leqslant 2 C \|Z_n^2 - Z_n \|\). This uses \(\varphi (x) - x = (1 - 2 x) (x^2 - x)\) and \(\text{def} (\varphi x) = (4 \hspace{0.17em} \text{def} \hspace{0.17em} x - 3) (\text{def} \hspace{0.17em} x)^2\), where \(\text{def} \hspace{0.17em} x = x^2 - x\).

  • (ii) Norm gap. If operators \(D_{\pi}\) satisfy the perfect relations and all \(\|D_{\pi} \| < \tfrac{1}{2}\), then all \(D_{\pi} = 0\), since \(\max \|D\| \leqslant 2 \max \|D\|^2\).

  • (iii) Exact intertwiners. Assume:

    • tuples \(B_n \to C\), with \(B_n\) (eventually) and \(C\) satisfying the perfect relations;

    • \(P = z (\Omega (C, C))\) is as in VI.1.s;

    • there is a closed additive subgroup \(\mathcal{F} \subset \text{End}_{\mathbb{C}} F^{\text{free}}_d\) containing \(\text{id}\) such that, eventually, \(z (\Omega (B_n, C))\) preserves \(\mathcal{F}\) and commutes with every \(\text{ad}_{B_{n, \pi}, C_{\pi}}\).

    Then for large \(n\) there is an invertible \(f \in \mathcal{F}\) with \(B_{n, \pi} f = fC_{\pi}\) for all \(\pi\).

Proof sketch of (iii).

  • Work in \(\text{End} (\text{End} \hspace{0.17em} F^{\text{free}}_d)\) with \(D_{\pi} (n) = \text{ad}_{B_{n, \pi}, C_{\pi}}\). These satisfy the perfect relations and converge to \(\text{ad}_{C_{\pi}, C_{\pi}}\).

  • The compressions \(D_{\pi} (n) Q_n\) satisfy the perfect relations and tend in norm to \(\text{ad}_{C_{\pi}, C_{\pi}} P = 0\). By (ii) they vanish for large \(n\).

  • Then \(f_n = Q_n (\text{id}) \to P (\text{id}) = \text{id}\), so \(f_n\) is invertible. It lies in \(\mathcal{F}\), and \(\text{ad}_{B_{n, \pi}, C_{\pi}} f_n = 0\).

4.10 Free-field stress algebra

Assume \(G\) is simple. The free stress satisfies the following finite-dimensional algebraic facts:

  • adjoint-invariant linear forms vanish, and invariant bilinear forms are multiples of the inner product.

  • Gauge-invariant composites of weight \(\leqslant 5\) have explicit normal forms.

  • The real symmetric \(B_4\)-covariant \(2\)-tensors of weight at most \(4\) form a space of dimension \(\leqslant 4\), spanned by the four-parameter family

\(\displaystyle T_{ab} = \delta_{ab} \left( w_0 + w_1 \sum_i \langle F_{ai}, F_{ai} \rangle + w_2 \sum_{ij} \langle F_{ij}, F_{ij} \rangle \right) + (1 - \delta_{ab}) \hspace{0.17em} w_3 \sum_i \langle F_{ai}, F_{bi} \rangle .\)

Here \(F_{ab}\) is the free curvature and \(w \in \mathbb{R}^4\). The Maxwell stress is \(w = (0, 1, - \tfrac{1}{4}, 1)\).

  • Wick's theorem computes the free one- and two-point tests. Centring and conservation force \(w = w_3 \cdot (0, 1, - \tfrac{1}{4}, 1)\).

These facts feed VII.2's statement that, in the free table, the divergence map from centred, real, symmetric, \(B_4\)-tensorial stresses in \((F^{\text{free}}_4)^{4 \times 4}\) to real \(B_4\)-vector fields in \((F^{\text{free}}_5)^4\) is onto and has a one-dimensional kernel (free_rank). They are not used in the Ward chain above.

5. Where the difficulty lies

The exterior flux estimate (VI.1.e) bears most of the analytic burden. Reflection positivity along axes of opposite parity gives the subpower estimate without a gap. Diagonal stress components require a separate conservation argument, since they have no odd reflection parity. The widths \(\lambda^{100}, \lambda^{150}, \lambda^{300}\) and the split at \(\lambda^{- 3000}\) make the powers in these estimates compatible.

Continuation off the line (VI.1.j–p) requires more than a smooth function with vanishing jets. The collinear Ward identities determine the transverse jets of the two-point function; Hilbert-space positivity turns those jets into uniform factorial derivative bounds and hence analyticity.

The descent (VI.1.s–u) constructs exact intertwiners from the free comparison. Lie brackets alone would give an action of a covering group after integration; they would not by themselves identify the required \(O (4)\) action or its signed-permutation subgroup. Covariance subsequently forces compatibility between degrees.

The remaining steps use compactly supported integration by parts, finite-dimensional tests, group generation, Haar averaging and uniqueness for linear ODEs.
