1. What this unit proves

The free Maxwell theory supplies the comparison model for the stress tensor of the Wilson limit. Its local Ward identities fix the sign and normalization of the free stress. For a simple gauge group, a finite-dimensional calculation identifies its centred conserved tensors of degree at most four as a line. Short-distance comparison transfers this line to the interacting limit; the translation Ward identity then fixes the normalization of the interacting stress.

Throughout, \(G\) is a gauge group as in I.1, \(\mathfrak{g}\) is its Lie algebra, and \(c = 1, \ldots, \dim \mathfrak{g}\) indexes an orthonormal colour basis for the invariant inner product. Spacetime indices range over \(0, 1, 2, 3\). Inner products of \(\mathfrak{g}\)-valued forms include the colour sum.

A Maxwell wave on an open set \(U \subset \mathbb{R}^4\) is a smooth \(\mathfrak{g}\)-valued antisymmetric field \(f_{ab}\) satisfying

\(\displaystyle \partial_a f_{bc} + \partial_b f_{ca} + \partial_c f_{ab} = 0, \qquad (\text{Div} \hspace{0.17em} f)_b := \sum_a \partial_a f_{ab} = 0.\)

Here \(\text{Div} = - d^{\ast}\) with the usual Euclidean codifferential convention. The space \(\text{Sh}_j\) consists of the homogeneous Taylor coefficients of order \(j\) satisfying these linear equations, not the cumulative jets through order \(j\). The algebra \(\mathcal{W}\) consists of complex polynomials in coordinates on these spaces; a coordinate from \(\text{Sh}_j\) has weight \(j + 2\). Its shell sector \(\mathcal{P}_d\) is the image of labels of degree at most \(d\) under substitution of on-shell Taylor coefficients. Equivalently, it consists of the \(\text{Ad} (G)\)-invariant polynomials of weight at most \(d\) (sector). A probe of order \(j\) is a real linear functional on \(\text{Sh}_j\); \(p (f) (x)\) denotes its value on the order-\(j\) Taylor coefficient of \(f\) at \(x\).

The curvature propagator is

\(\displaystyle \langle F^c_{im} (x) F^{c'}_{ab} (y) \rangle_0 = \delta^{cc'} \mathcal{C}_{im, ab} (x - y), \qquad G_0 (x) = \frac{1}{4 \pi^2 |x|^2}, \qquad - \Delta G_0 = \delta,\)

where \(\mathcal{C}\) is the curl–curl of \(G_0\). The generated field \(f^{p, x} (y) = \langle p (F) (x) F (y) \rangle_0\) is a Maxwell wave off \(x\). Correlations of Wick polynomials at distinct sites sum over complete pairings of legs between different sites; there are no contractions within a site.

Write

\(\displaystyle T^{\text{free}}_{ab} = \sum_{c, i} F^c_{ai} F^c_{bi} - \frac{1}{4} \delta_{ab} \sum_{c, i, k} F^c_{ik} F^c_{ik} \in \mathcal{P}_4 .\)

Its polarization and the current associated with a vector field \(v\) are

\(\displaystyle \Theta_{ab} (f, g) = \sum_i (\langle f_{ai}, g_{bi} \rangle + \langle f_{bi}, g_{ai} \rangle) - \frac{1}{2} \delta_{ab} \sum_{i, k} \langle f_{ik}, g_{ik} \rangle, \qquad j^v_a (f, g) = \sum_b v_b \Theta_{ab} (f, g) .\)

Thus \(\Theta (f, f) = 2 T^{\text{free}} (f)\). Both tensors are symmetric and traceless. For a real cutoff \(\chi \in C_c^{\infty} (\mathbb{R}^4)\), the outward flux is \(\Phi_{\chi} (j) = - \sum_a \int \partial_a \chi \hspace{0.17em} j_a\).

The free table on a register \(L\) is \(\mathcal{M}= \text{Bel} (L)\), with filtered field spaces \(\mathcal{F}^{\text{free}}_d\). The Wilson limit table \(S\) has spaces \(\mathcal{F}^S_d\). For a lighthouse \(\mathcal{T}\), write \(T_0\) for its free stress and \(\kappa\) for its Ward constant. Let \(\text{Conf}_n\) denote the distinct-point configurations. For \(R > 0\), set

\(\displaystyle K_{R, n} = \{y \in (\mathbb{R}^4)^n : |y_i - y_j |_{\infty} \geqslant R^{- 1} (i \ne j), \|y\| \leqslant R\},\)

where \(\|y\|\) is the Euclidean norm of the full configuration.

Proposition VI.2.1 (the free Maxwell table). For every register \(L\), the free table \(\mathcal{M}= \text{Bel} (L)\) has the following properties.

(a) For every \(d \geqslant 0\), substitution of on-shell Taylor coefficients induces a complex linear isomorphism \(\iota_d : \mathcal{F}^{\text{free}}_d \to \mathcal{P}_d\). A label is null exactly when its polynomial vanishes on every on-shell Taylor tower. At distinct points, field correlations equal the Wick moments of their images under \(\iota\).

(b) The table satisfies the Euclidean hierarchy of IV.1: permutation symmetry, the unit laws, covariance under signed coordinate permutations, reality, translation invariance, point and smeared reflection positivity, and smoothness on \(\text{Conf}_n\). The correlations are in fact real-analytic there. For each fixed tuple of fields and derivative order, there are constants \(A \geqslant 0\) and \(C \geqslant 0\) bounding that derivative by \(Cr^{- A}\) on configurations with mutual sup-distances at least \(r\), for \(0 < r \leqslant 1\).

(c) Differentiation of Taylor coefficients induces maps \(\partial^{\text{free}}_a : \mathcal{F}^{\text{free}}_d \to \mathcal{F}^{\text{free}}_{d + 1}\) satisfying the inclusion, derivative and orientation laws. In particular,

\(\displaystyle \langle (\partial^{\text{free}}_a B) (x_0) \cdots \rangle^{\text{free}} = \partial_{x_0^a} \langle B (x_0) \cdots \rangle^{\text{free}} .\)

The action of \(O (4)\) on coefficients induces actions \(R_g\) on all field spaces and generators \(\rho (\omega)\) for \(\omega \in \mathfrak{so} (4)\). These satisfy the rotation laws of VI.1: continuity, the group law, extension of signed permutations, reality, preservation of the unit and inclusions, vector covariance of derivatives, and the infinitesimal-generator identity. All free correlations are rotation invariant.

(d) The fields \(\iota_4^{- 1} (T^{\text{free}}_{ab})\), also denoted \(T^{\text{free}}_{ab}\), are symmetric, real, traceless and centred, and satisfy \(\sum_a \partial^{\text{free}}_a T^{\text{free}}_{ab} = 0\) in \(\mathcal{F}^{\text{free}}_5\).

Status: theorem for every gauge group in the setting of I.1; simplicity is not required. Lean: Maxwell.Free.hierarchy, fields, no_residual, scheme, gear, gear_laws, gear_motion, original, realStress.

Proposition VI.2.2 (local Ward charge). Let \(U \subset \mathbb{R}^4\) be open, \(x \in U\), and \(f\) a Maxwell wave on \(U\). Let \(v\) be a smooth conformal Killing field on \(\mathbb{R}^4\):

\(\displaystyle \partial_a v_b = - \partial_b v_a \quad (a \ne b), \qquad \partial_0 v_0 = \partial_1 v_1 = \partial_2 v_2 = \partial_3 v_3 .\)

Let \(\chi \in C_c^{\infty} (U ; \mathbb{R})\) equal one near \(x\). Since \(df = 0\), its Lie derivative is

\(\displaystyle (\mathcal{L}_v f)_{il} = \sum_b [\partial_i (v_b f_{bl}) - \partial_l (v_b f_{bi})] = (d \iota_v f)_{il} .\)

(a) For every zero-order component probe \(F^c_{il}\),

\(\displaystyle \Phi_{\chi} (j^v (f^{F^c_{il}, x}, f)) = - (\mathcal{L}_v f)^c_{il} (x) .\)

(b) If \(\mathcal{L}_v f\) is also a Maxwell wave on \(U\), then, for every order \(j \geqslant 0\) and every probe \(p\) of that order,

\(\displaystyle \Phi_{\chi} (j^v (f^{p, x}, f)) = - p (\mathcal{L}_v f) (x) .\)

For translations \(v = e_a\), this is \(- \partial_a [p (f)] (x)\). For rotations \(v (y) = \omega (y - x)\), it is \(- (\omega \cdot p) (f) (x)\), where \(\omega \cdot p\) is the infinitesimal label action. For dilation \(v (y) = y - x\), it is \(- (j + 2) p (f) (x)\).

(c) For any two Maxwell waves \(f, g\) on a neighbourhood of \(\text{supp} \hspace{0.17em} \chi\), the flux \(\Phi_{\chi} (j^v (f, g))\) vanishes. It also vanishes for two fields \(f^{p, x}, f^{q, x}\) generated at the same point, provided \(v\) is homogeneous about \(x\) of degree \(e \in \{0, 1\}\).

Status: theorem, with the propagator, polarization and outward-flux conventions above. Lean: Maxwell.Free.local_force, jet_force, drift_flux, angular_flux, radial_flux, sourcefree_flux, two_flux_zero; definitions Killing, variation, pulse, singleFlux.

Proposition VI.2.3 (Ward identities of the free table). Let \(n \geqslant 0\), \(x \in \text{Conf}_{n + 1}\), and \(p_0, \ldots, p_n \in \mathcal{W}\). Let \(\chi \in C_c^{\infty} (\mathbb{R}^4 ; \mathbb{R})\) equal one near \(x_0\), with \(x_1, \ldots, x_n \not\in \text{supp} \hspace{0.17em} \chi\). Take one of the pairs

\(\displaystyle (v, D) = (e_a, \partial_a), \qquad (\omega (y - x_0), \rho (\omega)), \qquad (y - x_0, \varepsilon),\)

where \(a\) is an axis, \(\omega \in \mathfrak{so} (4)\), and \(\varepsilon\) multiplies each monomial by its weight. The operators act on \(\mathcal{W}\) by derivations. Then

\(\displaystyle - \sum_{a, b} \int \partial_a \chi (y) \hspace{0.17em} v_b (y) \hspace{0.17em} \langle T^{\text{free}}_{ab} (y) p_0 (x_0) \cdots p_n (x_n) \rangle_0 \hspace{0.17em} dy = - \langle (Dp_0) (x_0) p_1 (x_1) \cdots p_n (x_n) \rangle_0 .\)

Consequently, for every \(\tau \in \mathbb{R}\), the stress \(\tau T^{\text{free}}\) satisfies the translation and rotation Ward identities with rows \(- \tau \partial^{\text{free}}\) and \(- \tau \rho\). The stress normalized to give \(+ \partial^{\text{free}}\) in the translation identity is \(- T^{\text{free}}\).

Status: theorem. Lean: Maxwell.Free.moment_force, straight_motion, rotational_motion, radial_motion, beacon_signal, Table.StressLaws.

Proposition VI.2.4 (the free stress line). Suppose \(G\) is simple. Let \(W\) be a derivative plan on the free table satisfying the derivative and orientation laws. The derivative law determines its derivative maps uniquely as \(\partial^{\text{free}}\). Define the following real vector spaces:

  • \(\mathcal{Z}\) consists of symmetric real tensors \(J \in (\mathcal{F}^{\text{free}}_4)^{4 \times 4}\) that are covariant under signed coordinate permutations and centred: \(\langle J_{ab} \rangle = 0\).

  • \(\mathcal{V}\) consists of real vectors in \((\mathcal{F}^{\text{free}}_5)^4\) covariant under signed coordinate permutations.

The divergence \(\text{div} : \mathcal{Z} \to \mathcal{V}\), \((\text{div} J)_b = \sum_a W_a J_{ab}\), is surjective, and

\(\displaystyle \dim_{\mathbb{R}} \mathcal{Z}= 3, \qquad \dim_{\mathbb{R}} \mathcal{V}= 2, \qquad \ker \text{div} =\mathbb{R}T^{\text{free}} .\)

Thus a lighthouse with such a derivative plan and stress \(\tau T^{\text{free}}\), \(\tau \ne 0\), satisfies (FD): its divergence is onto, its kernel has real dimension one, and its stress is a nonzero centred conserved tensor.

Status: theorem. Lean: FreeStress.rank_on, line_dim, lighting, certificate, FreeDiv, KernelSpace, Vectors, currentDiv.

Proposition VI.2.5 (Maxwell data). Suppose \(G\) is simple, let \(C\) be a Wilson family for \(G\), and let \(\tau \in \mathbb{R} \setminus \{0\}\).

(a) The lighthouse \(\mathcal{T}_{\tau} = (\partial^{\text{free}}, R, \tau T^{\text{free}}, \kappa = - \tau)\) satisfies all lighthouse laws of VI.1. These include the derivative, rotation and Ward laws of VI.2.1–3, a nonzero derivative in degree four, and the following selection property. There is \(N\) such that for every \(d \geqslant N\), finitely many derivatives of Ward sums at collinear configurations detect the line \(\mathbb{R} \partial^{\text{free}}\) among complex linear maps \(t_a : \mathcal{F}^{\text{free}}_d \to \mathcal{F}^{\text{free}}_{d + 1}\) satisfying:

  • they kill the unit, are real, and transform as a vector under signed permutations;

  • for every \(e \leqslant d\), \(t_a\) maps the image of \(\mathcal{F}^{\text{free}}_e\) into the image of \(\mathcal{F}^{\text{free}}_{e + 1}\).

For component \(t_b\), a diagram is an iterated directional derivative of

\(\displaystyle x \longmapsto \sum_i \langle w_1 (x_1) \cdots (t_b w_i) (x_i) \cdots w_n (x_n) \rangle^{\text{free}}, \qquad w_i \in \mathcal{F}^{\text{free}}_d,\)

evaluated at a distinct-point configuration on a coordinate axis perpendicular to \(e_b\). The selected diagrams all vanish exactly when \(t\) is a real multiple of the derivative tuple.

(b) There exists a Maxwell datum for \(C\) with lighthouse \(\mathcal{T}_{\tau}\). It also contains the free Euclidean hierarchy, the Casimir polynomial data of VI.1, compatible representations of the proper orthochronous Lorentz group on the field spaces and their union, continuous in each degree, and the following Ward-vanishing property. For every \(d \geqslant 4\), let \(T (u, v)\) be a real-bilinear family of complex linear endomorphisms of \(\mathcal{F}^{\text{free}}_d\), with \(u, v \in \mathfrak{so} (4)\). Suppose it is alternating, real, equivariant under orientation-preserving signed permutations, kills the unit, and preserves every lower-degree image. If

\(\displaystyle \sum_i \langle w_1 (x_1) \cdots (T (u, v) w_i) (x_i) \cdots w_m (x_m) \rangle = 0\)

for every \(m \geqslant 0\), every \(x \in \text{Conf}_m\), every \(w_i \in \mathcal{F}^{\text{free}}_d\) and every \(u, v\), then \(T = 0\). The Lorentz representation agrees with \(R\) on spatial rotations, commutes with time-reflected conjugation, and has boost generator \(- i \rho\) in each time–space plane.

(c) Every Maxwell datum can be replaced by one with the same derivative plan, rotations, hierarchy and Lorentz representation, stress \(\tau T^{\text{free}}\), full rotation covariance of that stress, and (FD). The Ward constant of any Maxwell datum with stress \(\tau T^{\text{free}}\) is \(- \tau\).

Status: theorem. The construction used in III.11 chooses \(\tau = 1\) and hence \(\kappa = - 1\). Lean: Maxwell.Free.herald, shines, maxwell_supplies, erasing, scanner, launched, FreeStress.fire, names, required; records Org.MaxwellData, Org.erases, Climb.Lighthouse, Lighthouse.Goes.

Proposition VI.2.6 (strict reflection positivity). Fix a coordinate axis \(a\), and let \(\theta_a\) reflect in \(y_a = 0\). Let \(\vartheta_a\) act on polynomials by the corresponding jet reflection and coefficient conjugation.

(a) For every \(x\) with \(x_a > 0\) and every nonzero \(p \in \mathcal{W}\),

\(\displaystyle \langle (\vartheta_a p) (\theta_a x) p (x) \rangle_0 > 0.\)

(b) For every finite set of distinct coordinate probes of the spaces \(\text{Sh}_j\), the reflected propagator Gram matrix at \((\theta_a x, x)\) is positive definite.

(c) The free table is reflection positive for finite families of words in the positive half-space, with distinct sites within each word.

(d) For every \(d \geqslant 0\), a polynomial \(p \in \mathcal{P}_d\) whose correlations with every finite family of spectators from \(\mathcal{P}_d\) vanish at every distinct-point configuration is zero.

Status: theorem. Lean: detects, coordGram, positive_table, ghosts_out.

Proposition VI.2.7 (a conserved stress on the limit table). Let \(C\) be a Wilson family satisfying the construction conditions of II.2. Let \(F\) be an extraction order with ordered smeared convergence and generic point limits to a table \(S\) with Euclidean hierarchy \(H\). Let \(W\) be a derivative plan on \(S\) satisfying the inclusion and derivative laws; its orientation law then follows from IV.1. Let \(H_0\) be the free Euclidean hierarchy, and \(\mathcal{T}\) a lighthouse whose plan satisfies the inclusion, derivative and orientation laws and for which (FD) holds. Assume the station property of VI.2.8 and

\(\displaystyle \dim_{\mathbb{C}} \mathcal{F}^S_d \leqslant \dim_{\mathbb{C}} \mathcal{F}^{\text{free}}_d \qquad (d \geqslant 0) .\)

(a) There exists a scaling \(X = (r_k, \xi_k, U_{k, d})\), where \(r_k > 0\) decreases strictly to zero, \(\xi_k > 0\), and \(U_{k, d} : \mathcal{F}^{\text{free}}_d \to \mathcal{F}^S_d\) is a complex linear isomorphism for every \(k, d\). It is approaching: for each fixed tuple \(v = (v_i)\) of free fields, the difference

\(\displaystyle K_k (y) = \langle U_k v_1 (r_k y_1) \cdots U_k v_n (r_k y_n) \rangle^S - \langle v_1 (y_1) \cdots v_n (y_n) \rangle^{\text{free}}\)

satisfies, for every compact \(K \subset \text{Conf}_n\),

\(\displaystyle \sup_{\text{\scriptsize{$\begin{array}{c} t \in C_c^{\infty} (\text{Conf}_n ; \mathbb{C})\\ \text{supp} \hspace{0.17em} t \subset K, \|t\|_{\infty} \leqslant 1 \end{array}$}}} \left| \int t (y) K_k (y) \hspace{0.17em} dy \right| \longrightarrow 0.\)

It is stable: the maps eventually commute with conjugation and signed permutations in each degree, preserve the unit in each degree, and intertwine each fixed inclusion of degrees. The initial choice may have \(\xi_k = 1\).

(b) For every approaching stable scaling, the divergence \(\text{div}^S : \mathcal{Z}^S \to \mathcal{V}^S\) is onto and has a one-dimensional real kernel. These spaces are defined as in VI.2.4, using \(S\) and \(W\).

(c) There exist a nonzero \(J \in \mathcal{Z}^S\) with \(\text{div}^S J = 0\) and an approaching stable refinement \(Y\) of \(X\), obtained by taking a subsequence and changing \(\xi_k\), such that

\(\displaystyle \xi_k^{- 1} U_{k, 4}^{- 1} (r_k^4 J_{ab}) \longrightarrow T_{0, ab} \qquad \text{for all } a, b.\)

(d) For every finite arity bound \(N\), degree bound \(d\), derivative order \(m\), and \(R \geqslant 1\), a tail of \(Y\) is a track: it intertwines symmetries, units and inclusions through degree \(\max (d, 4)\), satisfies (c), and has convergence of all correlation derivatives of orders \(0 \leqslant j \leqslant m\) uniformly on \(K_{R, n}\) for \(n \leqslant N\). The convergence is for each fixed tuple of fields of degrees at most \(d\); its tail may depend on that tuple and the error tolerance. In particular, \(J\) satisfies the comparison law of VI.1.

Status: theorem under these hypotheses. III.11 supplies the station estimates; the rank-corrected widths of the construction give the dimension bound; VI.2.4 gives (FD) for the canonical datum. To obtain the infrared and ultraviolet data used later, III.11 also supplies the shell estimates needed for the Ward rows of \(J\). Proposition VI.2.8 (weak comparison stations). Fix \(C, F, S, H, H_0\) as in VI.2.7. A station of depth \(D\) consists of \(r > 0\) and complex linear lifts \(A_{N, p, d} : \mathcal{F}^{\text{free}}_d \to \text{Ref}_d\) for \(d \leqslant D\) at every lattice cutoff \((N, p)\).

It is admissible for arity bound \(n_{\max}\), radius \(R \geqslant 1\) and tolerance \(\varepsilon > 0\) if:

  • the lifts are eventually uniformly bounded along the extraction order, real and signed-covariant, and preserve the degree-zero unit;

  • for every \(n \leqslant n_{\max}\), every tuple \(v_i\) of degrees at most \(D\), every smooth complex test \(t\) supported in \(K_{R, n}\), every \(M > 0\) with \(\|t\|_{\infty} \leqslant M\), and every \(\eta > 0\), eventually along the ordered cutoff tails,

\(\displaystyle \left. | \mathcal{S}_{N, p} (Av ; r^{- 4 n} t (\cdot / r)) - \int t (y) \langle v_1 (y_1) \cdots v_n (y_n) \rangle^{\text{free}} \hspace{0.17em} dy \right| \leqslant \varepsilon M \prod_i \|v_i \| + \eta .\)

Here \(\mathcal{S}_{N, p}\) is the lattice-smeared correlation of IV.1. The cutoff tail may depend on \(v, t, M, \eta\).

The station property requires an admissible station with \(r \leqslant \delta\) for every \(D, n_{\max}\), \(R \geqslant 1\) and \(\varepsilon, \delta > 0\).

(a) An admissible station yields a panel \(U_d : \mathcal{F}^{\text{free}}_d \to \mathcal{F}^S_d\) for \(d \leqslant D\). It is real, signed-covariant, preserves the degree-zero unit, and satisfies

\(\displaystyle \left| \int t (y) [\langle Uv_1 (ry_1) \cdots Uv_n (ry_n) \rangle^S - \langle v_1 (y_1) \cdots v_n (y_n) \rangle^{\text{free}}] dy \right| \leqslant \varepsilon M \prod_i \|v_i \|\)

for the same tuples and tests.

(b) Quantitative grid, strip and finite-difference estimates satisfying the balance conditions in VI.2.s, for every finite arity bound, degree bound and \(R \geqslant 1\), imply the station property.

Status: the station and balance conditions are definitions; (a) and (b) are theorems. III.11 supplies the balanced estimates through its complete weak-comparison result. Lean: Station, Station.Admissible, Stations, harvested, Granular, realizes_stations, MergeProof.East.CompleteWeak.

Proposition VI.2.9 (the normalized stress and its short-distance law). Let \(C\) be a Wilson family, \(F\) an extraction order, \(S\) a table with derivative plan \(W\), and \(M\) a Maxwell datum with lighthouse \(\mathcal{T}\) and Ward constant \(\kappa \ne 0\). Let \(I = (J_0, p^I, Y^I)\) and let \(X\) be a scaling. Assume the ultraviolet-data conditions of VIII.1 for \((C, F, S, W, M, I)\). In particular, they give a Euclidean hierarchy on \(S\), the orientation law for \(W\), a symmetric real conserved signed-covariant \(J_0\) with Ward rows \((p^I, Y^I)\), and tracks comparing \(J_0\) to \(T_0\). Assume also the inclusion and derivative laws for \(W\), and the following additional properties: \(J_0\) is a nonzero member of \(\mathcal{Z}^S\); \(\mathcal{T}\) satisfies (FD); and \(X\) is approaching and stable, with

\(\displaystyle \xi_k^{- 1} U_{k, 4}^{- 1} (r_k^4 J_{0, ab}) \longrightarrow T_{0, ab} .\)

Let \(J \in (\mathcal{F}^S_4)^{4 \times 4}\) be symmetric, real, conserved and covariant under signed permutations. Suppose it satisfies the translation Ward identity with rows \(W\) and the rotation Ward identity with some rotation rows. Then:

(a) \(U_{k, 4}^{- 1} (r_k^4 J_{ab}) \to \kappa^{- 1} T_{0, ab}\) for every \(a, b\).

(b) For every finite tuple of free fields \(q_i\), every assignment of slots to either \(U_k q_i\) or \(r_k^4 J_{a_i b_i}\), and every \(j \geqslant 0\), the resulting correlations at \(r_k y_i\) converge with their order-\(j\) derivatives uniformly on compact subsets of \(\text{Conf}_n\). The limit is the free correlation with each stress slot replaced by \(\kappa^{- 1} T_{0, a_i b_i}\).

(c) For the canonical datum with plan \(\partial^{\text{free}}\) and stress \(T^{\text{free}}\), \(\kappa = - 1\). Thus the Ward-normalized physical stress converges to \(- T^{\text{free}}\) without the factor \(\xi_k^{- 1}\).

Status: theorem. VI.1 supplies a stress \(J\) satisfying these Ward laws. The mixed-correlation conclusion follows from (a) and VI.2.u; the linked mixed-correlation theorem states its specialization to that stress. 3. Strategy

The free table is realized by invariant polynomials in on-shell curvature coefficients with Wick correlations. Translation and rotation covariance, analyticity and polynomial bounds follow from the propagator. Reflection positivity uses a Fock-space factorization. For two polynomials at mirror points, the reflected Gram matrix of their finitely many coordinate probes is positive definite. Factoring it as \(L^{\ast} L\) identifies their correlation with a Fock inner product after an invertible linear substitution. Strict positivity eliminates further null fields and identifies the free quotient with \(\mathcal{P}_d\).

For the Ward identity, replace the singular propagator by a smooth curl source. Its divergence is a derivative of an approximate identity. Two integrations by parts and removal of the regulator give the local charge \(-\mathcal{L}_v f\). Differentiation in the source point extends the charge to every Taylor order. Wick expansion then leaves three possibilities: both stress legs meet the marked site, giving zero by homogeneity; one meets that site and one a spectator, giving the desired derivation; or both meet spectators, giving a source-free current inside the cutoff.

The free stress line is computed in finite-dimensional spaces. Signed covariance allows three centred symmetric tensors in degree four and at most two vectors in degree five. Two explicit correlation tests prove that divergence has rank two. Its kernel is therefore the line generated by \(T^{\text{free}}\). A translation Ward test fixes the constant \(\kappa = - \tau\) for stress \(\tau T^{\text{free}}\).

Stations give comparison maps at scales tending to zero. Correct them recursively to intertwine inclusions. Finite separating tests make the corrected maps eventually injective; the dimension bound makes them isomorphisms. Integration by parts and the derivative law show that \(r_k U_k^{- 1} W_a U_k \to \partial^{\text{free}}_a\). The correlation differences, including those with convergent field coefficients, are closed under gradients, so the uniform test estimates improve to smooth local convergence.

Transport the interacting divergence to the free spaces, recentering tensors after transport. It converges to free divergence, so surjectivity and rank–nullity transfer the one-dimensional kernel to \(S\). Normalize a nonzero vector in this kernel to a sphere in the free stress space and pass to a subsequence. Its limit is a nonzero centred conserved free tensor, hence a multiple of \(T^{\text{free}}\); a single sign choice fixes the limit on the entire comparison sequence.

Finally, a Ward-normalized stress \(J\) on \(S\) has centred part proportional to the constructed stress: \(c (J - \langle J \rangle \boldsymbol{1}) = J_0\). Comparing the derivative and stress Ward identities along tracks gives \(c / \xi_k \to \kappa\). Thus \(U_k^{- 1} (r_k^4 J) \to \kappa^{- 1} T_0 = - T^{\text{free}}\) in the canonical normalization. This obtains the interacting stress from the finite-dimensional conservation law, then fixes its scale by the Ward identity.

Its transported limit would then contradict the nonzero free derivative. The kernel is a real line, so \(cJ_c = J_0\) for some \(c \ne 0\). Comparing their translation Ward identities gives \(p^I = cW\) in degree four.

Along tracks, the transported derivatives tend to \(\partial^{\text{free}}\), whereas the transported Ward rows of \(J_0\), divided by \(\xi_k\), tend to \(\kappa \partial^{\text{free}}\). Since the free derivative is nonzero, a separating linear functional gives \(c / \xi_k \to \kappa\). Therefore

\(\displaystyle U_{k, 4}^{- 1} (r_k^4 J_c) = \frac{\xi_k}{c} [\xi_k^{- 1} U_{k, 4}^{- 1} (r_k^4 J_0)] \longrightarrow \kappa^{- 1} T_0 .\)

The unit part tends to zero because \(r_k^4 \to 0\) and the maps eventually preserve the unit. VI.2.u applies to these convergent preimages in every combination of slots, giving the mixed-correlation limit. VI.2.n and the equality of the two displayed free-stress constructions give the canonical limit \(- T^{\text{free}}\). Lean: centered_line, ratio, meter_physical, mixed_leading, tracks_ratio.

5. Where the difficulty lies

The transfer to \(S\) depends on exact field-space isomorphisms, not merely comparison maps. Finite separating tests and the upper dimension bound provide them. The derivative law then turns the uniform test estimates into convergence of transported derivatives and smooth correlations. Recentring is necessary before transferring divergence: a field-space isomorphism need not preserve the expectation functional.

The normalization is a separate assertion. Conservation determines a line, while the Ward identity determines a point on it. Comparing that identity along tracks removes the initially unknown factor \(\xi_k\) and fixes the negative free-stress limit.

Two algebraic rigidity results support VI.1: detection of arbitrary-weight fields by bounded-degree spectators, and vanishing of alternating internal Ward rows. They require more than reflection positivity alone. The three-dimensional tensor space also reflects the chosen symmetry group: signed coordinate permutations permit three coefficients, and conservation reduces them to one. The local current calculation admits conformal Killing fields, although the polynomial Ward argument uses only translations, rotations and dilation.

Their production in III.11, including all parameter choices in the complete weak-comparison result and the shell estimates needed for the interacting Ward rows, remains to be verified in that unit. The excluded region here is precisely a band around coordinate-projection ties; it is not a neighbourhood of the full collision diagonal.

The scalar-ratio argument and its application here were checked; the underlying track Ward estimates A04–A05 remain inputs. The mixed-correlation theorem is stated in Lean for the stress assembled in VI.1; VI.2.9(b) gives the general consequence of the same convergent-preimage argument.
