Conventions. The parameter \(x\) is the coarse inverse-coupling parameter: in applications \(x = \hat{\beta}_m\). Throughout:

  • \(R (x) = \lceil (\log x)^2 \rceil\) is the cluster radius (radiusB).

  • \(t (x) = x^{- 1 / 2} e^{(\log \log x)^2}\) is the small-field threshold (temp).

  • \(\beta_F (x, L) = x + s_G \log L\) is the coupling one level finer.

  • \(t_L (x) = t (\beta_F (x, L))\) is the fine-level threshold (fiery).

  • \(g = \beta^{- 1 / 2}\).

  • "For large \(x\)" means: there is \(x_0\), depending only on the constants fixed before it, such that the statement holds for \(x \geqslant x_0\).

  • Write \(\|A\|_s\) for the maximum of the row and column sums \(\sum_j e^{s \hspace{0.17em} \text{dist} (i, j)} |A_{ij} |\). Positions lie on the coarse torus; several coordinates may have the same position. Matrix inequalities refer to quadratic forms. Put \(r_x = \lfloor R (x) / 60 \rfloor\).

1. What this unit proves

Setting. One block-spin step \(S\) maps a fine torus \(\Lambda_f\) (side \(f = Lc\), block size \(L\) odd) to a coarse torus \(\Lambda_c\). The push-forward of the density at fixed coarse field \(V\) is decomposed (III.1, III.9) into sectors. A sector is given by:

  • a store \(A\): the large-field data, made of cost cells, aisles and flagged sites, with a partition of unity \(\text{bill}_A\) given by an allocation \(D\);

  • a choice \(k\) of gauge-fixing tags;

  • a spanning forest \(F\) (tree gauge).

The integration variables are the free links of \(F\), with product Haar measure. In each sector the unit splits the free links as follows (chill):

  • frozen links \(\mathcal{F}_A\): a parent block of an endpoint lies within distance \(10 R (x)\) of \(A\). These are kept as Haar variables \(Y\).

  • active links \(\mathcal{A}_A\): all others. These are written \(u_e = \exp (a_e) \hspace{0.17em} \nu_e\), with \(a_e \in \mathfrak{g}\) and \(\nu = \iota V\) the copy of \(V\) on the fine lattice (replica).

We write \(n_a = |\mathcal{A}_A | \dim \mathfrak{g}\).

On selected stencils, the step energy contains a sum of affine squares \(Q_Y (a) = \tfrac{1}{2} \lvert Ma + s (Y) \rvert^2\) (guide):

  • \(M\) is a linear map from active coordinates to rows of linearised plaquette curvatures and block constraints;

  • \(s (Y)\) depends on the frozen links.

From these the unit defines three objects:

  • the precision \(W = M^{\top} M\) (Rows.gram);

  • the covariance \(C = W_{\mathcal{W}}\), the symmetrised windowed inverse of \(W\), with windows of coarse radius \(R (x) / 20\) (fog);

  • the mean \(m (Y) = - (W^{\text{per}})^{\top} M^{\top} s (Y)\) (meanLog). This is a window approximation to the minimiser \(- W^{- 1} M^{\top} s (Y)\); its gradient need not vanish.

The unit proves four things:

  • an exact Gaussian change of variables in every sector of every RG step;

  • the gap and locality of \(W\) and \(C\), uniformly in volume, store and field;

  • local expansions of the log-determinants, and a polymer form of each sector with large-field suppression;

  • Gaussian moment bounds, jet bounds, and the production of all post-\(L\) constants.

Proposition III.4.1 (exact Gaussian change of variables in one sector). Fix the following data:

  • a step \(S\) and a tube \(T\) satisfying the funding conditions of a teller: the prescribed kernel and stage, the multiscale metric bounds, the prescribed fine and coarse cutoffs, bounds on both path families, and compatible free metrics and towers;

  • a representation \(R\) of the fine density whose paths satisfy their bounds and whose activities satisfy the validity bounds, with \(\beta = \beta_R\);

  • \(x\), \(V\), \(k\), \(A\), \(F\), an allocation \(D\) and a cutoff profile \(\chi\);

  • a chart ratio \(J\), meaning:

    • \(J_{\text{sc}} > 0\) and \(\rho_J > 0\), and \(J\) is measurable;

    • \(J\) is real-analytic and positive on a ball \(B_{\rho_J} \subset \mathfrak{g}\);

    • \(J (0) = 1\);

    • \(\exp\) is injective on \(B_{\rho_J}\);

    • \(J_{\text{sc}} \hspace{0.17em} J (a) \hspace{0.17em} da\) is mapped by \(\exp\) onto normalised Haar measure restricted to \(\exp B_{\rho_J}\);

    • \(\exp B_{\rho_J}\) is open;

  • a number \(d > 0\).

Let \(C\) be a matrix on the active coordinates and \(m\) a map from frozen arrays to active coordinates. Assume they are permitted (Permitted):

  • \(C \succ 0\), \(\beta > 0\), \(m\) measurable, and \(4 d < \rho_J\);

  • the standing condition (Standing): whenever \(\text{bill}_A \ne 0\) and the gauge tags are eligible, every active link equals \(e^{a_e} \nu_e\) with \(\lvert a_e \rvert \leqslant d\).

Let \(\Phi_{A, k} = \text{bill}_A \cdot \text{exposed}_R \cdot \text{enters}_{T, \beta, k, V}\) be the sector integrand. Here \(\text{exposed}_R\) is the interacting density of \(R\), and \(\text{enters}\) is the tagged block-spin kernel times \(e^{- \beta \cdot \text{gap energy}}\). Then the following hold (Licenses):

  • (a) \(\Phi_{A, k}\) is integrable on the slice;

  • (b) for every \(Y\), \(\Psi (Y, \cdot) \in L^1 (\gamma_C)\), and \(\Psi \in L^1 (\text{Haar}^{\mathcal{F}_A} \otimes \gamma_C)\);

  • (c) the identity

\(\displaystyle \int \Phi_{A, k} \hspace{0.17em} d \text{Haar}^F = \text{pref}_S (\beta) \hspace{0.17em} J_{\text{sc}}^{\hspace{0.17em} \lvert \mathcal{A}_A \rvert} \hspace{0.17em} (2 \pi g^2)^{n_a / 2} (\det C)^{1 / 2} \int \text{Haar}^{\mathcal{F}_A} (dY) \int \gamma_C (dz) \hspace{0.17em} \Psi (Y, z) .\)

Here \(\gamma_C = N (0, C)\). Set \(a = m (Y) + gz\), and let \(U\) be the configuration with frozen links \(Y\), active links \(e^{a_e} \nu_e\) and tree links \(1\). Then (dyed)

\(\displaystyle \Psi (Y, z) = \tilde{J}_d (a) \hspace{0.17em} \chi_d (a) \hspace{0.17em} \text{bill}_A \hspace{0.17em} \text{lik}_k (U) \hspace{0.17em} \text{exposed}_R (U) \hspace{0.17em} \exp \hspace{-0.17em} \left( - \beta \hspace{0.17em} \overline{\text{res}} (Y, U, a) - \beta Q_Y (m (Y)) + \eta \cdot z + \tfrac{1}{2} z \cdot (C^{- 1} - W) z \right) .\)

In this formula:

  • \(\eta = - g^{- 1} (Wm (Y) + M^{\top} s (Y))\);

  • \(\overline{\text{res}}\) is the orientation-averaged step energy minus the affine squares;

  • \(\tilde{J}_d (a)\) is the product of the extended link Jacobians, each equal to \(J (a_e)\) for \(\lvert a_e \rvert \leqslant 2 d\); \(\chi_d\) is the product cutoff, equal to \(1\) when every \(\lvert a_e \rvert \leqslant d\).

Equivalently, \(\text{pref} \cdot \Psi (Y, z) = e^{\frac{1}{2} z \cdot C^{- 1} z} \hspace{0.17em} r (Y, m (Y) + gz)\), where \(r\) is the integrand in Lebesgue log-coordinates (recolored). The identity holds for every permitted \((C, m)\). No quantitative locality assumption is needed.

Status: theorem.

Proposition III.4.4 (window law). Let \(\sigma, \eta > 0\), \(K \geqslant 0\), and fix a nonnegative function \(B : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}\). There are \(u, E, g > 0\) such that for every sufficiently large integer \(r\) the following holds, uniformly in the torus and the finite coordinate set. Let the coordinate positions \(p_j\) satisfy \(\sum_j e^{- v \hspace{0.17em} \text{dist} (i, p_j)} \leqslant B (v)\) for every coarse site \(i\) and every \(v > 0\) (Revenue). Let \(A\) be a finite symmetric matrix with:

  • Schur decay \(\lVert e^{\sigma \hspace{0.17em} \text{dist}} A \rVert_{\text{Schur}} \leqslant K\);

  • gap \(\eta\);

  • windows \(\mathcal{W}_i\) that contain the \(r\)-ball around \(i\).

Let \(L\) be the windowed inverse, \(L_{\cdot i} = (A|_{\mathcal{W}_i})^{- 1} e_i\) extended by zero, and \(A_{\mathcal{W}} = \tfrac{1}{2} (L + L^{\top})\) (clouded). Then:

  • \(\lVert A_{\mathcal{W}} - A^{- 1} \rVert_{\text{Schur}} \leqslant Ee^{- ur}\);

  • \(A_{\mathcal{W}} \geqslant g = \eta / (2 (K + 1)^2)\) and \(A_{\mathcal{W}} \succ 0\).

A strengthened form (same_purchase) gives a rate \(s \in (0, \sigma]\) and a constant \(K_1\) such that \(A\), \(A^{- 1}\), \(L\) and \((A_{\mathcal{W}})^{- 1}\) all have \(s\)-weighted Schur norm \(\leqslant K_1\), and \(\lVert e^{s \hspace{0.17em} \text{dist}} (L - A^{- 1}) \rVert_{\text{Schur}} \leqslant K_1 e^{- sr}\).

Status: theorem. The constants depend only on \((\sigma, \eta, K, B)\).

Proposition III.4.5 (log-determinants as local path series). Let \(A \succ 0\) with windows \(\mathcal{W}\) (\(i \in \mathcal{W}_i\)). Let \(P_u\) be the column-wise windowed inverse of \(A + uI\), and \(\Delta_u = (A + uI) P_u - I\). Assume the following bounds (Priced), at a fixed nonnegative Schur rate, with \(C \geqslant 1\) and \(0 \leqslant q < 1\), for every \(u \geqslant 0\):

  • \(\lVert A \rVert \leqslant C\);

  • \(\lVert (A + u)^{- 1} \rVert, \lVert P_u \rVert \leqslant C / (1 + u)\);

  • \(\lVert \Delta_u \rVert \leqslant q / (1 + u)\);

  • \(\lVert S \rVert \leqslant q\), where \(S = AA_{\mathcal{W}} - 1\).

Assume also \(A_{\mathcal{W}} \succ 0\). Then

\(\displaystyle \log \det A = \sum_i \left( \ell_i + \sum_{n \geqslant 0} r_{i, n} \right), \qquad \log \det (1 + S) = \sum_i \sum_{n \geqslant 0} \frac{(- 1)^n (S^{n + 1})_{ii}}{n + 1}, \qquad \log \det A_{\mathcal{W}} = - \log \det A + \log \det (1 + S),\)

with:

  • \(\ell_i = \int_0^{\infty} [(1 + u)^{- 1} - (P_u)_{ii}] \hspace{0.17em} du = (\log A|_{\mathcal{W}_i})_{ii}\), the single-window letter;

  • \(r_{i, n} = - \int_0^{\infty} (P_u (- \Delta_u)^{n + 1})_{ii} \hspace{0.17em} du\) and \(\int \lvert \cdot \rvert \leqslant Cq^{n + 1}\).

All series converge absolutely. Moreover (labeled), the matrix products expand into closed index paths. A term \(r_{i, n}\) has one \(P_u\) factor and \(n + 1\) defect factors, hence \(n + 2\) jumps; the corresponding trace-series term has \(n + 1\) jumps. The absolute values, summed over the intermediate indices, are bounded by \(Cq^{n + 1}\) and \(q^{n + 1} / (n + 1)\), respectively.

Status: theorem.

3. Strategy

Keep links within \(10 R (x)\) of the large-field store as Haar variables. Write the others in exponential coordinates about the coarse-field copy \(\iota V\), and separate the affine squares \(Q_Y (a) = \tfrac{1}{2} |Ma + s (Y) |^2\) from the remaining energy. The substitution \(a = m (Y) + gz\) and division by an arbitrary Gaussian density give the exact identity of III.4.1. Choose the symmetrised windowed inverse for \(C\) and the windowed mean for \(m\). The endpoint windows give finite range, at most \(\lfloor R (x) / 20 \rfloor\); the linear correction and \(C^{- 1} - W\) remain in the integrand.

A local Poincaré inequality in tree gauge gives the gap of \(W\). Predictor-derivative comparison permits absorption of the gauge errors at small threshold. Path decay gives a uniform weighted Schur bound; Combes–Thomas and the window law then give \(C = W^{- 1} + O (e^{- cR (x)})\) and positivity. The resolvent integral for \(\log\), followed by a Neumann expansion, expresses the relative Gaussian normalisation as single-window and path terms. Regroup these near the large-field cores. Their logarithmic cost is absorbed by the packet energy \(\beta s^2 \gtrsim \text{hot} (z)^2\), leaving exponentially suppressed core factors and a small polymer remainder on the cutoff support.

Gaussian moment bounds control the exponential corrections, marked jets control background derivatives, and the annulus estimates recover affine squares away from the store. The construction follows the fluctuation-integral method, with window errors retained explicitly. III.4.12 chooses the constants in the order needed along the run.

5. Where the difficulty lies

The gap is a gauge-theoretic local Poincaré estimate: the predictor derivative must be compared with its flat-field value after a different gauge choice for each active link. Its smallness radius comes from continuity rather than an explicit formula. Inverse estimates then spend decay rate, while every bound must remain uniform in the volume, store, forest, tags and field.

The determinant comparison must accommodate different active dimensions, hence identity completion and the frozen-link normalisation. The resulting core costs grow polynomially in \(\log x\); the retimed large-field threshold supplies the stronger suppression \(e^{- \alpha \text{hot} (z)^2 |o|}\). The final powers \(x^{- 1 / 8}\), \(x^{- 1 / 24}\) and \(y^{- 1 / 48}\) allow the separate polymer budgets to be added. Gaussian transport and the affine-square identity are finite-dimensional; their application still requires the chart support and integrability conditions.
