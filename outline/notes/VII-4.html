Conventions.

  • \(G\) is the compact gauge group, with simple Lie algebra, and \(\mathcal{W}\) is the Wilson family of the construction. Its lattice levels \(k = 0, 1, 2, \ldots\) have spacings \(a_k \downarrow 0\). Larger \(k\) means finer. The sequence is gentle: \(\log (a_k / a_{k + 1}) / \log (1 / a_k) \to 0\).

  • \(I_{N, p} (v, x)\) is the renormalized lattice observable of a lattice label (token) \(v\) at the site \(x\). \(\|F\|_{\mathcal{T}_k}\) is the chessboard root of a lattice function \(F\) in the centred tiling of scale \(a_k\). Both follow IV.1.

  • \(S\) is the limit table along the refined extraction order. It has field spaces \(F_d\) and the inclusions \(\iota_{m, n} : F_m \to F_n\) (\(m \leqslant n\)) of the field algebra. A label list is \(a = (a_i)_{i \in I}\) with \(a_i \in F_{d_i}\), and its total degree is \(w (a) = \sum_i d_i\).

  • \(\mathcal{A}\) is the ground algebra of V.1: the commutative C*-algebra generated by the compressed slab operators \(M (u)\) of separated words \(u\). Its vacuum is \(\Omega\), its Gelfand spectrum is the phase space \(X\) with measure \(\nu\) of full support, and its characters are \(\chi_{\xi}\).

    • \(S^{\xi} (u) = \chi_{\xi} (M (u))\) and \(S^{\text{mix}} (u) = \langle \Omega, M (u) \Omega \rangle\).

    • Hence, for \(A \in \mathcal{A}\) and \(c \geqslant 0\): \(\|A\| \leqslant c\) if and only if \(|s (A) | \leqslant c\) for every state \(s \in X \sqcup \{ \text{mix} \}\) (glance_contract, Cards.pressure).

  • Clusters and clearance follow Definition 1.13 of the statement.

    • \((x, o)\) is an \((r, M, \eta)\)-cluster if \(|x_i - o| \leqslant Mr\) and \(|x_i - x_{i'} | \geqslant \eta r\) for \(i \ne i'\).

    • \(y \in (\mathbb{R}^4)^J\) is \(c\)-clear of \(o\) if \(|o - y_j | \geqslant c\) and \(|y_j - y_{j'} | \geqslant c\) for \(j \ne j'\).

    • \(S^s (a, x ; b, y)\) and \(M (a, x ; b, y)\) denote the concatenated word.

  • "For all sufficiently small \(r\)" means: there is \(r_0 > 0\), depending only on the data quantified before the phrase, such that the assertion holds for all \(r \in (0, r_0)\) and all configurations and states quantified after it.

Proposition VII.4.1 (separated OPE; properties (O1)–(O2) of the main theorem).

Setting.

  • \((\mathcal{W}, S, F)\) is a family, limit table and final record satisfying the RG hypotheses of Remark VII.4.7, denoted (RG).

  • \(\mathcal{P}\) is any coherent prescription (VII.4.6).

  • \(\mathcal{R}\) is the Reader built from \(\mathcal{P}\) and the rotation and reality structures of \(F\) (Lemma VII.4.c). It provides strata \(E_j \subset F_j\), bases fixed by conjugation \((O_{j, \kappa})_{\kappa \in \text{Ch}_j}\) of \(E_j\), and coefficients \(c_{j, \kappa} (a ; x, o)\).

Conclusions.

  • (O1) Fix a finite \(I\), labels \(a\), \(M \geqslant 0\), \(\eta > 0\), \(\varepsilon > 0\), \(j\) and \(\kappa \in \text{Ch}_j\). For all sufficiently small \(r\) and every \((r, M, \eta)\)-cluster \((x, o)\): \(|c_{j, \kappa} (a ; x, o) | \leqslant r^{\hspace{0.17em} j - w (a) - \varepsilon}\).

  • (O2) Fix finite \(I, J\), labels \(a, b\), \(M \geqslant 0\), \(\eta, c, \varepsilon > 0\) and \(D \in \mathbb{N}\). For all sufficiently small \(r\), every \((r, M, \eta)\)-cluster \((x, o)\) and every \(y\) that is \(c\)-clear of \(o\):

    • the points of \((x, y)\) are pairwise distinct, and so are those of \((o, y)\);

    • the remainder satisfies

\(\displaystyle | M (a, x ; b, y) - \sum_{j \leqslant D} \sum_{\kappa \in \text{Ch}_j} c_{j, \kappa} (a ; x, o) \hspace{0.17em} M \left( (j, O_{j, \kappa}), o ; \hspace{0.17em} b, y \right) |_{\mathcal{A}} \leqslant r^{\hspace{0.17em} D + 1 - w (a) - \varepsilon} ;\)

  • hence \(| S^s (a, x ; b, y) - \sum_{j \leqslant D} \sum_{\kappa} c_{j, \kappa} (a ; x, o) \hspace{0.17em} S^s ((j, O_{j, \kappa}), o ; b, y) | \leqslant r^{D + 1 - w (a) - \varepsilon}\) for every \(s \in X \sqcup \{ \text{mix} \}\).

The coefficients do not depend on \(b\), \(y\), \(s\) or \(D\). The thresholds do not depend on \(x\), \(o\), \(y\) or \(s\).

Proposition VII.4.2 (integrated remainder; item (D8)). Fix the data of (O2), a measurable space \(K\) and \(B \geqslant 0\). For all sufficiently small \(r\) the following holds.

Hypotheses. Let \(\mu\) be a positive measure on \(K\), \(w \in L^1 (\mu ; \mathbb{C})\) with \(\int |w| \hspace{0.17em} d \mu \leqslant B\), and \(k \mapsto (x (k), o (k), y (k))\) an a.e.-measurable configuration map. Assume that \(\mu\)-a.e. on \(\{w \ne 0\}\), \((x, o)\) is an \((r, M, \eta)\)-cluster and \(y\) is \(c\)-clear of \(o\).

Conclusion. Take for \(s (k)\) either the mixed state, or \(\xi (k)\) for an arbitrary a.e.-measurable phase map \(\xi : K \to X\). Then:

  • \(w \hspace{0.17em} S^s (a, x ; b, y)\), \(w\) times the truncated sum, and \(w\) times their difference are integrable;

  • the integrals satisfy

\(\displaystyle | \int_K w (k) \left[ S^{s (k)} (a, x ; b, y) - \sum_{j \leqslant D} \sum_{\kappa} c_{j, \kappa} (a ; x, o) \hspace{0.17em} S^{s (k)} ((j, O_{j, \kappa}), o ; b, y) \right] d \mu (k) | \leqslant B \hspace{0.17em} r^{D + 1 - w (a) - \varepsilon} .\)

In particular, for every probability measure \(\nu'\) on \(X\), the \(\nu'\)-mixture of the phases satisfies the bound of (O2) (probability_mixtures). The same pointwise threshold works for every \(K\) and \(B\).

Proposition VII.4.3 (nested OPE, i.e. composition of coefficients over trees; item (D10)).

Trees and plots.

  • \(t\) is a finite rooted tree whose root is an inner node, and \(v\) labels its leaves.

  • \(w_p\) is the total degree of the leaves below the inner node \(p\).

  • A plot \(z\) assigns points to the leaves and an anchor \(z_p\) to each inner node.

  • Radii \(s_p > 0\) are attached to the inner nodes, with \(\rho_p = s_p / s_{\text{parent} (p)}\) for non-root \(p\).

Admissibility. \((s, z)\) is \((r, M, \eta)\)-admissible if:

  • \(s_{\text{root}} = r\) and \(s_p \leqslant s_{\text{parent} (p)}\);

  • at every inner node \(p\), all positions of the subtree lie within \(Ms_p\) of \(z_p\);

  • positions in different children of \(p\) are at distance \(\geqslant \eta s_p\).

Costs. Put

\(\displaystyle \text{toll}_m (s) = r^{\hspace{0.17em} m - w (v) - \varepsilon} \prod_{p \ne \text{root}} \rho_p^{- w_p - \varepsilon}, \qquad \text{nest}_D (s) = \sum_{p \ne \text{root}} \rho_p^{\hspace{0.17em} D_p + 1} .\)

Conclusions. Fix \(m\), \(\kappa \in \text{Ch}_m\), truncation degrees \(D_p\) at the inner nodes, \(M \geqslant 0\) and \(\eta, \varepsilon > 0\). For all sufficiently small \(r\) and every admissible \((s, z)\):

  • (a) \(|c_{m, \kappa} (v ; z_{\text{leaves}}, z_{\text{root}}) | \leqslant \text{toll}_m (s)\).

  • (b) \(|c_{m, \kappa} (v ; \cdot) - c^{(D)}_{m, \kappa} (v ; z) | \leqslant \text{toll}_m (s) \hspace{0.17em} \text{nest}_D (s)\). Define \(c^{(D)}\) recursively: each inner child \(q\) contributes a channel \((j_q, \kappa_q)\) with \(j_q \leqslant D_q\), its composed coefficient, and the output field \(O_{j_q, \kappa_q}\) at \(z_q\). Each leaf child contributes its original label and the factor \(1\). Multiply these factors by the root coefficient for the resulting child labels, and sum over the inner children's channels. The root output is fixed at \((m, \kappa)\); \(D_{\text{root}}\) is unused.

  • (c) Fix a finite spectator list \(b\) and \(\delta > 0\) before taking \(r\) small. If \(y\) is at distance \(\geqslant \delta\) from every point of the plot, with mutual distances \(\geqslant \delta\), then the degree-\(m\) remainder of VII.4.1 for the leaves at \(z_{\text{root}}\) has \(\mathcal{A}\)-norm, and absolute value in every state, at most \(\text{toll}_{m + 1} (s)\).

  • (d) Fix a measurable space \(K\). For each small \(r\), keep the radii \(s\) fixed throughout the integration. Let \(\mu\) be a positive measure on \(K\) and \(q \in L^1 (\mu ; \mathbb{C})\) with \(\int |q| \hspace{0.17em} d \mu \leqslant B\). Let the plot, and in (c) the spectators, be a.e.-measurable maps satisfying the stated geometry a.e. where \(q \ne 0\). The direct and composed coefficients are integrable after multiplication by \(q\); their integral and the difference of their integrals satisfy (a)–(b) with an additional factor \(B\). The tested remainder has the integrated conclusion of VII.4.2 with bound \(B \hspace{0.17em} \text{toll}_{m + 1} (s)\), for the mixed state or any a.e.-measurable phase map. The thresholds are uniform in \(s, \mu, q, B\) and the configuration maps.

Proposition VII.4.4 (contact extensions; items (D2), (D11)–(D13)).

Setting.

  • \(\mathcal{M}\) is a system of contact devices on the final record. Its prescription is the Reader's (\(\mathcal{M}. \text{basis} =\mathcal{P}\); this is (D11)) and its rotation action is that of \(F\).

  • (RG) holds.

  • For labels \(a_1, \ldots, a_k\) of degrees \(d_i\) and a state \(s\):

    • let \(\text{cl} (n) > 0\) be the clearance of the devices up to degree \(n\);

    • put \(B_0 = \min (1, \text{cl} (w (a)))\) and fix a subtraction length \(s_0 \in (0, B_0 / 2]\); the public form uses \(s_0 = B_0 / 4\);

    • let \(\mathcal{D}^s_a (f) = \int \langle \text{full} \rangle_s (f ; x) \hspace{0.17em} dx\), the kernel being given by the forest formula of Lemma VII.4.r.

Conclusions.

  • (a) \(\mathcal{D}^s_a\) is a distribution of order at most \((2 + w (a)) \hspace{0.17em} k\). For every compact \(K\) there is \(C_K\) with \(\int | \langle \text{full} \rangle_s (f ; x) | \hspace{0.17em} dx \leqslant C_K \max_{| \alpha | \leqslant (2 + w (a)) k} \| \partial^{\alpha} f\|_{\infty}\) for all \(f\) supported in \(K\).

  • (b) (D2) \(\mathcal{D}^s_a (f) = \int f (x) \hspace{0.17em} S^s (a ; x) \hspace{0.17em} dx\) for every \(f \in C_c^{\infty}\) supported in \(\text{Conf}_k\).

  • (c) The same construction gives distributions on affine slices \(o + P (E)\), where \(P\) is a linear isometry whose range contains the normal space of the diagonal of a group \(g \subset \{1, \ldots, k\}\), and \(o\) lies on that diagonal.

  • (d) (D12) For the admissible stage families described below, the pairing equals the sum over subtraction choices and dyadic shells. Every shell kernel is integrable, the sum of their \(L^1\) norms is finite, and the iterated forest sums give the same pairing.

  • (e) (D12) For the single-block partition \(Q = \{g\}\) with \(g \ne \emptyset\), use pure dilation in its normal space \(K_g\), with \(R \in (0, 1]\). Put \(q_g = 4 (|g| - 1)\) and \(f_R (x) = R^{- q_g} f (R^{- 1} x_{K_g} + x_{K_g^{\perp}})\). If \(\text{supp} f\) lies in the rescaled stage and \(\|f\|_{C^N} \leqslant H\), where \(N = (2 + \sum_{i = 1}^k d_i) (|g| - 1)\), then for every \(\varepsilon > 0\) the integral of the absolute kernel is at most \(CHR^{\hspace{0.17em} u - w (a_g) - \varepsilon}\). Here \(u = 0\) for the full resolution, \(u = n\) for the weight-\(n\) coefficient and \(u = n + 1\) for the remainder after degree \(n\). For the last two bounds the stage must also lie within clearance \(\text{cl} (n)\) of the \(g\)-diagonal. For \(|g| > 1\), tests supported away from that diagonal give the same pairing as the punctured expression, which already includes proper-subcluster subtractions.

  • (f) (D13) The device strata have bases fixed by conjugation, and the basis in degree 0 contains the unit field.

  • (g) (D13) For every positive sequence \((f_n)\) there are thresholds \(0 < t_n \leqslant f_n\) with \(4 t_{n + 1} < t_n\), \(t_n \to 0\) and \(2 (n + 1) t_n \leqslant \text{cl} (n)\).

In (d)–(e), an admissible stage family consists of a partition \(Q\) of a group \(g\), affine placements \(o + P (E)\), positive subtraction lengths \(s\), and block scales \(R_i \in (0, 1]\). Each \(o\) lies on the block diagonals, and the range of \(P\) contains their normal spaces. A common ceiling \(B \leqslant \min (1, \text{cl} (\sum_i d_i))\) satisfies \(2 s \leqslant B\). At each placement a common linear frame acts by \(R_i\) on every block normal space and preserves each orthogonal complement. The closed stages lie in a fixed ball in frame coordinates and within normal distance \(B\) of each block diagonal; for nonsingleton blocks \(R_i \leqslant C_1 s\) with fixed \(C_1\). Original and spectator labels have uniform norm bounds. Spectator positions and labels are continuous on each stage and constant in each block's normal directions. All centroids of nonempty subgroups of different blocks, and all spectators, are uniformly separated by some \(\delta > 0\). These conditions are the geometric and field hypotheses for the summation and scaling estimates; their constants may depend on the fixed family.

Uniqueness, reflection positivity on overlapping supports, contact Ward identities for arbitrary subtractions and real-time products are not asserted.

Hypotheses. \((\mathcal{W}, f, S)\) is a seed:

Proposition VII.4.6 (coherent prescription).

Setting.

  • A probe is a finite separated configuration of tokens; its first point is the anchor, and the other points are taken relative to it.

  • \(\text{sp}_n : F_n \to \mathbb{C}^{\{ \text{probes} \}}\) sends \(\phi\) to the correlations of \(\phi\) at the anchor with the probe tokens. This map is injective, by the definition of \(F_n\) as a quotient by null fields, and compatible with the inclusions.

  • A mode is a pair \(i = (n, \alpha)\), where \(\alpha\) indexes a basis of \(F_n / \iota (F_{n - 1})\), with \(F_{- 1} = 0\).

Conclusion. There are, for every mode, a vector \(u_i \in F_n\) (needle) and a finitely supported family \(\lambda_i\) of complex weights on probes (sheet), with the following property. Write \(\lambda_i^{(m)} = \sum_p \lambda_i (p) \hspace{0.17em} \text{sp}_m (\cdot) (p)\) for the induced functional (test) on \(F_m\). Then for every \(n\):

  • \(\lambda_i^{(n)} (\iota u_j) = \delta_{ij}\) for all modes \(i, j\) of degree \(\leqslant n\);

  • \(\phi = \sum_{\deg i \leqslant n} \lambda_i^{(n)} (\phi) \hspace{0.17em} \iota u_i\) for all \(\phi \in F_n\).

Tests of degree \(> n\) vanish on \(F_n\). One prescription serves every truncation degree.

3. Strategy

A coherent system of probes reads every output degree from the mixed correlation table. For mode \(i\), the raw coefficient is \(\lambda_i (a ; x, o) = \sum_p \lambda_i (p) S (a, x - o ; \text{probe } p)\), with each reading set to zero when the combined configuration is not separated (Prescription.ope). Biorthogonality makes these readings exact on single fields (sumAll). Averaging over \(O (4)\), symmetrizing the input and output conjugations, and removing lower-degree components gives the Reader's real covariant strata. These choices are fixed before the spectator labels, state and truncation degree.

The RG estimate replaces a lattice cluster by one label \(Q\) of prescribed degree \(t\). Its remainder gains \((a_m / a_k)^{t + 1 / 16}\), while the label and remainder both pay the factor \(a_j^{- w} (a_m / a_j)^{4 |I|}\). Along the scale induction, nearby groups fuse and separated groups pass through the next block-spin step. After extraction, exact reconstruction of \(Q\) by the probes yields the grid OPE. Reflection positivity bounds decks of widely translated copies by products of chessboard roots, treating a cluster remainder as one factor. The deck limit gives all vacuum moments of \(u^{\ast} u\) in the ground algebra; full support of the phase measure and the Gelfand isometry then give \(\|u\|_{\mathcal{A}}\). Each spectator costs only its own one-field bound. The resulting norm estimate controls every phase and the mixed state with the same coefficients.

Exponential rates organize the choices of lattice scales. A downward induction in total degree extends the grid bounds to arbitrary configurations: each derivative raises the degree by one, while the distance to the fine grid recovers one power. At sufficiently high degree the crude bound already suffices to start the induction. Bringing the auxiliary scale heights together gives an arbitrary \(\varepsilon\)-loss; refinement arguments turn these rates into uniform small-radius estimates. Tree expansions repeat this replacement recursively, leaving leaf labels unchanged. Contacts use a forest recursion: each retained weight receives a normal Taylor subtraction of its own order, and the remainder gains enough radial power for integration. Partial collisions are handled by the same recursion on finer partitions. Separately, the slab-operator bound follows by applying the two-scale chessboard estimate to a reflection-positive train and using iterated Schwarz to remove the fixed exterior cost.

4.9 The slab bound of V.1

Lemma VII.4.o (point and train bounds). Take levels \(m \leqslant k \leqslant l \leqslant b\).

  • (a) Point bound. Assume:

    • all points are separated; main–main and main–exterior sup-distances exceed \(a_m\), and exterior–exterior sup-distances exceed \(a_l\);

    • main points are congruent mod \(a_m\) to one of \(P\) fixed points;

    • \(P \cdot 10 a_k < a_m\) and \(\sharp (\text{ext}) \cdot 6 a_b < a_l\);

    • \(A, B \geqslant 0\); main tokens have one-field bounds \(A\) at level \(k\) for all signed and reflected variants, and exterior tokens have bounds \(B\) at level \(b\), eventually along the extraction;

    • the cutoff family has the construction properties, \(S\) is smooth on separated configurations, and generic point limits hold along this extraction.

    Then \(|S (x ; v) | \leqslant A^{\sharp \text{main}} B^{\sharp \text{ext}}\).

  • (b) Train bound. Assume the Euclidean hierarchy and the same cutoff and point-limit hypotheses. Let \(w\) be \(r\)-buffered in a slab of width \(d \in a_m \mathbb{N}\), with \(a_m < r\). Let \(v\) be a word supported at positive times with buffer larger than \(a_l\). Require \(2 |w| \cdot 10 a_k < a_m\) and \(2 |v| \cdot 6 a_b < a_l\), and the one-field bounds in (a). Then for every \(q \in \mathbb{N}\) the Gram pairing satisfies

\(\displaystyle |S (\Theta v_q \cdot v_q) | \leqslant B^{2 |v|} A^{4 q |w|} .\)

Proof sketch.

  • (a) The two-scale chessboard estimate at the cutoff: main factors are in separated coarse cells, and exterior groups in a finer tiling, with no restriction on how many exterior factors share a coarse cell. A translation of less than one exterior cell fits all the margins. Point limits at generic configurations and continuity give the continuum bound.

  • (b) The Gram pairing is the correlation of \(4 q |w|\) main and \(2 |v|\) exterior insertions at separated points. Apply (a).

Lemma VII.4.q (Taylor deviation on shells). Let \(E\) be a finite-dimensional Euclidean space, \(K \subset E\) a subspace, \(m, n \in \mathbb{N}\), \(B \geqslant 1\), and \(U\) a compact subset of \(\{ \tfrac{1}{2} \leqslant |x_K | \leqslant 2\}\). Write \(D_a x = ax_K + x_{K^{\perp}}\). There is \(X \geqslant 1\), depending only on \(E, K, U, m, n, B\), with the following property. Let \(a, r, s > 0\) and let \(A : E \to E\) be a linear isomorphism satisfying \((Ax)_K = rx_K\) for every \(x\) and \(Ax = rx\) for \(x \in K\). Let \(f\) be smooth and assume \(ra / s \leqslant B\), \(r \leqslant Bs\), and

\(\displaystyle \text{supp} (f \circ A) \subset \{x : |x_K | \leqslant B / 2\}, \qquad \|f \circ A\|_{C^{n + m + 1}} \leqslant H.\)

Then the cut-off normal Taylor operator satisfies

\(\displaystyle | (f - T^K_{m, s} f) \circ A \circ D_a |_{C^n (U)} \leqslant XH \left\{ \begin{array}{ll} a^{m + 1}, & a \leqslant 1,\\ a^m, & a > 1. \end{array} \right.\)

Proof sketch. Taylor's formula with remainder, together with jet bounds for the smooth cut-off.

Lemma VII.4.r (the subtraction formula).

Notation.

  • \(\varphi_i\) is the field \(a_i\) at \(x_i\).

  • For a group \(h \subset \{1, \ldots, k\}\), \(|h| \geqslant 2\): \(x^{\perp}_h\) is the component of \(x\) normal to the \(h\)-diagonal, \(\bar{x}_h\) the centroid, and \(L_h = \max \left( 0, \sum_{i \in h} d_i + 1 - 4 (|h| - 1) \right)\).

  • \(T^h_m f (x) = \chi (|x_h^{\perp} |^2 / s_0^2) \sum_{l \leqslant m} \frac{1}{l!} D^l f (x - x^{\perp}_h) [x^{\perp}_h, \ldots, x^{\perp}_h]\), with \(\chi = 1\) near \(0\) and \(\chi (|x^{\perp}_h |^2 / s_0^2) = 0\) for \(|x^{\perp}_h | \geqslant 2 s_0\).

  • \(R^h_w\) replaces a field polynomial by the weight-\(w\) component of its OPE projection, placed at \(\bar{x}_h\). The device detectors use finite probe sets, a linear decoder, an \(O (4)\)-average and simultaneous input and output conjugation, followed by the device's graded splitting.

Definition. Set \(C_{\{i\}} = \varphi_i \otimes 1\) and, for \(|h| \geqslant 2\),

\(\displaystyle C_h = - \sum_{w < L_h} R^h_w (\Pi_h) \cdot T^h_{L_h - 1 - w}, \qquad \Pi_h = \sum_{\pi} \prod_{\beta \in \pi} C_{\beta}, \qquad \text{full}_h = \Pi_h + C_h,\)

where \(\pi\) runs over partitions of \(h\) into at least two blocks. For a singleton the full expression is \(\varphi_i \otimes 1\); for the empty group it is the unit. The kernel of \(\mathcal{D}^s_a\) at \(x\) is the \(s\)-expectation of \(\text{full}_{\{1.. k\}}\) applied to \(f\). Expanding the recursion gives a Zimmermann-type forest sum. The child expressions are constructed recursively first. On the test function, the parent Taylor operator acts first, followed by the operators already present in the child expression; nested operators need not commute.

Per-group form. \(\left[ \Pi_h - \sum_{w < L_h} R^h_w \Pi_h \right] f + \sum_{w < L_h} R^h_w (\Pi_h) \hspace{0.17em} (1 - T^h_{L_h - 1 - w}) f\).

  • Put \(d_h = \sum_{i \in h} d_i\) and \(q_h = 4 (|h| - 1)\). After retaining weights \(w < L_h\), the remainder has radial order \(O (|x^{\perp}_h |^{L_h - d_h - \varepsilon})\), with \(L_h - d_h \geqslant 1 - q_h\).

  • A retained weight-\(w\) term has coefficient order \(O (|x^{\perp}_h |^{w - d_h - \varepsilon})\) and test deviation \(O (|x^{\perp}_h |^{L_h - w})\).

  • For \(0 < \varepsilon < 1\) these radial powers are integrable in \(q_h\) normal dimensions. Angular sectors containing partial collisions require the subcluster induction of Lemma VII.4.s.

  • Each weight-\(w\) coefficient has scaling degree at most \(d_h - w\), and receives its own Taylor subtraction.

  • If \(\sum_{i \in h} d_i + 1 \leqslant 4 (|h| - 1)\), nothing is subtracted.

Lemma VII.4.s (absolute bound; summation and scaling). Fix the devices, labels, state and subtraction length as in VII.4.4. For every compact \(U \subset (\mathbb{R}^4)^k\) there is \(C > 0\) such that

\(\displaystyle \int | \langle \text{full} \rangle_s (f ; x) | \hspace{0.17em} dx \leqslant C \hspace{0.17em} \|f\|_{C^{(2 + w (a)) k}} \quad \text{whenever } \text{supp} f \subset U.\)

The summation formulas and scaling bounds hold under the stage-family hypotheses of VII.4.4(d)–(e).

Proof sketch.

  • Patching. Cover \(U\) by finitely many patches. On each patch the relevant partition is the set of coincidence classes of its centre. A smooth partition of unity and the Leibniz rule combine the patch bounds.

  • Induction. On a patch, use strong induction on \(|h|\) minus the number of blocks.

    • Base case: the separated correlation times \(f\), bounded pointwise.

    • Inductive step: split into the remainder arm and the weight-\(w\) arms. Cut into dyadic normal shells \(|x^{\perp}_h | \sim 2^{- n}\) and angular fans. Rescale each sector to a finer partition, and apply the induction hypothesis.

  • OPE input. The raw coefficient rate and the remainder rate in \(\mathcal{A}\) from Lemma VII.4.j.

  • Shell exponent. Including normal volume, the effective exponent is \(4 (|h| - 1) + L_h - d_h \geqslant 1\):

    • the remainder gains \(L_h\);

    • a weight-\(w\) arm gains \(w\) from the coefficient and \(L_h - w\) from Lemma VII.4.q.

  • Summing. Choose \(\gamma > 0\) with \((3^{|g| - |Q|} + 1) \gamma \leqslant 1\), where \(|Q|\) is the number of blocks. The accumulated exponent losses leave a factor \(2^{- n \gamma}\) in the shell sum. The corresponding geometric series converges.

5. Where the difficulty lies

Routine but long.

  • The rate bookkeeping (Lemmas VII.4.a, j).

  • The \(O (4)\)-covariant Reader (Lemmas VII.4.c, k).

  • The tree combinatorics (Lemma VII.4.n).

  • The geometry of normal shells, angular sectors and compact patches.

Main estimates.

  1. The OPE comes from the RG. The basic estimate (Lemmas VII.4.f–g) is a lattice statement: a cluster of renormalized observables equals one observable of degree \(t\) plus a remainder that gains \((a_m / a_k)^{t + 1 / 16}\), at a cost \((a_m / a_j)^{4 |I|}\). The margin \(\tfrac{1}{16}\) is set by the transport gain \((a_k / a_m)^{1 / 8}\) of Part III's RG, not by the contraction \((a_m / a_k)^{t + 1}\) of the irrelevant part. This is where Part III enters.

  2. Uniformity in states and spectators comes from the C*-norm. The norm of \(\mathcal{A}\) is computed by chessboard bounds on decks of translated copies, together with Gelfand and the full support of \(\nu\) (Lemmas VII.4.d–e). The spectators then cost only their own one-field bounds, at their own scale, and the bound holds in every phase at once.

  3. The derivative bootstrap (Lemma VII.4.i). Each derivative costs one power of the fine spacing, so a first-order comparison with the grid gains nothing. The induction therefore starts from high degree, where the crude bound is already strong enough.

  4. One coefficient system for every truncation degree. It comes from a coherent prescription (VII.4.6) and the strata of the Reader.

Unusual, weaker or stronger than expected.

  • Exponents. The estimates have an arbitrary \(\varepsilon\)-loss and do not determine logarithmic corrections. The degree \(w\) is the sum of the label degrees; the statement does not identify it with a scaling dimension.

  • Coefficients. They depend on the prescription and the Reader, and nothing relates them to perturbation theory (VII.5, VII.6).

  • Spectator distance. The remainder bound holds with power-separated spectators, \((r / \rho)^{N + 1}\). The main theorem uses only a fixed clearance.

  • Contacts. The contact extension is an existence statement. Its order \((2 + w (a)) k\) is crude. Its OPE projection uses the device detectors, with the same prescription as the Reader but separate probe sets.

  • Interface to Part III. The cluster replacement requires the numerical and geometric hypotheses in Lemma VII.4.f.

7. What remains unexplained

  1. RG inputs below the cluster replacement. Lemmas VII.4.f–g identify the scalar inequalities and the scale induction. The polymer norm estimates inside the one-step representation, fusion and final remainder transport have not been checked line by line. The dependence of their constants, and the complete Part III proofs of these inputs, remain to be reconciled with the stated RG package.

  2. Proofs used through their statements. The full geometry behind the vacuum deck limit and the two-scale continuum transfer belongs to V.1. The rotated comparison estimates used in Lemma VII.4.k, and the triangular replacement and high-horizon lemmas used in VII.4.n, have not been checked internally. Their uses and exponent arithmetic agree with their stated conclusions.

  3. Contact sector induction. The global patching argument, subtraction recursion, shell Taylor bound and scaling normalization have been checked. The finer angular decompositions and the full induction through partially coincident strata remain checked only at the level of their theorem calls. Lemma VII.4.r's radial estimate alone does not settle these partial collisions.

  4. Contact projection versus Reader coefficients. The devices share the Reader's prescription but use separate detectors and a separate graded splitting. An identity between their weight-\(w\) rows and \(\sum_{\kappa} c_{w, \kappa} O_{w, \kappa}\) has not been located. No such identity is used in the contact statement above.

  5. Limits of the conclusions. The results do not assert uniqueness of coefficients or contact extensions, independence of the extraction subsequence, convergence as \(D \to \infty\), reflection positivity or Ward identities for the extensions, or agreement with perturbative coefficients.
