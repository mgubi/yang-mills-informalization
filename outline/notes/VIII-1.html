Size: 12,060 lines in 1,674 declarations.

The construction chooses one Wilson family for each gauge group and block-spin parameter \(b > 0\). At each requested extraction order, it assembles the continuum table, phases, fields, reconstruction and OPE on one refinement. Fixed-volume ultraviolet convergence removes the mesh subsequence. The bridges then give the public theorem in statement.md.

1. What this unit proves

Conventions. The notation follows statement.md:

  • \((m, G, \mathfrak{g})\) is a gauge group: \(m \geqslant 1\), \(G\) is a closed subgroup of \(U (m)\), and \(\mathfrak{g}= \{X : \exp (tX) \in G \text{for all } t \in \mathbb{R}\}\). Simplicity includes nonabelianness.

  • \(\mathcal{W}= (L_p, D_N, \beta_{N, p}, h, \mathcal{T}, I_{N, p})\) is a Wilson family, with \(a_N = 1 / D_N\), smeared moments \(C_{N, p} (v ; f)\) and separated configurations \(\text{Conf}_n\).

  • A hierarchy has a Wightman theory \(\mathfrak{W}\), finite-dimensional field spaces \(F_d\), label maps \(\iota_d\), token projection \(\pi\), phases \((X, \nu, \xi_{\ast})\), correlations \(S^s\), time continuations \(\textsf{F}_n\), OPE data \((\text{Ch}_j, O_{j, \kappa}, c_{j, \kappa})\), and \(\Theta \in F_4\). Write \(\mathbb{F}= \bigsqcup_d F_d\). The function \(G_{\Theta} (r)\) is the selected two-point correlation of \(\Theta\) at time-axis points \(- r / 2\) and \(r / 2\).

  • (Reg1)–(Reg4), (R1)–(R15), (O1)–(O2), (T1)–(T4), (P1)–(P4) and (D1)–(D16) are as in statement.md.

  • \(b_0 = 11 C_{\mathfrak{g}} / (48 \pi^2)\), where \(C_{\mathfrak{g}}\) is the adjoint Casimir in the construction normalization. All Lie-algebra dimensions are real.

Internal objects:

  • An extraction order is a pair \(\sigma = (\sigma^{\text{m}}, \sigma^{\text{v}})\) of strictly increasing maps \(\mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}\): a mesh sequence and a volume sequence.

  • Its refinement by \(\tau\) is \(\sigma \circ \tau := (\sigma^{\text{m}} \circ \tau^{\text{m}}, \sigma^{\text{v}} \circ \tau^{\text{v}})\). The full sequence is written \(\text{id}\).

  • An internal family \(\mathcal{C}\) is the record of a renormalization-group construction: trajectory, couplings \(\beta_{N, p} (m)\) at levels \(m \leqslant N\), labels and local lattice sections. (D3) is its construction predicate, and \(\mathcal{W} (\mathcal{C})\) is its Wilson family, with \(\beta_{N, p} := \beta_{N, p} (N)\).

  • A table \(S\) is a family of continuum correlation functions \(S (v ; x)\) indexed by token lists.

Ordered limit. \(\text{Ord}_{\sigma} (\mathcal{C}, S)\) means the following. For every \(n\), \(v \in \mathcal{T}^n\) and \(t \in C^{\infty}_c (\text{Conf}_n)\):

  • \(t\) is \(\sigma^{\text{v}} (k)\)-admissible for large \(k\);

  • there are numbers \(R_p\) with \(C_{\sigma^{\text{m}} (j), p} (v ; t) \to R_p\) for every admissible \(p\);

  • \(R_{\sigma^{\text{v}} (k)} \to \int tS (v ; \cdot)\).

Final object. A final object \(\mathfrak{F}\) on \((\mathcal{C}, \sigma, S)\) consists of five parts:

  1. Chosen renormalization data and step witnesses whose trajectory is exactly that of \(\mathcal{C}\) (D6).

  2. A seed: (D3), \(\text{Ord}_{\sigma} (\mathcal{C}, S)\), point limits, the Euclidean hierarchy axioms for \(S\), cell hypotheses and corrected-field widths.

  3. A field algebra: derivatives, rotations and a stress tensor.

  4. A phase selection with its certificate.

  5. Analytic continuations of the mixed and selected correlations, and a Wightman theory realizing the selected one.

Proposition VIII.1.1 (main theorem, public form). Let \((m, G, \mathfrak{g})\) be a gauge group with \(\mathfrak{g}\) simple. Then there is a Wilson family \(\mathcal{W}\) for \(G\) satisfying (Reg1)–(Reg4), with the following property. For every strictly increasing \(p : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}\) there are a strictly increasing \(q\) and a hierarchy over \(\mathcal{W}\) satisfying (P1)–(P4) along \(p \circ q\):

  • (P1) For every \(n\), every \(v \in \mathcal{T}^n\) and every \(f \in C^{\infty}_c (\text{Conf}_n)\), \(f\) is \(p (q (k))\)-admissible for all large \(k\). There are numbers \(R_{p'}\) such that \(C_{N, p'} (v ; f) \to R_{p'}\) along all \(N\) for every \(p'\) for which \(f\) is admissible, and \(R_{p (q (k))} \to \int f \hspace{0.17em} S^{\text{mix}} (\pi (v) ; \cdot)\).

  • (P2) The selected phase carries a Wightman theory satisfying (W0)–(W3), with faithful fields. The mixed correlations are the \(\nu\)-average of continuous phase correlations, with \(\nu\) a probability measure of full support. The continuation is holomorphic in the complex times at fixed real spatial positions, and its boundary values on product Schwartz tests are the selected Wightman functions. All clauses (R1)–(R15) hold.

  • (P3) The OPE bounds (O1)–(O2) hold with one system of coefficients, uniformly in the state \(s \in X \sqcup \{ \text{mix} \}\).

  • (P4) \(\Theta\) has zero vacuum expectation, \(A_{\mathfrak{g}} := 3 \dim_{\mathbb{R}} \mathfrak{g}/ \pi^4 > 0\), and \(r^8 (\log 1 / r)^2 G_{\Theta} (r) \to A_{\mathfrak{g}}\) as \(r \downarrow 0\). For every finite Wick profile and every \(\delta > 0\), the profile differs from \(G_{\Theta}\) at some \(r \in (0, \delta)\).

Status: theorem. Lean: YangMills.jointExistence, statement JointExistence, with WilsonRegularity, Hierarchy, Properties, Converges, Reconstructed, OperatorProductExpansion and TraceTest.

Proposition VIII.1.5 (ultraviolet endpoint). Let \(\mathfrak{g}\) be simple, let \(\textsf{R}\) be a run with campaign \(T\) and pillars \(W\), and assume the stationary-point and amplification condition, the delay condition, and the weight-and-tableau data \((\vartheta, v)\) of (Fd). Then the ultraviolet package (UV\(_{\infty}\)) holds:

  1. (D3) holds for \(\mathcal{C}_{\textsf{R}}\), and the weight data exist.

  2. Sampler. Every \(\sigma\) has a refinement \(\tau\) carrying a table \(S\) and a final object on \((\mathcal{C}_{\textsf{R}}, \sigma \circ \tau, S)\), and \(\text{Ord}_{(\text{id}, (\sigma \circ \tau)^{\text{v}})}\) holds.

  3. The trajectory at every \((p, N)\) competes in, and is issued by, the fixed tuning recursion.

  4. Tuning and convergence. The parameter \(s \in [- 1, 1]\) specifies the target coupling \(\bar{\beta} + ws\) at level \(0\), where \(w > 0\) is the fixed tuning width. For every \((p, N, s)\) there is a successful trajectory, and its bare coupling at level \(N\) is unique within the prescribed recursion. These bare couplings are values of a function analytic on the open unit disc in \(s\). At each fixed \((m, p)\), the couplings and activities of successful trajectories converge as \(N \to \infty\) whenever their level-zero targets converge. For complex targets in the disc, convergence of the analytic couplings and activities, with every parameter derivative, is uniform on compact subsets; activities are measured in the fixed weighted norm at \((m, p)\).

  5. For every fixed \((m, p)\) there is \(c_{m, p} > 0\) such that

    \(\displaystyle e^{- \text{scalar}_{N, p, m}} Z_{N, p} \geqslant c_{m, p} \qquad (N \geqslant m),\)

    where \(Z_{N, p}\) is the bare Wilson partition function relative to probability Haar measure and \(\text{scalar}_{N, p, m}\) is the field-independent scalar in the level-\(m\) action. Its scalar density \(v_{N, p, m}\) tends to \(- \infty\) as \(N \to \infty\).

  6. The volume-uniform bounds hold, as stated in VIII.1.c, step 1.

  7. (D5) holds along the full mesh sequence.

  8. For every fixed \(p\) there is a family of successful trajectories with target \(\bar{\beta} + \lambda ws\), \(s \in [- 1, 1]\), for some \(\lambda > 0\), whose central member is the chosen family. They obey the same step relations and insertion normalization. For each token list, each compact \(K \subset \text{NoHit}_p\) and each finite spatial derivative order \(h\), there are an open neighbourhood \(\Omega\) of \(K\) in \(\text{NoHit}_p\) and a smaller positive parameter width, independent of \(N\). In the resulting unit-disc coordinate, the limiting correlation is holomorphic in the parameter and \(C^h\) in position on \(\Omega\). All fixed coordinate differences of order at most \(h\), their interpolants and their admissible smearings converge to the corresponding derivatives, uniformly on compact parameter and position sets, with every parameter derivative.

Status: theorem. It is assembled here; its ingredients are in III.12–III.15. Lean: FullVolume.Final.jewelOf17 and Jewel.

Proposition VIII.1.6 (the ultraviolet limit along the full spacing sequence). For \(p \in \mathbb{N}\), let \(\text{NoHit}_p\) be the set of configurations no two of whose points are congruent modulo \(L_p \mathbb{Z}^4\).

(a) For the family of a run satisfying VIII.1.5's hypotheses, the following holds for every \(p\), \(n\) and \(v \in \mathcal{T}^n\). There is \(f_{p, v} \in C^{\infty} (\text{NoHit}_p)\) such that:

  • \(C_{N, p} (v ; t) \to \int t \hspace{0.17em} f_{p, v}\) as \(N \to \infty\) along all \(N\), for every \(p\)-admissible \(t\);

  • for every fixed finite list of normalized forward, backward or centered coordinate differences, with fixed integer stencil widths, the interpolated lattice differences converge locally uniformly on \(\text{NoHit}_p\) to the corresponding derivatives of \(f_{p, v}\). The same limit holds whenever lattice configurations approach a point of \(\text{NoHit}_p\), along any cutoff indices tending to infinity. The smeared differences converge to the integral of the derivative against the test, and that integrand is integrable.

The cutoff thresholds may depend on \(p, n, v\), the compact set, the tests and the difference stencils.

(b) Suppose a final object exists on \((\mathcal{C}, \sigma, S)\) and (a) holds for \(\mathcal{C}\). Then \(\text{Ord}_{(\text{id}, \sigma^{\text{v}})} (\mathcal{C}, S)\) holds.

Status: theorems. Lean: (a) Campaign.realUV, with the definition PositionsUV. (b) FullVolume.Final.from_spec.

3. Strategy

Choose one run \(\textsf{R}_{G, b}\) before any extraction order, observable or test. Given \(\sigma\), first extract a refinement \(\sigma \circ g\) on which the couplings at every fixed level have ordered limits. Next extract \((\sigma \circ g) \circ w\), carrying a continuum table and a final object. The later reconstruction, trace, OPE and contact results attach to that object. Passing to further subsequences preserves the earlier limits, so \(\tau = g \circ w\) serves every conclusion.

At each fixed volume, the renormalization-step estimates make the lattice correlations Cauchy on a dense union of grids. Equicontinuity gives convergence on separated configurations; discrete Taylor estimates recover all derivatives. Dominated convergence gives the smeared limits. Uniqueness of limits identifies these full-sequence limits with the rows of the extracted table, removing the mesh subsequence.

Specialize the detailed theorem to \(b = 1\) and \(\sigma = (\text{id}, p)\). The volume part of \(\tau\) is the required \(q\). The public hierarchy is read from the final object and its OPE reader. The bridges identify the Wilson measures and moments, reconstruction axioms, OPE estimates and trace tests. The plaquette identity, equality of Wick profiles and evaluation of the trace amplitude are the required computations.

4. Steps

5. Where the difficulty lies

The assembly preserves the order of choices. A family fixed before \(\sigma\) must support the coupling limits, continuum table and all later structures on one refinement. In particular, the fixed-volume ultraviolet convergence must concern this same family. Uniqueness of row limits then removes the mesh subsequence without replacing the final object or its point-limit data.

The momentum-space Wick exclusion requires a further argument: every compactly supported relative-momentum test must be realizable as \(\overline{\mathcal{F}_+ u} \mathcal{F}_+ w\). Fourier surjectivity and an auxiliary transform of modulus one on the test support give that realization. Exponential integrability then permits passage to Euclidean Laplace transforms.

The public theorem retains less than the detailed construction. Its Wilson limit concerns the mixed state, while its trace test concerns the selected phase. The Euclidean Wick exclusion follows from (T2)–(T3); the detailed theorem also gives momentum-space and Maxwell relative-locality countertests. The low-energy and heat bounds are polynomial and imply no mass gap. Coupling limits, all-shapes OPE and sharp Gram allocations occur in (Goal) but are omitted from VIII.1.3. Neither independence of the volume subsequence nor agreement between different penalties or run choices is asserted.
