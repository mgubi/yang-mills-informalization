The main result identifies the order-zero lattice correlations with the free Maxwell hierarchy. The short-distance limit of the interacting correlations is stated in FirstExtension (VI.2), established by firstTarget in VIII.1. The results are:

  • the order-zero term of lattice perturbation theory converges, as the spacing tends to zero, to the Wick-ordered free Maxwell hierarchy evaluated on the on-shell images of the labels (VII.1.1–VII.1.3);

  • the free hierarchy detects every label outside the Maxwell equation ideal through a single two-point function at a fixed pair of points (VII.1.4–VII.1.5);

  • using this detection, the measuring charts of the construction can be corrected to charts that are exactly compatible with the filtration, the symmetries and the unit (VII.1.6);

  • estimates on backward windows and local expansions used in the rank comparison (VII.1.7).

1. What this unit proves

Setting.

Gauge group and continuum objects.

  • \(G\) is a compact matrix gauge group whose Lie algebra \(\mathfrak{g}\) is real simple and nonabelian, with an invariant inner product and an orthonormal basis of colours.

  • A key \(a = (j, \alpha)\) is a coordinate of the real radial-gauge potential space \(T_j\): homogeneous \(\mathfrak{g}\)-valued 1-forms of polynomial degree \(j + 1\) with \(x \cdot A (x) = 0\). Curl identifies \(T_j\) with the closed homogeneous curvature jets of degree \(j\). Each key has engineering weight \(j + 2\).

  • \(\text{Pol}\) is the complex polynomial algebra in the keys. \(V_d\) consists of its \(\text{Ad} (G)\)-invariant polynomials of total engineering weight at most \(d\). The free field space \(F_d\) is the quotient of \(V_d\) by the free null space (VI.2).

  • \(\text{Sh}_j\) is the space of curvature tensors, symmetric in the \(j\) Taylor slots and antisymmetric in the two form indices, whose homogeneous fields solve \(dF = d \ast F = 0\). Taylor coefficients are normalized by \(1 / j!\). Write \(\kappa : \text{Sh}_j \to T_j\) for the inverse radial-gauge curl map (gaugeJet).

  • \(\rho : \text{Pol} \to \text{WickPol} =\mathbb{C} [\text{shell coordinates}]\) restricts a polynomial to on-shell jets (reduceEq).

  • The equation ideal \(\mathcal{I}_d = V_d \cap \ker \rho\) consists of labels that vanish on every on-shell Taylor tower (equationIdeal).

  • \(\omega_d = \rho \circ \text{sec}_d : F_d \to \text{WickPol}\) is the on-shell image of a label. Here \(\text{sec}_d\) is the representative polynomial of VII.3, obtained by projecting radial curvature coordinates onto the Maxwell shell (sections, fixedPolynomial).

The free Maxwell hierarchy (III.16).

  • \(\Gamma (x) = 1 / (4 \pi^2 |x|^2)\) (Green).

  • The kernel bareCurl is \(\delta_{bk} \partial_a \partial_m \Gamma - \delta_{bm} \partial_a \partial_k \Gamma - \delta_{ak} \partial_b \partial_m \Gamma + \delta_{am} \partial_b \partial_k \Gamma\). This is \(\langle F_{ab} (x) F_{km} (y) \rangle\) at \(x - y\) for \(F = dA\), where \(A\) is the Feynman-gauge free field with \(\langle A^c_{\mu} (x) A^{c'}_{\nu} (y) \rangle = \delta^{cc'} \delta_{\mu \nu} \Gamma (x - y)\).

  • For linear forms \(P\) on \(\text{Sh}_j\) and \(Q\) on \(\text{Sh}_l\), \(K (P, Q) (x - y)\) is \(P\) applied to the \(j\)-jet in \(x\) and \(Q\) to the \(l\)-jet in \(y\) of this kernel, colour-diagonal (legKernel). The value is independent of extensions of \(P, Q\) to full curvature tensors, because the kernel solves the free equations in each variable.

  • For a key \(a\), write \(x_a\) for its real coordinate functional on \(T_j\) and \(\kappa_a = x_a \circ \kappa\), and write \(K_{ab} = K (\kappa_a, \kappa_b)\). The Taylor coefficients in both legs include their factorial denominators; differentiation at the second endpoint contributes \((- 1)^l\).

  • The Wick moment \(\mathcal{W}_x (q_1, \ldots, q_r)\), for \(x \in \text{Conf}_r\) and \(q_i \in \text{WickPol}\), is multilinear (Wick.moment). On monomials it is the sum, over perfect matchings of the variable occurrences that never pair two occurrences at the same point, of the product of the entries \(K (\cdot, \cdot) (x_i - x_j)\). It is the correlation of the Wick-ordered products \(: \hspace{-0.17em} q_i \hspace{-0.17em} : (x_i)\) of the Gaussian jet field with covariance \(K\).

  • The free hierarchy of the labels is \(x \mapsto \mathcal{W}_x (\omega_d p_1, \ldots, \omega_d p_r)\) (Ward.disc of the free table).

The lattice prescription at order zero (definitions from VII.3).

  • \(\Lambda_N = (\mathbb{Z}/ N)^4\) with \(N\) even, \(N \geqslant 4\).

  • The field space \(\mathcal{A}_N\) consists of \(\mathfrak{g}\)-valued link variables that are odd under the point reflection: \(A (- x - e_i, i) = - A (x, i)\), i.e. \(A\) is invariant under \(x \mapsto - x\) as a 1-form (LinkP).

  • The quadratic form \(\textsf{Q}_N (A) = \tfrac{1}{2} \left( \sum_{x, i < j} | \text{curl}_{ij} A (x) |^2 + \sum_x | \text{div} A (x) |^2 \right)\) is the Hessian at the identity of the Wilson action plus the Feynman gauge-fixing term \(\tfrac{1}{4} \sum | \text{div} A|^2\) (form, action).

  • \(\textsf{Q}_N\) is positive definite on \(\mathcal{A}_N\): a closed and divergence-free field is constant, and a reflection-odd constant field is zero. \(\mu_N\) is the centred Gaussian with density proportional to \(\exp (- \textsf{Q}_N (A) / 2)\) (data).

  • Expectations are formal power series in the coupling. The interaction exponent has order-\(n\) coefficient equal to minus the order-\((n + 2)\) action coefficient for \(n \geqslant 1\), and zero constant coefficient. Its exponential is multiplied by the Taylor series of the Haar Jacobian and Faddeev–Popov density. Each jet-coordinate series uses its order-\((n + 1)\) Taylor coefficient at order \(n\) (weight, seriesLex). Order zero is therefore a \(\mu_N\)-expectation; \(\langle \cdot \rangle^{(0)}\) denotes the order-zero coefficient.

  • \(\ell^h_x : \mathcal{A}_N \to T_j\) is the radial-gauge image of the linear lattice curvature jet at site \(x\) (slope). It is obtained from the \(j\)-fold forward difference of the plaquette curl divided by \(j!\) by symmetrization and the radial-gauge map, scaled by \(h^{- (j + 2)}\) and averaged over the hyperoctahedral group \(W_4\) of signed axis permutations. \(\ell^h_{x, a}\) is its \(a\)-th coordinate.

  • The order-zero lattice representative of a polynomial \(p \in \text{Pol}\) using only keys of rank at most \(d\), at \(o\) is

\(\displaystyle R_h (p, o) (A) =\mathbb{E}_{\xi} \hspace{0.17em} p (\ell^h_o (A) + i \xi),\)

where \(\xi\) is an independent centred Gaussian on the keys of rank \(\leqslant d\) with covariance

\(\displaystyle S_h (a, b) = (2 \pi)^{- 4} \int_{[- \pi, \pi]^4} \hat{\Delta} (k)^{- 1} \sum_c \lambda_a (c, k) \lambda_b (c, k) \hspace{0.17em} dk.\)

Here \(\hat{\Delta} (k) = \sum_i 4 \sin^2 (k_i / 2)\), \(c\) runs over cosine/sine, axis and colour channels, and \(\lambda_a (c, \cdot)\) is \(\ell^h_{0, a}\) applied to the corresponding plane wave on \(\mathbb{Z}^4\). So \(S_h\) is the covariance of the jet stencils at one site for the Feynman-gauge Gaussian on the infinite lattice \(\mathbb{Z}^4\), and \(R_h (p, o) = \hspace{0.17em} : \hspace{-0.17em} p (\ell^h_o) \hspace{-0.17em} :_{S_h}\) is Wick-ordered with respect to the infinite-lattice self-contraction (column, link).

  • Regulators. For an integer \(L \geqslant 2\) set \(h_m = L^{- m}\) and \(N_m = 4 L^{m^2 + m + 1}\). Lattice points are reported relative to the centre \(c_m = (N_m / 4, \ldots, N_m / 4)\), through \(o \mapsto h_m (o - c_m)\) with representatives in \((- N_m / 2, N_m / 2]^4\).

  • Profile. For \(f : (\mathbb{R}^4)^r \to \mathbb{C}\) and \(p \in F_d^{\hspace{0.17em} r}\),

\(\displaystyle \Pi_m (f ; p) = h_m^{4 r} \sum_{o \in \Lambda_{N_m}^r} f (h_m (o - c_m)) \hspace{0.17em} \left\langle \prod_j R_{h_m} (\text{sec}_d \hspace{0.17em} p_j, o_j) \right\rangle^{(0)}_{N_m}\)

(profile, row).

  • Itinerary. An itinerary is a sequence \((N_i, h_i)\) with \(N_i\) even, \(h_i > 0\), \(N_i h_i\) a positive integer tending to \(\infty\), \(h_i \to 0\), and \(N_i h_i^k \to \infty\) for every \(k \in \mathbb{N}\) (Itinerary). Sites \(o_i \in \Lambda_{N_i}\) are homing to \(z\) if \(h_i (o_i - c_{N_i}) \to z\) (Home).

Proposition VII.1.1 (regulator removal at order zero). Fix an integer \(L \geqslant 2\), \(d, r \in \mathbb{N}\), label frames, and \(p_1, \ldots, p_r \in F_d\). Let \(f\) be continuous with compact support and \(\text{supp} \hspace{0.17em} f \subset \text{Conf}_r\), where support is closed. Then

\(\displaystyle \lim_{m \to \infty} \Pi_m (f ; p) = \int f (x) \hspace{0.17em} \mathcal{W}_x (\omega_d p_1, \ldots, \omega_d p_r) \hspace{0.17em} dx.\)

In board form: let \(Q\) be a position board whose profiles are smooth test functions compactly supported in \(\text{Conf}\) ("clean profiles"). Then the order-zero board readings converge to the free readings, slot by slot and for every tuple of basis labels. Status: theorem. There is no hypothesis on the construction; it concerns lattice Gaussian integrals and the continuum kernel only. Lean: Rig.zerothProfile. Board form: Position.finite_leading, using the definitions finite, white and CleanProfiles (all VII.3).

Proposition VII.1.2 (pointwise convergence of order-zero correlations). Let \((N_i, h_i)\) be an itinerary with \(4 \mid N_i\). Let \(z \in \text{Conf}_r\), and let \(o_{i, j} \in \Lambda_{N_i}\) be homing to \(z_j\) for each \(j\). Then for all \(p_1, \ldots, p_r \in F_d\),

\(\displaystyle \left\langle \prod_j R_{h_i} (\text{sec}_d p_j, o_{i, j}) \right\rangle^{(0)}_{N_i} \longrightarrow \mathcal{W}_z (\omega_d p_1, \ldots, \omega_d p_r) .\)

Status: theorem. Lean: Warp.row_limit, through poly_limit and earth.

Proposition VII.1.3 (corrected two-leg covariance). Let \((N_i, h_i)\) be an itinerary with \(4 \mid N_i\). Let \(\iota\) be any index set, let \(z : \iota \to \mathbb{R}^4\) be injective, and for every \(j \in \iota\) let \(o_{i, j}\) be sites homing to \(z_j\). For indices \(j, l \in \iota\) and arbitrary keys \(a, b\), define

\(\displaystyle \kappa_h (j, a ; l, b) = \text{Cov}_{\mu_N} (\ell^h_{o_j, a}, \ell^h_{o_l, b}) - \delta_{jl} \hspace{0.17em} S_h (a, b) .\)

Then

\(\displaystyle \kappa_{h_i} (j, a ; l, b) \to \left\{ \begin{array}{ll} K_{ab} (z_j - z_l), & j \ne l,\\ 0, & j = l. \end{array} \right.\)

Status: theorem. Lean: Align.corrected_limit, from orbit_limit, orbit_contact and shell_exact.

Proposition VII.1.4 (two-point detection). Let \(e_{\text{t}}\) be the unit vector of the time axis, \(\sigma\) the time reflection \(x_{\text{t}} \mapsto - x_{\text{t}}\) acting on labels, and \(\bar{v}\) the complex conjugate label. If \(v \in V_d \setminus \mathcal{I}_d\), then

\(\displaystyle \text{Re} \hspace{0.27em} \mathcal{W}_{(e_{\text{t}}, 0)} (\rho v, \rho (\sigma \bar{v})) > 0.\)

More generally, let \(\Phi\) and \(\Psi\) be the algebra maps sending shell coordinates to the diagonalizing and reflected probe variables of Lemma VII.1.m. For every nonzero \(q \in \text{WickPol}\), \(\text{Re} \hspace{0.17em} \mathcal{W}_{(e_{\text{t}}, 0)} (\Phi q, \Psi \bar{q}) > 0\). Here \(\Phi\) is surjective, and \(\rho (\sigma \bar{v}) = \Psi (\bar{q})\) whenever \(\rho v = \Phi q\). Status: theorem, about the free kernel only. No Wilson family and no reflection positivity of a measure is involved. Lean: Free.two_detect, Free.positive, detectors.

Proposition VII.1.5 (Kelvin formulas for the free kernel).

(a) Let \(A\) be a real symmetric \(j\)-tensor on \(\mathbb{R}^4\) whose diagonal \(x \mapsto A (x, \ldots, x)\) is harmonic. Then for \(x \ne 0\)

\(\displaystyle \frac{1}{j!} A (\partial, \ldots, \partial) \hspace{0.17em} \Gamma (x) = \frac{(- 2)^j}{4 \pi^2} \hspace{0.17em} |x|^{- 2 (j + 1)} A (x, \ldots, x) .\)

(b) Let \(Y \in \text{Sh}_j\) be \(\mathfrak{g}\)-valued, with homogeneous Maxwell solution \(Y^{\natural} (x) = Y (x, \ldots, x)\). Contract the first leg of the curvature kernel at \(x\) with \(Y\), using its rank-\(j\) Taylor coefficient and summing over colours, ordered form indices and jet indices, without a factor \(1 / 2\). For \(x \ne 0\) the result equals

\(\displaystyle \gamma_j \hspace{0.17em} (\iota^{\ast} Y^{\natural}) (x), \qquad \gamma_j = \frac{2^j (j + 1) (j + 2)}{\pi^2} .\)

Here \(\iota (x) = - x / |x|^2\) and \(\iota^{\ast}\) is the pullback of 2-forms:

\(\displaystyle (\iota^{\ast} F)_{km} (x) = |x|^{- 4} \left[ B_{km} - 2| x|^{- 2} \left( x_k \sum_a x_a B_{am} - x_m \sum_a x_a B_{ak} \right) \right], \qquad B_{ab} = F_{ab} (\iota x) .\)

Status: theorem. Lean: Kelvin.harmonic_Green (with R \(= |x|^{- 2}\)), and Kelvin.response_eq (with inverted and gain).

Fix a Wilson family \(C\) with a realization of its defining conditions, a construction record \(U\) with limit table \(S\) and extraction order \(f\), and OPE table data as in VII.4. Let \(D \in \mathbb{N}\), and let \(u\) be a nontrivial filter on \(\mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{N}\) finer than the extraction filter of \(f\).

Assume a weak measuring portfolio \(X\) through degree \(D\). Its data include the observable bounds, Haar bounds, a Haar chart ratio, three price panels, and a constant dominating the label costs through \(D\). It supplies an index \(g (k) \to \infty\), finite-regulator linear label maps, and limiting maps \(\Phi_{k, d}\) on the reference label-coordinate spaces. Eventually in \(k\):

  • \(g (k) \leqslant k\) and \(\text{scale} (k) \leqslant \text{scale} (g (k)) + \log \hat{\beta}_k\);

  • their rescaled read-out maps converge to \(\Phi_{k, d}\) along the same filter \(u\), for every \(d \leqslant D\) and every reference label.

Let \(\lambda_d\) be the fixed equivariant representative of the free quotient in reference label coordinates, and let \(\pi^S_d\) be projection to \(S_d\). 3. Strategy

At order zero, the normalized perturbative expectation is Gaussian. Wick ordering introduces an independent imaginary Gaussian for each insertion, so the pair contraction is the torus stencil covariance minus the infinite-lattice self-covariance at a common insertion. Fourier analysis identifies the contractions at separated points with the Maxwell kernel. At one insertion, the remaining finite-volume error tends to zero because the period grows faster than every inverse power of the spacing. The lattice moments and the continuum Wick moments satisfy the same deletion recursion; induction on the number of distinguishable legs gives VII.1.2. Compactness and dominated convergence then give the tested limit VII.1.1.

For detection, inversion and translation turn a homogeneous Maxwell solution of degree \(j\) into a field whose Taylor expansion at the detector begins at order \(j\). Adding the corresponding lower-rank probes gives a triangular change of variables. In an orthogonal shell basis, the reflected one-leg pairing becomes diagonal with positive entries. Wick's rule then makes distinct monomials orthogonal and gives every monomial a positive squared norm, proving VII.1.4.

The interacting comparison uses the rank estimates of III.11. They imply convergence of all mixed two-point readings in one portfolio. Free detection makes the raw charts eventually invertible and forces their filtration defects to tend to zero. Fixed equivariant retractions of the free inclusions remove these defects recursively, giving the exactly compatible charts of VII.1.6. Proposition VII.1.7 supplies the backward-window and local-expansion estimates used in this construction.

4. Steps

4.1 The free lattice covariance

Lemma VII.1.a (covariance of link forms). For links \(a = (x, i)\), \(b = (y, j)\) and colours \(u, t\),

\(\displaystyle \text{Cov}_{\mu_N} (\langle A (a), u \rangle, \langle A (b), t \rangle) = \langle u, t \rangle \hspace{0.17em} \boldsymbol{1}_{i = j} \hspace{0.17em} [g_N (y - x) - g_N (y + x + e_i)] .\)

Here \(g_N\) is the mean-zero torus Green function, \(- \Delta g_N = \delta_0 - N^{- 4}\) and \(\sum_x g_N = 0\), written as a Fourier sum over \((\mathbb{Z}/ N)^4\) with the zero mode omitted (toroid, lap_eq). Proof sketch. Summation by parts gives \(\textsf{Q}_N (A, B) = - \tfrac{1}{2} \sum \langle A (x, i), \Delta B_i (x) \rangle\), the Feynman-gauge identity curl² + div² = \(- A \cdot \Delta A\) (total). Lemma VII.1.b (separated legs). Let \((N_i, h_i)\) be an itinerary. For a torus site \(x\), write \([x]_N\) for its centred lift in \((- N / 2, N / 2]^4\). Let \(x_i, y_i\) satisfy \(h_i [x_i - y_i + p]_{N_i} \to z \ne 0\) and \(\|h_i [x_i + y_i + p]_{N_i} \| \to \infty\) for every fixed \(p \in \mathbb{Z}^4\). Then for real linear forms \(P\) on \(T_j\) and \(Q\) on \(T_k\),

\(\displaystyle \text{Cov}_{\mu_{N_i}} (P \circ \ell^{h_i}_{x_i}, Q \circ \ell^{h_i}_{y_i}) \to K (P \circ \kappa, Q \circ \kappa) (z) .\)

The image condition holds automatically when both families are homing and \(4 \mid N_i\), since then each coordinate of \(x_i + y_i\) is \(N_i / 2 + O (h_i^{- 1})\) modulo \(N_i\) (finale_home). Proof sketch.

  1. Average over \(W_4\) and expand the forms in component functionals. By Lemma VII.1.a each component pairing becomes \(h^{- (|D| + 2)}\) times a forward difference \(\nabla^+_D\) of \(g_N\), evaluated at \(x - y\) (direct term) and at \(x + y + e\) (image term). The Fourier symbol of \(\nabla^+_D\) is \(\prod_{k \in D} (e^{ip_k} - 1)\).

  2. Direct term (joint_casual). At physical momentum scale \(s\), set \(t = hs\) and write lattice momentum as \(tk\). The unscaled Fourier multiplier becomes \(N^{- 4} t^{|D| - 2}\) times a rescaled symbol; after multiplication by \(h^{- (|D| + 2)}\), the prefactor is \((Nh)^{- 4} s^{|D| - 2}\). The rescaled symbol is jointly smooth near \((0, k)\) for \(k \ne 0\) and equals \(\prod_{l \in D} (ik_l) / |k|^2\) at \(t = 0\). Split momentum space with a smooth bump into a low-momentum piece, dyadic annuli and a tail.

    • Each annulus is a Riemann sum of a compactly supported rescaled integrand and converges to its continuum integral.

    • For separations bounded below by \(R > 0\), the tail is bounded by \(C (D, R) / s\) uniformly in \(N\) once the mesh is fine enough relative to \(hs\) (upper_tail). The proof uses \(|D| + 3\) summations by parts in momentum and the lower bound \(|e^{2 \pi i [x]_f / N} - 1| \gtrsim \|h [x]_N \| / (Nh)\) for a suitable coordinate \(f\).

    • The low piece is \(O (s^{|D| + 2})\).

    • The same bounds hold for the continuum integral after heat regularization. A \(3 \varepsilon\) argument concludes (wrap_tube).

    • Besides \(h \to 0\), this part requires only \(Nh \to \infty\), rather than faster volume growth.

  3. Image term. The same decomposition gives \(0\) when the norm of the centred image displacement tends to infinity in physical units (joint_far).

  4. The limit is identified with \(K\) on rotated on-shell jets, and \(W_4\)-averaging returns \(K (P \circ \kappa, Q \circ \kappa)\).

Lemma VII.1.c (coincident legs). Let \((N_i, h_i)\) be an itinerary and let \(x_i \in \Lambda_{N_i}\) satisfy \(\|h_i [2 x_i + p]_{N_i} \| \to \infty\) for every fixed \(p \in \mathbb{Z}^4\). For real linear forms \(P\) on \(T_j\) and \(Q\) on \(T_k\),

4.2 Order zero as a Gaussian recursion

Lemma VII.1.e (order zero is a joint Gaussian expectation). Fix \(h > 0\), an even period \(N \geqslant 4\), insertion sites \(o_1, \ldots, o_r\), and \(d \in \mathbb{N}\). Let \(s\) be a finite set of distinguishable occurrences, with insertion map \(\tau : s \to \{1, \ldots, r\}\) and key map \(a : s \to \{ \text{keys of rank at most } d\}\). Repeated keys are allowed. Set \(\text{root} (s)_j = \prod_{\nu : \tau (\nu) = j} x_{a (\nu)}\). Then

\(\displaystyle \left\langle \prod_j R_h (\text{root} (s)_j, o_j) \right\rangle^{(0)} = E_h (s) := \mathbb{E} \left[ \prod_{\nu \in s} \left( \ell^h_{o_{\tau (\nu)}, a (\nu)} + i \hspace{0.17em} \xi^{(\tau (\nu))}_{a (\nu)} \right) \right],\)

under \(\mu_N \otimes \bigotimes_{j = 1}^r \mathcal{N} (0, S_h)\). Proof sketch. The weight and the vacuum both have constant coefficient \(1\), so the order-zero coefficient of the normalized expectation is a \(\mu_N\)-integral. The constant coefficient of each lattice jet is its linear part \(\ell^h\). Fubini over the independent auxiliary copies finishes the proof. Lean: Warp.amplitude_zero. The pair covariance of two legs is \(\kappa_h\) (node_wage): the auxiliary copies contribute \(i^2 S_h = - S_h\) for legs assigned to the same insertion and \(0\) otherwise, even if two insertion sites happen to coincide.

Lemma VII.1.f (symmetric deletion identity). Let \(\mu\) be a centred Gaussian on a finite-dimensional real space (possibly degenerate) and \(P_a\), \(a \in s\), real linear functionals with values in \(\mathbb{C}\). Then

\(\displaystyle |s| \hspace{0.17em} \mathbb{E} \prod_{a \in s} P_a = \sum_{a \in s} \sum_{b \in s \setminus \{a\}} \mathbb{E} [P_a P_b] \hspace{0.27em} \mathbb{E} \prod_{c \in s \setminus \{a, b\}} P_c .\)

The products are bilinear, without complex conjugation. For the legs of Lemma VII.1.e, write \(\kappa_h (\nu, \mu) = \kappa_h (\tau (\nu), a (\nu) ; \tau (\mu), a (\mu))\); then \(|s| E_h (s) = \sum_{\nu \ne \mu} \kappa_h (\nu, \mu) E_h (s \setminus \{\nu, \mu\})\). Proof sketch. For powers of a single real functional \(L\) of variance \(v\), \(\mathbb{E}L^{n + 1} = nv \hspace{0.17em} \mathbb{E}L^{n - 1}\) for \(n \geqslant 1\), with \(\mathbb{E}L = 0\), read off from the moment generating function (ev_next). The multilinear case follows by polarization (packet_cancel). The complex case follows by splitting into real and imaginary parts. Lean: exchange_s, Warp.eq_cancel.

Lemma VII.1.g (the continuum recursion). For \(z \in \text{Conf}_r\) set \(c_z (s) =\mathcal{W}_z (\rho \circ \text{root} (s))\). Then for every \(\nu \in s\),

\(\displaystyle c_z (s) = \sum_{\mu \in s \setminus \{\nu\}} \boldsymbol{1}_{\tau (\mu) \ne \tau (\nu)} \hspace{0.17em} K_{a (\nu), a (\mu)} (z_{\tau (\nu)} - z_{\tau (\mu)}) \hspace{0.17em} c_z (s \setminus \{\nu, \mu\}),\)

and summing over \(\nu \in s\) gives the analogue of Lemma VII.1.f. Proof sketch. Removing one on-shell variable \(\rho (x_{a (\nu)})\) at the point \(z_{\tau (\nu)}\) from a Wick moment amounts to applying, at every other point, the derivation that replaces one variable by its pairing with \(a (\nu)\) (take_line). On a monomial the derivation produces the sum over its variables (D_kill). There are no same-point pairings. Lean: cut_one, cut_cancel.

Lemma VII.1.h (convergence of leg products). In the setting of VII.1.2, \(E_{h_i} (s) \to c_z (s)\) for every finite set of legs. Proof sketch. The proof is by strong induction on \(|s|\).

  • For \(s = \emptyset\) both sides equal \(1\) (probability measures).

  • Otherwise divide the identities of Lemmas VII.1.f and VII.1.g by \(|s|\). Use VII.1.3 for the coefficients and the induction hypothesis for the shorter products.

Lemma VII.1.l (Wick rule under linear substitution). Fix \(x \in \text{Conf}_r\) and a finite list of variable occurrences assigned to its insertions. Replace each occurrence \(a\) by a finite complex linear combination \(u_a\) of shell coordinates, allowing different Taylor ranks. Extend \(K\) to these combinations by complex bilinearity. The Wick moment of the resulting products equals

\(\displaystyle \sum_{\operatorname{matchings}} \prod_{\operatorname{pairs}} K (u_a, u_{a'}) (x_{i (a)} - x_{i (a')}) .\)

Only matchings between distinct insertions are included. Proof sketch. Expand the linear forms in the coordinate basis. Multilinearity reduces the formula to the defining Wick sum on coordinate monomials. Lean: Free.linear_substitution.

Lemma VII.1.m (diagonal one-leg kernel). With the bilinear extension of \(K\) from Lemma VII.1.l, choose in each \(\text{Sh}_j\) a basis \((Y_a)\) orthogonal for full component contraction, and set \(\lambda_a = \gamma_j \langle Y_a, Y_a \rangle > 0\). For \(Y \in \text{Sh}_j\), let \(Y_i\) be the order-\(i\) Taylor coefficient of \(Y^{\natural}\) at \(e_{\text{t}}\) and let \(\langle Y_i, \cdot \rangle\) denote the full-contraction probe. Define

\(\displaystyle \epsilon_a = \sum_{i = 0}^j \frac{\gamma_j}{\gamma_i} \langle (Y_a)_i, \cdot \rangle, \qquad \epsilon^{\sigma}_a = \epsilon_a \circ \sigma .\)

Then

\(\displaystyle K (\epsilon_a, \epsilon^{\sigma}_b) (e_{\text{t}}) = \delta_{ab} \lambda_a .\)

Proof sketch. Expand the translated field \(x \mapsto Y^{\natural} (e_{\text{t}} + x)\) into its homogeneous Taylor pieces and apply the Kelvin formula to each piece. Since \(e_{\text{t}} + \iota (e_{\text{t}}) = 0\), its inverted field has no Taylor coefficient at \(e_{\text{t}}\) below order \(j\); its order-\(j\) coefficient is \((- 1)^j\) times the time-reflected tensor \(Y\). The weighted sum defining \(\epsilon_a\) produces exactly \(\gamma_j\) times this inverted translated field. The second-endpoint sign cancels \((- 1)^j\) on the diagonal. Thus pairings with lower ranks vanish and equal-rank pairings are \(\gamma_j\) times full contraction. Symmetry gives vanishing for the opposite rank ordering, and the chosen orthogonal bases give the displayed matrix.

Lemma VII.1.n (orthogonality and positivity; VII.1.4). For finitely supported exponent vectors \(\alpha, \beta\) in the probe variables,

\(\displaystyle \mathcal{W}_{(e_{\text{t}}, 0)} (\Phi x^{\alpha}, \Psi x^{\beta}) = \delta_{\alpha \beta} s_{\alpha}, \qquad s_{\alpha} = \prod_a \alpha_a ! \lambda_a^{\alpha_a} > 0.\)

5. Where the difficulty lies

The main analytic work is the control of finite differences of the torus Green function, uniformly as the spacing shrinks and the volume grows. The Fourier argument must retain oscillatory cancellation; an absolute bound on the full momentum integral would not give the required derivative limits.

Wick ordering uses the infinite-lattice covariance. Consequently the same-insertion contraction is not exactly zero at finite volume. For each fixed stencil offset, the available estimate is \(Cr^2 + A / (Nr^4)\), multiplied by \(h^{- n}\). Taking \(r = h^{n + 1}\) gives \(Ch^{n + 2} + A / (Nh^{5 n + 4})\). The chosen period handles every fixed \(n\) at once. This is a sufficient growth condition; no optimal volume rate is asserted.

The reflection-odd field space removes constant zero modes but introduces an image term. Reporting around the quarter period sends that image to infinite physical distance. Detection requires a separate algebraic argument: the translated inversion formula and the triangular probe substitution establish strict positivity on every nonzero on-shell polynomial, including constants. The chart correction then uses finite-dimensional perturbation theory and the interacting estimates of III.11.

The remaining lower-level estimates for periodic annular sums, the low-momentum bound and the annular Riemann-sum comparison have not been checked line by line. Their uniformity in the period and spacing remains an input to the sketches. (probable)

  2. Imported interacting estimates. The two-point limit follows from the simultaneous portfolio conditions and the rank-comparison error bound. The full analytic proofs supplying those conditions, including the station estimates and curvature-lifting estimates used in VII.1.7, are outside this verification. (probable)

  3. Other assigned packages. The remaining RG local-expansion packages and the perturbative-extension packages are not described here; no proof outline for their internal estimates is supplied. Their placement relative to III.13, III.15 and VII.6 remains to be settled. (tentative)

  4. Scope of the comparison. The Gaussian \(\mu_N\) is defined independently of the RG Gaussian measures. Proposition VII.1.1 requires no identification with them. The interacting short-distance theorem and its combination with the tested order-zero limit are the linked results in VIII.1; no all-orders perturbative comparison follows from this unit.

  5. References and size estimates. The literature citations and attributed source-line counts have not been independently verified. (tentative)
