\section{Path actions and the coupling reset}\label{sec:partII-C}

The path action retains curvature terms through fourth order after each integration. We choose its quadratic part so that $\beta$ is the coefficient of the squared Wilson curvature at zero momentum at every level. Quadratic terms independent of $\beta$ have two vanishing displacement moments. The marginal part generated by the determinant is removed from these terms and absorbed into the coupling.

The path bounds enter Theorem~\ref{prop:MT}, and the coupling formula enters Theorem~\ref{prop:RG}. We evaluate the leading action at its stationary background and express its cubic and quartic terms in transported curvatures. We compare the blocked determinant with a regulated fine-lattice determinant, with an error uniform in the block ratio. The vector and ghost bubbles give the logarithmic coefficient; an affine equation bounds the remaining reset. Lemmas~\ref{lem:C5}--\ref{lem:C8} carry out these steps.
The inputs from Section~\ref{sec:partII-B} are the branches of Lemma~\ref{lem:B1}, the free quadratic forms and their positive row factors, the free interpolation, the rooted frame and axial tree, the stationary inverse estimate, and the block-scaled vertex estimates. Section~\ref{sec:partII-D} supplies the activity construction, its pre-reset estimate, continuity and an exact density representation. The reset here identifies the coupling in that representation.

Throughout, the group and metric are those of Definition~\ref{def:D01}, and the scales are those of Section~\ref{sec:renormalization}. At a step $m+1\to m$ put $\ell=\ell_m$ and $j=J_{m+1}$. Arrays depend on $N,m$ and the intervening block ratios, and are chosen on $\Z^4$ before periodic evaluation. They are independent of the volume index~$p$ and of the running coupling. Constants declared uniform in~$\ell$ are chosen before~$\ell$.

\subsection*{Curvature coordinates}

A path transports curvature to an anchor, where invariant colour contractions can be taken. Write $U_\gamma$ for the ordered link product along~$\gamma$, $\mathfrak p=(a,b)$, $a<b$, for an orientation, and $U_{x;\mathfrak p}$ for a plaquette holonomy. The footprint of a slot is the set of vertices visited by its path and plaquette boundary. The footprint of an atom is the union of its slot footprints and the origin, translated to its anchor. Health means disjointness from $\flags_m(U)$: no plaquette with a vertex in that footprint has chord exceeding~$t_m$.

\begin{definition}[Curvature slots]\label{def:D22}
A slot $s=(\gamma,\mathfrak p)$, with $\gamma:0\to z_s$, has length $|\gamma|+4$ and value
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal F_s(U,x)=\operatorname{Ad}(U_{x+\gamma})\operatorname{proj}_{\g}
\frac{U_{x+z_s;\mathfrak p}-U_{x+z_s;\mathfrak p}^{-1}}2\,.
\end{equation*}
Transport is from $x+z_s$ to~$x$. Set $\mathcal L_{\mathfrak p}^{\rm path}=\sum_i r_{\mathfrak p i}\mathcal F_{\mathfrak p i}$. For $k=2,3,4$, the atoms $\mathfrak a_i^{(k)}$ are real invariant $k$-linear expressions in slots, masked by health on their footprints. Atom length sums slot lengths; $|\mathfrak a_i^{(k)}|$ sums absolute colour coefficients. These arrays retain local terms through fourth order.
\end{definition}

Here $r_{\mathfrak p i}\in\R$ and $\mathcal F_{\mathfrak p i}$ denotes the slot assigned to row~$\mathfrak p$ and index~$i\in\N$. More explicitly, in the orthonormal colour basis $(e_b^{\g})_b$ of Definition~\ref{def:D01}, an atom has the form
\begin{equation*}
\mathfrak a_i^{(k)}(U,x)=\mathbf1_{X_i+x\ {\rm healthy}}
\sum_{b_1,\ldots,b_k} c_i(b_1,\ldots,b_k)
\prod_{\nu=1}^k\langle e_{b_\nu}^{\g},\mathcal F_{s_{i,\nu}}(U,x)\rangle_{\g}\,.
\end{equation*}
The coefficients are invariant under the simultaneous adjoint action on all slots. The finite set $X_i$ is the footprint just defined.

For each stage~$k$, let $\gamma_k>0$ be a uniform lower bound for the free effective plaquette form required at that stage, supplied by Section~\ref{sec:partII-B}. Take it small enough for the top form and both possible incoming stages. The clipping coefficients must leave a nonnegative remainder in this form. Write $c_{\rm dir}=c_{{\rm dir},J_m}>16c_{\rm lin}=16c_{{\rm lin},J_m}>0$. The relaxed health test permits a larger chord farther from the anchor.

\begin{definition}[Path action]\label{def:D23}
Require $c_{\rm dir}\le\min(\gamma_{J_m},1)$, and put
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{clip}_t(u)&=\begin{cases}c_{\rm dir}u^2/2,&u\le t,\\c_{\rm lin}tu,&u>t,\end{cases}\\
\chi^{\rm hlth}_{m,x}(U)&=\mathbf1\{\chord(U_{y;\mathfrak p})\le t_m e^{\operatorname{dist}(x,y)/R_{J_m}^{\rm rel}}\text{ for every }y,\mathfrak p\}\,.
\end{align*}
Define
\begin{align*}
(\pathaction)^{\rm raw}_{N,m}(\beta,U)
&=\beta\sum_{x,\mathfrak p}\left[\operatorname{clip}_{t_m}(\chord(U_{x;\mathfrak p}))+
\tfrac12\chi^{\rm hlth}_{m,x}|\mathcal L_{\mathfrak p}^{\rm path}(U,x)|^2\right]\\
&\quad+\beta\sum_{x,i}(\mathfrak a_i^{(3)}+\mathfrak a_i^{(4)})(U,x)+\sum_{x,i}\mathfrak a_i^{(2)}(U,x),\\
\pathaction_{N,m}(\beta,U)&=\frac1{|\hypercubic|}\sum_{\varsigma\in\hypercubic}(\pathaction)^{\rm raw}_{N,m}(\beta,\varsigma^*U)\,.
\end{align*}
This separates the kinetic coefficient from the loop correction.
\end{definition}

The sums over $x$ are over the torus at level~$m$, and $i\in\N$. The relaxation length satisfies $R_k^{\rm rel}w_k^{\rm path}\ge12$ as in Section~\ref{sec:constants}. The bounds below control actual path lengths on every period.

\begin{lemma}[Path bounds]\label{lem:C1}
For every $N$ and $m\le N$, the construction below chooses arrays independent of $p$ and $\beta$, with
\begin{align*}
\sum_i|r_{\mathfrak p i}|e^{w_{J_m}^{\rm path}\operatorname{length}(\mathcal F_{\mathfrak p i})}&\le A_{J_m}^{\rm path},\\
\sum_i|\mathfrak a_i^{(k)}|e^{w_{J_m}^{\rm path}\operatorname{length}(\mathfrak a_i^{(k)})}
&\le\begin{cases}\Lambda_m^{\rm loop},&k=2,\\A_{J_m}^{\rm path},&k=3,4.\end{cases}
\end{align*}
All these series converge. The constants $A_k^{\rm path},w_k^{\rm path}>0$ depend only on the fixed stage data.
\end{lemma}

The joint proof of Lemmas~\ref{lem:C1} and~\ref{lem:C2} follows Lemma~\ref{lem:C7}. To state the cancellations, put
\begin{equation*}
s_{\mathfrak p\mathfrak q}=\sum_{i:\,\operatorname{orientation}(\mathcal F_{\mathfrak p i})=\mathfrak q}r_{\mathfrak p i}\,.
\end{equation*}
For fixed ordered orientations $\mathfrak q,\mathfrak r$ and colours $b_1,b_2$, let $c_i^{\mathfrak q\mathfrak r;b_1b_2}$ be the corresponding coefficient of the quadratic atom, zero unless its slots have those orientations. Write $z_{i,1},z_{i,2}$ for their offsets. The cancellations remove the kinetic component from the loop atoms.

\begin{lemma}[Kinetic and quadratic cancellations]\label{lem:C2}
For each pair of orientations $\mathfrak q,\mathfrak r$,
\begin{equation*}
c_{{\rm dir},J_m}\delta_{\mathfrak q\mathfrak r}+
\sum_{\mathfrak p}s_{\mathfrak p\mathfrak q}s_{\mathfrak p\mathfrak r}=\delta_{\mathfrak q\mathfrak r}\,.
\end{equation*}
For each ordered orientation-colour pair,
\begin{equation*}
\sum_i c_i^{\mathfrak q\mathfrak r;b_1b_2}=0,
\qquad \sum_i c_i^{\mathfrak q\mathfrak r;b_1b_2}(z_{i,2}-z_{i,1})=0\,.
\end{equation*}
All these moment series converge absolutely.
\end{lemma}

\subsection*{Fourth-order matching}\label{subsec:C-matching}

Near the flat field all health indicators equal one. By $\chord^2=2\wilsonterm$ from Definition~\ref{def:D01}, the leading analytic action is obtained from Definition~\ref{def:D23} by replacing the clipped term by $c_{\rm dir}\wilsonterm$ and omitting the indicators and loop atoms. Denote this action, without its factor~$\beta$, by $\mathsf S_{N,m}$. Its prescribed quadratic part is
\begin{equation*}
\frac12\langle d_1a,\mathsf H_{N,m}d_1a\rangle,
\qquad \mathsf H_{N,m}=c_{{\rm dir},J_m}I+\rho_{N,m}^*\rho_{N,m},
\qquad \widehat{\mathsf H}_{N,m}(0)=I\,.
\end{equation*}
Here $d_1$ is the lattice curl. Section~\ref{sec:partII-B} constructs the free forms along the chosen history: free block integration takes the form at $m+1$ to the form at~$m$. Since $c_{{\rm dir},J_m}\le\gamma_{J_m}$, the difference $\mathsf H_{N,m}-c_{{\rm dir},J_m}I$ has the exponentially decaying positive row factor~$\rho_{N,m}$. Its coefficients, placed on coordinate paths, give $\mathcal L_{\mathfrak p}^{\rm path}$. Thus the free step preserves the coefficient~$\beta$.

We need a division that expresses a gauge-invariant Taylor difference in curvatures. For a momentum~$\xi$ put $q_a(\xi)=e^{i\xi_a}-1$; its exterior action $d_1(\xi)a=q(\xi)\wedge a$ is the curl symbol. A kernel is transverse in one leg when contraction of that leg with~$q$ is zero. Momentum conservation makes one external momentum the negative sum of the others.

\begin{lemma}[Curl division]\label{lem:C3}
For $k\ge3$, a periodic analytic $k$-leg Taylor kernel transverse in every leg factors through a curl in each leg on a smaller complex strip. The plaquette kernel has exponential Fourier decay. For $s\ge0$, a transverse two-leg kernel vanishing to momentum order $s+2$ admits such a factorization with plaquette order~$s$. Scalar division requires vanishing when all momentum components of the divided leg vanish.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We give the division used for the Taylor differences of Definition~\ref{def:D23}; the last hypothesis specifies the scalar case. For one leg let $\delta=\iota_q$ be contraction on exterior tensors. Split a $\delta$-closed tensor as $f=e_a\wedge u+v$, with $u,v$ containing no $e_a$. Define
\begin{equation*}
D_af(\xi)=\frac{f(\xi)-f(\xi)|_{\xi_a=0}}{q_a(\xi)},
\qquad h_af=e_a\wedge D_av,
\qquad R_af=v|_{\xi_a=0}\,.
\end{equation*}
The identity $\delta f=0$ gives $\delta'v=-q_au$, where $\delta'$ contracts the other axes. Consequently
\begin{equation*}
\delta h_af=q_aD_av-e_a\wedge D_a\delta'v
=v-v|_{\xi_a=0}+e_a\wedge u=f-R_af\,.
\end{equation*}
The remainder $R_af$ is again closed and has no component containing~$e_a$. Applying this in the four axes gives the homotopy
\begin{equation*}
f=\delta\left(\sum_{a=1}^4h_aR_{a-1}\cdots R_1f\right)+R_4R_3R_2R_1f\,.
\end{equation*}
The final remainder vanishes in positive exterior degree because it contains none of the four axes. In degree zero it is the restriction to zero momentum of this leg, which is zero by the stated hypothesis. In that case the formula reads $f=\sum_aq_aD_a(R_{a-1}\cdots R_1f)$.

The quotients are analytic even at the zeros of~$q_a$. On a Fourier monomial the identity
\begin{equation*}
\frac{e^{in\xi_a}-1}{e^{i\xi_a}-1}=
\begin{cases}\sum_{v=0}^{n-1}e^{iv\xi_a},&n>0,\\
-\sum_{v=n}^{-1}e^{iv\xi_a},&n<0,\\0,&n=0\end{cases}
\end{equation*}
bounds the quotient on a smaller strip by a constant times $|n|$ times its original exponential weight. The strip loss absorbs $|n|$, so the Fourier series converges absolutely. Each division loses at most one momentum order.

For independent legs, their remaining transverse subspaces do not depend on the divided momentum, so each restriction and division preserves them. For the dependent momentum $\xi_0=-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\xi_i$, first shear the coordinates to replace $\xi_1$ by~$\xi_0$. The other independent contractions remain unchanged. The one-leg formula gives $\delta_0X=f$, retaining the contractions in legs $2,\ldots,k-1$. Write $c_1$ for contraction in leg~$1$. Its defect obeys $\delta_0(c_1X)=c_1f=0$. Solve $\delta_0Y=c_1X$ by the same formula.

On $\xi_1=0$, the restriction $Y_0$ is $\delta_0$-closed. Recursively solve $\delta_0Z=Y_0$ with one fewer independent momentum and extend~$Z$ independently of~$\xi_1$. Then $Y'=Y-\delta_0Z$ vanishes on $\xi_1=0$, and scalar division gives $c_1D=Y'$. Thus
\begin{equation*}
V=X-\delta_0D,\qquad \delta_0V=f,\qquad c_1V=c_1X-\delta_0Y'=0\,.
\end{equation*}
All corrections preserve the other independent contractions. Each recursion removes one independent momentum and raises the dependent exterior degree by two. For three or more legs it terminates above exterior degree four, where the tensor is zero. The independent-leg divisions then finish the factorization.

For two legs, the restriction $Y_0$ is at zero external momentum. Starting from order $s+2$, the first division loses one order, contraction restores one, and the solve for~$Y$ leaves order $s+1\ge1$. Hence $Y_0=0$ directly. The correction and final division leave order~$s$. This verifies the scalar vanishing hypothesis where it is needed. For the Taylor differences of Definition~\ref{def:D23}, gauge invariance supplies transversality because all lower Taylor differences vanish.
\end{proof}

For $z=(z_1,\ldots,z_{k-1})$, contour displacement gives $|K(z)|\le Ce^{-\zeta\sum_\nu|z_\nu|_1}$. Choose the anchor at zero and coordinate paths to~$z_\nu$. Their total slot length is at most $4k+\sum_\nu|z_\nu|_1$. Every path width below~$\zeta$ therefore gives a convergent weighted sum. Reality and simultaneous adjoint invariance pass to the coefficients by conjugation and invariant projection.

At a step, let $v$ be the coarse chart variable and $z$ the fine axial variable. Let $S(v,z)$ be the fine leading action plus $\blockpenalty\sum_e\wilsonterm$ of the predictor discrepancy, without~$\beta'$. It excludes the inherited loop action. Section~\ref{sec:partII-B} supplies
\begin{equation*}
D_zS(v,z(v))=0,\qquad z(0)=0,\qquad
\mathsf Q=D_z^2S(0,0)\ge\nu_jI,\qquad\nu_j>0\,.
\end{equation*}
Put $Wv=(v,Dz(0)v)$ and $B(v,v)[u]=D^3S(0)[Wv,Wv,(0,u)]$. For the estimates below, the kernel norm is the supremum of the absolute Fourier entries on the fixed smaller strip in block units; colour and direction sets are finite. The inverse constant~$E$ includes the stationary inverse and the interpolation factors in these units.

The next formula constructs the higher arrays while keeping their bound independent of the input amplitude after the block ratio has been enlarged.

\begin{lemma}[Classical matching]\label{lem:C4}
The stationary action $\mathsf S_{\rm cl}(v)=S(v,z(v))$ satisfies
\begin{align*}
D^3\mathsf S_{\rm cl}(0)[v^3]&=D^3S(0)[(Wv)^3],\\
D^4\mathsf S_{\rm cl}(0)[v^4]&=D^4S(0)[(Wv)^4]-3B(v,v)\mathsf Q^{-1}B(v,v)\,.
\end{align*}
Its factorial-normalized kernels have bounds $C_3/6$ and $(C_4+3C_3^2E)/24$, where
\begin{equation*}
C_3\le a+\ell^{-1}a',\qquad C_4\le c+\ell^{-1}c'\,.
\end{equation*}
Here $a,c,E$ depend only on the preceding stage data; $a',c'$ may also depend on the input path bound.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use the block map of Definition~\ref{def:D04} and the stationary estimates of Section~\ref{sec:partII-B}. Differentiating $D_zS=0$ once gives $D^2S(0)[Wv,(0,u)]=0$; differentiating twice gives $D^2z(0)[v,v]=-\mathsf Q^{-1}B(v,v)$. In the third derivative of $S(v,z(v))$, the terms containing $D^2z$ or $D^3z$ vanish by these two stationary identities. In the fourth derivative the surviving terms involving $w=D^2z(0)[v,v]$ are
\begin{equation*}
6B(v,v)[w]+3\langle w,\mathsf Qw\rangle
=-3B(v,v)\mathsf Q^{-1}B(v,v)\,.
\end{equation*}
The other term is $D^4S(0)[(Wv)^4]$. The block-scaled inverse and interpolation bounds give the two displayed kernel estimates, after division by $3!$ and~$4!$.

For the bounds on $C_3,C_4$, split the fine leading vertices into Wilson vertices and their difference. The quadratic, cubic and quartic differences have momentum orders at least $3,2,1$, respectively. The quadratic order follows from $\widehat{\mathsf H}(0)=I$ and the two curl factors. The higher orders follow by differentiating gauge invariance after this quadratic cancellation; curvature atoms contribute one derivative per slot, with their nonlinear transport terms included. Thus these differences have at least one more momentum power than the corresponding Wilson vertices, of orders $2,1,0$. Substitution of the free interpolation and rescaling sends that extra power to a factor~$\ell^{-1}$. The Wilson vertices and block penalty give $a,c$, independent of the input atom bound. The differences give $a',c'$. This proves the stated inequalities using the vertex estimates from Section~\ref{sec:partII-B}.

Increase $\ell$ until $\ell^{-1}a',\ell^{-1}c'\le1$. Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:C3} to the cubic difference between $\mathsf S_{\rm cl}$ and the output row action. Subtract the resulting full nonlinear cubic atoms before treating degree four. The remaining quartic difference is transverse, and a second application gives quartic atoms with a fixed bound. This makes the two actions agree through degree four. At $m=N$, the free form acts as the identity on curls, so the same construction matches the Wilson energy through degree four and starts with zero loop atoms. The higher Taylor remainder belongs to the regular activities of Section~\ref{sec:partII-D}.
\end{proof}

The common factor~$\beta'$ in the penalty and fine leading action cancels from the stationary equation; hence the arrays are independent of~$\beta'$. In $\log\det(\beta'M)$, the term $(\dim M)\log\beta'$ is independent of the field and contributes only to the vacuum scalar.

\subsection*{The determinant comparison}\label{subsec:C-determinant}

The Gaussian integration in Section~\ref{sec:partII-B} gives the one-loop action
\begin{equation*}
\mathsf T(v)=\mathcal L_{m+1}(U_v)+\tfrac12\log\det M(v)-\log J_{\rm ax}(v)\,.
\end{equation*}
Here $U_v$ is the stationary fine configuration, $\mathcal L_{m+1}$ is the inherited quadratic loop action, $M$ is the axial Hessian without~$\beta'$, and $J_{\rm ax}$ is the Haar chart ratio, normalized to one at zero. 

For a quadratic Taylor kernel $Y$, including the Taylor factor~$1/2!$, define
\begin{equation*}
[t^2]g(t)=\tfrac12g''(0),\qquad
\alpha_b(Y)=2\operatorname{Re}[t^2]Y_{(2,b),(2,b)}(te_1),\qquad
\alpha_{N,m}=\alpha_b(Y^{\rm blk}_{N,m})\,.
\end{equation*}
The kernel $Y^{\rm blk}_{N,m}$ is the infinite-volume quadratic kernel of $\mathsf T$. Its periodic evaluation has a small finite-period error retained in the activities. The prescribed kinetic kernel has $\alpha_b=1$. Simplicity makes $\alpha_{N,m}$ independent of~$b$, as shown in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:C2}; by construction it is independent of~$p$ and~$\beta'$.

Let $\mathsf J$ be the left-coordinate Hessian of the joint fine action and block penalty, including the coarse frame. Let $R$ insert infinitesimal gauge transformations and $Q$ the axial slice. For $\mu>0$, put
\begin{equation*}
(d_U\phi)_e=\phi(e^-)-\operatorname{Ad}(U_e)\phi(e^+),\qquad
\Delta_U=d_U^*d_U,\qquad
\mathfrak m_\mu(U)=\mu^2[\mu^2(\Delta_U+\mu^2I)^{-1}]^9\,.
\end{equation*}
For the rooted frame $\Psi$ of Section~\ref{sec:partII-B}, define
\begin{equation*}
X=D_{w=0}\bigl[-\log\bigl(\Psi(U,h_{\rm fr})^{-1}\Psi(e^w(U,h_{\rm fr}))\bigr)\bigr]\,.
\end{equation*}
Frame covariance gives $XR=I$. With $P_f$ the projection to fine links, set
\begin{equation*}
F_\mu=d_U^*P_f+\mathfrak m_\mu X,\qquad
\mathsf C_\mu=\mathsf J+F_\mu^*F_\mu,\qquad
\mathsf G_\mu=\Delta_U+\mathfrak m_\mu\,.
\end{equation*}
The completed Hessian has the following local determinant identity.

\begin{lemma}[Gauge completion]\label{lem:C5}
For $v$ in a neighbourhood of zero,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:C-FP}
\mathsf T(v)=\mathcal L_{m+1}(U_v)+\tfrac12\log\det\mathsf C_\mu(v)-\log\det\mathsf G_\mu(v)\,.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use Definitions~\ref{def:D01}, \ref{def:D04} and the axial coordinates and stationary branch of Section~\ref{sec:partII-B}. Gauge invariance and stationarity give $\mathsf JR=0$. Ordering the axial tree by height makes the gauge block triangular with diagonal entries $\pm1$, while the slice block is the identity; hence $\det(R,Q)^2=1$. Since $F_\mu R=\mathsf G_\mu$, in these columns the completed form is
\begin{equation*}
\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf G_\mu&F_\mu Q\\0&I\end{pmatrix}^{\!*}
\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\0&Q^*\mathsf JQ\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf G_\mu&F_\mu Q\\0&I\end{pmatrix}\,.
\end{equation*}
Thus $\det\mathsf C_\mu=(\det\mathsf G_\mu)^2\det(Q^*\mathsf JQ)$. If $s$ changes axial coordinates to left increments at the stationary point, then
\begin{equation*}
\det M=(\det Ds)^2\det(Q^*\mathsf JQ),\qquad J_{\rm ax}=|\det Ds|\,.
\end{equation*}
The second equality follows by comparing the two Haar densities. Positivity of $M$, $J_{\rm ax}$ and $\mathsf G_\mu$ near zero permits logarithms and proves~\eqref{eq:C-FP}.
\end{proof}

The auxiliary fine determinant used for comparison is
\begin{equation*}
\Pi_\mu(U)=\tfrac12\log\det(\mathsf A(U)+d_Ud_U^*+\mathsf M_\mu(U))
-\log\det(\Delta_U+\mathfrak m_\mu(U))\,.
\end{equation*}
Here $\mathsf A$ is the left-coordinate Hessian of the fine leading action and $\mathsf M_\mu$ applies $\mathfrak m_\mu$ to each link component. Let $Y_\mu$ be its infinite-volume quadratic Taylor kernel. The following comparison controls the coupling coefficient.

\begin{lemma}[Block-to-fine coefficient comparison]\label{lem:C6}
For $\mu=\ell^{-1}$, there is a constant $d_{\rm cmp}\ge0$, depending only on the fixed stage bounds, such that
\begin{equation*}
|\alpha_{N,m}-\alpha_b(Y_\mu)|\le d_{\rm cmp}\,.
\end{equation*}
The constant is independent of $N,p,\ell$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use Lemmas~\ref{lem:C2}, \ref{lem:C5} and the free interpolation, stationary inverse and vertex estimates of Section~\ref{sec:partII-B}. Here Lemma~\ref{lem:C2} is used for the input arrays, in descending induction from $m=N$. The full completed Hessian contains a contact term: the second derivative of the predictor multiplied by the first derivative of the penalty. Along the free interpolation, write $a(\xi)$ for this penalty derivative and $B(\xi)$ for the mixed fine/frame trace. The stationary Ward identity gives $a(\xi)=O(|\xi|^2)$ and $B(\xi)=O(|\xi|)$. The change in the tested entry on removing the contact term consists of $aB$, its reflected counterpart, and a term quadratic in~$a$, with bounded analytic factors. It is $O(|\xi|^3)+O(|\xi|^4)$ and has zero~$\alpha_b$.

For the remaining comparison, let $D$ denote external field differentiation. Differentiate the logarithm and use the resolvent identity:
\begin{equation*}
D^2\log\det A=\tr(A^{-1}D^2A-A^{-1}DA\,A^{-1}DA),\qquad
A^{-1}-B^{-1}=A^{-1}(B-A)B^{-1}\,.
\end{equation*}
The difference consists of the block penalty, coarse-frame terms and regulator terms. Each of the first two factors through a space of fixed dimension per block. In the block-scaled norms supplied by Section~\ref{sec:partII-B}, each resulting product has bounded operator norm. Its trace is therefore bounded by that dimension times the norm, independently of~$\ell$.

For the regulator put $P_\mu=\mu^2(\Delta_U+\mu^2I)^{-1}$. At zero background the aliases $k_z=(\xi+2\pi z)/\ell$, in centered representatives, obey
\begin{equation*}
\frac{\mu^2}{|q(k_z)|^2+\mu^2}\le\frac C{1+|z|^2},\qquad
\tr|P_\mu^3|\le C\sum_{z\in\Z^4}(1+|z|^2)^{-3}<\infty\,.
\end{equation*}
The same bounds hold on a fixed smaller complex strip in coarse momentum. In $P_\mu^9=(P_\mu^3)^3$, two derivatives leave at least one undifferentiated cube. A regulator insertion has the prefactor~$\mu^2$, paired with an inverse bounded in block units by $C\mu^{-2}$. Derivatives of that inverse and of $P_\mu$ are bounded in the same scaled norms. Thus the two powers cancel before taking the trace. Put the undifferentiated cube in trace norm and all other factors in operator norm; $|\tr XY|\le\tr|X|\,\|Y\|$ gives a uniform bound~$C_*$ for the analytic difference kernel on a strip of width~$\zeta_*>0$.

To extract the coefficient, restrict its tested entry to $\xi=te_1$. On a circle $|t|=\zeta_*/2$, the analytic bound gives $|[t^2]g|\le4C_*/\zeta_*^2$; hence $|\alpha_b(g)|\le8C_*/\zeta_*^2$. This gives $d_{\rm cmp}$. The estimate applies after the zero-coefficient contact term has been removed.

Finally, folding the auxiliary operator into coarse momentum does not alter~$\alpha_b$. The averaging symbol in Definition~\ref{def:D04} vanishes at every nonzero alias to order at least two in each external leg. These aliases contribute $O(|\xi|^4)$. The zero alias in the tested entry is
\begin{equation*}
\ell^4\ell^{-2}Y_\mu(\xi/\ell)+O(|\xi|^3)\,.
\end{equation*}
The factors are the cell volume and $\ell^{-1}$ per interpolated field. Extracting the quadratic coefficient supplies another~$\ell^{-2}$, so $\ell^4\ell^{-2}\ell^{-2}=1$. This proves the comparison. For a period of side~$n$, Fourier coefficients at nonzero multiples of~$n$ give an error in each quadratic coefficient tested on Fourier basis modes bounded by $C_\ell|\Lambda|e^{-c_\ell n}$. The Fourier basis modes here have unit amplitude at every site, so the bound includes the volume factor; Section~\ref{sec:partII-D} retains it in the activities.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{The logarithmic coefficient}\label{subsec:C-coefficient}

The vertex comparison in Lemma~\ref{lem:C4} gives momentum orders $3,2,1$ for the quadratic, cubic and quartic differences from Wilson. We use it for the input arrays, in descending induction from $m=N$.
The resulting logarithmic term is the part of the one-step drift which remains after the block ratio has been chosen.

\begin{lemma}[One-loop coefficient]\label{lem:C7}
There is a constant $d_j\ge0$, depending only on the fixed stage data, such that, for every step $m+1\to m$ at stage~$j$,
\begin{equation*}
|\alpha_{N,m}+\slope\log\ell_m|\le d_j\,.
\end{equation*}
It is independent of $N,p,\ell_m$ and the running coupling.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use Definition~\ref{def:D01}, Lemmas~\ref{lem:C2}, \ref{lem:C6} and the vertex orders just described.
Choose a colour basis index $b$, external polarization~$e_2$ and external momentum $te_1$. Signed permutations reduce other transverse axis pairs to this one. For nonzero loop momentum $k$, write $\varkappa=|k|$. The leading ghost vertex is $-2ik_2\operatorname{ad}(e_b^{\g})$. The vector vertex has the same orbital term on its four components and the spin term
\begin{equation*}
2it(\delta_{i2}\delta_{j1}-\delta_{i1}\delta_{j2})\operatorname{ad}(e_b^{\g})\,.
\end{equation*}
Simplicity and the trace metric give $\tr_{\g}(\operatorname{ad}(e_b^{\g})^2)=-\casimir$. The bubble pairs vertices at $te_1$ and $-te_1$. The orbital factor $-2ik_2$ is unchanged when $k\mapsto k+te_1$ and $te_1\mapsto-te_1$. Put $J=E_{21}-E_{12}$. Since $\tr J=0$, the orbital--spin cross terms vanish; the spin product is $(2itJ)(-2itJ)=4t^2J^2$, with trace $-8t^2$. The directional traces of the paired vertices are therefore $4(-2ik_2)^2-8t^2$ for the vector and $(-2ik_2)^2$ for the ghost. Divide both by $\varkappa^2|k+te_1|^2$ and differentiate twice at zero. In $\tfrac12\log\det\mathsf C_\mu-\log\det\mathsf G_\mu$, the bubble terms have weights $-1/4$ for the vector and $+1/2$ for the ghost. The extraction of $\alpha_b$ supplies the factor~$2$. Thus ghost minus half vector, with the external Taylor factor included, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:C-pole}
h(k)=4\casimir\left(\frac{k_2^2}{\varkappa^6}-\frac{4k_2^2k_1^2}{\varkappa^8}-\frac1{\varkappa^4}\right)\,.
\end{equation}
Expanding the denominator gives $\varkappa^{-4}-2tk_1\varkappa^{-6}+t^2(4k_1^2\varkappa^{-8}-\varkappa^{-6})+O(t^3)$; substitution yields~\eqref{eq:C-pole} with its sign.

Let $k_{\mu,b}(k)$ be the integrand of $\alpha_b(Y_\mu)$, obtained with these weights and Taylor factors. Then
\begin{equation*}
\alpha_b(Y_\mu)=(2\pi)^{-4}\int_{[-\pi,\pi]^4}\operatorname{Re}k_{\mu,b}(k)\,dk\,.
\end{equation*}
For constants $A_{\rm int},D_{\rm int}\ge0$ depending only on the fixed fine-stage bounds, uniformly in $\mu$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:C-integrand}
|k_{\mu,b}(k)|\le\frac{A_{\rm int}}{(\varkappa+\mu)^4},\qquad
|k_{\mu,b}(k)-h(k)|\le D_{\rm int}\left(\varkappa^{-3}+\mu^2\varkappa^{-6}\right)\quad(\varkappa\ge\mu)\,.
\end{equation}
To obtain them, use $q_a(k)=ik_a+O(\varkappa^2)$ and the vertex orders above. Each free inverse is bounded by $C(\varkappa+\mu)^{-2}$. External derivatives of the inverse follow from $D(A^{-1})=-A^{-1}(DA)A^{-1}$ and, combined with the momentum factors of the vertices, give at worst $(\varkappa+\mu)^{-4}$ in the trace expression. On $\varkappa\ge\mu$, a lattice-to-leading replacement gains one power of~$\varkappa$, giving $C\varkappa^{-3}$. A regulator insertion contributes at most $C\mu^2\varkappa^{-6}$. The terms with a single second-order vertex are bounded by $C(\varkappa^{-2}+\mu^2\varkappa^{-6})$ and so satisfy the same bound on the bounded momentum cube. The product rule gives the same bounds for the ninth-power regulator. Summing proves~\eqref{eq:C-integrand}.

On the spherical shell $\mu<\varkappa\le1$, rotational symmetry gives angular means $k_2^2/\varkappa^2=1/4$ and $k_2^2k_1^2/\varkappa^4=1/24$. For the latter, write the fourth moments as $a=\mathbb E\theta_1^4$ and $b=\mathbb E\theta_1^2\theta_2^2$ on the unit sphere: rotation by $\pi/4$ gives $a=3b$, and $(\sum_i\theta_i^2)^2=1$ gives $4a+12b=1$. Since the sphere has area $2\pi^2$,
\begin{align}\label{eq:C-slope}
(2\pi)^{-4}\int_{\mu<\varkappa\le1}h(k)\,dk
&=\frac{8\pi^2\casimir}{(2\pi)^4}\left(\frac14-\frac16-1\right)\log\mu^{-1}\\
&=-\frac{11\casimir}{24\pi^2}\log\mu^{-1}=-\slope\log\mu^{-1}\,.\notag
\end{align}
The errors in~\eqref{eq:C-integrand} have shell integrals at most $2\pi^2D_{\rm int}$ and $\pi^2D_{\rm int}$. On $\varkappa\le\mu$ the bound is $A_{\rm int}\mu^{-4}$ and the volume is $\pi^2\mu^4/2$; outside $\varkappa\le1$ the bound is $A_{\rm int}$. Hence
\begin{equation*}
|\alpha_b(Y_\mu)+\slope\log\mu^{-1}|
\le(2\pi)^{-4}\{A_{\rm int}\pi^2/2+A_{\rm int}(2\pi)^4+3\pi^2D_{\rm int}\}\,.
\end{equation*}

Combine this bound with Lemma~\ref{lem:C6} and put $\mu=\ell^{-1}$. Their sum gives~$d_j$. The computed slope is $\slope=2b_0$ of Definition~\ref{def:D01}, with its stated trace metric.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemmas~\ref{lem:C1} and~\ref{lem:C2}]
Use Definitions~\ref{def:D01}, \ref{def:D04}, \ref{def:D22}--\ref{def:D23}, the free forms of Section~\ref{sec:partII-B}, and Lemmas~\ref{lem:C3}, \ref{lem:C4}, \ref{lem:C7}. Start at $m=N$ and descend. The condition $c_{{\rm dir},k}\le\gamma_k$ supplies the positive row factor at every level. Its zero-momentum entries give
\begin{equation*}
\widehat{\mathsf H}(0)_{\mathfrak q\mathfrak r}
=c_{{\rm dir},J_m}\delta_{\mathfrak q\mathfrak r}
+\sum_{\mathfrak p}s_{\mathfrak p\mathfrak q}s_{\mathfrak p\mathfrak r}
=\delta_{\mathfrak q\mathfrak r}\,.
\end{equation*}
This proves the first cancellation and also forces $c_{{\rm dir},J_m}\le1$ by taking a diagonal entry.

Subtract $\alpha_{N,m}$ times the kinetic kernel from the quadratic kernel of~$\mathsf T$. The residual is transverse. Its colour bilinear form is scalar: its self-adjoint representing operator commutes with the adjoint action, and its eigenspaces are ideals, so simplicity leaves one eigenvalue. Signed permutations identify the diagonal orientation coefficients. Reflection in an axis belonging to exactly one of two distinct orientations cancels the off-diagonal coefficient. Thus there is one degree-two coefficient, removed by the subtraction. Reflection through the origin removes degree three. The residual link kernel consequently vanishes to order four.

Apply the two-leg part of Lemma~\ref{lem:C3}. Removing its two curl symbols leaves a plaquette kernel of order two. If its Fourier coefficients for a fixed orientation-colour pair are $a(z)$, then
\begin{equation*}
\sum_z a(z)=0,\qquad\sum_z z_a a(z)=0\quad(a=1,\ldots,4)\,.
\end{equation*}
Exponential decay permits termwise differentiation. Put the coefficients on coordinate paths to obtain the quadratic atoms and their absolutely convergent moments. Inherited loop atoms already have these moments, hence a link kernel of order four. Pullback by the free interpolation preserves this order. Only the quadratic Taylor kernel of~$\mathsf T$ is assigned to these atoms; its cubic and higher terms go into the regular activities of Section~\ref{sec:partII-D}.

For the bounds, contour displacement and the path-length estimate after Lemma~\ref{lem:C3} give a sufficiently small width $w_k^{\rm path}>0$. Choose $A_k^{\rm path}$ to bound the top construction and the two possible incoming stages $k,k+1$. Lemma~\ref{lem:C4} permits this choice before the corresponding ratios, by making the input-dependent terms at most one. Choose the widths small enough for these same three constructions.

The new loop contribution has a weighted bound $a_j\ell^4+b_j\log\ell+c_j$. Here $\ell^4$ bounds the trace dimension per coarse cell, and the logarithm comes from the subtracted coefficient. For an inherited order-four link kernel, rescaling gives
\begin{equation*}
\ell^4\ell^{-2}O(|\xi/\ell|^4)=O(\ell^{-2}|\xi|^4)\,.
\end{equation*}
Its weighted pullback is therefore bounded by $C_j\ell^{-2}\Lambda_{m+1}^{\rm loop}$. Choose $\ell$ so that $C_j\ell^{-2}\le1/4$, then raise the coupling entry threshold until
\begin{equation*}
2(a_j\ell^4+b_j\log\ell+c_j)\le\Lambda_m^{\rm loop},\qquad
\Lambda_{m+1}^{\rm loop}\le2\Lambda_m^{\rm loop}\,.
\end{equation*}
The sum is at most $\tfrac12\Lambda_m^{\rm loop}+\tfrac14\cdot2\Lambda_m^{\rm loop}=\Lambda_m^{\rm loop}$. These choices are in the order of Section~\ref{sec:constants}: widths and amplitudes, ratios, then entry thresholds. The first inequality is possible because $2(\cutoffsize_m)^{\theta_{J_m}}$ diverges at each fixed stage. For the second, antitonicity of~$\theta_k$, $J_m\le J_{m+1}\le J_m+1$, and $\theta_k\le1$ give, once $\cutoffsize_m>1$,
\begin{equation*}
\frac{\Lambda_{m+1}^{\rm loop}}{\Lambda_m^{\rm loop}}
\le\left(\frac{\cutoffsize_{m+1}}{\cutoffsize_m}\right)^{\theta_{J_m}}
\le\frac{\cutoffsize_{m+1}}{\cutoffsize_m}\longrightarrow1
\end{equation*}
at each fixed stage. The descent starts with zero loops and the fourth-order Wilson matching of Lemma~\ref{lem:C4}. The kernels and paths are chosen once for all periods. Finally $|\mathcal F_s|\le2$ and $|\mathfrak a_i^{(k)}(U,x)|\le2^k|\mathfrak a_i^{(k)}|$ give absolute convergence of the action.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{The scalar reset}\label{subsec:C-reset}

Use the effective density and weighted sums of Definitions~\ref{def:D06}, \ref{def:D05a}--\ref{def:D05b}, and Lemmas~\ref{lem:C1}--\ref{lem:C2}. Write $\pathaction_{N,m}(\beta,U)=\beta\mathsf K^{\rm kin}_m(U)+\mathcal L_m(U)$. Fix an input curve $\rho'_u$ as in Theorem~\ref{prop:RG} and set $H_u=\blockmap{m}_{\beta'_u}\density\rho'_u$. Section~\ref{sec:partII-D} constructs final regular and forced activities independent of the trial correction~$d$. Write $E_u^{\rm sum}$ and $\mathcal F_u$ for their regular and forced sums. With $b_u=\beta'_u+\alpha_{N,m}$, their normalized trial density is
\begin{equation*}
q_{u,d}(U)=e^{-(b_u+d)\mathsf K^{\rm kin}_m(U)-\mathcal L_m(U)-E_u^{\rm sum}(U)}\mathcal F_u(U)\,.
\end{equation*}
The final regular activities vanish at~$1$; its forced sum equals one, so $q_{u,d}(1)=1$. Take the trial half-width $r_{\rm res}=1$ before choosing $\omega_j^{\rm RG}\ge d_j+1$ in Section~\ref{sec:constants}.

Section~\ref{sec:partII-D} supplies a pre-reset regular sum $E_u^{\rm pre}$ with weighted bound $c\epsilon_m^{\rm reg}/4$, a final regular bound $\epsilon_m^{\rm reg}$ as in~\eqref{eq:I12-activity-caps}, and a value $\Delta_u\in[-1,1]$ satisfying $q_{u,\Delta_u}=H_u/H_u(1)$. Here $c\ge4$ is a fixed stage constant. The estimate on $\Delta_u$ below uses the pre-reset expansion; the all-configuration identity is supplied by that activity construction.

The remaining scalar correction can be read from a single configuration with a definite kinetic energy. 

\begin{lemma}[Coupling reset]\label{lem:C8}
For each input curve of Theorem~\ref{prop:RG}, the coupling in the representation supplied by Section~\ref{sec:partII-D} is continuous and satisfies
\begin{equation*}
\beta_u=\beta'_u-\slope\log\ell_m+e_u,\qquad
|e_u|\le d_j+1\le\omega_j^{\rm RG}\,.
\end{equation*}
The bound is independent of $N,p,u$ and the input curve.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use Definitions~\ref{def:D01}, \ref{def:D06}, Lemmas~\ref{lem:C1}, \ref{lem:C7}, and the pre-reset and final activity estimates just specified. Recall $t=t_m\le1$ from row~7 of Section~\ref{sec:constants}. Put a group element of chord $h_{\rm pr}=\tau t$ on direction-$1$ links whose second coordinate is even, and the identity elsewhere. Continuity along a nonconstant one-parameter subgroup gives $\varepsilon_G>0$ such that every chord in $[0,\varepsilon_G]$ occurs. Choose
\begin{equation*}
0<\tau\le\min\left(\varepsilon_G,\frac14,
\frac{c_{{\rm dir},J_m}}{512(1+A_{J_m}^{\rm path})}\right)\,.
\end{equation*}
Every $(1,2)$ plaquette has chord $h_{\rm pr}$ and all other plaquettes have chord zero. Thus this configuration~$V$ is everywhere healthy. All periods here are even because $4\mid L_p$. Signed permutations preserve the sum of direct plaquette charges, so the direct contribution to $\mathsf K^{\rm kin}_m(V)$ is at least $|\Lambda|c_{{\rm dir},J_m}h_{\rm pr}^2/2$. Row squares are nonnegative. Using the bound $4h_{\rm pr}\le1$ for the curvature factors, the cubic and quartic sums contribute at most
\begin{equation*}
|\Lambda|A_{J_m}^{\rm path}((4h_{\rm pr})^3+(4h_{\rm pr})^4)
\le |\Lambda|c_{{\rm dir},J_m}h_{\rm pr}^2/4\,.
\end{equation*}
The same estimate holds after averaging. Consequently
\begin{equation*}
K_{\rm pr}=\mathsf K^{\rm kin}_m(V)\ge|\Lambda|\chi_{\rm pr}t^2,
\qquad\chi_{\rm pr}=c_{{\rm dir},J_m}\tau^2/4>0\,.
\end{equation*}

The pre-reset representation makes $H_u(1),H_u(V)>0$, and fixes the vacuum scalar $v_u=|\Lambda|^{-1}\log H_u(1)$. Put $\varepsilon=\epsilon_m^{\rm reg}$. On an everywhere-healthy configuration define $\mathsf K^{\rm kin}_{2,m}$ by removing the cubic and quartic atoms from~$\mathsf K^{\rm kin}_m$. It is the full nonlinear direct and row functional, not merely its quadratic Taylor polynomial. The pre-reset identity gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:C-prereset}
\log\frac{H_u(1)}{H_u(V)}
=\beta'_u\mathsf K^{\rm kin}_m(V)+\alpha_{N,m}\mathsf K^{\rm kin}_{2,m}(V)
+\mathcal L_m(V)+E_u^{\rm pre}(V)-E_u^{\rm pre}(1)\,.
\end{equation}
The pre-reset bound of Section~\ref{sec:partII-D}, summed over anchors as in Definition~\ref{def:D05b}, bounds each of the last two values by $|\Lambda|c\varepsilon/4$. No vanishing condition at~$1$ is needed for this preparatory sum.

On everywhere-healthy configurations every curvature slot has norm at most~$t$. Lemma~\ref{lem:C1} and $t\le1$ therefore give
\begin{equation*}
|\mathsf K^{\rm kin}_m-\mathsf K^{\rm kin}_{2,m}|\le2|\Lambda|A_{J_m}^{\rm path}t^3\,.
\end{equation*}
After $\ell$ is fixed, Lemma~\ref{lem:C7} bounds $|\alpha_{N,m}|$ by a stage constant. Raise the coupling entry threshold until $2|\alpha_{N,m}|A_{J_m}^{\rm path}t^3\le c\varepsilon/2$. This is possible since $t^3/\varepsilon=\refbeta_m^{-2/5}(\cutoffsize_m)^3\to0$. Together with~\eqref{eq:C-prereset} this yields
\begin{equation*}
\left|b_uK_{\rm pr}+\mathcal L_m(V)-\log\frac{H_u(1)}{H_u(V)}\right|
\le|\Lambda|c\varepsilon\,.
\end{equation*}
For the final regular sum, Lemma~\ref{lem:D1} and~\eqref{eq:I12-activity-caps} give
\begin{equation*}
|E_u^{\rm sum}(V)|\le\sum_x\sum_{o_i=x}e_i^{\rm maj}
\le|\Lambda|\mathcal E_m\le|\Lambda|\varepsilon\,.
\end{equation*}

Since the activity families are fixed as $d$ varies, the scalar discrepancy is affine:
\begin{align*}
f(u,d)&=(b_u+d)K_{\rm pr}+\mathcal L_m(V)+E_u^{\rm sum}(V)
-\log\frac{H_u(1)}{H_u(V)}\\
&=f(u,0)+dK_{\rm pr}\,.
\end{align*}
Its unique root satisfies
\begin{equation*}
d_u=-f(u,0)/K_{\rm pr},\qquad
|d_u|\le\frac{(c+1)\epsilon_m^{\rm reg}}{\chi_{\rm pr}t_m^2}\le1\,.
\end{equation*}
Increase the entry threshold to obtain the final inequality, using $\epsilon_m^{\rm reg}/t_m^2=\refbeta_m^{-1/10}(\cutoffsize_m)^{-2}\to0$. The block integral and the activity sums are continuous in~$u$ by Section~\ref{sec:partII-D}, so $d_u$ is continuous.

The value $\Delta_u$ constructed in that section satisfies $f(u,\Delta_u)=0$, by evaluating $q_{u,\Delta_u}=H_u/H_u(1)$ at~$V$. Uniqueness gives $\Delta_u=d_u$. Hence multiplication by $H_u(1)=e^{v_u|\Lambda|}$ gives the density identity at~$d_u$. Its coupling is $\beta_u=\beta'_u+\alpha_{N,m}+d_u$. Lemma~\ref{lem:C7} and $|d_u|\le1$ prove the claimed drift.
\end{proof}
