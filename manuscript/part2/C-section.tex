\section{Path actions and the coupling reset}\label{sec:partII-C}

The path action retains curvature terms through fourth order after each integration. We choose its quadratic part so that $\beta$ is the coefficient of the squared Wilson curvature at zero momentum at every level. Quadratic terms independent of $\beta$ have two vanishing displacement moments. The marginal part generated by the determinant is removed from these terms and absorbed into the coupling.

The path bounds enter Theorem~\ref{prop:MT}, and the coupling formula enters Theorem~\ref{prop:RG}. We evaluate the leading action at its stationary background and express its cubic and quartic terms in transported curvatures. We compare the coupling coefficient of the blocked determinant with that of a regulated fine-lattice determinant, with an error uniform in the block ratio. The vector and ghost bubbles give the logarithmic coefficient; an affine equation bounds the remaining reset. Lemmas~\ref{lem:C3}--\ref{lem:C8} carry out these steps.
The inputs are the branches, free forms, stationary interpolation, Gaussian coordinates, vertex, Ward and insertion estimates of Lemmas~\ref{lem:B1}--\ref{lem:B7}. Lemmas~\ref{lem:D1}, \ref{lem:D4}--\ref{lem:D6} supply the activity bounds, pre-reset expansion, exact density identity and continuity. Their proofs belong to Sections~\ref{sec:partII-B} and~\ref{sec:partII-D}; the reset here bounds and identifies the correction in that identity.

Throughout, the group and metric are those of Definition~\ref{def:D01}, and the scales are those of Section~\ref{sec:renormalization}. At a step $m+1\to m$ put $\ell=\ell_m$ and $j=J_{m+1}$. Arrays depend on $N,m$ and the intervening block ratios, and are chosen on $\Z^4$ before periodic evaluation. They are independent of the volume index~$p$ and of the running coupling. Constants declared uniform in~$\ell$ are chosen before~$\ell$.

The arrays are constructed by descending induction on~$m$. Write $(I_m)$ for the path bounds and cancellations of Lemmas~\ref{lem:C1}--\ref{lem:C2} at level~$m$. The top-level Wilson matching in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:C4} starts the induction. Under $(I_{m+1})$, Lemmas~\ref{lem:C4}, \ref{lem:C6}--\ref{lem:C7} treat the step $m+1\to m$; the joint proof after Lemma~\ref{lem:C7} constructs the output arrays and proves~$(I_m)$. Curl division and the gauge determinant identity are independent of this induction.

\subsection*{Curvature coordinates}

A path transports curvature to an anchor, where invariant colour contractions can be taken. Write $U_\gamma$ for the ordered link product along~$\gamma$, $\mathfrak p=(a,b)$, $a<b$, for an orientation, and $U_{x;\mathfrak p}$ for a plaquette holonomy. The footprint of a slot is the set of vertices visited by its path and plaquette boundary. The footprint of an atom is the union of its slot footprints and the origin, translated to its anchor. Health means disjointness from $\flags_m(U)$: no plaquette with a vertex in that footprint has chord exceeding~$t_m$.

\begin{definition}[Curvature slots]\label{def:D22}
A slot $s=(\gamma,\mathfrak p)$, with $\gamma:0\to z_s$, has length $|\gamma|+4$ and value
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal F_s(U,x)=\operatorname{Ad}(U_{x+\gamma})\operatorname{proj}_{\g}
\frac{U_{x+z_s;\mathfrak p}-U_{x+z_s;\mathfrak p}^{-1}}2\,.
\end{equation*}
Transport is from $x+z_s$ to~$x$. Set $\mathcal L_{\mathfrak p}^{\rm path}=\sum_i r_{\mathfrak p i}\mathcal F_{\mathfrak p i}$. For $k=2,3,4$, the atoms $\mathfrak a_i^{(k)}$ are real invariant $k$-linear expressions in slots, masked by health on their footprints. Atom length sums slot lengths; $|\mathfrak a_i^{(k)}|$ sums absolute colour coefficients. These arrays retain local terms through fourth order.
\end{definition}

Here $r_{\mathfrak p i}\in\R$ and $\mathcal F_{\mathfrak p i}$ denotes the slot assigned to row~$\mathfrak p$ and index~$i\in\N$. More explicitly, in the orthonormal colour basis $(e_b^{\g})_b$ of Definition~\ref{def:D01}, an atom has the form
\begin{equation*}
\mathfrak a_i^{(k)}(U,x)=\mathbf1_{X_i+x\ {\rm healthy}}
\sum_{b_1,\ldots,b_k} c_i(b_1,\ldots,b_k)
\prod_{\nu=1}^k\langle e_{b_\nu}^{\g},\mathcal F_{s_{i,\nu}}(U,x)\rangle_{\g}\,.
\end{equation*}
The coefficients are invariant under the simultaneous adjoint action on all slots. The finite set $X_i$ is the footprint just defined.

For each stage~$k$, take $\gamma_k>0$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:B2}, so the free plaquette forms for the top and both incoming stages are at least $2\gamma_k I$. Write $c_{\rm dir}=c_{{\rm dir},J_m}$ and $c_{\rm lin}=c_{{\rm lin},J_m}$. Whenever $c_{\rm dir}\le\gamma_{J_m}$, the form $\mathsf H_{N,m}-c_{\rm dir}I$ is at least $\gamma_{J_m}I$ and has the positive row factor used below. In the relaxed health test, $\operatorname{dist}$ is the unit-lattice maximum distance on the torus. This test permits a larger chord farther from the anchor.

\begin{definition}[Path action]\label{def:D23}
Require $0<16c_{\rm lin}<c_{\rm dir}\le\min(\gamma_{J_m},1)$, and put
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{clip}_t(u)&=\begin{cases}c_{\rm dir}u^2/2,&u\le t,\\c_{\rm lin}tu,&u>t,\end{cases}\\
\chi^{\rm hlth}_{m,x}(U)&=\mathbf1\{\chord(U_{y;\mathfrak p})\le t_m e^{\operatorname{dist}(x,y)/R_{J_m}^{\rm rel}}\text{ for every }y,\mathfrak p\}\,.
\end{align*}
Define
\begin{align*}
(\pathaction)^{\rm raw}_{N,m}(\beta,U)
&=\beta\sum_{x,\mathfrak p}\left[\operatorname{clip}_{t_m}(\chord(U_{x;\mathfrak p}))+
\tfrac12\chi^{\rm hlth}_{m,x}|\mathcal L_{\mathfrak p}^{\rm path}(U,x)|^2\right]\\
&\quad+\beta\sum_{x,i}(\mathfrak a_i^{(3)}+\mathfrak a_i^{(4)})(U,x)+\sum_{x,i}\mathfrak a_i^{(2)}(U,x),\\
\pathaction_{N,m}(\beta,U)&=\frac1{|\hypercubic|}\sum_{\varsigma\in\hypercubic}(\pathaction)^{\rm raw}_{N,m}(\beta,\varsigma^*U)\,.
\end{align*}
This separates the kinetic coefficient from loop corrections.
\end{definition}

The sums over $x$ are over the torus at level~$m$, and $i\in\N$. The clip has a downward jump immediately to the right of~$t$: its value at~$t$ is $c_{\rm dir}t^2/2$, whereas its right limit is $c_{\rm lin}t^2$. For $u>t$ its linear branch gives $\operatorname{clip}_t(u)=c_{\rm lin}tu\ge c_{\rm lin}t^2$, the large-field lower bound used in Lemma~\ref{lem:B-D1}. The bounds below control actual path lengths on every period.

\begin{lemma}[Path bounds]\label{lem:C1}
For every $N$ and $m\le N$, the construction below chooses arrays independent of $p$ and $\beta$, with
\begin{align*}
\sum_i|r_{\mathfrak p i}|e^{w_{J_m}^{\rm path}\operatorname{length}(\mathcal F_{\mathfrak p i})}&\le A_{J_m}^{\rm path},\\
\sum_i|\mathfrak a_i^{(k)}|e^{w_{J_m}^{\rm path}\operatorname{length}(\mathfrak a_i^{(k)})}
&\le\begin{cases}\Lambda_m^{\rm loop},&k=2,\\A_{J_m}^{\rm path},&k=3,4.\end{cases}
\end{align*}
All these series converge. The constants $A_k^{\rm path},w_k^{\rm path}>0$ depend only on the fixed stage data.
\end{lemma}

The joint proof of Lemmas~\ref{lem:C1} and~\ref{lem:C2} follows Lemma~\ref{lem:C7}. To state the cancellations, put
\begin{equation*}
s_{\mathfrak p\mathfrak q}=\sum_{i:\,\operatorname{orientation}(\mathcal F_{\mathfrak p i})=\mathfrak q}r_{\mathfrak p i}\,.
\end{equation*}
For fixed ordered orientations $\mathfrak q,\mathfrak r$ and colours $b_1,b_2$, let $c_i^{\mathfrak q\mathfrak r;b_1b_2}$ be the corresponding coefficient of the quadratic atom, zero unless its slots have those orientations. Write $z_{i,1},z_{i,2}$ for their offsets. The cancellations remove the kinetic component from the loop atoms.

\begin{lemma}[Kinetic and quadratic cancellations]\label{lem:C2}
For each pair of orientations $\mathfrak q,\mathfrak r$,
\begin{equation*}
c_{{\rm dir},J_m}\delta_{\mathfrak q\mathfrak r}+
\sum_{\mathfrak p}s_{\mathfrak p\mathfrak q}s_{\mathfrak p\mathfrak r}=\delta_{\mathfrak q\mathfrak r}\,.
\end{equation*}
For each ordered orientation-colour pair,
\begin{equation*}
\sum_i c_i^{\mathfrak q\mathfrak r;b_1b_2}=0,
\qquad \sum_i c_i^{\mathfrak q\mathfrak r;b_1b_2}(z_{i,2}-z_{i,1})=0\,.
\end{equation*}
All these moment series converge absolutely.
\end{lemma}
The joint proof is given after Lemma~\ref{lem:C7}.

\subsection*{Fourth-order matching}\label{subsec:C-matching}

Near the flat field all health indicators equal one. By $\chord^2=2\wilsonterm$ from Definition~\ref{def:D01}, the leading analytic action is obtained from Definition~\ref{def:D23} by replacing the clipped term by $c_{\rm dir}\wilsonterm$ and omitting the indicators and loop atoms. Denote this action, without its factor~$\beta$, by $\mathsf S_{N,m}$. Its prescribed quadratic part is
\begin{equation*}
\frac12\langle d_1a,\mathsf H_{N,m}d_1a\rangle,
\qquad \mathsf H_{N,m}=c_{{\rm dir},J_m}I+\rho_{N,m}^*\rho_{N,m},
\qquad \widehat{\mathsf H}_{N,m}(0)=I\,.
\end{equation*}
Here $d_1$ is the lattice curl. Lemma~\ref{lem:B2} gives the exact adjacent free relation and the row factor, with $\mathsf H_{N,m}-c_{{\rm dir},J_m}I\ge\gamma_{J_m}I$. Placing its coefficients on coordinate paths gives $\mathcal L_{\mathfrak p}^{\rm path}$. The cell volume contributes $\ell^4$ and each curvature contributes $\ell^{-2}$; hence $\ell^4(\ell^{-2})^2=1$, preserving the coefficient~$\beta$.

We need a division that expresses a gauge-invariant Taylor difference in curvatures. For a momentum~$\xi$ put $q_a(\xi)=e^{i\xi_a}-1$; its exterior action $d_1(\xi)a=q(\xi)\wedge a$ is the curl symbol. Let $\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_{k-1}$ be independent momenta and $\xi_0=-\sum_{\nu=1}^{k-1}\xi_\nu$. Write $\delta_\nu=\iota_{q(\xi_\nu)}$ for bilinear contraction in leg~$\nu$. Consider periodic analytic kernels $f$ valued in $\bigotimes_{\nu=0}^{k-1}\Lambda^1\C^4$, with $\delta_\nu f=0$ in every leg. On the strip $|\operatorname{Im}\xi_\nu^a|<\sigma$, assume each coordinate of~$f$ is bounded by~$A\ge0$. Momentum order~$s$ means vanishing of all Taylor coefficients of total momentum degree less than~$s$.

\begin{lemma}[Curl division]\label{lem:C3}
For $k\ge3$ and $\sigma>0$, there are $0<\eta\le\sigma$ and $K\ge0$, depending only on $k,\sigma$, such that every such $f$ admits a periodic analytic kernel $g$ valued in $\bigotimes_{\nu=0}^{k-1}\Lambda^2\C^4$, with
\begin{equation*}
f=\delta_0\cdots\delta_{k-1}g,\qquad \sup_{\eta\text{-strip}}|g|\le KA\,.
\end{equation*}
Its Fourier coefficients decay exponentially. For $k=2$ the same conclusion holds if $f$ has momentum order $s+2$, with $g$ of order~$s$; $\eta,K$ are independent of $s\ge0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We give the division for a general transverse kernel. Scalar division requires vanishing when the divided leg has zero momentum; positive exterior degree requires only closedness. For one leg let $\delta=\iota_q$ be contraction on exterior tensors. Split a $\delta$-closed tensor as $f=e_a\wedge u+v$, with $u,v$ containing no $e_a$. Define
\begin{equation*}
D_af(\xi)=\frac{f(\xi)-f(\xi)|_{\xi_a=0}}{q_a(\xi)},
\qquad h_af=e_a\wedge D_av,
\qquad R_af=v|_{\xi_a=0}\,.
\end{equation*}
The identity $\delta f=0$ gives $\delta'v=-q_au$, where $\delta'$ contracts the other axes. Since $q_b$ is independent of $\xi_a$ for $b\ne a$, $D_a$ commutes with~$\delta'$, and $D_a(q_au)=u$. Consequently
\begin{equation*}
\delta h_af=q_aD_av-e_a\wedge D_a\delta'v
=v-v|_{\xi_a=0}+e_a\wedge u=f-R_af\,.
\end{equation*}
The remainder $R_af$ is again closed and has no component containing~$e_a$. Applying this in the four axes gives the homotopy
\begin{equation*}
f=\delta\left(\sum_{a=1}^4h_aR_{a-1}\cdots R_1f\right)+R_4R_3R_2R_1f\,.
\end{equation*}
The final remainder vanishes in positive exterior degree because it contains none of the four axes. In degree zero it is the restriction to zero momentum of this leg, which must vanish for scalar division. In that case the formula reads $f=\sum_aq_aD_a(R_{a-1}\cdots R_1f)$.

The quotients are analytic even at the zeros of~$q_a$. On a Fourier monomial the identity
\begin{equation*}
\frac{e^{in\xi_a}-1}{e^{i\xi_a}-1}=
\begin{cases}\sum_{v=0}^{n-1}e^{iv\xi_a},&n>0,\\
-\sum_{v=n}^{-1}e^{iv\xi_a},&n<0,\\0,&n=0\end{cases}
\end{equation*}
bounds the quotient on a smaller strip by a constant times $|n|$ times its original exponential weight. The strip loss absorbs $|n|$, so the Fourier series converges absolutely. Each division loses at most one momentum order.

For independent legs, their remaining transverse subspaces do not depend on the divided momentum, so each restriction and division preserves them. The dependent-leg induction has the following claim: with $r$ independent momenta and dependent exterior degree $d\ge1$, a tensor closed in the dependent leg and transverse in all independent legs has a dependent-leg primitive preserving those transverse conditions, provided $d+2r>4$. Analytic bounds hold after a strip reduction depending only on $r,d$ and the original strip. For $r=0$ the condition gives $d>4$, so the tensor is zero. The induction step below reduces $(r,d)$ to $(r-1,d+2)$, preserving the condition. Initially $r=k-1,d=1$, so it applies for $k\ge3$.

For the dependent momentum $\xi_0=-\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\xi_i$, first shear the coordinates to replace $\xi_1$ by~$\xi_0$. The other independent contractions remain unchanged. The one-leg formula gives $\delta_0X=f$, retaining the contractions in legs $2,\ldots,k-1$. Write $c_1$ for contraction in leg~$1$. Its defect obeys $\delta_0(c_1X)=c_1f=0$. Solve $\delta_0Y=c_1X$ by the same formula. After $c_1$, leg~$1$ has exterior degree zero, so restoring that leg will require only scalar division.

On $\xi_1=0$, the restriction $Y_0$ is $\delta_0$-closed, since $c_1X$ vanishes there. The induction hypothesis with one fewer independent momentum and dependent degree two higher gives $\delta_0Z=Y_0$, preserving the other transverse conditions. Extend~$Z$ independently of~$\xi_1$. Then $Y'=Y-\delta_0Z$ vanishes on $\xi_1=0$, and scalar division gives $c_1D=Y'$. Thus
\begin{equation*}
V=X-\delta_0D,\qquad \delta_0V=f,\qquad c_1V=c_1X-\delta_0Y'=0\,.
\end{equation*}
All corrections preserve the other independent contractions. The two successive solves $f\mapsto X$ and $c_1X\mapsto Y$ each raise the dependent exterior degree by one; contraction $c_1$ changes only leg~$1$. Thus the restricted problem for $Y_0$ has one fewer independent momentum and dependent degree two higher. For three or more legs it terminates above exterior degree four, where the tensor is zero. The independent-leg divisions then finish the factorization.

For two legs, the restriction $Y_0$ is at zero external momentum. Starting from order $s+2$, the first division loses one order, contraction restores one, and the solve for~$Y$ leaves order $s+1\ge1$. Hence $Y_0=0$ directly. The correction and final division leave order~$s$. This verifies the scalar vanishing hypothesis where it is needed. For the Taylor differences of Definition~\ref{def:D23}, gauge invariance supplies transversality because all lower Taylor differences vanish.
\end{proof}

For $z=(z_1,\ldots,z_{k-1})$, contour displacement gives $|K(z)|\le Ce^{-\zeta\sum_\nu|z_\nu|_1}$. Choose the anchor at zero and coordinate paths to~$z_\nu$. Their total slot length is at most $4k+\sum_\nu|z_\nu|_1$. Every path width below~$\zeta$ therefore gives a convergent weighted sum. Reality and simultaneous adjoint invariance pass to the coefficients by conjugation and invariant projection.

At a step, let $v$ be the coarse chart variable and $z$ the fine axial variable. Let $S(v,z)$ be the fine leading action plus $\blockpenalty\sum_e\wilsonterm$ of the predictor discrepancy, without~$\beta'$. It excludes the inherited loop action. Lemma~\ref{lem:B3} supplies
\begin{equation*}
D_zS(v,z(v))=0,\qquad z(0)=0,\qquad
\mathsf Q=D_z^2S(0,0)\ge\nu_jI,\qquad\nu_j>0\,.
\end{equation*}
Put $Wv=(v,Dz(0)v)$ and $\mathsf B(v,v)[u]=D^3S(0)[Wv,Wv,(0,u)]$. For the bounds use the joint fine-link and centered-frame vertices of Lemma~\ref{lem:B5}, before choosing an axial representative. Let $C_3,C_4$ bound their scalar Fourier entries on the fixed smaller strip, both on normalized smooth coarse legs and with one arbitrary fine leg of block norm at most one. In particular, the mixed cubic functional has dual block norm at most~$C_3$. Take $E=\max(1,C_j^{\rm ins})$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:B7}, bounding the completed inverse from the dual block norm to the primal block norm. The mixed functionals annihilate joint gauge directions, so their pairing through this inverse equals their axial pairing through~$\mathsf Q^{-1}$, by Lemma~\ref{lem:B5}. Thus $E$ is independent of~$\ell$ and of the input path amplitude. The constants $a_3,c_4$ depend only on preceding stage data; $a_3^\prime,c_4^\prime$ may also depend on the input path bound.

The next formula constructs the higher arrays while keeping their bound independent of the input amplitude after the block ratio has been enlarged. The stationary identities hold for every admitted ratio; only the uniform array bound requires a larger ratio. Lemma~\ref{lem:B5} gives $C_3\le a_3+\ell^{-1}a_3^\prime$ and $C_4\le c_4+\ell^{-1}c_4^\prime$.

\begin{lemma}[Classical matching]\label{lem:C4}
The stationary action $\mathsf S_{\rm cl}(v)=S(v,z(v))$ satisfies
\begin{align*}
D^3\mathsf S_{\rm cl}(0)[v^3]&=D^3S(0)[(Wv)^3],\\
D^4\mathsf S_{\rm cl}(0)[v^4]&=D^4S(0)[(Wv)^4]-3\mathsf B(v,v)\mathsf Q^{-1}\mathsf B(v,v)\,.
\end{align*}
Its factorial-normalized kernels have bounds $C_3/6$ and $(C_4+3C_3^2E)/24$. For $\ell$ above a threshold depending on the input path data, cubic and quartic arrays satisfying the bounds of Lemma~\ref{lem:C1} make $\mathsf S_{N,m}$ agree with $\mathsf S_{\rm cl}$ through degree four. Their bound depends only on fixed stage data. The degree-$r$ fine-minus-Wilson vertex has momentum order $5-r$, for $r=2,3,4$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use the block map of Definition~\ref{def:D04} and Lemmas~\ref{lem:B3}, \ref{lem:B5}. Differentiating $D_zS=0$ once gives $D^2S(0)[Wv,(0,u)]=0$; differentiating twice gives $D^2z(0)[v,v]=-\mathsf Q^{-1}\mathsf B(v,v)$. In the third derivative of $S(v,z(v))$, the terms containing $D^2z$ or $D^3z$ vanish by these two stationary identities. In the fourth derivative the surviving terms involving $w=D^2z(0)[v,v]$ are
\begin{equation*}
6\mathsf B(v,v)[w]+3\langle w,\mathsf Qw\rangle
=-3\mathsf B(v,v)\mathsf Q^{-1}\mathsf B(v,v)\,.
\end{equation*}
The other term is $D^4S(0)[(Wv)^4]$. In the joint coordinates of Lemma~\ref{lem:B5}, the cubic and quartic stationary kernels have the same identities with smooth interpolated legs. Each mixed cubic functional has dual block norm at most~$C_3$, and the completed inverse has dual-to-primal norm at most~$E$. Their pairing is therefore bounded by~$C_3^2E$. There are three pairings in the symmetric fourth derivative. This gives the kernel estimates after division by $3!$ and~$4!$.

The fine-minus-Wilson vertices have orders $3,2,1$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:B5}. The quadratic order uses $\widehat{\mathsf H}(0)=I$ and two curls. Differentiated gauge invariance then gives the higher orders, including nonlinear curvature transport. Wilson has orders $2,1,0$: the extra power becomes $\ell^{-1}$ under free interpolation and rescaling. The Wilson vertices and penalty give $a_3,c_4$, independently of the input atom bound; the differences give $a_3^\prime,c_4^\prime$.

Increase $\ell$ until $\ell^{-1}a_3^\prime,\ell^{-1}c_4^\prime\le1$. Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:C3} to the cubic difference between $\mathsf S_{\rm cl}$ and the output row action. Subtract the resulting full nonlinear cubic atoms before treating degree four. The remaining quartic difference is transverse, and a second application gives quartic atoms with a fixed bound. This makes the two actions agree through degree four. At $m=N$, the free form acts as the identity on curls, so the same construction matches the Wilson energy through degree four and starts with zero loop atoms. Lemma~\ref{lem:D3} places the remaining top-level difference, including clipping and unhealthy configurations, in the initial activities.
\end{proof}

The common factor~$\beta'$ in the penalty and fine leading action cancels from the stationary equation; hence the arrays are independent of~$\beta'$. In $\log\det(\beta'M)$, the term $(\dim M)\log\beta'$ is independent of the field and contributes only to the vacuum scalar.

\subsection*{The determinant comparison}\label{subsec:C-determinant}

The Gaussian change of coordinates in Lemma~\ref{lem:B4} gives the one-loop action
\begin{equation*}
\mathsf T(v)=\mathcal L_{m+1}(U_v)+\tfrac12\log\det M(v)-\log J_{\rm ax}(v)\,.
\end{equation*}
Here $U_v$ is the stationary fine configuration, $\mathcal L_{m+1}$ is the inherited quadratic loop action, $M$ is the axial Hessian without~$\beta'$, and $J_{\rm ax}$ is the Haar chart ratio, normalized to one at zero. 

For a quadratic Taylor kernel $Y$, including the Taylor factor~$1/2!$, define
\begin{equation*}
[t^2]g(t)=\tfrac12g''(0),\qquad
\alpha_b(Y)=2\operatorname{Re}[t^2]Y_{(2,b),(2,b)}(te_1),\qquad
\alpha_{N,m}=\alpha_b(Y^{\rm blk}_{N,m})\,.
\end{equation*}
The kernel $Y^{\rm blk}_{N,m}$ is the infinite-volume quadratic kernel of $\mathsf T$. Its periodic evaluation has a small finite-period error retained in the activities. The prescribed kinetic kernel has $\alpha_b=1$. Simplicity makes $\alpha_{N,m}$ independent of~$b$, as shown in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:C2}; by construction it is independent of~$p$ and~$\beta'$.

Let $\mathsf J$ be the left-coordinate Hessian of the joint fine action and block penalty, including the coarse frame. Let $R$ insert infinitesimal gauge transformations and $Q$ the axial slice. For $\mu>0$, put
\begin{equation*}
(d_U\phi)_e=\phi(e^-)-\operatorname{Ad}(U_e)\phi(e^+),\qquad
\Delta_U=d_U^*d_U,\qquad
\mathfrak m_\mu(U)=\mu^2[\mu^2(\Delta_U+\mu^2I)^{-1}]^9\,.
\end{equation*}
For the rooted frame $\Psi$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:B4}, define
\begin{equation*}
X=D_{w=0}\bigl[-\log\bigl(\Psi(U,h_{\rm fr})^{-1}\Psi(e^w(U,h_{\rm fr}))\bigr)\bigr]\,.
\end{equation*}
Frame covariance gives $XR=I$. With $P_f$ the projection to fine links, set
\begin{equation*}
F_\mu=d_U^*P_f+\mathfrak m_\mu X,\qquad
\mathsf C_\mu=\mathsf J+F_\mu^*F_\mu,\qquad
\mathsf G_\mu=\Delta_U+\mathfrak m_\mu\,.
\end{equation*}
The completed Hessian has the following local determinant identity.

\begin{lemma}[Gauge completion]\label{lem:C5}
For $v$ in a neighbourhood of zero,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:C-FP}
\mathsf T(v)=\mathcal L_{m+1}(U_v)+\tfrac12\log\det\mathsf C_\mu(v)-\log\det\mathsf G_\mu(v)\,.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use Definitions~\ref{def:D01}, \ref{def:D04} and Lemmas~\ref{lem:B3}--\ref{lem:B4}. Gauge invariance and stationarity give $\mathsf JR=0$. Ordering the axial tree by height makes the gauge block triangular with diagonal entries $\pm1$, while the slice block is the identity; hence $\det(R,Q)^2=1$. Since $F_\mu R=\mathsf G_\mu$, in these columns the completed form is
\begin{equation*}
\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf G_\mu&F_\mu Q\\0&I\end{pmatrix}^{\!*}
\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\0&Q^*\mathsf JQ\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf G_\mu&F_\mu Q\\0&I\end{pmatrix}\,.
\end{equation*}
Thus $\det\mathsf C_\mu=(\det\mathsf G_\mu)^2\det(Q^*\mathsf JQ)$. If $s$ changes axial coordinates to left increments at the stationary point, then
\begin{equation*}
\det M=(\det Ds)^2\det(Q^*\mathsf JQ),\qquad J_{\rm ax}=|\det Ds|\,.
\end{equation*}
The second equality follows by comparing the two Haar densities. Positivity of $M$, $J_{\rm ax}$ and $\mathsf G_\mu$ near zero permits logarithms and proves~\eqref{eq:C-FP}.
\end{proof}

The auxiliary fine determinant used for comparison is
\begin{equation*}
\Pi_\mu(U)=\tfrac12\log\det(\mathsf A(U)+d_Ud_U^*+\mathsf M_\mu(U))
-\log\det(\Delta_U+\mathfrak m_\mu(U))\,.
\end{equation*}
Here $\mathsf A$ is the left-coordinate Hessian of the fine leading action and $\mathsf M_\mu$ applies $\mathfrak m_\mu$ to each link component. Let $Y_\mu$ be its infinite-volume quadratic Taylor kernel. The following comparison controls the coupling coefficient. Enlarge the threshold $u_j$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:B7} to include that of Lemma~\ref{lem:B5}; rows~5 and~8 of Section~\ref{sec:constants} choose odd ratios above it.

\begin{lemma}[Block-to-fine coefficient comparison]\label{lem:C6}
For every odd $\ell\ge u_j$ as in rows~5 and~8 of Section~\ref{sec:constants}, put $\mu=\ell^{-1}$. There is a constant $d_{\rm cmp}\ge0$, depending only on the fixed stage bounds, such that
\begin{equation*}
|\alpha_{N,m}-\alpha_b(Y_\mu)|\le d_{\rm cmp}\,.
\end{equation*}
The constant is independent of $N,p,\ell$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use Lemmas~\ref{lem:C2}, \ref{lem:C5} and Lemmas~\ref{lem:B3}, \ref{lem:B5}--\ref{lem:B7}. The input arrays satisfy Lemma~\ref{lem:C2} by~$(I_{m+1})$. The full completed Hessian contains a contact term: the second derivative of the predictor multiplied by the first derivative of the penalty. Along the free interpolation, write $\mathsf a(\xi)$ for this penalty derivative and $\mathsf b(\xi)$ for the mixed fine/frame trace. Lemma~\ref{lem:B6} gives $\mathsf a(\xi)=O(|\xi|^2)$ and $\mathsf b(\xi)=O(|\xi|)$. The change in the tested entry on removing the contact term consists of $\mathsf a\mathsf b$, its reflected counterpart, and a term quadratic in~$\mathsf a$, with bounded analytic factors. It is $O(|\xi|^3)+O(|\xi|^4)$ and has zero~$\alpha_b$.

For the remaining comparison, let $D$ denote external field differentiation. Differentiate the logarithm and use the resolvent identity:
\begin{equation*}
D^2\log\det A_0=\tr(A_0^{-1}D^2A_0-A_0^{-1}DA_0\,A_0^{-1}DA_0),\qquad
A_0^{-1}-A_1^{-1}=A_0^{-1}(A_1-A_0)A_1^{-1}\,.
\end{equation*}
The difference consists of the block penalty, coarse-frame terms and regulator terms. The block penalty factors through the coarse links and the frame terms through the coarse frame variables: their dimensions per block are $4\dimG$ and $\dimG$. Lemma~\ref{lem:B7} bounds the resulting products in the block-scaled operator norms. Its trace is therefore bounded by that dimension times the norm, independently of~$\ell$.

For the regulator put $P_\mu=\mu^2(\Delta_U+\mu^2I)^{-1}$. At zero background and coarse momentum $\xi\in[-\pi,\pi]^4$, its fibre is diagonal in the $\ell^4$ aliases $k_z=(\xi+2\pi z)/\ell$, where $z$ ranges over centered representatives of $(\Z/\ell\Z)^4$. These lie in the fine momentum cube. Since $|q(k)|\ge2|k|_\infty/\pi$, $|\xi+2\pi z|_\infty\ge\pi|z|_\infty$ and $\mu=\ell^{-1}$,
\begin{equation*}
\frac{\mu^2}{|q(k_z)|^2+\mu^2}\le\frac C{1+|z|^2},\qquad
\tr_{\rm fibre}|P_\mu^3|\le C\sum_{z\in\Z^4}(1+|z|^2)^{-3}<\infty\,.
\end{equation*}
Here $C$ includes the component count, at most $4\dimG$; the trace is at one coarse momentum, with no sum over coarse sites. The same bounds hold on a fixed smaller complex strip in coarse momentum. In $P_\mu^9=(P_\mu^3)^3$, two derivatives leave at least one undifferentiated cube. A regulator insertion has the prefactor~$\mu^2$, paired with an inverse bounded in block units by $C\mu^{-2}$. Lemma~\ref{lem:B7} supplies these bounds through two field insertions for the inverse and $P_\mu$. Thus the two powers cancel before taking the trace. Put the undifferentiated cube in trace norm and all other factors in operator norm; $|\tr XY|\le\tr|X|\,\|Y\|$ gives a uniform bound~$C_*$ for the analytic difference kernel on a strip of width~$\zeta_*>0$.

To extract the coefficient, restrict its tested entry to $\xi=te_1$. On a circle $|t|=\zeta_*/2$, the analytic bound gives $|[t^2]g|\le4C_*/\zeta_*^2$; hence $|\alpha_b(g)|\le8C_*/\zeta_*^2$. This gives $d_{\rm cmp}$. The estimate applies after the zero-coefficient contact term has been removed.

Finally, folding the auxiliary operator into coarse momentum does not alter~$\alpha_b$. The symbol of the weights $w^{\rm av}$ in~\eqref{eq:I12-predictor} vanishes at every nonzero alias to order at least two in each external leg. These aliases contribute $O(|\xi|^4)$. The zero alias in the tested entry is
\begin{equation*}
\ell^4\ell^{-2}Y_\mu(\xi/\ell)+O(|\xi|^3)\,.
\end{equation*}
The factors are the cell volume and $\ell^{-1}$ per interpolated field. Extracting the quadratic coefficient supplies another~$\ell^{-2}$, so $\ell^4\ell^{-2}\ell^{-2}=1$. This proves the comparison. For a period of side~$n$, Fourier coefficients at nonzero multiples of~$n$ give an error in each quadratic coefficient tested on Fourier basis modes bounded by $C_\ell|\Lambda|e^{-c_\ell n}$. The Fourier basis modes here have unit amplitude at every site, so the bound includes the volume factor; the preparatory expansion of Lemma~\ref{lem:D4} retains it in the activities.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{The logarithmic coefficient}\label{subsec:C-coefficient}

The vertex comparison in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:C4} gives momentum orders $3,2,1$ for the quadratic, cubic and quartic differences from Wilson. The input arrays satisfy its hypotheses by~$(I_{m+1})$.
The resulting logarithmic term is the part of the one-step drift which remains after the block ratio has been chosen.

\begin{lemma}[One-loop coefficient]\label{lem:C7}
There is a constant $d_j\ge0$, depending only on the fixed stage data, such that, for every step $m+1\to m$ at stage~$j$ with odd $\ell_m\ge u_j$ as in Section~\ref{sec:constants},
\begin{equation*}
|\alpha_{N,m}+\slope\log\ell_m|\le d_j\,.
\end{equation*}
It is independent of $N,p,\ell_m$ and the running coupling.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use Definition~\ref{def:D01}, Lemmas~\ref{lem:C2}, \ref{lem:C6} and the vertex orders just described.
Choose a colour basis index $b$, external polarization~$e_2$ and external momentum $te_1$. Signed permutations reduce other transverse axis pairs to this one. For nonzero loop momentum $k$, write $\varkappa=|k|$. The leading ghost vertex is $-2ik_2\operatorname{ad}(e_b^{\g})$. The vector vertex has the same orbital term on its four components and the spin term
\begin{equation*}
2it(\delta_{i2}\delta_{j1}-\delta_{i1}\delta_{j2})\operatorname{ad}(e_b^{\g})\,.
\end{equation*}
Simplicity and the trace metric give $\tr_{\g}(\operatorname{ad}(e_b^{\g})^2)=-\casimir$. The bubble pairs vertices at $te_1$ and $-te_1$. The orbital factor $-2ik_2$ is unchanged when $k\mapsto k+te_1$ and $te_1\mapsto-te_1$. Put $S_{\rm sp}=E_{21}-E_{12}$. Since $\tr S_{\rm sp}=0$, the orbital--spin cross terms vanish; the spin product is $(2itS_{\rm sp})(-2itS_{\rm sp})=4t^2S_{\rm sp}^2$, with trace $-8t^2$. The directional traces of the paired vertices are therefore $4(-2ik_2)^2-8t^2$ for the vector and $(-2ik_2)^2$ for the ghost. Include the colour trace, divide by $\varkappa^2|k+te_1|^2$ and take the coefficient of~$t^2$. In $\tfrac12\log\det\mathsf C_\mu-\log\det\mathsf G_\mu$, the bubble terms have weights $-1/4$ for the vector and $+1/2$ for the ghost. The extraction of $\alpha_b$ supplies the factor~$2$. Before taking the coefficient, ghost minus half vector is
\begin{equation*}
-\frac{4\casimir(k_2^2+t^2)}{\varkappa^2|k+te_1|^2}\,.
\end{equation*}
Its quadratic coefficient is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:C-pole}
h(k)=4\casimir\left(\frac{k_2^2}{\varkappa^6}-\frac{4k_2^2k_1^2}{\varkappa^8}-\frac1{\varkappa^4}\right)\,.
\end{equation}
Expanding the denominator gives $\varkappa^{-4}-2tk_1\varkappa^{-6}+t^2(4k_1^2\varkappa^{-8}-\varkappa^{-6})+O(t^3)$; substitution yields~\eqref{eq:C-pole} with its sign.

Let $k_{\mu,b}(k)$ be the integrand of $\alpha_b(Y_\mu)$, obtained with these weights and Taylor factors. Then
\begin{equation*}
\alpha_b(Y_\mu)=(2\pi)^{-4}\int_{[-\pi,\pi]^4}\operatorname{Re}k_{\mu,b}(k)\,dk\,.
\end{equation*}
For constants $A_{\rm int},D_{\rm int}\ge0$ depending only on the fixed fine-stage bounds, uniformly in $\mu$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:C-integrand}
|k_{\mu,b}(k)|\le\frac{A_{\rm int}}{(\varkappa+\mu)^4},\qquad
|k_{\mu,b}(k)-h(k)|\le D_{\rm int}\left(\varkappa^{-3}+\mu^2\varkappa^{-6}\right)\quad(\varkappa\ge\mu)\,.
\end{equation}
To obtain them, use $q_a(k)=ik_a+O(\varkappa^2)$ and the vertex orders above. For the free inverse $R_\mu(k)$, differentiation of $R_\mu A_\mu=I$ gives
\begin{equation*}
\|R_\mu\|\le C(\varkappa+\mu)^{-2},\quad
\|\partial R_\mu\|\le C(\varkappa+\mu)^{-3},\quad
\|\partial^2R_\mu\|\le C(\varkappa+\mu)^{-4}\,.
\end{equation*}
Each first-order vertex has order $\varkappa$, with bounded first external derivative. In a bubble, two momentum derivatives on one inverse give $\varkappa^2(\varkappa+\mu)^{-2}(\varkappa+\mu)^{-4}\le(\varkappa+\mu)^{-4}$; moving derivatives to vertices gives the same bound. On $\varkappa\ge\mu$, a lattice-to-leading replacement gains one power of~$\varkappa$, giving $C\varkappa^{-3}$. A regulator insertion contributes at most $C\mu^2\varkappa^{-6}$.

The terms with one second-order vertex are absent from~$h$. Their quadratic coefficients have bounds
\begin{equation*}
C\left((\varkappa+\mu)^{-2}+\mu^2(\varkappa+\mu)^{-6}\right).
\end{equation*}
The first term comes from one inverse and a bounded second-order vertex derivative; the second retains the regulator factor~$\mu^2$ with the differentiated inverse bounds. On the cube, $\varkappa\le2\pi$ and $\mu\le1$, so this is at most $C' (\varkappa+\mu)^{-4}$. On $\varkappa\ge\mu$, it is at most $C(2\pi\varkappa^{-3}+\mu^2\varkappa^{-6})$. Thus these terms satisfy both inequalities of~\eqref{eq:C-integrand}. The product rule gives the same bounds for the ninth-power regulator. Summing proves~\eqref{eq:C-integrand}.

On the spherical shell $\mu<\varkappa\le1$, rotational symmetry gives angular means $k_2^2/\varkappa^2=1/4$ and $k_2^2k_1^2/\varkappa^4=1/24$. For the latter, write the fourth moments as $a=\mathbb E\theta_1^4$ and $b=\mathbb E\theta_1^2\theta_2^2$ on the unit sphere: rotation by $\pi/4$ gives $a=3b$, and $(\sum_i\theta_i^2)^2=1$ gives $4a+12b=1$. Since the sphere has area $2\pi^2$,
\begin{align}\label{eq:C-slope}
(2\pi)^{-4}\int_{\mu<\varkappa\le1}h(k)\,dk
&=\frac{8\pi^2\casimir}{(2\pi)^4}\left(\frac14-\frac16-1\right)\log\mu^{-1}\\
&=-\frac{11\casimir}{24\pi^2}\log\mu^{-1}=-\slope\log\mu^{-1}\,.\notag
\end{align}
The errors in~\eqref{eq:C-integrand} have shell integrals at most $2\pi^2D_{\rm int}$ and $\pi^2D_{\rm int}$. On $\varkappa\le\mu$ the bound is $A_{\rm int}\mu^{-4}$ and the volume is $\pi^2\mu^4/2$; outside $\varkappa\le1$ the bound is $A_{\rm int}$. Hence
\begin{equation*}
|\alpha_b(Y_\mu)+\slope\log\mu^{-1}|
\le(2\pi)^{-4}\{A_{\rm int}\pi^2/2+A_{\rm int}(2\pi)^4+3\pi^2D_{\rm int}\}\,.
\end{equation*}

Combine this bound with Lemma~\ref{lem:C6} and put $\mu=\ell^{-1}$. Their sum gives~$d_j$. The computed slope is $\slope=2b_0$ of Definition~\ref{def:D01}, with its stated trace metric.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Lemmas~\ref{lem:C1} and~\ref{lem:C2}]
Use Definitions~\ref{def:D01}, \ref{def:D04}, \ref{def:D22}--\ref{def:D23}, the free forms of Lemma~\ref{lem:B2}, and Lemmas~\ref{lem:C3}, \ref{lem:C4}, \ref{lem:C7}. Start at $m=N$ and descend. The condition $c_{{\rm dir},k}\le\gamma_k$ supplies the positive row factor at every level. Its zero-momentum entries give
\begin{equation*}
\widehat{\mathsf H}(0)_{\mathfrak q\mathfrak r}
=c_{{\rm dir},J_m}\delta_{\mathfrak q\mathfrak r}
+\sum_{\mathfrak p}s_{\mathfrak p\mathfrak q}s_{\mathfrak p\mathfrak r}
=\delta_{\mathfrak q\mathfrak r}\,.
\end{equation*}
This proves the first cancellation and also forces $c_{{\rm dir},J_m}\le1$ by taking a diagonal entry.

Subtract $\alpha_{N,m}$ times the kinetic kernel from the quadratic kernel of~$\mathsf T$. The residual is transverse. Its colour bilinear form is scalar: its self-adjoint representing operator commutes with the adjoint action, and its eigenspaces are ideals, so simplicity leaves one eigenvalue. Signed permutations identify the diagonal orientation coefficients. Reflection in an axis belonging to exactly one of two distinct orientations cancels the off-diagonal coefficient. Thus there is one degree-two coefficient, removed by the subtraction. Reflection through the origin removes degree three. The residual link kernel consequently vanishes to order four.

Apply the two-leg part of Lemma~\ref{lem:C3}. Removing its two curl symbols leaves a plaquette kernel of order two. If its Fourier coefficients for a fixed orientation-colour pair are $a_{\rm loop}(z)$, then
\begin{equation*}
\sum_z a_{\rm loop}(z)=0,\qquad\sum_z z_a a_{\rm loop}(z)=0\quad(a=1,\ldots,4)\,.
\end{equation*}
Exponential decay permits termwise differentiation. Put the coefficients on coordinate paths to obtain the quadratic atoms and their absolutely convergent moments. Inherited loop atoms already have these moments, hence a link kernel of order four. Pullback by the free interpolation preserves this order. Only the quadratic Taylor kernel of~$\mathsf T$ is assigned to these atoms; its higher terms enter the preparatory expansion of Lemma~\ref{lem:D4}.

For the bounds, contour displacement and the path-length estimate after Lemma~\ref{lem:C3} give a sufficiently small width $w_k^{\rm path}>0$. Choose $A_k^{\rm path}$ to bound the top construction and the two possible incoming stages $k,k+1$. Lemma~\ref{lem:C4} permits this choice before the corresponding ratios, by making the input-dependent terms at most one. Choose the widths small enough for these same three constructions.

The new loop contribution has a weighted bound $a_j^{\rm lp}\ell^4+b_j^{\rm lp}\log\ell+c_j^{\rm lp}$. Here $\ell^4$ bounds the trace dimension per coarse cell, and the logarithm comes from the subtracted coefficient. For an inherited order-four link kernel, rescaling gives
\begin{equation*}
\ell^4\ell^{-2}O(|\xi/\ell|^4)=O(\ell^{-2}|\xi|^4)\,.
\end{equation*}
Its weighted pullback is therefore bounded by $C_j^{\rm pb}\ell^{-2}\Lambda_{m+1}^{\rm loop}$. Choose $\ell$ so that $C_j^{\rm pb}\ell^{-2}\le1/4$, then raise the coupling entry threshold until
\begin{equation*}
2(a_j^{\rm lp}\ell^4+b_j^{\rm lp}\log\ell+c_j^{\rm lp})\le\Lambda_m^{\rm loop},\qquad
\Lambda_{m+1}^{\rm loop}\le2\Lambda_m^{\rm loop}\,.
\end{equation*}
The sum is at most $\tfrac12\Lambda_m^{\rm loop}+\tfrac14\cdot2\Lambda_m^{\rm loop}=\Lambda_m^{\rm loop}$. These choices are in the order of Section~\ref{sec:constants}: widths and amplitudes, ratios, then entry thresholds. The first inequality is possible because $2(\cutoffsize_m)^{\theta_{J_m}}\to\infty$ as $\refbeta_m\to\infty$ with $\ell_m,J_m$ fixed. For the second, antitonicity of~$\theta_k$, $J_m\le J_{m+1}\le J_m+1$, and $\theta_k\le1$ give, once $\cutoffsize_m>1$,
\begin{equation*}
\frac{\Lambda_{m+1}^{\rm loop}}{\Lambda_m^{\rm loop}}
\le\left(\frac{\cutoffsize_{m+1}}{\cutoffsize_m}\right)^{\theta_{J_m}}
\le\frac{\cutoffsize_{m+1}}{\cutoffsize_m}\longrightarrow1
\end{equation*}
as $\refbeta_m\to\infty$ with $\ell_m$ fixed. The descent starts with zero loops and the fourth-order Wilson matching of Lemma~\ref{lem:C4}. The kernels and paths are chosen once for all periods. Finally $|\mathcal F_s|\le2$ and $|\mathfrak a_i^{(k)}(U,x)|\le2^k|\mathfrak a_i^{(k)}|$ give absolute convergence of the action.
\end{proof}

\subsection*{The scalar reset}\label{subsec:C-reset}

Use Definitions~\ref{def:D06}, \ref{def:D05a}--\ref{def:D05b} and Lemmas~\ref{lem:C1}--\ref{lem:C2}. Write $\pathaction_{N,m}(\beta,U)=\beta\mathsf K^{\rm kin}_m(U)+\mathcal L_m(U)$. Here $\mathsf K^{\rm kin}_m$ contains the clipped term, row squares, and cubic and quartic atoms; $\mathcal L_m$ contains the quadratic atoms, with the hypercubic average in both. For an input curve $\rho'_u$ of Theorem~\ref{prop:RG}, put $\mathcal H_u=\blockmap{m}_{\beta'_u}\density\rho'_u$ and $b_u=\beta'_u+\alpha_{N,m}$. Lemma~\ref{lem:D5} supplies regular and forced sums $E_u^{\rm sum},\mathcal F_u$, independent of $d$, with trial density
\begin{equation*}
q_{u,d}(U)=e^{-(b_u+d)\mathsf K^{\rm kin}_m(U)-\mathcal L_m(U)-E_u^{\rm sum}(U)}\mathcal F_u(U)\,.
\end{equation*}
The flat-field normalizations give $q_{u,d}(1)=1$. Fix $r_{\rm res}=1$ before $\omega_j^{\rm RG}\ge d_j+1$ as in Section~\ref{sec:constants}.

Lemma~\ref{lem:D4} gives a preparatory sum $E_u^{\rm pre}$ with bound $c_{\rm pre}\epsilon_m^{\rm reg}/4$, for a stage constant $c_{\rm pre}\ge4$. Lemmas~\ref{lem:D1}, \ref{lem:D5} give the final bound~\eqref{eq:I12-activity-caps} and $q_{u,\Delta_u}=\mathcal H_u/\mathcal H_u(1)$ for some real $\Delta_u$. The estimate below proves $|\Delta_u|\le1$; that bound is no input to the identity.

The remaining scalar correction can be read from a single configuration with a definite kinetic energy. 

\begin{lemma}[Coupling reset]\label{lem:C8}
For each input curve of Theorem~\ref{prop:RG}, take the families and real $\Delta_u$ supplied by Lemma~\ref{lem:D5}, with no bound on $\Delta_u$ assumed. Then $|\Delta_u|\le1$, and $\beta_u=\beta'_u+\alpha_{N,m}+\Delta_u$ is continuous and satisfies
\begin{equation*}
\beta_u=\beta'_u-\slope\log\ell_m+e_u,\qquad
|e_u|\le d_j+1\le\omega_j^{\rm RG}\,.
\end{equation*}
The bound is independent of $N,p,u$ and the input curve.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use Definitions~\ref{def:D01}, \ref{def:D06}, Lemmas~\ref{lem:C1}, \ref{lem:C7}, and Lemmas~\ref{lem:D1}, \ref{lem:D4}--\ref{lem:D6}. Recall $t=t_m\le1$ from row~7 of Section~\ref{sec:constants}. Put a group element of chord $h_{\rm pr}=\tau t$ on direction-$1$ links whose second coordinate is even, and the identity elsewhere. Continuity along a nonconstant one-parameter subgroup gives $\varepsilon_G>0$ such that every chord in $[0,\varepsilon_G]$ occurs. Choose
\begin{equation*}
0<\tau\le\min\left(\varepsilon_G,\frac14,
\frac{c_{{\rm dir},J_m}}{512(1+A_{J_m}^{\rm path})}\right)\,.
\end{equation*}
Every $(1,2)$ plaquette has chord $h_{\rm pr}$ and all other plaquettes have chord zero. Thus this configuration~$V$ is everywhere healthy. All periods here are even because $4\mid L_p$. Signed permutations preserve the sum of direct plaquette charges, so the direct contribution to $\mathsf K^{\rm kin}_m(V)$ is at least $|\Lambda|c_{{\rm dir},J_m}h_{\rm pr}^2/2$. Row squares are nonnegative. Since $|\operatorname{proj}_{\g}(U-U^{-1})/2|\le\chord(U)$ and adjoint transport is isometric, every slot has norm at most $h_{\rm pr}\le1/4$. Lemma~\ref{lem:C1} bounds the absolute sum of cubic and quartic terms by
\begin{equation*}
2|\Lambda|A_{J_m}^{\rm path}h_{\rm pr}^3
\le |\Lambda|c_{{\rm dir},J_m}h_{\rm pr}^2/4\,.
\end{equation*}
Each signed image of~$V$ has the same plaquette chords with a single nonzero orientation. Hence the bounds apply to every term of the hypercubic average. Consequently
\begin{equation*}
K_{\rm pr}=\mathsf K^{\rm kin}_m(V)\ge|\Lambda|\chi_{\rm pr}t^2,
\qquad\chi_{\rm pr}=c_{{\rm dir},J_m}\tau^2/4>0\,.
\end{equation*}

Lemma~\ref{lem:D4} gives $\mathcal H_u(1),\mathcal H_u(V)>0$. Put $v_u=|\Lambda|^{-1}\log \mathcal H_u(1)$ and $\varepsilon=\epsilon_m^{\rm reg}$. Here $\mathsf K^{\rm kin}_{2,m}$ is the nonlinear direct and row functional of Lemma~\ref{lem:D4}, omitting cubic and quartic atoms. Taking the logarithmic contrast gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:C-prereset}
\log\frac{\mathcal H_u(1)}{\mathcal H_u(V)}
=\beta'_u\mathsf K^{\rm kin}_m(V)+\alpha_{N,m}\mathsf K^{\rm kin}_{2,m}(V)
+\mathcal L_m(V)+E_u^{\rm pre}(V)-E_u^{\rm pre}(1)\,.
\end{equation}
The pre-reset bound of Lemma~\ref{lem:D4}, summed over anchors as in Definition~\ref{def:D05b}, bounds each of the last two values by $|\Lambda|c_{\rm pre}\varepsilon/4$. No vanishing condition at~$1$ is needed for this preparatory sum.

On everywhere-healthy configurations every curvature slot has norm at most~$t$. Lemma~\ref{lem:C1} and $t\le1$ therefore give
\begin{equation*}
|\mathsf K^{\rm kin}_m-\mathsf K^{\rm kin}_{2,m}|\le2|\Lambda|A_{J_m}^{\rm path}t^3\,.
\end{equation*}
After $\ell$ is fixed, Lemma~\ref{lem:C7} bounds $|\alpha_{N,m}|$ by a stage constant. Raise the coupling entry threshold until $2|\alpha_{N,m}|A_{J_m}^{\rm path}t^3\le c_{\rm pre}\varepsilon/2$. This is possible since $t^3/\varepsilon=\refbeta_m^{-2/5}(\cutoffsize_m)^3\to0$. Together with~\eqref{eq:C-prereset} this yields
\begin{equation*}
\left|b_uK_{\rm pr}+\mathcal L_m(V)-\log\frac{\mathcal H_u(1)}{\mathcal H_u(V)}\right|
\le|\Lambda|c_{\rm pre}\varepsilon\,.
\end{equation*}
For the final regular sum, Lemma~\ref{lem:D1} and~\eqref{eq:I12-activity-caps} give
\begin{equation*}
|E_u^{\rm sum}(V)|\le\sum_x\sum_{o_i=x}e_i^{\rm maj}
\le|\Lambda|\mathcal E_m\le|\Lambda|\varepsilon\,.
\end{equation*}

Since the activity families are fixed as $d$ varies, the scalar discrepancy is affine:
\begin{align*}
f(u,d)&=(b_u+d)K_{\rm pr}+\mathcal L_m(V)+E_u^{\rm sum}(V)
-\log\frac{\mathcal H_u(1)}{\mathcal H_u(V)}\\
&=f(u,0)+dK_{\rm pr}\,.
\end{align*}
Since $V$ has no flagged sites, $\mathcal F_u(V)=1$. Evaluating the exact identity of Lemma~\ref{lem:D5} at $V$ gives $f(u,\Delta_u)=0$. As $K_{\rm pr}>0$, this determines
\begin{equation*}
\Delta_u=-f(u,0)/K_{\rm pr},\qquad
|\Delta_u|\le\frac{(c_{\rm pre}+1)\epsilon_m^{\rm reg}}{\chi_{\rm pr}t_m^2}\le1\,.
\end{equation*}
Increase the entry threshold to obtain the final inequality, using $\epsilon_m^{\rm reg}/t_m^2=\refbeta_m^{-1/10}(\cutoffsize_m)^{-2}\to0$. The block integral and activity sums are continuous in~$u$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:D6}, so $\Delta_u$ is continuous.

Multiplication by $\mathcal H_u(1)=e^{v_u|\Lambda|}$ gives the density identity at~$\Delta_u$. Its coupling is $\beta_u=\beta'_u+\alpha_{N,m}+\Delta_u$. Lemma~\ref{lem:C7} and $|\Delta_u|\le1$ prove the claimed drift.
\end{proof}
