\section{Slabs, phases and reconstruction}\label{sec:partII-H}

We prove the phase decomposition of Theorem~\ref{prop:PH} and the reconstruction of Theorem~\ref{prop:RC}, starting from the correlations of Theorem~\ref{prop:IV} and quotient fields of Proposition~\ref{prop:FC}. The phase construction uses the rotations of Lemmas~\ref{lem:G3.1} and~\ref{lem:G3.2} and the tensor of Theorem~\ref{prop:ST}. The rotation proof in Section~\ref{sec:partII-G.3} needs coordinate spectral estimates before continuous rotations are available. Lemma~\ref{lem:H-G3} supplies them; they are not used later in this section.

Reflection positivity enters at several stages. Repeated reflected insertions bound the operator that inserts a word into a time slab. Compressing these operators to zero energy gives a commutative algebra; its characters define the phases. A lattice variance inequality then makes each character invariant under reflection, and small chessboard roots preserve the rank of a finite family of field tests in every character. Neither conclusion follows from commutativity alone.

Slab bounds control continuation to complex times and its boundary distributions. Vacuum uniqueness initially holds almost everywhere in the character space. Locality gives a phase-uniform low-energy estimate; continuity of heat pairings and full support remove the exceptional set. The selected phase is chosen by its Euclidean ground space before reconstruction, and retained throughout. The energy estimate is polynomial; no positive lower bound on nonzero energies is used.

Throughout, a word is $w=\prod_{i=1}^n A_i(x_i)$ with distinct points, and $\tau_z w=\prod_i A_i(x_i+z)$. Fix a norm $\|\cdot\|_{V_d}$ on each coefficient space. Token bounds refer to representatives in the spaces~$V_d$ of Definition~\ref{def:D08}; the resulting operators depend only on their classes in~$F_d$. Write $\vartheta w$ for linear time reflection, acting on labels by the hypercubic action of~$\mathsf s_4^{\rm refl}$, and put $\theta w=\vartheta\bar w$ with the order reversed. Thus $\theta$ is the antilinear reflection in the OS form of Definition~\ref{def:D14}.

\subsection{Slabs and phases}\label{sec:partII-H.1}

Put $E_t=e^{-tH_4^{\rm OS}}$, $P_0=P_4^0$ and $\Omega=\Omega_4^{\rm OS}$ in this subsection. Spatial translations on the OS space are denoted by~$U_y$, $y^4=0$. The insertion rule and buffer are those preceding Lemma~\ref{lem:F4}: $A_wv=w\,\tau_{h_{\rm sl}e_4}v$, and an $r$-buffered word in width~$h_{\rm sl}$ has mutual sup-distance at least~$r$ and times in $[r,h_{\rm sl}-r]$.

Write $\partial_{i,a}w$ for the word with $A_i$ replaced by $\partial_aA_i$. The reflected-word bound eliminates the exterior-dependent constant when one takes arbitrarily high powers. This gives the bounded operators needed for compression. Below words are $r$-buffered, $d$ bounds degrees, $R$ bounds coefficient norms, and $h,k$ are admissible slab widths.
\begin{lemma}[Slab operators]\label{lem:H1.1}
For $d\in\N$, $R\ge0$, there are $C>0$, $Q\in\N$ such that each admitted length-$n$ word has a bounded insertion operator satisfying
\begin{equation*}
\|\mathsf B_{h_{\rm sl}}(w)\|\le C^n(n/r)^{Qn}\qquad(h_{\rm sl}>0,\ 0<r\le1)\,.
\end{equation*}
\begin{align}
\mathsf B_h(w)^*&=\mathsf B_h(\tau_{he_4}\theta w),\qquad
\mathsf B_h(w)\mathsf B_k(v)=\mathsf B_{h+k}(w\,\tau_{he_4}v),\label{eq:H-slab-laws}\\
\partial_{x_i^a}\mathsf B_h(w)&=\mathsf B_h(\partial_{i,a}w).\label{eq:H-slab-derivative}
\end{align}
Derivatives are in operator norm; empty words give~$E_h$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use Definition~\ref{def:D14}, Lemmas~\ref{lem:F3} and~\ref{lem:F4}, and replace~$R$ by $\max(1,R)$. The translation rule defines contractions: on the span of positive words,
\begin{equation*}
\|E_tv\|^2=\langle v,E_{2t}v\rangle\le\|v\|\,\|E_{2t}v\|\,.
\end{equation*}
E7 bounds the separated orbit; iteration gives $\|E_tv\|\le\|v\|$ and preserves null vectors. Translation invariance gives symmetry and commuting unitary spatial translations; Gram continuity gives strong continuity. Since $E_1\psi=0$ implies $E_{2^{-j}}\psi=0$, strong continuity excludes zero spectral mass. Spectral calculus~\cite[Theorem~2.5.1 and Proposition~4.2.3]{Landsman1998} defines $H_4^{\rm OS}=\int_{(0,1]}-\log v\,dP(v)$ from the spectral measure of~$E_1$. Positive roots and continuity give $E_t=e^{-tH_4^{\rm OS}}\to P_0$ strongly.

For a nonempty word, Lemma~\ref{lem:F4} supplies a width~$s$ and $D=C_0^n(n/r)^{Qn}$. On the word span put $A=A_w$, $B=A_{w^\dagger}$, $T=BA$. The OS form gives $\langle Av,u\rangle=\langle v,Bu\rangle$; the reflected-word bound gives $\|T^jv\|\le K_vD^{2j}$. Thus $d_j=D^{-2j}\|T^jv\|$ is bounded and $d_j^2\le\|v\|d_{2j}$. Taking suprema yields $\sup_jd_j\le\|v\|$, hence $\|Av\|^2=\langle v,Tv\rangle\le D^2\|v\|^2$. The bounded extension is unique by density. Right multiplication by $E_{h_{\rm sl}-s}$ gives the required width.

Positive-word matrix entries prove~\eqref{eq:H-slab-laws}. FC bounds differentiated-label operators in a smaller buffer. Twice integrating their matrix entries along segments gives an operator norm Taylor remainder; induction proves~\eqref{eq:H-slab-derivative} and norm smoothness.
\end{proof}

With $e_d$ as time, Lemma~\ref{lem:H1.1} gives $\mathcal H_d^{\rm OS}$, $E_t^{(d)}=e^{-tH_d^{\rm OS}}$ and commuting transverse translations $U_x^{(d)}$, $x^d=0$, using only coordinate reflection positivity and signed permutations. Their joint projection-valued measure $P_d$ is on $[0,\infty)\times\R^3$, with variables $(E,p)$: apply Gelfand theory to the commutative unital C$^*$-algebra generated by $E_1^{(d)}$ and the translation representation of $C_0(\R^3)$~\cite[Theorems~2.4.1, 3.7.11, equation~(3.89) and Proposition~4.2.3]{Landsman1998}, then take the logarithm of the heat coordinate.

For a word combination~$U$, let $v_{d,u}(U)$ be its OS vector translated by~$ue_d$ into positive times. Undo its damping by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H-coordinate-measure}
d\nu_d^U(E,p)=e^{2uE}\,d\langle v_{d,u}(U),P_d(E,p)v_{d,u}(U)\rangle .
\end{equation}
This is independent of~$u$ and finite on $E\le M$. Polarize to~$\nu_d^{UV}$ on integrable tests. For nonnegative integrable~$g_1,g_2$, spectral Cauchy--Schwarz gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H-coordinate-CS}
\left|\int f\,d\nu_d^{UV}\right|^2\le\int g_1\,d\nu_d^U\int g_2\,d\nu_d^V
\quad\text{if }|f|^2\le g_1g_2.
\end{equation}
For $\vartheta_jU=\varepsilon_jU$, $\varepsilon_j\in\{1,-1\}$, ordinary conjugation gives, with $x^d=0$ and $t$ separating the words,
\begin{equation*}
S\bigl(\bar U^{\rm rev}\,\tau_{te_d+x}U\bigr)
=\varepsilon_d\int e^{-tE+ip\cdot x}\,d\nu_d^U(E,p)\,.
\end{equation*}
Here the bar includes scalar conjugation. Both point fields and collinear words are represented.

Opposite reflection signs compare coordinate spectra. Fix a set of axes $Q$ and a finite word combination~$U$ whose offsets have zero coordinates in~$Q$, with $\vartheta_jU=\varepsilon_jU$ for $j\in Q$. Assume that both signs occur. For even nonnegative $f_j\in C_c^\infty(\R)$, set $f_{j,v}(p)=v^{-1}f_j(p/v)$. The next estimate is used in Lemma~\ref{lem:H-G3}.
\begin{lemma}[Opposite reflection signs]\label{lem:H-coordinate-signs}
For every $i\in Q$, $q<0$, $\beta_j\le0$ and $\delta>0$,
\begin{equation*}
\int_{E\le1}\frac{\prod_{j\in Q\setminus\{i\}}f_{j,\lambda^{\beta_j}}(p_j)}{E+\lambda^q}\,d\nu_i^U
=O_\delta(\lambda^\delta)\qquad(\lambda=e^s,\ s\in\N)\,.
\end{equation*}
Constants may depend on $U,Q,q,\beta,f,\delta$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use Definitions~\ref{def:D10}, \ref{def:D14}, E1--E7 and the coordinate derivative law of Proposition~\ref{prop:FC}.

For a placement of margin~$r>0$, E7 bounds the $2m$th transverse derivative of the reflected-pair correlation by $Cr^{-D}$. Its centered difference has spectral integrand $[2\sin(tp_j/2)/t]^{2m}e^{-2uE}$. The integral remainder formula and Fatou give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H-coordinate-moments}
\int e^{-2uE}|p_j|^{2m}\,d\nu_d^U\le Cr^{-D}.
\end{equation}
Extra heat supplies energy powers. Thus for every admissible~$u$ and $m\in\N$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H-all-moments}
\int e^{-2uE}(1+E+|p|)^m\,d\nu_d^U<\infty\,.
\end{equation} For offsets perpendicular to~$d$, take $u=t/2$, $r\asymp t$. Integrating $Ct^{-D}$ against $t^D\,dt$ on $(0,1)$ gives, enlarging~$D$,
$\int(1+E)^{-D}|p_j|^{2m}\,d\nu_d^U<\infty$.

Choose even $\sigma\ge0$ of integral one supported in $1\le|v|\le2$. For $a_j=j+2$, let $L_j$, $0\le j<m$, be the Lagrange basis polynomials of degree $m-1$ with $L_j(a_i^2)=\delta_{ij}$. For radius~$\varrho>0$, define the annular correction by
\begin{equation*}
\int\mathcal C_{m,\varrho}f(q)g(q)\,dq
=\int\sigma(v)\int f(p)\sum_{j<m}L_j((p/(\varrho v))^2)g(\varrho va_j)\,dp\,dv.
\end{equation*}
This gives smooth annular profiles with moment coefficients containing $\varrho^{-2r}$. Since $\sum_jL_j(x)a_j^{2i}=x^i$ for $i<m$, one has $\int p^{2i}(f-\mathcal C_{m,\varrho}f)\,dp=0$ for $i<m$; for even~$f$ the residual has $2m$ vanishing moments. Put $\operatorname{Lap}f(E)=\int e^{-E|t|}\widehat f_+(t)\,dt$. Interpolating $(p^2+E^2)^{-1}$ gives its residual response
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H-residual-response}
\mathcal R_{m,\varrho}f(E)=2E\int\frac{f(p)}{p^2+E^2}\int\sigma(v)
\prod_{j<m}\frac{(\varrho va_j)^2-p^2}{(\varrho va_j)^2+E^2}\,dv\,dp.
\end{equation}
At $E=0$ its value is $2\pi f(0)$. For $f\ge0$ supported in $[-\varrho,\varrho]$, all factors lie in $[0,1]$. For $\varrho\ge1$ with $\operatorname{supp}f_v\subset[-\varrho,\varrho]$, $f_v(p)=v^{-1}f(p/v)$ and sufficiently large~$m$, the moment bound gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H-residual-cost}
\int\mathcal R_{m,\varrho}f_v(E)|p_j|^{2r}\,d\nu_d^U\le C(v^{-1}+\varrho^D).
\end{equation}
Choose $m\ge D/2$ after the moment exponent~$D$. Indeed, let $f\ge0$ be even, supported in $[-1,1]$ and equal to one on $[-\tfrac12,\tfrac12]$, and let $0<v\le1\le\varrho$. Then
\begin{align*}
\frac{c_m}{E+v}&\le\mathcal R_{m,\varrho}f_v(E)\le2\pi\|f_v\|_\infty
&& (0\le E\le1),\\
\mathcal R_{m,\varrho}f_v(E)&\le C(\varrho/E)^D
&& (E\ge1,\ m\ge D/2),
\end{align*}
with $C=C(m,D,\|f\|_{L^1})$. The upper bounds follow from $2E\int(p^2+E^2)^{-1}dp=2\pi$ and from bounding the product by $\prod_j(2\varrho a_j/E)^2$. For the lower bound, the product is at least $2^{-m}(1+E)^{-2m}$ for $|p|\le v$; integrate over $0<p<vE/(2(E+v))$. The value at zero follows by continuity.

Thus $\mathcal R_{m,\varrho}f=\operatorname{Lap}(f-\mathcal C_{m,\varrho}f)$. For $i,j\in Q$, write $\operatorname{Resp}_i(g)=\int\operatorname{Lap}g_i(E)\prod_{j\in Q\setminus\{i\}}g_j(p_j)\,d\nu_i^U$.
For $Q$ containing all axes, the same product test gives
\begin{equation*}
(2\pi)^{-3}\int S(\bar U^{\rm rev}\tau_xU)\prod_k\widehat g_{k,+}(x_k)\,dx
=\varepsilon_i\operatorname{Resp}_i(g)=\varepsilon_j\operatorname{Resp}_j(g)\,.
\end{equation*}
Indeed $\int e^{ipx}\widehat g_+(x)\,dx=2\pi g(-p)=2\pi g(p)$ for the even profiles, whereas the time integral is $\operatorname{Lap}g(E)$. Both temporal profiles have the zero moments required at collisions. For smaller~$Q$, choose an even cutoff $h\in C_c^\infty(\R)$ equal to one near zero and put $g_k(p)=h(p/T)$ in omitted axes, then let $T\to\infty$ by~\eqref{eq:H-residual-cost}. Hence $\operatorname{Resp}_i(g)=-\operatorname{Resp}_j(g)$ when $\varepsilon_i=-\varepsilon_j$. Subtract annular corrections $a_i,a_j$ from the two head profiles. Positivity of the residual responses gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H-sign-transfer}
\operatorname{Resp}_i(g_i-a_i;g_{\ne i})\le
\operatorname{Resp}_i(g_i-a_i;g_j\mapsto a_j)+\operatorname{Resp}_j(g_j-a_j;g_i\mapsto a_i).
\end{equation}
Expand the signed identity and discard $\operatorname{Resp}_j(g_j-a_j;g_{\ne j})\ge0$.

Write $\lambda=e^t$, $t\in\N$; the index~$s$ below counts visits. We prove a stronger response bound by backward induction along a finite walk $i_0,\ldots,i_L$ in~$Q$ with opposite signs at consecutive visits. Choose the walk so that $i_0=i$, every axis occurs among $i_0,\ldots,i_{L-1}$, and $i_L$ has already occurred. Such a walk exists because both signs occur: insert an axis of the opposite sign between any two visits of the same sign. Put $V_s=\{i_0,\ldots,i_{s-1}\}$, the axes visited before step~$s$.

Fix $K\ge0$ and $\delta>0$. The induction claim is that there is $a_s>0$ such that, for every fixed family of even nonnegative compactly supported profiles~$f_j$, every choice of exponents
\begin{equation*}
0\le\omega_j\le a_s\quad(j\in V_s),\qquad
-K\le\omega_j\le a_s\quad(j\in Q\setminus V_s),
\end{equation*}
and every $0\le\eta\le a_s$ with $\omega_{i_s}\le\eta$, fix $R_0\ge1$ so that the head profile is supported in $[-R_0\lambda^\eta,R_0\lambda^\eta]$. All sufficiently large subtraction orders~$m$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\tag{$\mathrm H_s$}\label{eq:H-sign-induction}
0\le\int\mathcal R_{m,R_0\lambda^\eta}f_{i_s,\lambda^{\omega_{i_s}}}(E)
\prod_{j\in Q\setminus\{i_s\}}f_{j,\lambda^{\omega_j}}(p_j)\,d\nu_{i_s}^U
\le C\lambda^\delta.
\end{equation}
The constant and the threshold in~$\lambda$ may depend on the fixed profiles, exponents, $R_0$ and~$m$. In particular, the claim includes nonpositive width exponents on all unvisited axes; allowing small positive exponents there permits subsequent visits.

At $s=L$ every width is at least one, so all transverse profile suprema are bounded. Equation~\eqref{eq:H-residual-cost} with $r=0$ bounds the integral by $C(\lambda^{-\omega_{i_L}}+\lambda^{\eta D})$. Choose $a_LD<\delta$; the lower bound $\omega_{i_L}\ge0$ proves the terminal case of~\eqref{eq:H-sign-induction}.

For the induction step put $i=i_s$, $j=i_{s+1}$ and $P=K|Q\setminus\{i,j\}|$. Make the choices in this order:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Take $\alpha'=a_{s+1}>0$ from the next case of~\eqref{eq:H-sign-induction}.
\item Choose $r\in\N$ with $2r\alpha'>P+\max(K,\delta)+1$.
\item Take the moment exponent~$D$ in~\eqref{eq:H-residual-cost} for $|p_j|^{2r}$. The subtraction orders will be taken above the required moment and collision orders.
\item Choose $0<\alpha=a_s<\min(\alpha',\delta/(D+1))$.
\end{enumerate}
Correct the head~$i$ at radius $R_0\lambda^\eta$ and the next head~$j$ at radius $\lambda^{\alpha'}$. The correction in~$i$ is bounded in absolute value by a fixed even nonnegative annular profile of width~$\lambda^\eta$: its finitely many coefficients contain only factors $\lambda^{2r'(\omega_i-\eta)}\le1$. The second term of~\eqref{eq:H-sign-transfer} is therefore bounded by the next case of~\eqref{eq:H-sign-induction}, since the exponent in axis~$i$ has become nonnegative. In its first term, the correction in~$j$ is bounded by $C_r\lambda^{-2r\alpha'}|p_j|^{2r}$; its coefficients are bounded because $\omega_j<\alpha'$. The other transverse suprema cost at most~$\lambda^P$. Equation~\eqref{eq:H-residual-cost} thus bounds this first term by
\begin{equation*}
C\lambda^{P-2r\alpha'+\max(-\omega_i,\eta D)}
\le C\lambda^{P-2r\alpha'+\max(K,\alpha D)}
=O(\lambda^\delta).
\end{equation*}
Both residual responses are nonnegative. Applying~\eqref{eq:H-sign-transfer} with the positive annular majorants proves~\eqref{eq:H-sign-induction}; the finitely many terms may have different subtraction orders, each chosen large enough for the preceding integral identities.

Apply~\eqref{eq:H-sign-induction} at $s=0$ with $K\ge\max(-q,\max_j(-\beta_j))$, $\omega_i=q$, $\omega_j=\beta_j$ for $j\ne i$, and $\eta=0$. Take the head profile $f_i$ to be the bump used in the lower response bound, and keep the given transverse profiles. On $E\le1$ the lower response bound makes the stated integral at most $c_m^{-1}$ times the integral in~\eqref{eq:H-sign-induction}. End on an already visited axis; all widths in~$Q$, including the head, are then at least one. The preceding upper and lower response bounds prove the claim.


\end{proof}

Fix $a\ne b$, a line axis~$l$, and $\lambda=e^s$, $s\in\N$. Set $c_a=c_b=-100$, $c_l=-150$ when $l\notin\{a,b\}$, and $c_j=-300$ for $j\notin\{a,b,l\}$.
For fixed even nonnegative $h\in C_c^\infty(\R)$ put
\begin{equation*}
w_j=\lambda^{1+c_j},\quad H_\lambda^{(d)}(p)=\prod_{j\ne d}w_j^{-1}h(p_j/w_j),\quad
I_d(U;q)=\int_{E\le1}\frac{H_\lambda^{(d)}(p)}{E+\lambda^q}\,d\nu_d^U.
\end{equation*}
Set $B=2^{-4}\prod_j(1+\varepsilon_j\vartheta_j)U$ for a word combination~$U$ with offsets on $\R e_l$, $\varepsilon_a=\varepsilon_b=-1$ and the other signs positive. Let $J_{ij}\in F_4$ be real, symmetric, $\hypercubic$-tensorial and conserved: $\sum_j\partial_jJ_{jk}=0$ for every~$k$. The index $J_{db}$ in a measure denotes the one-point word $J_{db}(0)$. No Ward normalization is assumed.

For the exterior estimate, consider tests $\Psi_\lambda(E,p)$ such that, for some fixed $K$ and every $L\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H-scaled-tests}
|\Psi_\lambda(E,p)|\le C_L\lambda^K e^{-\lambda^{-c_d}E}
\prod_{j\ne d}(1+\lambda^{-c_j}|p_j|)^{-L}\,.
\end{equation}
Write $X_\lambda=\{E>1\}\cup\bigcup_{j\ne d}\{|p_j|>w_j\}$. The scaled Fourier--Laplace tests in Section~\ref{sec:partII-G.3}, with fixed polynomial prefactors, have this bound. All constants below may depend on the fixed words, tensor, profiles and exponents.

These are the coordinate inputs to the rotation proof; their proof uses no result of Section~\ref{sec:partII-G}.
\begin{lemma}[Coordinate spectral estimates]\label{lem:H-G3}
For every projected collinear~$B$ and $\delta>0$,
\begin{align*}
I_d(B;-3000)&=O(\lambda^{\kappa_d}),&\kappa_d&=\begin{cases}1&d=l,\\-c_l&d\ne l,\end{cases}\\
I_d(J_{db};c_d)&=O_\delta(\lambda^\delta)&&(d\ne b),\\
\int_{E\le1}\frac{EW^4}{(E+W)^4}H_\lambda^{(d)}\,d\nu_d^{J_{dd}}&=O_\delta(\lambda^\delta W^4),&W&=\lambda^{-99}.
\end{align*}
The pairings obey~\eqref{eq:H-coordinate-CS} and~\eqref{eq:H-all-moments}; one-insertion heat matrix elements are holomorphic for $\operatorname{Re}t>0$. Moreover, for $M\ge0$,
\begin{equation*}
I_d(J_{dd};0)=O(\lambda^{900}),\qquad
\int_{X_\lambda}\Psi_\lambda\,d\nu_d^{UV}=O_M(\lambda^{-M})\,.
\end{equation*}
\end{lemma}
We prove the sign bounds first, then the diagonal estimate by conservation.
\begin{proof}
In direction~$l$, choose $2u$ separating the offsets and use
$R_v(E)=\int e^{-E|t|}(1-\chi(t))\phi(vt)\,dt$, where $0\le\chi\le1$ is compactly supported and equals one on $[-2u,2u]$, and $\phi=\widehat{\sigma*\sigma}_+\ge0$, $\phi(0)=1$. It satisfies $C/(E+v)\le R_v(E)\le C'v^{-1}e^{-2uE}$ for $E\le1$ and small~$v$. For the lower bound integrate over $R<t<R+c/(E+v)$ beyond $\operatorname{supp}\chi$; for the upper bound drop $1-\chi$ and scale. The removed profile has bounded supremum. Finite moments~\eqref{eq:H-coordinate-moments} permit the induction~\eqref{eq:H-sign-transfer} through an opposite transverse sign. Taking $\delta=1$ in this sign-transfer argument gives $O(\lambda)$ when $d=l$. For $d\ne l$, omit the $l$ profile, apply Lemma~\ref{lem:H-coordinate-signs} on $Q=\{j:j\ne l\}$, and restore its supremum $O(\lambda^{-1-c_l})$. Both signs occur in~$Q$ for the specified parity. Taking $\delta=1$ gives $O(\lambda^{-c_l})$. The same lemma applied to the point word~$J_{db}$, $d\ne b$, gives the second estimate.

It remains to treat the diagonal tensor. Write $C_{ab,cd}(x)=S(J_{ab}(0)J_{cd}(x))$, $x\ne0$. Choose $D_{\rm col}$ bounding the collision powers of these pairs and their first derivatives. For even $\chi=1$ near zero with compact support put $\Pi_{D_{\rm col}}f(t)=\chi(t)\sum_{r<D_{\rm col}}f^{(r)}(0)t^r/r!$, and define
\begin{equation*}
R^0_{ab,cd}(f)=\int C_{ab,cd}(x)\left[\prod_jf_j(x_j)-\prod_j\Pi_{D_{\rm col}}f_j(x_j)\right]dx.
\end{equation*}
Taylor vanishing gives integrability; a zero Taylor factor recovers the unsubtracted integral. Average under signed symmetries and pair exchange to obtain~$R$. Conservation makes $\sum_i\partial_iR_{ib,cd}$ a jet at zero. Inversion $x\mapsto-x$ makes~$R$ even, eliminating even divergence orders. To remove the linear orders, put $\chi_0(x)=\prod_j\chi(x_j)$ and $q_{ab,cd}=\sum_i R_{ib,cd}(\partial_i(x_a\chi_0))$, and replace the position extension by $R_{ab,cd}-q_{ab,cd}\delta_0$. Reflections kill coefficients unless indices occur in pairs; permuting them proves the pair symmetries. The subtraction preserves these and cancels linear divergence. The Fourier transform, still~$R$, has divergence polynomials with no terms below degree three. Write $F_{b,cd}=\sum_i p_iR_{ib,cd}$ for the Fourier divergence polynomial. Symmetry gives
\begin{equation*}
\sum_{j,k\ne d}p_jp_kR_{kd,jd}
=\sum_{j\ne d}p_j(F_{d,jd}-p_dR_{dd,jd})
=p_d^2R_{dd,dd}+\sum_{j\ne d}p_jF_{d,jd}-p_dF_{d,dd}\,.
\end{equation*}
Applying the same two contractions to the other index pair gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H-tensor-responses}
\begin{split}
\mathcal Q_0&=R_{dd,dd},\\
\mathcal Q_1&=\sum_{j,k\ne d}p_jp_kR_{kd,jd}=p_d^2\mathcal Q_0+D_1,\\
\mathcal Q_2&=\sum_{j,k,r,s\ne d}p_jp_kp_rp_sR_{sk,rj}=p_d^2\mathcal Q_1+D_2.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
Here multiplication by $p_j$ acts on the test, and $D_1,D_2$ are the polynomial remainders.

We also need profiles that do not annihilate the remainders. On a fixed temporal annulus, Lemma~\ref{lem:H-coordinate-signs} and~\eqref{eq:H-coordinate-CS} bound each $R_{ab,cd}$ by $O_\delta(\lambda^\delta)$. For a diagonal entry, divide the annular profile by $p_d$ and move the derivative to the label, which then has both signs. Moment subtraction justifies this integration by parts at the collision. To remove the remaining zero-moment restrictions, expand one transverse profile into its residual and its fixed annular correction. For an odd profile~$f$ supported in $[-r_0,r_0]$, use $p^{-1}\mathcal C_{m,\varrho}(pf)$ as correction; for $f_v(p)=v^{-1}f(p/v)$, splitting at $E=v$ gives $\int_{|p|\le r_0v}|pf_v(p)|/(p^2+E^2)\,dp\le C/(E+v)$ from $|f(p)|\le C|p|$. The kernel factor $(1+E)^{-2m}$ makes the even lower bound dominate this residual, including zero. Equations~\eqref{eq:H-coordinate-CS} and~\eqref{eq:H-sign-transfer} control it. Each correction fixes one more transverse annulus with bounded coefficients $\lambda^{2r\beta}$, $\beta\le0$. Induction ends after at most three coordinates; finite Taylor terms are bounded on normalized shrinking profiles. 

Since each transverse momentum costs at most~$W$, the fixed-annulus bounds for $\mathcal Q_0,\mathcal Q_1,\mathcal Q_2$ are respectively $O_\delta(\lambda^\delta)$, $O_\delta(\lambda^\delta W^2)$ and $O_\delta(\lambda^\delta W^4)$. The errors cost $O(W^4)$ and $O(W^6)$: divergence has degree at least three, and the contractions add one and three powers. Choose one subtraction order~$m$ above these degrees; its zero temporal moments annihilate the errors.

Put $g_{v,r}(p)=v^{-1}(p/v)^{-2r}\sigma(p/v)$, so $p^2g_{v,r+1}=v^2g_{v,r}$. Keep the transverse profile~$H_\lambda^{(d)}$ fixed and set
\begin{equation*}
c_r(t)=W^{-2r}\operatorname{Re}\mathcal Q_r(g_{tW,r}),\qquad
d_r=W^{-2r}\operatorname{Re}\mathcal Q_r\bigl((1-\mathcal C_{m,8})(g_{W,r}-g_{2W,r})\bigr).
\end{equation*}
Conservation gives $c_{r+1}(t)=t^2c_r(t)+O_\delta(\lambda^\delta W^2)$ for $t=1,2$, $r=0,1$. The correction of $g_{W,r}-g_{2W,r}$ has zero zeroth moment and other even moments $O(W^2)$, so the annular bounds give $d_r=c_r(1)-c_r(2)+O_\delta(\lambda^\delta W^2)$. Reflection positivity for~$J_{dd}$ makes the spectral representation of~$\mathcal Q_0$ positive. On corrected profiles it gives $d_0\ge0$, $d_1\le0$, $d_2\ge0$, since multiplication by $p_d^2$ becomes $-E^2$. Consequently $4d_0=5d_1-d_2+O_\delta(\lambda^\delta W^2)$ and $d_0=O_\delta(\lambda^\delta W^2)$.

The response difference is nonnegative. Subtracting $(E^2+4W^2v^2)^{-1}$ from $(E^2+W^2v^2)^{-1}$ in~\eqref{eq:H-residual-response}, with $1\le|v|\le2$, bounds it below by $c_mEW^2/(E+W)^4$ for $0<E\le1$; both vanish at zero. Since the response difference and $H_\lambda^{(d)}$ are nonnegative,
\begin{equation*}
c_m\int_{E\le1}\frac{EW^2}{(E+W)^4}H_\lambda^{(d)}\,d\nu_d^{J_{dd}}
\le d_0=O_\delta(\lambda^\delta W^2);
\end{equation*}
multiplying by $W^2/c_m$ gives the third estimate.


On $E>1$,~\eqref{eq:H-scaled-tests} gives $C_M\lambda^{-M}e^{-t_0E}$ for fixed admissible~$t_0$. On $|p_j|>w_j$, the inequality $\lambda^{-c_j}|p_j|>\lambda$ gives arbitrary Schwartz gain. Integrate by~\eqref{eq:H-all-moments} and use~\eqref{eq:H-coordinate-CS} for mixed terms. Finiteness of $\nu_d^{J_{dd}}(E\le1)$ and $\|H_\lambda^{(d)}\|_\infty=O(\lambda^{897})$ give the crude bound. By Lemma~\ref{lem:H1.1}, a positively buffered insertion has matrix elements $\langle v,Ae^{-tH_d^{\rm OS}}u\rangle$ with bounded~$A$, holomorphic for $\operatorname{Re}t>0$ by spectral calculus.
\end{proof}

Rotations make spatial translations trivial at zero energy. Compression then removes slab width and placement.
\begin{lemma}[Compression algebra]\label{lem:H1.2}
The operators $M^0(w)$ are independent of admissible slab width and placement. They generate a separable commutative unital C$^*$-algebra~$\mathfrak A_0$ on $\ker H_4^{\rm OS}$, with cyclic and separating vector~$\Omega$. Its spectrum~$\phases$ and vacuum measure~$\nu$ satisfy
\begin{equation*}
S^\xi(w)=\xi(M^0(w)),\qquad S(w)=\int_{\phases}\xi(M^0(w))\,d\nu(\xi)\,.
\end{equation*}
The space~$\phases$ is compact metric and $\nu$ is a full-support probability measure.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use Definition~\ref{def:D14}, Lemmas~\ref{lem:H1.1} and~\ref{lem:G3.2}. For fixed words and large~$z^4$, the correlation is $\langle v,U_{\vec z}E_{z^4-c}v'\rangle$. Strong heat convergence makes fixed time shifts negligible, uniformly in~$\vec z$.

Choose an orthogonal reflection~$K$ taking $y+e_4$ to $|y+e_4|e_4$. Since $\langle K(y+e_4),Ke_4\rangle=1$, its ray $tKe_4$ has positive time component. Rotating the displacements $te_4+y+e_4$ and~$te_4$ makes their difference purely temporal, so their correlations become equal in the limit. Removing the extra~$e_4$ as above gives $U_yP_0=P_0$ by density.

The insertion identities $\mathsf B_{h+t}(w)=\mathsf B_h(w)E_t$ and $\mathsf B_{h+t}(\tau_{te_4}w)=E_t\mathsf B_h(w)$ give width and placement independence of $M^0(w)=P_0\mathsf B_h(w)|_{\ker H_4^{\rm OS}}$, and $M^0(w)\Omega=P_0[w]$, $\langle\Omega,M^0(w)\Omega\rangle=S(w)$.

Successive use of~\eqref{eq:H-slab-laws} proves the cluster identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H-clusters}
\lim_{j\to\infty}S\bigl(\tau_{z_1(j)}w_1\cdots\tau_{z_m(j)}w_m\bigr)
=\langle\Omega,M^0(w_1)\cdots M^0(w_m)\Omega\rangle
\end{equation}
whenever $(z_{i+1}(j)-z_i(j))^4\to\infty$. In the induction, $\psi_j\to\psi$ and $t_j\to\infty$ imply $U_{y_j}E_{t_j}\psi_j\to P_0\psi$ for arbitrary~$y_j$, by contraction and spatial invariance on the ground space.

Place four words at $0$, $j(e_4+2e_1)$, $j(2e_4+e_1)$ and $3j(e_4+e_1)$. Exchanging the first and fourth axes interchanges the two middle time orders. Covariance and~\eqref{eq:H-clusters} equate the resulting products after simultaneous rotation of their labels and points. A second application with all displacements along $e_4+e_1$ removes that simultaneous rotation. The middle factors therefore commute on the dense span $P_0[w]=M^0(w)\Omega$. This also gives cyclicity; the empty word gives the unit. If $a\Omega=0$, then $ab\Omega=0$ on this dense span, so $a=0$.

Basis labels and dense countable position sets generate~$\mathfrak A_0$ by norm continuity. Gelfand theory and the vacuum state give its compact metric spectrum and probability measure~\cite[Theorems~2.4.1 and~2.8.2]{Landsman1998}. Separation gives full support: $\int|\xi(a)|^2d\nu=\|a\Omega\|^2$ vanishes only for $a=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Phase fields and selection]\label{lem:H1.3}
For every $\xi\in\phases$, its table satisfies E1--E7, retains the inclusions, derivatives, conjugation, rotations and stress laws of the mixture, and

\end{equation*}
Each separated value is continuous in~$\xi$. For $\nu$-almost every~$\xi$, the time-axis OS ground space is one-dimensional.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We first prove the operator identities
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H-reflection}
M^0(\vartheta w)=M^0(w),\qquad M^0(hw)=M^0(w)\quad(h\in O(4))\,.
\end{equation}
Use Lemma~\ref{lem:H1.2}, the point limits of Lemma~\ref{lem:E3.1} and~\eqref{eq:F-conf}. For a real grid word set $Z=M^0(w)-M^0(\vartheta w)$. Equation~\eqref{eq:H-slab-laws} gives $Z^*=-Z$, so $a=\langle\Omega,Z^2\Omega\rangle=-\|Z\Omega\|^2\le0$. Lattice variance gives
\begin{equation*}
0\le\left\langle\left(\sum_{i=0}^qg_i\right)^2\right\rangle
\le(q+1)R_\varepsilon^2+2\sum_{i<j}\left(\operatorname{Re}\langle f_if_j\rangle+\varepsilon\right)\,.
\end{equation*}
For fixed $q,T$, intersect the finitely many eventual sets for the bounds and pair estimates. Take the ordered cutoffs, then $T\to\infty$. Expand each pair into four separated moments; their signed sum tends to~$a$ by~\eqref{eq:H-clusters}. Thus $0\le(q+1)[R_\varepsilon^2+q(a+\varepsilon)]$. First $q\to\infty$, then $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, gives $a\ge0$. Separation of~$\Omega$ gives $Z=0$. Grid density, norm continuity and multilinearity give all words.

The cluster identity gives simultaneous orthogonal invariance of two-factor vacuum entries along rays with positive time before and after rotation. Such a ray exists unless time is reversed, when composition with~$\vartheta$ suffices. Writing $v=M^0(u)\Omega$ and $x=M^0(w)\Omega$, simultaneous invariance and time-reflection invariance give $\langle v,x\rangle=\langle g^{-1}v,\vartheta g^{-1}x\rangle=\langle v,g\vartheta g^{-1}x\rangle$; transformations on vectors act on their words. Density gives every hyperplane reflection. Successively fixing basis images expresses an orthogonal map as at most four reflections, proving the second identity.

Use also Definitions~\ref{def:D10}, \ref{def:D14}, FC and ST. Testing on positive words gives the permutation, unit and linearity identities and annihilates mixed null labels. Thus $\nullspace{d}(S)\subset\nullspace{d}(S^\xi)$. Character evaluation gives reality, covariance and smooth phase values, continuous in~$\xi$.

For positive words~$w_i$ in a common slab set $Y_i=\mathsf B_h(w_i)|_{\ker H_4^{\rm OS}}$. Direct evaluation of the OS form gives

Spectral permanence and character evaluation preserve positivity~\cite[Theorems~2.5.1(1) and~2.6.4]{Landsman1998}; coordinate rotations give every axis. Complete this continuous positive Gram kernel. For the reverse inclusion use the rows of Lemma~\ref{lem:E2-free-tests}, corrected lifts of Lemma~\ref{lem:E2.10} and coefficient limits of Lemma~\ref{lem:F2}. \end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Continuation and boundary distributions in Lemmas~\ref{lem:H2.1} and~\ref{lem:H2.4}]
Use Definitions~\ref{def:D14a}--\ref{def:D15}, Lemmas~\ref{lem:H1.1}--\ref{lem:H1.3} and FC. Vacuum uniqueness follows in the comparison proof. For a label~$a$ put $B_{\xi,\epsilon}(a)=\mathsf B_{2\epsilon}^\xi(a(\epsilon e_4))$. For $\operatorname{Im}z>2\epsilon$ set $G_{\xi,\epsilon}(z,y)=e^{-(\operatorname{Im}z-2\epsilon)H_\xi^{\rm OS}}e^{i\operatorname{Re}zH_\xi^{\rm OS}}U_y^\xi$.

\begin{equation}\label{eq:H-continuation}
\mathsf F_n^\xi(a;z,\vec x)=\langle\Omega_\xi^{\rm OS},B_{\xi,\epsilon}(a_1)
\prod_{j=2}^n\bigl[G_{\xi,\epsilon}(z_j-z_{j-1},\vec x_j-\vec x_{j-1})B_{\xi,\epsilon}(a_j)\bigr]\Omega_\xi^{\rm OS}\rangle\,.

Choose $0<\epsilon<1$ with $4\epsilon<\min_j\operatorname{Im}(z_{j+1}-z_j)$; set $\mathsf F_0^\xi=1$. At imaginary times the insertion rule gives~$S_n^\xi$ independently of~$\epsilon$; the identity theorem extends this agreement. It is holomorphic in time and continuous in space; extend by zero outside ordered times. Taking $\epsilon=\delta/8$ gives

\quad\left(0<\delta\le1,\ y_{j+1}-y_j\ge\delta\right),
\end{equation}
uniformly in~$x$ and~$\xi$.

To construct the boundary, write $\psi_\xi(y;f)=\int\mathsf F_n^\xi(a;x^4+iy,\vec x)f(x)\,dx$, let $\rho_j=j$, and put $\mathcal A=-i\sum_j\rho_j\partial_{x_j^4}$. \begin{equation}\label{eq:H-boundary}
\psi_\xi(y;f)=\sum_{k=0}^m\frac{(-1)^k}{k!}\psi_\xi(y+\rho;\mathcal A^kf)
-\int_0^1\frac{(-t)^m}{m!}\psi_\xi(y+t\rho;\mathcal A^{m+1}f)\,dt\,.
\end{equation}
The integrand is bounded by $C\|\mathcal A^{m+1}f\|_{L^1}/m!$, uniformly in~$y,\xi$. At $y=0$ this defines~$W_n^\xi$. Dominated convergence gives every ordered boundary limit and a common Schwartz seminorm bound.

The mixed operators of Lemma~\ref{lem:H1.1} define $\mathsf F^{\rm mix}$ by~\eqref{eq:H-continuation} and $W^{\rm mix}$ by~\eqref{eq:H-boundary}, with the same bound. Display the $j$th label in $\psi_\xi(y;f;\ldots,a_j,\ldots)$, keeping the others fixed. Differentiating Euclidean $(4,i)$ covariance and using the time Cauchy--Riemann identity gives
\begin{equation*}
\sum_j\psi_\xi(y;f;\ldots,-i\mathsf L(K_{4i})a_j,\ldots)

Define $\widetilde W_n^\xi(b)=W_n^\xi(\widehat b_+(-\cdot))$. For compactly supported smooth frequency tests~$b$, differentiating the height and the exponential multiplier gives cancellation, hence

Let $\chi=\mathbf1_{\{\operatorname{dist}(p,\mathcal V_n)<2\}}*\rho$, where $\rho$ is a nonnegative smooth unit-integral function supported in the unit ball. Then $\chi=1$ near~$\mathcal V_n$, has bounded derivatives, and vanishes at distance greater than three. For consecutive imaginary differences in the open forward cone define
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal W_n^\xi(z)=(2\pi)^{-4n}\widetilde W_n^\xi\left(\chi(p)e^{i\sum_jp_j\cdot z_j}\right)\,.
\end{equation*}
Put $q_j=\sum_{k=j}^np_k$. Then $\sum_jp_j\cdot\operatorname{Im}z_j=\sum_{j\ge2}q_j\cdot\operatorname{Im}(z_j-z_{j-1})\ge c|p|$ on~$\mathcal V_n$. On $\operatorname{supp}\chi$ this holds up to a bounded subtraction, giving a holomorphic Schwartz test. Equation~\eqref{eq:H-Laplace} identifies this with~\eqref{eq:H-continuation}. On the closed cone the damping multipliers act continuously on Schwartz tests. Thus joint tests admit continuous partial boundaries as any consecutive imaginary differences vanish. Set the arity-zero value to one.

Tie the heights of two adjacent spacelike points, retaining strict height differences elsewhere. For their difference $(v,t)$, $|t|<|v|$, an imaginary boost along $v/|v|$ gives imaginary difference $\sin b\,(t\,v/|v|,|v|)$ in the forward cone for small $b>0$; all other differences stay in the cone. The opposite boost treats the exchanged order. Let $\rho_n^\vee$ act on label tensors, so $\mathcal W(\Lambda z)=\rho_n^\vee(\Lambda)\mathcal W(z)$. Define the two germs by
\begin{equation*}
G_\beta(z)=\rho_n^\vee(\Lambda_{i\beta})^{-1}\mathcal W(\Lambda_{i\beta}z),\qquad
N_\beta=\{z:\Lambda_{i\beta}z\text{ lies in that order's tube}\}.
\end{equation*}
Choose the sign of~$\beta$ for that order. The cone inequalities make $N_\beta$ a neighbourhood of the tied point. Real covariance and the boost identity theorem identify $G_\beta$ with the tube function on overlap. This works along the path scaling real times to zero: spacelikeness and the other strict height margins persist. At zero real times, Euclidean permutation symmetry identifies every derivative on the totally real Wick slice, hence the germs. The overlap identities carry this equality through a finite chain covering the compact path. Letting the tied heights vanish gives equality on Schwartz tests supported in the spacelike set, proving locality.

Apply the propagators and insertions in~\eqref{eq:H-continuation} to the vacuum, with an initial heat interval, and smear against a word test~$b$. Reflection and the insertion rule identify mixed-height Gram entries with $\mathsf F^\xi(b^*c)$. Their limits as both height lists vanish are $W^\xi(b^*c)$ by~\eqref{eq:H-boundary}. The vectors are therefore Cauchy; their limits $v_\xi(b)\in\mathcal K_\xi$ have this Gram form. The common seminorm bound and product-test density extend it to joint tests. Hence $W^\xi(F^*F)=\|\sum_b c_bv_\xi(b)\|^2\ge0$.

Complete the word form modulo null vectors to obtain~$\mathcal H_\xi$, with $\Phi_\xi(a,f)[b]=[(a,f)b]$. For null~$x$, the inserted vector pairs with~$c$ as $\langle x,(a^*,\bar f)c\rangle=0$. Taking $c$ to be that vector preserves nullity and gives the adjoint law. Test continuity gives strongly continuous covariance unitaries. Translations integrate to a nondegenerate representation of $C^*(\R^4)=C_0(\R^4)$~\cite[Theorem~3.7.11 and equation~(3.89)]{Landsman1998}; its spectral measure comes from~\cite[Proposition~4.2.3]{Landsman1998}. The Fourier support puts its spectrum in~$\forwardcone$. Locality passes to the word domain. A zero-acting label has zero Euclidean correlations by~\eqref{eq:H-Laplace}, symmetry and continuity; Lemma~\ref{lem:H1.3} and response injectivity give faithfulness. Only vacuum uniqueness remains.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Stress assertion of Lemma~\ref{lem:H2.4}]
Use ST, Lemma~\ref{lem:H1.3} and the continuation. The factors give $T^{\rm M}_{44}=T_{44}$, $T^{\rm M}_{4i}=iT_{4i}$, $T^{\rm M}_{ij}=-T_{ij}$. Euclidean symmetry and~\eqref{eq:H-star} give symmetry and Hermiticity; differentiation and exponentiation of covariance give the Lorentz action. Continuing the derivative identity gives $\Phi_\xi(\iota_{d+1}\partial_a A,f)=-\zeta_a^{-1}\Phi_\xi(\iota_dA,\partial_af)$. Hence Euclidean conservation gives $\sum_a\eta_{aa}T^{\rm M}_{ab}(\partial_af)=0$ on the common word domain.

For real~$c$, insert $c(y)h(t-t_0)$ into ST and integrate conservation on each side of~$t_0$. The $\nabla c$ term stays away from the distinguished point and remains bounded, so $\sum_b\int c(y)K_{\rm E}^b(y,t)T_{4b}(y,t)\,dy$ is Cauchy on each side. Its jump is $\Sigma_KA+\sum_bK_{\rm E}^b\partial_bA$, where $K_{\rm E}^b(y)=\zeta_bK^b(\vec y,iy^4)$.

Continue this jump as follows. Fix strictly ordered heights~$y_j$ for all fields other than the current, including the distinguished field with index~$0$. For their real times~$t_j$ put $z_j(\varsigma)=iy_j+\varsigma t_j$, and place the current at $z_0(\varsigma)\pm i\varepsilon$. The center follows the distinguished time, retaining Euclidean separation~$\varepsilon>0$. Write
\begin{equation*}
I_\varepsilon^\pm(\varsigma)=\sum_b\int c(x)K_{\rm E}^b(x,-iz_0(\varsigma)\pm\varepsilon)
\,\mathsf F_{\pm}^\xi(\ldots,T_{4b}(x,z_0(\varsigma)\pm i\varepsilon),\ldots)\,dx,
\end{equation*}
where $\mathsf F_\pm$ inserts the current immediately after or before the distinguished field. Choose a common complex neighbourhood~$\mathcal U$ of $[0,1]$ preserving the other strict height differences. On $\operatorname{supp}\nabla c$ the spacelike germs bound the side integrals, uniformly for small~$\varepsilon$ and $\varsigma$ in compact $L\subset\mathcal U$. Conservation gives
\begin{equation*}
\sup_{\varsigma\in L}|I_\varepsilon^\pm(\varsigma)-I_{\varepsilon'}^\pm(\varsigma)|\le C_L|\varepsilon-\varepsilon'|.
\end{equation*}
The limits are holomorphic. At small imaginary~$\varsigma$ the Euclidean calculation identifies their jump; the identity theorem extends it to $\varsigma=1$. Test against $m,f$ and take the continuous partial boundaries. Integration by parts differentiates $K^am$, giving~\eqref{eq:H-charge}. Schwartz cutoffs and the joint bounds extend from compact spectators. For complex~$c$, its imaginary part has charge commuting with the distinguished field by locality. Constant and rotational~$K$ give the required actions, retaining~$\xi_*$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Comparison and low energy: Lemmas~\ref{lem:H2.2} and~\ref{lem:H2.3}]
Use Definitions~\ref{def:W0}--\ref{def:W3} and~\ref{def:D14d}, with Lemma~\ref{lem:H1.3}.

The isometry $J=\mathsf J_\xi^{\rm OS}:[b]\mapsto v_\xi(b)$ takes vacuum to vacuum and intertwines spatial translations and heat: $Je^{-sH_\xi}=E_s^\xi J$ by~\eqref{eq:H-Laplace}. If two linear isometries have these boundary-word values, they agree on the dense word span and hence everywhere. After surjectivity, strong heat convergence identifies ground projections.

For compact frequency~$b$, let $f_y=\widehat{e^{-\sum_jy_jp_j^4}b}_+(-\cdot)$. Equation~\eqref{eq:H-Laplace} equates the norms and mixed pairing of its boundary-word image under~$J$ and the corresponding positive-height OS vector. They therefore agree. Frequency-test density and closed range include every smeared positive-height vector; approximate identities give point vectors. Permutation and perturbation of equal times give all positive words, proving surjectivity.

We prove the low-energy estimate for compactly supported words, subtracting the full zero-energy projection $Q_\xi=\mathsf E_\xi(\{\mathbf p^4=0\})$. Let $A^\dagger$ be its word adjoint, reversing order and applying label and test conjugation, and put $\alpha_zA=\mathsf T_\xi(z)A\mathsf T_\xi(z)^*$. Bound all support times and spatial norms by~$r$. Set $R=4r+1$, $\mu=\mu_{A\Omega_\xi}$ and $\lambda=\mu_{A^\dagger\Omega_\xi}$. The common distribution bounds give $\mu(\R^4)+\lambda(\R^4)\le M$, uniformly in~$\xi$. Covariance and locality imply
\begin{equation*}
a(y,t)=\widehat\mu_+(y,t)-\widehat\lambda_+(-y,-t)
=\langle\Omega_\xi,[A^\dagger,\alpha_{(y,t)}A]\Omega_\xi\rangle=0
\quad(|y|>R,\ |t|\le1)\,.
\end{equation*}
Here $\widehat\mu_+(z)=\int e^{ip\cdot z}\,d\mu(p)$, and likewise for~$\lambda$. Write $E=p^4$ for energy. The time average $\bar a(y)=\int_0^1[a(y,t)-a(y,-t)]/(2i)\,dt$ is bounded by~$M$ and supported in~$\overline B_R$. It is the Fourier transform of two positive pushforwards: $\pi(E)d\mu$ under $p\mapsto\vec p$ and $\pi(E)d\lambda$ under $p\mapsto-\vec p$, where $\pi(E)=(1-\cos E)/E$, $\pi(0)=0$. On $0\le E\le1$, $\pi(E)\ge E/\pi$.

Choose a smooth momentum bump $b_u$, equal to one on $B_u$, supported in~$B_{2u}$ and between zero and one. Its transform $\fourierplus{b_u}(-y)$ has absolute value at most $8|B_1|u^3$. Fourier inversion and positivity therefore give
\begin{equation*}
(2\pi)^3\frac{u}{2\pi}\,\mu(\{u/2<E\le u\})
\le\left|\int\bar a(y)\fourierplus{b_u}(-y)\,dy\right|
\le8|B_1||B_R|M u^3\,.
\end{equation*}
Here $|\vec p|\le E$ on the spectral cone makes $b_u=1$ on the indicated shell. Summing shells $u2^{-j}$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H-low}
\mu(\{0<E\le u\})\le C R^3M u^2\qquad(0<u\le1)\,.
\end{equation}
For $u>1$ use the total mass bound. Since $e^{-sE}=\int_E^\infty se^{-st}\,dt$, Tonelli gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H-heat}
0\le\langle A\Omega_\xi,(e^{-sH_\xi}-Q_\xi)A\Omega_\xi\rangle
=\int_{E>0}e^{-sE}\,d\mu
\le C_A s\int_0^\infty t^2e^{-st}\,dt=2C_As^{-2}\,.
\end{equation}

The heat pairing is continuous in~$\xi$:~\eqref{eq:H-boundary} gives continuous Schwartz frequency cutoffs, with error $Me^{-sN}$ outside radius~$2N$ on~$\forwardcone$. Subtract $|\langle\Omega_\xi,A\Omega_\xi\rangle|^2$. Almost-everywhere uniqueness and full support extend~\eqref{eq:H-heat} to this continuous pairing in every phase. Let $s\to\infty$ to obtain
\begin{equation*}
\|Q_\xi A\Omega_\xi\|^2=|\langle\Omega_\xi,A\Omega_\xi\rangle|^2\,.
\end{equation*}
Since $Q_\xi\Omega_\xi=\Omega_\xi$, density of compact word vectors gives $Q_\xi=P_{0,\xi}$. Surjectivity and heat intertwining give the OS vacuum line, completing W0.

For irreducibility, let $B$ satisfy the weak relation in Lemma~\ref{lem:H2.2}. The field adjoint law and iteration move words across matrix entries without requiring $B$ to preserve the domain. For a word~$A$, put $u=A\Omega_\xi$, $v=A^\dagger\Omega_\xi$ and, in the upper half-plane,
\begin{equation*}
p(z)=\langle u,e^{izH_\xi}B\Omega_\xi\rangle,\qquad
q(z)=\langle B^*\Omega_\xi,e^{izH_\xi}v\rangle\,.
\end{equation*}
These functions are bounded and continuous to the boundary, where covariance and the weak relation give $p(t)=q(-t)$. Morera and Liouville make their bounded pasted continuation constant. Hence $\langle u,e^{-sH_\xi}B\Omega_\xi\rangle=\langle u,B\Omega_\xi\rangle$. Heat convergence gives $B\Omega_\xi=b\Omega_\xi$. Moving words again gives $\langle A'\Omega_\xi,BA\Omega_\xi\rangle=b\langle A'\Omega_\xi,A\Omega_\xi\rangle$, so $B=b1$ by density. A scalar satisfies the relation by the adjoint law. This proves the equivalence in Lemma~\ref{lem:H2.2}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{prop:RC}]
Lemmas~\ref{lem:H2.1} and~\ref{lem:H2.2} prove the faithful W0--W3 theories, Definitions~\ref{def:D14f}--\ref{def:D15}, the unitary comparison, vacuum lines and scalar weak commutant required by Theorem~\ref{prop:RC}. Lemmas~\ref{lem:H2.3} and~\ref{lem:H2.4} supply the uniform rates, phase averages and continued stress, retaining~$\xi_*$.
\end{proof}
