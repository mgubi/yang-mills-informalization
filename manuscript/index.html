📄 Second draft (12 November 2026), 265 pages. Revised after expert and independent reviews; every section has been through its review cycle. Sections may still change until they are marked checked. Do not cite it as a finished proof.

A manuscript, written in public, giving a complete mathematical proof of the result formalized in OpenAI's Lean proof of the construction of four-dimensional quantum Yang–Mills theory (source, commit 3456e5e). The reader we write for is a mathematician working in constructive field theory or probability. It is written in collaboration with Martin Hairer's humanise-yang-mills project, a crowd-sourced effort to turn the Lean formalization into a human-readable monograph.

  • Part I. The theorem and the architecture of its proof (about 40 pages, with the main theorem on p. 14; Sections 8 and 9 give the exact formal statement and the constants): the setting and notation, the main theorem, the principal statements on which the proof rests, each stated exactly with the idea of its proof, and the complete deduction of the main theorem from them.

  • Part II. Proofs (about 220 pages, Sections 10–19): the proofs of the principal propositions, with every estimate specific to the construction written out and standard results cited.

The PDF is main.pdf. It is updated as sections are written; until a section is marked checked below, it is a draft.

The process by which it is written and checked is described in How this manuscript is written.

How it is written

The manuscript is written with AI models under human direction, by the process described in How this manuscript is written. In short: the paper claims nothing the Lean does not prove; statements are fixed first and kept short; writers propose, one editor integrates; and every statement is reviewed against the Lean by a separate reviewer. The correspondence between the numbered statements and the Lean declarations will be published with the manuscript.

For shorter routes into the proof, see the rest of this repository, which presents it at several levels of detail, from the theorem in one page to outlines of its thirty-two units linked to the Lean; this manuscript is the most detailed level. Corrections are welcome as issues.
