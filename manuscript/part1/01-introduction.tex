\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

We construct continuum correlation functions of gauge-invariant local fields from four-dimensional Wilson lattice gauge theory. The lattice spacing is removed at each fixed physical volume; along a refinement of any prescribed increasing sequence of volumes, a subsequent limit gives a mixture of phases. Each phase has a relativistic reconstruction with positive energy, a unique vacuum and local fields. The same construction gives a conserved stress tensor, a quantitative operator product expansion and a short-distance law for the centered stress trace that excludes the finite Wick profiles defined below. The precise existence and construction statements are Theorems~\ref{thm:A} and~\ref{thm:B}.

Let $G\subset U(n_G)$ be a closed matrix group whose real Lie algebra $\g=\operatorname{Lie}(G)$ is simple and nonabelian; connectedness of~$G$ is not required. On the periodic lattice $\Lambda_s=(\Z/s\Z)^4$, a configuration assigns $U(y,a)\in G$ to the positive link from~$y$ in coordinate direction~$a$, with inverse on the reversed link. Here $a=1,\ldots,4$ and $e_a$ is the corresponding coordinate vector. For $\beta>0$, the plaquette holonomy and Wilson measure are
\begin{align*}
U_{y;ab}&=U(y,a)U(y+e_a,b)U(y+e_b,a)^{-1}U(y,b)^{-1},\\
E_s^{\rm W}(U)&=\sum_{y\in\Lambda_s}\sum_{a<b}\bigl(1-n_G^{-1}\operatorname{Re}\tr U_{y;ab}\bigr),\\
d\mu_{s,\beta}(U)&=Z_{s,\beta}^{-1}e^{-\beta E_s^{\rm W}(U)}\prod_{y,a}dh(U(y,a))\,.
\end{align*}
The measure~$dh$ is probability Haar measure, and $Z_{s,\beta}$ is the integral normalizing the density. Thus~$\beta$ penalizes curvature around plaquettes. Section~\ref{sec:wilson} fixes the normalization and defines the lattice observables.

The construction chooses increasing integers~$D_N,L_p$, spacings~$a_N=D_N^{-1}$ and bare couplings~$\beta_{N,p}$, and uses~$\mu_{N,p}=\mu_{L_pD_N,\beta_{N,p}}$. The physical torus has side~$L_p$. The bare inverse coupling~$\beta_{N,p}$ is tuned separately for each volume so that blocking reaches one common, sufficiently large reference value~$\bar\beta$ at level zero. This level has spacing~$a_0=1$ and fixes the reference length unit; the theorem does not identify it with a measured mass scale. For the trace metric $\langle X,Y\rangle_\g=-n_G^{-1}\operatorname{Re}\tr(XY)$, let $(e_\alpha^\g)$ be an orthonormal basis and put
\begin{equation*}
\dimG=\dim_\R\g,\qquad
\casimir=-\dimG^{-1}\sum_\alpha\operatorname{tr}_\g\bigl(\operatorname{ad}(e_\alpha^\g)^2\bigr),\qquad
b_0=\frac{11\casimir}{48\pi^2}\,.
\end{equation*}
Here $\operatorname{ad}(X)Y=[X,Y]$, and $\operatorname{tr}_\g$ is the real operator trace. These constants specify the running law proved by the construction:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-running}
\frac{\beta_{N,p}}{\log(1/a_N)}\longrightarrow\slope=2b_0
\qquad(N\to\infty),\quad\text{uniformly in }p\,.
\end{equation}
An insertion label is a pair~$(d,\mathbf c)$, where $\mathbf c$ specifies an invariant connection-jet polynomial of engineering weight at most~$d$. These polynomials describe gauge-invariant local expressions in curvature and its covariant derivatives, such as curvature square. Their engineering degree counts curvature with weight two and each covariant derivative with weight one. Their normalization is fixed by prescribing the coefficient vector at a reference level and inverting its evolution under blocking; the Maxwell comparison identifies the resulting short-distance fields afterwards. Each has a fixed finite stencil, is continuous and gauge invariant, and has coefficients depending on the cutoff and volume. Definition~\ref{def:D08} gives their formula; Propositions~\ref{prop:MC} and~\ref{prop:SC} identify their short-distance comparison with invariant polynomials in free Maxwell curvature.

The renormalization flow stops at level zero. Reflection positivity and the chessboard estimate give bounds uniform in the physical volume; compactness then supplies the volume refinement, without a mass gap (Sections~\ref{sec:partII-E.2} and~\ref{sec:partII-F}).

In words, Theorem~\ref{thm:A} asserts that one regular Wilson family, with continuous gauge-invariant insertions on fixed finite stencils and a positive volume-uniform logarithmic running slope, can be chosen before any increasing sequence of volume indices is prescribed. Every such sequence has a refinement on which the correlations converge to a phase average. A selected phase continues to a nonzero Wightman theory, and the same hierarchy has an operator product expansion and a centered degree-four field with the trace asymptotics below. Theorem~\ref{thm:A} requires a positive logarithmic running slope; the particular value in~\eqref{eq:intro-running} is supplied by Theorem~\ref{thm:B}.

To spell out the order of limits, let $v=(v_1,\ldots,v_n)$ be a finite list of lattice field labels, with insertions $I_{N,p}(v_i,y_i;U)$. Their smeared correlation is
\begin{equation*}
\latsmear_{N,p}(v;f)=a_N^{4n}\sum_{y\in\Lambda_{L_pD_N}^n}f(\hat y)
\int\prod_{i=1}^n I_{N,p}(v_i,y_i;U)\,d\mu_{N,p}(U)\,.
\end{equation*}
Here $\hat y_i=a_Nr_{L_pD_N}(y_i)$, where $r_s$ takes the integer representative in $(-s/2,s/2]$ coordinatewise. Take a smooth compactly supported test~$f$ whose support avoids every collision $x_i=x_j$. For every prescribed strictly increasing~$\mathsf p:\N\to\N$, a strictly increasing refinement~$\mathsf q$ gives retained indices $P=\mathsf p\circ\mathsf q$ and limiting kernels~$S_n(v;x)$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-ordered-limit}
\lim_{k\to\infty}\left(\lim_{N\to\infty}\latsmear_{N,P(k)}(v;f)\right)
=\int f(x)S_n(v;x)\,dx\,.
\end{equation}
The inner limit exists along the full cutoff sequence at every volume containing the support of~$f$ in its open centered cell. One refinement serves all field lists and all such tests. Theorems~\ref{prop:UV} and~\ref{prop:IV} prove these two limits; Definition~\ref{def:D09} states the convergence precisely.

Theorem~\ref{thm:B} identifies more of this construction. The fields have finite-dimensional spaces in each degree and a conserved symmetric stress tensor normalized by its translation and rotation fluxes. A shrinking cluster has an expansion into local fields with coefficients independent of the spectators and of the phase. Theorem~\ref{prop:OP} gives the truncation error and its uniformity over separated positions, phases and the mixture. Contact distributions extend these correlations through collision diagonals without changing their values away from collisions; the field spaces and the separated correlation functions are those already constructed.

Write $S^\xi$ for the phase correlations. Their compact parameter space~$\phases$ carries a full-support probability measure~$\nu$, and the mixed correlations are their~$\nu$-average. Each phase reconstructs fields on common observable labels. These fields act on a cyclic domain in a Hilbert space, satisfy Poincar\'e covariance and spacelike commutativity, and have energy-momentum spectrum in the forward cone. The reconstruction is faithful: every nonzero observable label gives a nonzero smeared field for some test function. Theorem~\ref{prop:RC} states the reconstruction and its relation to the Euclidean functions.

The construction selects a phase~$\xi_*$ with a one-dimensional time-axis ground space before proving reconstruction in every phase. Theorem~\ref{prop:TR} states the trace law only in this selected phase. Its comparison estimates are uniform in the phase during the proof, but we do not assert an almost-everywhere or every-phase trace theorem.

For the stress tensor~$T_{ab}$ in that phase, define
\begin{equation*}
\tau=\sum_{a=1}^4T_{aa},\qquad
\Theta=\tau-S^{\xi_*}(\tau)\unitfield{4},\qquad
\tracetwopoint(r)=S_2^{\xi_*}(\Theta,\Theta;-re_4/2,re_4/2)\,,
\end{equation*}
where $\unitfield{4}$ is the unit field. Centering removes the selected phase's vacuum expectation. This field expresses the trace anomaly: the free Maxwell stress tensor is traceless, whereas the comparison below has a nonzero curvature-square coefficient of order~$1/\log(1/r)$. Its square gives the inverse logarithm squared in the two-point law. The short-distance conclusion is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-trace-law}
\lim_{r\downarrow0}r^8\log^2(1/r)\tracetwopoint(r)=\frac{3\dimG}{\pi^4}>0\,.
\end{equation}
The limit holds through all positive radii. Theorem~\ref{prop:TR} proves it, and Proposition~\ref{prop:NF} shows that~$\tracetwopoint$ agrees with no finite Wick profile on any interval~$(0,\delta)$. Such profiles are finite sums of finite products of mass-shell Laplace kernels weighted by energy powers and homogeneous momentum factors, as specified in Definition~\ref{def:D17}. This is the precise free-field comparison excluded here.

\subsection{Scope}

The Clay Yang--Mills existence and mass gap problem~\cite[\S4]{JaffeWitten2006} asks for both existence and a positive mass gap. The present construction gives Wightman existence in each phase; no mass gap is proved. Uniqueness of the vacuum in a reconstructed phase means that the zero-energy space is one-dimensional; it gives no positive lower bound on the remaining energy spectrum. Nor do we prove uniqueness of the infinite-volume limit: the limiting table may depend on~$\mathsf p$ and~$\mathsf q$. The phase space is not shown to be a singleton, and the selected phase is not shown to be unique. Nor is non-Gaussianity proved in general: the trace law excludes finite Wick profiles, but a generalized free field with the spectral measure of~$\Theta$ has the same two-point function.

The construction also depends on a positive block penalty~$\blockpenalty$, which weights the discrepancy between fine transport and coarse links in the probability kernel of Definition~\ref{def:D04}. Theorem~\ref{thm:B} holds for every fixed~$\blockpenalty>0$; independence of the resulting theory from this parameter is not proved. Theorem~\ref{thm:A} follows, for example, by fixing~$\blockpenalty=1$.

Convergence in~\eqref{eq:intro-ordered-limit} is tested on separated configurations. The contact extensions are additional distributions, constructed after these limits; the theorem does not assert convergence of the unsubtracted lattice correlations against tests meeting collision diagonals. The product theorem gives an estimate at each finite truncation degree, rather than convergence as that degree tends to infinity. Neither uniqueness, positivity of the extension nor contact Ward identities are asserted.

\subsection{Earlier work and the method}

Wilson's lattice formulation~\cite{Wilson1974} makes the finite-cutoff theory a probability law on compact group-valued links. Ba\l{}aban's programme supplies much of the constructive gauge-theory setting: nonlinear group averages, gauge fixing, stationary backgrounds, local propagators and marginal curvature-square extraction. Analytic stationary backgrounds are treated in~\cite[Theorem~1 and Proposition~6]{Balaban1985Variational}; symmetric block kernels and the marginal analysis occur in~\cite[pp.~252--254 and \S\S4--5]{Balaban1987RG}. The large-field result~\cite[Theorem~1, p.~355]{Balaban1989Large} assumes sufficiently small effective couplings, and its discussion on p.~356 leaves physical observables for further analysis. The present construction controls running couplings and local composite fields together.

Magnen, Rivasseau and S\'en\'eor~\cite{MagnenRivasseauSeneor1993} give a four-dimensional ultraviolet construction for pure $SU(2)$ theory in the trivial topological sector, in a regularized axial gauge with a fixed infrared cutoff. They control background-dependent normalization and formulate a gauge-fixed Schwinger-function limit with Slavnov identities. Their account explicitly defers a fully detailed convergence proof (p.~327), retains the infrared cutoff (p.~327), and sets aside renormalized physical composite observables (p.~329). The endpoint here concerns an infinite-volume hierarchy of gauge-invariant local fields, together with its phases, stress tensor and local products.

Several operations used below have antecedents beyond gauge theory. Gaw\k{e}dzki and Kupiainen's critical lattice $\varphi^4_4$ construction~\cite[p.~212, (4.8), and pp.~218--221]{GawedzkiKupiainen1985} uses disjoint polymers covering the large-field region and selective exponentiation of the remaining terms; its asymptotic freedom is an infrared statement for a scalar model. Brydges and Yau~\cite[\S\S1--2 and pp.~378--384]{BrydgesYau1990} give exact polymer extraction and reblocking identities, analytic norms and Gaussian integration estimates. Covering the bad region, extracting marginal terms and restoring the interaction class are established operations.

The particular effective-density representation used here is closely related to OpenAI's 2026 sigma-model papers, especially the exact map in~\cite[\S5, pp.~38--55]{OpenAI2026O4} and its adaptation in~\cite[\S3.3, pp.~23--28]{OpenAI2026O3}. The former combines a clipped kinetic term, localized quadratic terms, regular errors and a compulsory covering sum, with an exact coupling reset. Those features have gauge-theoretic counterparts below. The comparison concerns their combination; it does not assign these operations a new origin: compulsory covering and selective exponentiation already occur in~\cite[p.~212]{GawedzkiKupiainen1985}. The sigma-model papers have different infrared conclusions, which are not inputs here.

Reflection positivity of the Wilson measure and its transfer matrix go back to Osterwalder--Seiler~\cite{OsterwalderSeiler1978}; see~\cite[\S2a, Theorem~2.1]{Seiler1982}. Chessboard estimates are due to Fr\"ohlich--Israel--Lieb--Simon~\cite[\S4, Theorems~4.1--4.2]{FrohlichIsraelLiebSimon1978}; \cite[Theorem~2.2]{Seiler1982} gives a lattice version for local matter functions. Analytic continuation follows the Osterwalder--Schrader programme~\cite{OsterwalderSchrader1973,OsterwalderSchrader1975}, including the growth and boundary-value issues addressed in the second paper. Section~\ref{sec:partII-H} supplies the slab and distribution estimates for these fields. Reconstruction does not follow from reflection positivity alone.

The logarithmic running in~\eqref{eq:intro-running} has the one-loop slope of the asymptotic freedom found perturbatively by Gross--Wilczek and Politzer~\cite{GrossWilczek1973,Politzer1973}; the background-field coefficient is reviewed in~\cite[\S3.3]{Abbott1982}. What requires proof here is its compatibility with the exact gauge block map, coupling reset and terminal-coupling tuning, followed by continuum identifications on one family. The substantive conclusions are simultaneous control of composite fields, restoration of rotations through stress-tensor fluxes, the all-radii trace law and product bounds uniform over phases. Operator product expansions with state-independent coefficients go back to Wilson~\cite[\S II]{Wilson1969}, the trace anomaly to Collins--Duncan--Joglekar~\cite[(3.19)--(3.21)]{CollinsDuncanJoglekar1977}, and Taylor subtraction at collision diagonals to~\cite[\S5.2]{BrunettiFredenhagen2000}. We make no priority claim for these renormalization-group principles or physical predictions.

\subsection{Outline of the proof}\label{sec:intro-outline}

The deduction in Section~\ref{sec:main-theorems} fixes one lattice family, takes its limits, and identifies the resulting fields. The estimates apply to that same family throughout. We describe their roles using the tags in Table~\ref{tab:principal-results}.

\paragraph{Exact integration and the running coupling.}
Section~\ref{sec:partII-A} realizes invariant connection-jet polynomials as local lattice functions, supplying the observables' finite stencils. Section~\ref{sec:partII-B} constructs a normalized probability kernel relating fine and coarse links, with symmetric gauge choices and alternatives where minimizing branches fail. Normalization preserves the partition integral exactly. The resulting density is described by a curvature action, a vacuum scalar, regular local terms and terms covering every large-field site.

On the small-field region, integration about a stationary background gives a Gaussian integral with determinant and Haar-coordinate factors. A large-field lower bound pays for the terms forced to cover the exceptional region. Section~\ref{sec:partII-C} matches the Wilson energy through fourth order, extracts the one-loop curvature-square coefficient and resets the kinetic coupling. Section~\ref{sec:partII-D} assembles the local terms and proves RG: exact integration returns a density in the next controlled class. For running coupling~$\beta_{N,p,m}$ and odd block ratio~$\ell_m$, the drift is
\begin{equation*}
\beta_{N,p,m}=\beta_{N,p,m+1}-\slope\log\ell_m+e_{N,p,m}\,,
\end{equation*}
with the error bounded as in RG. Blocking lowers the level from~$m+1$ to~$m$.

Section~\ref{sec:partII-E.1} starts from the Wilson density and varies the bare coupling continuously. Successive restrictions to parameter intervals keep the running coupling in its allowed bands and reach one common terminal value~$\bar\beta$ at level zero, proving MT. Summing the drift gives~\eqref{eq:intro-running}. At each fixed level the couplings and activity coordinates converge as the cutoff is removed (Proposition~\ref{prop:fixed-level}); this permits comparison of different cutoffs. These choices precede the prescribed volume sequence.

\paragraph{Local fields and the two limits.}
Section~\ref{sec:partII-E.2} follows insertions through the integration. An invertible map to their coefficients at a fixed reference level determines their normalization. Estimates for densities carrying the insertions follow their size through blocking; combined with chessboard bounds, they give UB, the volume-uniform bounds on correlations and physical lattice differences. In Section~\ref{sec:partII-E.3}, fixed-level convergence compares normalized integrals at a common intermediate level. Increasing that level makes the comparison error small; taking both cutoffs large afterwards makes the original correlations Cauchy. UB extends convergence to all separated positions and derivatives. This proves UV along the full sequence at fixed volume.

Section~\ref{sec:partII-F} uses these bounds for a diagonal extraction along the prescribed volumes, followed by a further refinement fixing the corrected coefficient maps; together they give~$\mathsf q$ and prove IV. Reflection positivity passes to the limit, and corrected coefficient maps identify inclusions and derivatives between degrees. For invariant polynomial labels~$V_d$ of weight at most~$d$, the observable fields are
\begin{equation*}
F_d=V_d/\nullspace{d}(S)\,,
\end{equation*}
where $\nullspace{d}(S)$ consists of labels whose every separated correlation with arbitrary spectators vanishes. The quotient retains the fields detectable by correlations. FC gives inclusions, positional derivatives and dimensions.

\paragraph{Stress, rotations and phases.}
Section~\ref{sec:partII-G} compares short-distance correlations with free Maxwell fields, proving MC. The comparison identifies a line of conserved degree-four tensors. Their shell fluxes around one insertion act on fields; identifying these actions with translations and rotations fixes the stress normalization. The angular flux through a distant enclosing surface tends to zero; the remaining insertion fluxes give the rotation identities and recover full rotation covariance. This proves ST; SC adds differentiated comparison with the normalized stress insertions.

Section~\ref{sec:partII-H} bounds insertion operators between positive-time slabs and projects their action to the zero-energy subspace of the time-translation generator. The resulting commutative algebra has compact character space~$\phases$; its vacuum state gives~$\nu$, and its characters give the phase correlations. This is PH, on the same quotient fields. Select~$\xi_*$ from the full-measure set with one-dimensional time-axis ground space. Continuation and boundary estimates prove RC. Uniform low-energy estimates and continuity in the phase extend vacuum uniqueness to every character. The selected theory supplies the relativistic part of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}.

\paragraph{The trace and local products.}
Section~\ref{sec:partII-I} centers the stress trace in the selected phase. After multiplying each trace insertion by~$r^4$, its leading coefficient in the free curvature-square comparison is asymptotic to $-1/\log(1/r)$. The free two-point amplitude is $3\dimG/\pi^4$; squaring the coefficient gives~\eqref{eq:intro-trace-law}. The comparison can be stopped at levels chosen for an arbitrary sequence of radii. Positivity of the spectral measure makes the time-axis two-point function monotone, allowing nearby comparison radii to bound it from both sides and prove TR through all radii. Finite Wick profiles have finite-order asymptotic expansions in integer powers of the radius and nonnegative logarithmic powers. Their incompatibility with the nonzero inverse-logarithmic limit proves NF.

Section~\ref{sec:partII-J} recovers fields from finitely many correlations against fixed probe words and uses these projections to define product coefficients. Bounds in the compression algebra pass to every character and the mixture, proving OP with spectator-independent coefficients. The section treats nested clusters and constructs contact extensions by subtracting Taylor jets along collision diagonals. The contact projections and their clearance are separate choices; they use the existing field spaces and agree with the existing correlations away from collisions. This proves CT and completes Theorem~\ref{thm:B}.

To obtain the hierarchy data of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}, the deduction maps a lattice label to its quotient field by $\pi_{\rm quo}(d,\mathbf c)=(d,[\mathbf c])$ and assigns arbitrary correlation values on collision diagonals. Representative independence identifies the lattice limit with $S^{\rm mix}\circ\pi_{\rm quo}$. The phase decomposition and reconstruction give Q2--Q4, the product theorem gives Q5, and TR with NF gives Q6. Fixing~$\blockpenalty=1$ gives Theorem~\ref{thm:A}.

\subsection{Notation and conventions}

Throughout, $\N=\{0,1,\ldots\}$, coordinate indices run from $1$ to $4$, and $e_a$ is the $a$th coordinate vector. Time is the fourth coordinate. For the later relativistic statements write
\begin{equation*}
\minksquare(x)=(x^4)^2-|\vec x|^2,\qquad
\forwardcone=\{\mathbf p:|\vec{\mathbf p}|\le\mathbf p^4\}\,.
\end{equation*}
Inner products are antilinear in the first variable, and spacetime integrals use Lebesgue measure. Arity zero is included; empty sums are zero and empty products are one. The group $\hypercubic$ consists of the signed permutation matrices; $\mathsf s_a^{\rm refl}$ denotes reflection in coordinate~$a$.
