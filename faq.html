Does this prove a mass gap?

No mass gap is proved. The public theorem gives a spectrum condition and a unique invariant vacuum in a selected phase, but neither supplies a positive lower bound on nonzero excitation energies. The detailed construction's low-energy and heat bounds are polynomial; they do not establish either a gap or gaplessness. See what is not claimed and the reconstruction account.

Is this a solution of the Clay Millennium problem?

It does not establish the full Yang–Mills existence and mass-gap claim discussed in the Jaffe–Witten formulation: the mass gap is absent from its conclusions. It constructs theories with the properties specified on the statement page, subject to the qualifications there. The comparison with Jaffe–Witten explains the differences; this repository does not certify a prize claim.

What makes the constructed theory Yang–Mills?

Its mixed Euclidean correlations come from Wilson lattice gauge theories for the specified compact matrix group, with local gauge-invariant insertions and logarithmically growing bare inverse coupling. The construction identifies local fields with quotients of curvature-polynomial labels, constructs a normalized stress tensor and proves short-distance comparison with free Maxwell fields. Some of these identifications belong to the construction rather than the public theorem itself. See the objects and their interpretation and the additional conclusions.

What is new here?

The mathematical result presented here is the construction with the precise limits, reconstruction, OPE and trace properties in the statement. The summary's discussion of earlier work describes features of this argument, including the common family of observables, fixed-volume convergence and recovery of continuous symmetry. These descriptions are not a systematic literature comparison or a claim of priority for each ingredient. This repository's contribution is an explanatory map of the released formal proof; the full manuscript is under construction. For a component-by-component account of which parts of the proof follow Balaban's programme, which follow OpenAI's 2026 papers on two-dimensional sigma models, which are standard and which appear new, see Where the ideas come from.

Why is there only a subsequence of volumes?

The infinite-volume argument uses compactness to extract a convergent subsequence from any prescribed increasing sequence of volume indices. It does not show that different extractions give the same limit. By contrast, at each admissible fixed volume the ultraviolet limit uses the whole construction-chosen sequence of spacings. See ordered convergence, IV.1 and the summary.

Why are there phases, and how is the selected phase chosen?

The lattice limit is a mixed correlation hierarchy, represented as an average over a compact space of phases with a probability measure of full support. In the construction the selected phase is chosen only to have a one-dimensional ground-state space; no trace condition is imposed in that choice. The public theorem reconstructs that selected phase, while the detailed theorem reconstructs every phase. Neither equality of the phases nor uniqueness of the infinite-volume construction follows from the unique vacuum in each reconstructed theory. See phase selection, V.1 and V.3.

What exactly does “not free” mean?

The selected two-point function of a centered degree-4 field on the time axis has a positive \(r^{- 8} \log^{- 2} (1 / r)\) leading law. No finite sum of the products of mass-shell kernels specified in Definition 1.15 agrees with that function throughout any interval next to zero. This does not exclude continuous-mass generalized free fields, infinite sums or every composite of Gaussian fields. The detailed construction supplies additional Wick and Maxwell tests with their own hypotheses; see the trace test and D9.

Why correlations rather than a measure on connections?

The constructed continuum objects are correlation functions away from coincident points, their phase decomposition and reconstructed relativistic fields. The detailed theorem also supplies distributional extensions across coincident points, without asserting uniqueness of those extensions. It does not construct a continuum probability measure on connections; this is a limit of what was proved, not a claim that such a measure cannot exist. See the nonclaims and OPE and contact extensions.

Does the OPE agree with perturbation theory to all orders?

No such agreement is proved. For every finite truncation the theorem gives coefficient and remainder bounds on separated configurations, with one coefficient system for the mixed state and every phase and for all spectators. The small-radius threshold can depend on the spectator labels and separation parameters. Coefficient uniqueness and convergence of the infinite expansion are not asserted; see the exact OPE and VII.4.

What was checked by Lean, and what by people or AI?

Lean's kernel checks formal proof terms against formal types; the released verification log records type, axiom and kernel-replay checks for seventeen named results. The statement page, summaries, glossary and outlines are AI-assisted interpretations and explanations, with the review scopes described in method.md. The Yang–Mills group's assessment is a separate account of its validation. Collaboration on this repository does not mean that every outline has received an independent human proof review.

Is the English statement faithful to the Lean?

The public theorem on statement.md was transcribed and compared definition by definition with the pinned Lean, including quantifiers, field spaces and the order of choices. This is a documented interpretation check, and the page exposes both the formal text and its source correspondence for inspection. The additional construction clauses D1–D16 have a narrower review scope: D3 and D5–D15 were not all unfolded to the last definition. A Lean check of the proof does not itself verify any English transcription.

How much should I trust an edited outline?

“Edited” records a third round of AI review of the exposition after drafting and source checking. It does not mean every subsidiary analytic estimate was independently read or that the outline is a complete informal proof. Each unit's “What remains unexplained” section states local limits, and a draft outline does not imply its formal theorem is unproved. See status meanings, the unit index and the summary's incomplete steps.

Does the dependency graph verify the explanations?

No: it records declaration references in elaborated types and proof terms. Agreement with OpenAI's record tests the reachable sets for the seventeen named results, not every edge, the mathematical role of each reference or an English proof sketch. Cohen's κ = 0.72 concerns agreement between readers' importance labels; it is not a correctness score. See graph scope and statement selection.

How can I check the result myself?

For a mathematical clause, the clause-to-explanation guide connects its exact definition to outline results, the summary and pinned Lean references. For formal verification, method.md gives a fresh-checkout build and the released type, axiom and replay checks. The compiled release helps load and explore the source, but reusing those files is not an independent compilation. Reading an outline and rebuilding a formal proof answer different questions, as the table below explains.

How do I report a mismatch or cite what I read?

Open an issue with the page and heading, the sentence in question, and a proposed correction or pinned Lean link. Include the repository revision and unit or result identifier so that the wording can be recovered after later edits. Use CITATION.cff and the citation guidance; change notes record reader-facing corrections.
